Flood Safety for Deerfield Beach Renters After November's Deluge

After flooding, Deerfield Beach renters should stay out of standing water, avoid wet electrical equipment, leave immediately if they smell gas and photograph damage before moving or cleaning anything. Notify the landlord or property manager in writing the same day. Then contact your insurer and begin drying the home only after the electrical system and structure are safe.

Receding water does not mean a rental is ready to occupy. Hidden moisture, contaminated materials, damaged wiring and unstable drywall can remain dangerous after the street outside looks normal.

What happened in Deerfield Beach on November 18 and 19

Flooding disrupted daily life across Broward County

Heavy rain affected southeast Florida on November 18 and 19, 2026. The National Weather Service in Miami reported ongoing flash flooding in parts of Miami-Dade and Broward counties and kept a Flood Watch in effect across southeast Florida through Thursday evening.

The watch specifically included Deerfield Beach and warned that excessive runoff could flood urban, low-lying and poorly drained locations. Broward County public schools and district offices were closed on both Wednesday and Thursday because of excessive rain and flooding, according to BocaNewsNow.

Those closures give renters some context for the severity of the weather. When schools, roads and public facilities are disrupted, a damp apartment, townhouse or house should not be treated as an ordinary maintenance problem. Conditions may still be changing, especially around low spots, canal-adjacent streets, parking lots and ground-floor entrances.

The threat extended beyond the rain

The National Weather Service also warned of strong coastal winds, dangerous surf and rip currents. Its November 18 advisory listed east winds of 25 to 30 mph with gusts up to 45 mph in affected coastal and metro areas.

While inspecting a rental, check for water at floor level as well as roof leaks, displaced screens, water around sliding doors, fallen branches and ceiling stains. Stay away from Deerfield Beach's shoreline until local warnings are lifted, even if the rain has paused.

Before entering, inspect from outside

Stay out when the structure looks questionable

Stand on dry ground and look over the building before opening the door. Bowed walls, new foundation cracks, a sagging ceiling, a shifted porch, a leaning tree, a fallen power line or a visibly damaged roof are reasons to stay outside and request a professional inspection.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention advises people not to enter a home with obvious structural damage, downed trees or power lines, or cracks in the walls or foundation. Return during daylight so you can inspect the property without operating switches or carrying an open flame.

Standing water and electricity mean stay out

If water is touching outlets, appliances, an electrical panel or extension cords, do not step into it. Never reach a breaker panel through standing water. The CDC's flooded-home guidance says to have an electrician shut off power when the main switch cannot be reached from a dry location. The electrical system should also be checked before power is restored.

  • Do not test a wet outlet, light, appliance or garage-door opener.
  • Do not use a corded pump or shop vacuum until an electrician confirms the circuit is safe.
  • Use battery-powered lighting instead of candles or gas lanterns.
  • Treat buzzing, sparking, hot plastic smells or repeated breaker trips as electrical emergencies.

Gas odors require immediate evacuation

If you smell gas or hear hissing, leave without touching lights, switches or anything that could produce a spark. Once you are outside and away from the building, call the gas provider or emergency services and notify property management.

Do not reenter until the appropriate authority says it is safe. The CDC also warns against operating a generator, pump or other gasoline engine indoors or within 20 feet of a door, window or vent because of the risk of carbon-monoxide poisoning.

Document water damage before cleanup

Start with a slow video walkthrough

Once entry is safe, begin at the doorway and record one continuous video through each affected room. Say the date, room, apparent water source and what you see. Record the waterline, wet baseboards, flooring, cabinets, doors, closets, ceiling stains and the route the water appears to have taken.

Next, take wide photographs of every wall and close-ups of individual losses. Photograph manufacturer labels and serial numbers on electronics and appliances. The National Flood Insurance Program recommends recording both wide and close views before cleaning, repairing or discarding damaged property.

Build a room-by-room inventory

Use a note or spreadsheet with one row for each item. Include its description, brand, model, serial number, approximate age, original cost if known and estimated replacement cost. Add receipts, order confirmations, credit-card records or older photographs when available.

What to report to the landlord immediately

Send one clear written notice

Call if the problem is urgent, then follow up with an email or maintenance-portal request. State when you discovered the damage, where the water entered, whether water remains, whether electricity is affected and whether you can safely occupy the home.

Attach representative photographs instead of sending an unexplained folder of images. Ask for written confirmation of the inspection plan, the responsible contractor and what you should do with wet belongings. Do not authorize structural demolition or permanent repairs yourself unless the lease or manager specifically permits it.

Report conditions that can spread or become dangerous first

  • Standing water inside the dwelling, garage or electrical-room area.
  • Water touching outlets, wiring, an air-conditioning unit or the breaker panel.
  • A roof, ceiling, window, sliding-door or plumbing leak that is still active.
  • Wet drywall, insulation, cabinets, carpet or flooring.
  • Sewage odors, backed-up drains or contaminated floodwater.
  • A sagging ceiling, cracked wall, soft floor or door that suddenly will not close.
  • Loss of safe water, sanitation, electricity or secure exterior access.

Florida maintenance duties depend on the lease and property type

Under Florida Statutes section 83.51, landlords generally must comply with applicable building, housing and health codes and maintain specified structural and plumbing components. The statute also permits some obligations to be modified in writing for a single-family home or duplex. Read the lease before assuming who handles a particular item.

A maintenance request does not automatically satisfy the formal notice required for every legal remedy. Section 83.56 contains specific written-notice provisions involving material noncompliance and a seven-day period. Get qualified Florida legal advice before withholding rent, terminating a lease or sending a statutory notice.

Drying, mold and professional remediation

The first drying window matters

After safety checks and initial documentation, remove standing water and begin drying as soon as management and the insurer allow. The CDC says a flooded home that could not be thoroughly dried within 24 to 48 hours should be assumed to have mold growth.

Open windows when the weather and security permit. Use dehumidifiers and outward-facing fans only when electricity is safe and the HVAC system is not suspected of contamination. If floodwater reached the air handler, ducts or return opening, do not run the system until a qualified technician evaluates it.

Watch for early warning signs

  • A musty or earthy odor that persists after surface water is removed.
  • Discoloration or fuzzy growth on drywall, baseboards, furniture or closet walls.
  • Bubbling paint, swollen trim, warped laminate or crumbling drywall.
  • Condensation that returns despite air-conditioning or dehumidification.
  • Coughing, wheezing or eye and skin irritation that appears indoors.

Mold can grow behind baseboards, under carpet padding, inside cabinets and on the reverse side of drywall. Painting over stains or spraying fragrance does not fix trapped moisture.

When to call a professional

The Environmental Protection Agency says many people can address an area smaller than about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot-by-three-foot patch. Extensive water damage, contaminated water, suspected HVAC contamination or mold beyond that area calls for professional evaluation.

Renters should not cut open walls or remove building materials without written authorization. Ask management how moisture levels will be measured, which materials will be removed, how unaffected rooms will be protected and what clearance or completion documentation will be provided.

Renter's insurance and landlord coverage

Start with the cause of the water

Coverage often depends on how the water entered. A standard renter's policy may treat a sudden roof leak or burst pipe differently from rising surface water. Most ordinary homeowners and renter's policies do not cover flood damage, according to the National Flood Insurance Program.

A renter's NFIP contents policy can cover eligible belongings inside the rental, while building coverage is generally for the property owner. The owner's policy does not ordinarily insure a tenant's furniture, clothing, computers or other personal possessions.

Ask each insurer direct questions

  • Is this event classified as flood, storm intrusion, sewer backup or another peril?
  • Are my belongings covered at replacement cost or actual cash value?
  • Is temporary lodging or loss-of-use coverage available under this policy?
  • What deductible applies?
  • May I begin disposal and cleanup before the adjuster arrives?
  • What proof and deadlines apply to the claim?

NFIP contents coverage does not include temporary housing or additional living expenses, according to FloodSmart. A separate renter's policy may provide loss-of-use benefits for a covered peril. Report the loss to every potentially relevant insurer and let each carrier make its own coverage decision.

Keep building and contents losses separate

Send photographs of damaged walls, flooring and fixtures to management, but list tenant-owned items separately for your contents claim. Keep a claim number and adjuster contact for each policy. Save every receipt, even if you are not yet certain it is reimbursable.

If you are planning a future move after recovery, compare available homes through the general rental listings. Keep the flood claim and lease questions separate from any relocation decision.

Vehicle, commute and neighborhood precautions

Never test the depth of a flooded road

Do not drive through water covering Hillsboro Boulevard, Federal Highway, Powerline Road, Southwest Tenth Street or a neighborhood entrance simply because another driver made it across. Water can hide an open drain, displaced cover, stalled vehicle, electrical hazard or washed-out pavement.

The CDC advises against walking or driving across flooded roads because people and vehicles can be swept away. Turn around and use a verified dry route.

Check a flooded vehicle before starting it

If water reached the door sill, cabin, exhaust, battery or engine compartment, photograph the waterline and call the auto insurer or roadside-assistance provider before starting the vehicle. Starting a water-damaged engine can worsen mechanical or electrical damage.

Keep children and pets away from ponded water. Floodwater can contain sewage, fuel, sharp objects and displaced wildlife. Wear waterproof boots and gloves if contact cannot be avoided, then wash exposed skin promptly.

A 24-hour post-flood checklist

Safety and notification

  1. Confirm that local officials and property management consider the area safe to enter.
  2. Inspect the roofline, walls, trees, power lines and entry from dry ground.
  3. Stay out if water touches electrical equipment or structural damage is visible.
  4. Leave immediately if you smell gas.
  5. Send written notice to the landlord or manager with the time, location and severity of the damage.

Evidence and claims

  1. Record a continuous room-by-room video before moving belongings.
  2. Photograph waterlines, building damage and every damaged personal item.
  3. Record brands, models, serial numbers, age and estimated replacement cost.
  4. Open claims with the renter's, flood and auto insurers that may apply.
  5. Save receipts, claim numbers, adjuster names and copies of every message.

Drying and follow-up

  1. Begin water extraction only after electrical and structural clearance.
  2. Separate wet belongings without blocking exits or creating a new hazard.
  3. Ask management for a written drying and inspection schedule.
  4. Monitor odors, humidity, wall surfaces, flooring and HVAC vents.
  5. Refer sewage, spreading moisture, suspected HVAC contamination or significant mold to qualified professionals.

Decision rule: If entering, energizing, cleaning or discarding something could endanger you or weaken an insurance claim, pause and get written direction from the appropriate professional.

FAQ

Can I enter my Deerfield Beach rental if floodwater has receded?

Enter only after local authorities or property management say the area is safe. Stay out if you see structural damage, downed power lines, standing water touching electrical equipment or other obvious hazards.

Should I turn off the breaker when water is inside?

Only if you can reach the panel from a completely dry location. Never enter standing water to reach a breaker. Call the utility, property manager or a licensed electrician when access is unsafe.

How quickly should a flooded rental be dried?

Drying should begin as soon as the electrical and structural conditions are safe and the initial damage is documented. CDC guidance says to assume mold growth if the home and contents could not be dried within 24 to 48 hours.

Does ordinary renter's insurance cover flood damage?

Most standard renter's policies exclude rising surface-water flooding. A separate flood contents policy may cover eligible belongings. A standard policy may cover other water events depending on the cause and its terms.

What should I include in a landlord maintenance notice?

Include when and where you found the water, the apparent source, whether water remains, any electrical, sewage, gas or structural concerns, whether the home is safe to occupy, and photographs. Keep proof that the notice was delivered.

Can I throw away soaked belongings immediately?

Photograph and inventory them first, then ask the adjuster. NFIP guidance generally recommends keeping damaged items until inspection unless they pose a health hazard or local law requires disposal.

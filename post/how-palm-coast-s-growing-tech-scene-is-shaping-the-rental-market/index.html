How Palm Coast's Growing Tech Scene Is Shaping the Rental Market

​Palm Coast, Florida, has emerged as a burgeoning tech hub, attracting companies and professionals to its evolving landscape. This influx has significantly influenced the local rental market, leading to notable shifts in housing demand and affordability.

Tech Industry Growth in Palm Coast

Palm Coast, Florida, is rapidly emerging as a significant player in the technology sector, experiencing substantial growth that is reshaping its economic landscape.​

Economic Impact and Job Growth

In 2022, the city's tech industry contributed approximately $250 million to the local economy, marking a 20% increase from the previous year. This surge underscores Palm Coast's growing prominence as a tech hub. Notably, the city has witnessed a 41.6% growth in high-tech industries over the past five years, positioning it as the second-highest increase nationwide. Projections indicate a 15% growth in tech job openings between 2022 and 2024, highlighting the expanding opportunities in this sector. ​

Leading Tech Sectors

The most dynamic areas within Palm Coast's tech industry include:​

  • Software Development and Engineering: Companies such as Coastal Cloud are at the forefront, driving innovation and creating numerous employment opportunities. ​

  • Cybersecurity and Network Administration: Firms like CyberShield Solutions are enhancing the city's reputation in cybersecurity, addressing the increasing demand for digital protection services. ​

  • Data Science and Analytics: This sector is experiencing rapid growth, with professionals analyzing complex data to inform business strategies.​

Salary Competitiveness

Tech professionals in Palm Coast earn an average annual salary of $75,000, surpassing the state average. This competitive compensation, combined with a cost of living 2% lower than the national average, makes the city an attractive destination for tech talent. ​

Infrastructure and Connectivity

Palm Coast is on track to become Florida's second Certified Gigabit City, with ongoing construction to provide 10-gigabit fiber optic internet across the entire city. This advancement is set to enhance the technological infrastructure, supporting both businesses and residents.

Strategic Location and Lifestyle

Strategically situated between Jacksonville and Daytona Beach, Palm Coast offers a unique blend of coastal living and technological innovation. This positioning facilitates access to larger markets while maintaining a high quality of life, attracting professionals seeking a balanced lifestyle. ​

Future Outlook

The future appears promising for Palm Coast's tech industry, with a projected 112% increase in engineering and aerospace manufacturing jobs by 2033. This anticipated growth reflects the city's commitment to fostering a robust and diverse technological ecosystem.

As Palm Coast continues to evolve as a tech hub, it presents abundant opportunities for professionals and businesses alike, solidifying its position in the national technological landscape.


Impact on the Rental Market

​Palm Coast, Florida, is experiencing a significant transformation in its rental market, driven by the city's burgeoning tech industry. The influx of tech professionals has heightened housing demand, leading to historically low vacancy rates and escalating rental prices.​

Escalating Rental Prices

The increased demand has led to a notable rise in rental prices. As of March 2025, the average rent for all property types in Palm Coast stands at $2,045, reflecting a $45 increase from the previous month. This trend underscores the financial challenges faced by residents seeking affordable housing options. ​

Market Dynamics

The rental market's competitiveness is further evidenced by the limited availability of affordable units. As of November 2024, there were 639 rentals listed on Zillow, with median rents around $1,979 per month. Notably, three-bedroom units averaged $1,934, while one-bedroom units were approximately $1,267.

Future Outlook

The ongoing expansion of Palm Coast's tech sector suggests that housing demand will remain robust. Addressing this challenge requires strategic urban planning and investment in housing infrastructure to ensure that the city's growth remains inclusive and sustainable.​

Short-Term Vacation Rentals

​Palm Coast, Florida, has witnessed a significant increase in short-term vacation rentals (STRs) over the past two years, contributing to the city's housing challenges.​

Rapid Growth of Short-Term Rentals

Between 2022 and 2024, the number of properties used as vacation rentals in Palm Coast surged from 342 to 581, marking a 70% increase. This growth predominantly involves single-family homes, condos, and townhouses, reflecting a broader trend across Flagler County. ​

Impact on Housing Availability

The expansion of STRs has reduced the availability of long-term rental options for residents. As more properties are converted into vacation rentals, the supply of housing for permanent residents diminishes, exacerbating the local housing crunch.

​Regulatory Measures

In response to the proliferation of STRs, the Palm Coast City Council has been developing regulations to balance property rights with neighborhood integrity. Proposed measures include capping occupancy, implementing registration fees, and enforcing safety and operational standards.

The rapid increase in short-term vacation rentals in Palm Coast underscores the need for strategic planning to ensure that the city's growth remains sustainable and beneficial for all residents.​


Challenges and Considerations

​Palm Coast, Florida, is experiencing rapid growth, particularly in its burgeoning tech sector. This expansion brings to the forefront several challenges that require strategic planning to ensure sustainable development and maintain residents' quality of life.​

Infrastructure Investment

The city's swift development necessitates substantial infrastructure enhancements. In June 2023, Palm Coast secured $54.55 million in state funding for critical projects. This allocation includes $25 million for expanding transportation access west of the railroad tracks at Matanzas Woods Parkway and $23.3 million for transportation improvements, notably advancing Phase II of the Old Kings Road North Widening Project. Additionally, $5 million is designated for constructing a new fire station in the Seminole Woods area, addressing public safety needs amid population growth. ​

Economic Diversification

Historically, Palm Coast has relied heavily on residential development for economic growth. However, this approach is unsustainable in the long term. As of December 2023, Flagler County had the 13th highest unemployment rate in Florida, with 92% of ad valorem taxes sourced from residents due to a limited commercial tax base. City officials are advocating for attracting diverse industries and manufacturing to create a more balanced and resilient economy. ​

Housing Affordability

The median price of single-family homes in Palm Coast has risen by $140,000 over four years, raising concerns about housing affordability for many residents. This trend underscores the need for policies that promote affordable housing options to accommodate the city's growing workforce. ​

Commercial Development Constraints

Efforts to attract commercial businesses are hindered by a lack of pad-ready land. In the past year, Palm Coast received 24 inquiries from businesses interested in relocating; however, 12 were declined due to insufficient existing space. The city is exploring solutions, such as investing in speculative buildings, to address this issue and foster economic diversification. ​

As Palm Coast continues its evolution into a tech-centric city, addressing these challenges through strategic infrastructure investment, economic diversification, and housing affordability initiatives will be crucial to ensuring sustainable and inclusive growth.

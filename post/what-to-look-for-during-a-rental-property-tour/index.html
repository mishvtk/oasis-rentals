What to Look for During a Rental Property Tour

A rental property tour is your chance to ensure a potential home meets your needs and expectations. By carefully inspecting the property and asking key questions, you can avoid surprises and feel confident in your decision. Here’s a guide to help you make the most of your tour.


1. Inspect the Exterior

The outside of the property gives clues about overall maintenance and care.

Curb Appeal: Is the property clean and well-kept? Look for tidy landscaping and clear walkways.

Structural Issues: Check for signs of damage, such as cracks, peeling paint, or broken gutters.

Parking: Verify the availability and convenience of parking spaces.



2. Examine the Interior

The interior needs a thorough walkthrough to ensure it’s livable and free of problems.

Walls and Floors: Look for cracks, water stains, or scratches that might indicate neglect or damage.

Doors and Windows: Ensure they open and close properly, and check that locks are secure.

Lighting: Test all light switches and fixtures to confirm they work.

3. Check the Kitchen

The kitchen is a focal point in any home, so make sure it meets your expectations.

Appliances: Test the stove, refrigerator, and dishwasher to confirm functionality.

Cabinet Space: Assess storage capacity and check for loose doors or shelves.

Plumbing: Run the water to test pressure and temperature, and look under the sink for leaks or mold.



4. Inspect the Bathrooms

Bathrooms are often a source of hidden issues, so don’t overlook them.

Plumbing: Flush the toilets, run the faucets, and check drainage in the sinks and shower.

Ventilation: Ensure there’s proper airflow to prevent mold and mildew buildup.

Cleanliness: Look for signs of water damage, such as soft spots or discoloration.

5. Evaluate Storage Space

Adequate storage is essential for a comfortable living experience.

Closets: Open all closet doors to gauge their size and condition.

Extra Storage: Ask if additional storage areas, like garages or attics, are available.

6. Test Utilities

Make sure all utilities and systems are in good working condition.

Heating and Cooling: Test the air conditioning, heating, or any ceiling fans.

Outlets and Switches: Bring a phone charger to confirm outlets work properly.

Wi-Fi Availability: Check for strong internet connectivity in the area.

7. Listen for Noise Levels

Noise can significantly impact your quality of life.

Traffic: Pay attention to noise from nearby streets or highways.

Neighbors: Listen for sounds from adjoining units, especially in multi-family buildings.

Airplanes or Trains: Consider the property’s proximity to airports or railways.



8. Prioritize Safety and Security

Your safety is a top priority when choosing a rental.

Locks: Ensure all door and window locks are functional.

Lighting: Look for well-lit entrances, hallways, and parking areas.

Fire Safety: Confirm the presence of smoke detectors and fire extinguishers.

9. Ask About Maintenance

A responsive landlord can make or break your rental experience.

Repairs: Ask how maintenance requests are handled and how quickly issues are typically resolved.

Pest Control: Inquire about any history of pests and how the property is treated.

Responsibility: Clarify who is responsible for lawn care, trash removal, or minor repairs.



10. Take Notes and Photos

Documenting the property during the tour can save you trouble later.

Pictures: Take photos of anything notable, especially damages, to avoid being held accountable later.

Details: Write down your impressions and any questions to discuss with the landlord.

11. Confirm Key Details in Rental Property Tour

Before leaving, clarify important aspects of the rental agreement.

Utilities: Find out which utilities are included in the rent and which are your responsibility.

Move-In Costs: Confirm the security deposit, application fees, and other upfront costs.

Pet Policies: If you have pets, ensure they are allowed and check for fees or restrictions.

Red Flags to Watch For

During your tour, be cautious of the following warning signs:

Unpleasant Odors: Strong smells may indicate hidden issues like mold or pests.

Quick Fixes: Fresh paint or patches might be covering underlying problems.

Evasive Landlords: A landlord unwilling to answer questions could be hiding something.


A rental property tour is a critical step in finding the perfect home. By following this checklist and staying vigilant, you can ensure the property meets your needs and avoid unexpected issues. Taking the time to evaluate the property thoroughly will give you peace of mind and help you make an informed decision.

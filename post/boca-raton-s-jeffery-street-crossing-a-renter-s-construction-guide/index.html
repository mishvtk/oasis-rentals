Boca Raton’s Jeffery Street Crossing: A Renter’s Construction Guide

Boca Raton has begun building a new railroad crossing at Jeffery Street, just east of NW Second Avenue where Clint Moore Road ends. The project will extend this east-west route across the Florida East Coast Railway corridor. The existing NW 28th Street crossing will eventually close.

Nearby renters should prepare for changing traffic patterns, construction noise and less predictable trips through northeast Boca Raton through 2027. As of November 3, however, the City had not published a closure or detour schedule specifically for Jeffery Street. Renters should separate the project's confirmed scope from disruptions that are reasonable to expect but have not been announced.

What began at Jeffery Street crossing

The October groundbreaking

The City announced the groundbreaking on October 30, 2026, when construction began on the Jeffery Street Railroad-Highway Grade Crossing. According to the official groundbreaking announcement, the crossing will be east of NW Second Avenue at the eastern end of Clint Moore Road.

The project goes beyond repairing an existing crossing. It will create a public route through a gap in Boca Raton's northeast transportation network and replace the crossing at NW 28th Street. The City says the finished connection should improve neighborhood access, traffic circulation and emergency-response flexibility. It will also provide access to North Park.

Why renters should care now

A groundbreaking does not change every nearby road at once. Still, renters considering homes near Jeffery Street, Clint Moore Road, NW Second Avenue, Dixie Highway or North Federal Highway should factor the construction period into the location, just as they would consider railway noise or access to Interstate 95.

Households that depend on this corridor for every trip will feel the effects most. A renter with another practical route to Yamato Road or Spanish River Boulevard has more flexibility than someone living in a building with only one convenient entrance facing the work area.

Confirmed project scope and schedule

What the City has announced

The City is coordinating the new crossing with the Florida East Coast Railway and the Florida Department of Transportation. A mobile version of the City announcement confirms that a $2 million Congressional Community Project Funding award will help support the project.

The official announcement lists these goals:

  • Reconnect neighborhoods on opposite sides of the rail corridor.
  • Give police, fire rescue and other emergency personnel another access point.
  • Improve traffic circulation in northeast Boca Raton.
  • Support nearby economic activity and future investment.
  • Create convenient access to North Park, which is also under construction.

More detailed project material describes a roadway extension and widening, intersection work, signal modifications, sidewalks, drainage, lighting, signage, pavement striping and rail-crossing safety improvements. Congressional documentation says the new public crossing will replace NW 28th Street Crossing No. 272502L under an agreement among the City, FDOT and the railway.

What the schedule says

Published reporting based on City information places design, construction and crossing activation between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2027. The project schedule reported by Progressive Railroading says completion is expected before the end of 2027.

That is a project window, not a promise that every work zone will stay in the same place for a year. Railroad coordination, utility conflicts, inspections and South Florida weather can affect individual phases. If a prospective landlord promises that nearby work will end by a particular move-in date, ask for the source of that claim.

Where the affected corridor sits

The local street network

Jeffery Street continues Clint Moore Road east of NW Second Avenue, also called Boca Raton Boulevard in this area. The project fills the connection toward Dixie Highway and, ultimately, North Federal Highway, or US 1, while crossing the FEC railway.

The work is north of Yamato Road and close to Boca Raton's northern edge. It matters most to people traveling between homes and businesses on opposite sides of the FEC tracks, entering the future North Park or using local roads as alternatives to the larger east-west corridors.

Commutes worth testing

Before signing a nearby lease, test the trip you expect to make. Relevant routes include travel west toward Interstate 95 and employment areas around Congress Avenue, east toward Federal Highway, south toward central Boca Raton and north toward Delray Beach.

Test other regular trips too, including school drop-off, evening work shifts, medical appointments, grocery runs and weekend travel. A route that looks simple on a rental map may feel quite different when a railway crossing, work truck or temporary lane shift interrupts the local street grid.

Our broader Boca Raton renter guides can help you compare this corridor with other parts of the city. During active construction, though, a live route check should carry more weight than a neighborhood description.

Confirmed closures versus anticipated impacts

What is official

The permanent closure connected with this project is the existing NW 28th Street railroad crossing. The new Jeffery Street crossing is intended to replace it, as stated in both the City announcement and the Congressional funding request.

As of November 3, 2026, Boca Raton's current and upcoming road-closure page did not list a specific active lane-closure schedule or signed detour for Jeffery Street. That page changes as projects advance, so the absence of a listed detour on that date does not guarantee uninterrupted access later.

What renters can reasonably anticipate

Roadway extension, drainage, utility and signal work can lead to temporary lane restrictions, flagging operations, shifted sidewalks and construction vehicles entering the road. These are possible effects based on the confirmed scope, not announced Jeffery Street closures.

Noise may vary by phase as well. Earthwork, paving and utility work sound different from the installation and testing of a railroad crossing. A home screened by another building may experience the project differently than a unit with windows or a balcony facing Jeffery Street.

Useful decision rule: Consider a closure official only when the City, FDOT, law enforcement or an on-site traffic-control plan identifies the road, direction, dates and detour. Discuss and monitor everything else as a possibility.

Access and travel-time variability

Even without a full closure, short trips can become inconsistent when equipment moves through an intersection or traffic shifts to a parallel route. Allow extra time for important trips instead of relying on one fixed estimate.

Pedestrians and cyclists should watch for temporary sidewalk endings and railway work areas. Never walk around a barricade or make your own path across railway property. Follow the posted pedestrian route, even if it is less direct.

How to verify conditions before leaving

Check three sources

Construction information can become outdated quickly. Before a rental tour, early work shift or school trip, follow these steps:

  1. Check the City's current road-closure page for named streets, dates and hours.
  2. Review Florida 511 for incidents and congestion on larger connecting roads.
  3. Open live navigation shortly before leaving, then follow posted barricades and signs when you arrive.

For recurring notifications, subscribe through the City's Notify Me page, which includes Boca Raton police emergency and traffic alerts. Save the project announcement too, since it directs readers to City project information.

Test the commute before signing

Drive or ride the route during the hours when you will actually use it. A weekend afternoon tour will not show the same conditions as a weekday school arrival or an evening shift change.

Test a primary route and a fallback. Note railway crossings, unprotected turns, sidewalk gaps and entrances where a moving truck or delivery vehicle might have trouble. If you bike or walk, complete the test that way instead of judging the route from a car.

Questions for the landlord or property manager

Ask about the property, not rumors

A landlord cannot control City construction, but the manager should be able to answer questions about the rental's access and existing building conditions. Ask:

  • Which driveway or gate remains usable if traffic control appears on Jeffery Street?
  • Is there a second legal vehicle entrance?
  • Where may residents and guests park if curb access is restricted?
  • Which rooms face the railway or anticipated work area?
  • Are the windows impact-rated or otherwise effective at reducing outside noise?
  • Does the lease address construction noise, loss of an amenity or temporary access changes?
  • How will management deliver notices from the association, City or contractor?
  • Are any building, utility or association projects scheduled during the same lease term?

Ask for written answers when they affect a material part of your decision. A verbal statement such as "traffic should be fine" cannot replace a lease term or an official traffic plan.

Inspect at a realistic time

Stand inside the bedrooms with the air conditioner off for a few minutes and listen for trains, road traffic and equipment. Then inspect the parking entrance, package-delivery area and pedestrian path outside.

If you are comparing available rental homes, use the same access checklist for every candidate. Even a home farther from the project, such as this three-bedroom Boca Raton home, should be evaluated by testing the resident's actual commute instead of measuring straight-line distance on a map. Listing availability can change, so this is a comparison method, not a promise that a particular home is open.

What the completed crossing could change

Driving and emergency access

The clearest long-term change will be a new east-west connection through a part of Boca Raton where the rail corridor interrupts local travel. The City expects the crossing to improve circulation and give emergency responders another route across the tracks.

Some trips may not become faster. A new crossing can redistribute vehicles among Jeffery Street, NW Second Avenue, Dixie Highway and Federal Highway. Renters renewing after completion should still test their routes instead of assuming that a citywide benefit will shorten a door-to-door commute.

Walking, cycling and park access

Sidewalks and related safety improvements are included in the published scope. The route is also intended to improve access to North Park, giving nearby residents a more direct connection once both projects are ready for public use.

The final crossings, signals and completed approaches will determine how practical that connection is. Renters who plan to walk or cycle should inspect the finished route before considering the park or shops east of the railway comfortably accessible.

A pre-lease and renewal checklist

Use this checklist if a rental depends on Jeffery Street or the surrounding northeast Boca Raton road network:

  • Map the exact address and its relationship to Jeffery Street, NW Second Avenue, Dixie Highway, Federal Highway and the FEC railway.
  • Check official closures on the City page on the day of every tour and again before signing.
  • Test two routes. Complete the normal trip at the normal time, then try a practical fallback.
  • Listen from inside. Visit during active work hours when possible and check bedrooms, workspaces and balconies.
  • Inspect pedestrian access. Confirm that the route to a bus stop, school trip or everyday errand does not require crossing a barricaded area.
  • Read the lease for parking, access, construction, amenity and notice provisions.
  • Ask about overlapping work. Building repairs and association projects can add to public-road disruption.
  • Save the update links for City closures, Notify Me and Florida 511.
  • Recheck before renewal. Compare the latest construction phase with your expected renewal term instead of relying on information from move-in.

FAQ

Where is the Jeffery Street Crossing project?

The project is in northeast Boca Raton, just east of NW Second Avenue where Clint Moore Road ends. It will create a roadway connection across the Florida East Coast Railway corridor toward Dixie Highway and Federal Highway.

When is the Jeffery Street Crossing expected to be completed?

Published project information schedules design, construction and crossing activation from the fourth quarter of 2026 through the fourth quarter of 2027. Completion is expected before the end of 2027, although individual phases may change.

Is Jeffery Street currently closed for construction?

As of November 3, 2026, Boca Raton's current road-closure page did not list a closure or detour schedule specifically for Jeffery Street. Renters should recheck the City page because restrictions may be added as construction advances.

Which railroad crossing will close?

The existing NW 28th Street crossing is scheduled to close under the agreement allowing the new Jeffery Street public crossing. Renters should wait for an official date and traffic plan before changing a regular route.

How can renters monitor construction and traffic changes?

Check Boca Raton's Current and Upcoming Road Closures page, subscribe to City traffic alerts through Notify Me, review Florida 511 and consult live navigation immediately before traveling.

What should I ask before renting near the project?

Ask about alternate vehicle entrances, guest parking, rooms facing the work area, window noise reduction, pedestrian access, management notices and any building or association work scheduled during the lease.

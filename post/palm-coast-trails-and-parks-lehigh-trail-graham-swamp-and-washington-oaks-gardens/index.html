Palm Coast trails and parks: Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens

Palm Coast is known for more than its beaches and waterfront neighborhoods. The city and surrounding Flagler County offer a variety of outdoor spaces where residents and visitors can walk, hike, bike, observe wildlife, and enjoy Florida's natural landscape.

Three places that stand out are Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens State Park. Each provides a very different outdoor experience, from a paved multi-use trail to a large wetland preserve and a historic coastal garden.


Lehigh Trail: A Scenic Multi-Use Trail in Palm Coast

For Palm Coast residents and renters who enjoy spending time outdoors, Lehigh Trail offers an easy and convenient way to experience the area's natural surroundings. The paved multi-use trail follows the route of an old railroad corridor and provides opportunities for walking, cycling, running, wildlife viewing, and other outdoor activities.

The trail stretches approximately 6.7 miles from the U.S. 1 area through Palm Coast and Graham Swamp, ending near Colbert Lane. It also parallels the Lehigh Canal for much of its route. Flagler County describes the trail as running through a largely tree-lined landscape, giving visitors a taste of Florida's natural environment without requiring a difficult hike.

A Trail Built on an Old Railroad Corridor

One of the interesting features of Lehigh Trail is its history. The trail occupies the bed of an old railroad spur that once connected the Lehigh Portland Cement Company with the Florida East Coast Railroad.

Today, that former transportation corridor has been transformed into a recreational trail. The paved surface makes it accessible for a variety of activities, including walking, running, and cycling. Flagler County identifies Lehigh Trail as a paved multi-use trail and lists hiking, bicycling, pets, and wildlife viewing among its features.

The trail also provides a quieter outdoor alternative to walking along busy streets. With trees surrounding much of the route, visitors can enjoy a more natural setting while still having a relatively accessible trail.

Lehigh Trailhead Amenities

The Lehigh Trailhead on Colbert Lane adds several amenities that make the area useful for both residents and visitors.

The City of Palm Coast lists amenities including:

  • Parking

  • Restrooms

  • Direct trail access

  • Community gardens

  • Dog parks

  • Separate areas for small and large dogs

The trailhead is also located next to the Southern Recreation Center, creating an opportunity to combine trail time with other recreational activities.

The community garden area includes 18 plots, including standard and elevated ADA-accessible plots. The dog park similarly provides separate spaces for large and small dogs.

As with any public recreational facility, visitors should check current operating information before visiting because maintenance or temporary closures can occur.

Why Renters and Residents May Like Lehigh Trail

For people considering a home or rental in Palm Coast, access to outdoor recreation can be an important lifestyle benefit. Lehigh Trail provides residents with an opportunity to exercise and enjoy nature without having to drive to the beach or a large regional park.

It may be particularly attractive to:

  • Walkers and runners who want a paved outdoor route

  • Cyclists looking for a recreational trail

  • Dog owners who appreciate nearby dog-park facilities

  • Families looking for accessible outdoor activities

  • Nature lovers interested in Florida's trees, wildlife, and natural landscape

  • Residents who prefer easier trails rather than challenging hiking terrain

The trail's connection through the Palm Coast area and Graham Swamp also gives it a broader recreational appeal. Graham Swamp itself contains more than 2,500 acres of wetland hardwood swamp and supports a variety of wildlife, including birds, mammals, and reptiles.

Lehigh Trail and the Palm Coast Lifestyle

One of the advantages of living in Palm Coast is the combination of residential neighborhoods and access to natural spaces. Lehigh Trail is a good example of how residents can incorporate outdoor recreation into everyday life.

Instead of treating nature as something that requires a special weekend trip, residents can use the trail for a morning walk, an afternoon bike ride, a run, or a quick outing with the dog.

For renters comparing different Palm Coast neighborhoods, nearby recreational amenities can therefore be worth considering alongside rent, commute times, shopping, schools, parking, and home features.

What to Know Before Visiting

Lehigh Trail is designed for recreational use, but visitors should still prepare for Florida's outdoor conditions. Comfortable shoes, water, sun protection, and insect repellent can be useful, particularly during warmer months.

The City of Palm Coast currently lists the Lehigh Trailhead at 1350 Belle Terre Parkway with hours of operation from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. The City also recommends checking for current maintenance information and closures.

Lehigh Trail is more than a simple neighborhood walking path. Its paved surface, approximately 6.7-mile route, wooded surroundings, connection to Graham Swamp, and convenient trailhead amenities make it one of Palm Coast's useful outdoor recreational resources.

For renters and residents who value walking, cycling, running, dogs, and easy access to nature, living near Lehigh Trail can add an appealing lifestyle benefit to a Palm Coast home.


Graham Swamp: A Wild Florida Experience in Palm Coast

For residents and visitors who want to experience a more natural side of Palm Coast, Graham Swamp offers something very different from a traditional city park. Instead of manicured lawns, paved paths, and heavily developed recreation areas, the Graham Swamp Conservation Area features wetlands, forests, marshes, and other natural Florida habitats.

The conservation area covers more than 3,000 acres, with Flagler County reporting that more than 2,500 acres consist of wetland hardwood swamp. A 2025 Flagler County management-plan update describes the conservation area as approximately 3,184.82 acres, with more than 90% of the property classified as wetlands.

A Major Natural Area in Palm Coast

Graham Swamp is one of the important natural areas surrounding Palm Coast. The swamp forms the historic headwaters of Bulow Creek and contains a variety of natural communities, including basin swamp, floodplain swamp, marshes, hydric hammock, and different types of flatwoods.

The area also serves an important environmental purpose. According to Flagler County, Graham Swamp helps retain and filter stormwater runoff from surrounding development. This makes the preserve valuable not only as recreational land but also as part of the region's natural water-management system.

The conservation area supports a wide range of wildlife, including birds, mammals, reptiles, and other species. Flagler County's 2025 management-plan information notes that the property is home to at least four state or federally protected wildlife species as well as numerous rare plants.

Hiking, Biking, Fishing, and Wildlife Viewing

Graham Swamp provides several ways to enjoy the outdoors. Flagler County lists hiking, bicycling, fishing, wildlife viewing, and nature study among the recreational opportunities available in the conservation area. The preserve includes unpaved hiking and nature trails, while mountain-bike trails provide another way to explore the property.

The east preserve at 5140 Colbert Lane includes a mountain-bike trail, unpaved hiking and nature trails, bicycle maintenance facilities, fishing access, and wildlife-viewing opportunities.

There is also a western preserve area at 1125 Old Kings Road N., where visitors can find unpaved hiking and nature trails, fishing, a fishing dock, a picnic area, and wildlife-viewing opportunities.

A Connection to Lehigh Trail

One of the most interesting aspects of Graham Swamp for outdoor enthusiasts is its connection to the broader Palm Coast trail network.

The Graham Swamp Conservation Area provides access to both the Lehigh Trail and the City of Palm Coast Graham Swamp Trail. Flagler County's updated management-plan information also identifies approximately six miles of mountain-bike trails within the conservation area.

This connection gives cyclists and other trail users an opportunity to experience several different environments within the same broader recreational network.

The Lehigh Trail itself travels through Graham Swamp before ending at Colbert Lane, creating an important link between Palm Coast's multi-use trail system and the surrounding conservation lands.

What Makes Graham Swamp Different?

Graham Swamp is better suited to people looking for a natural Florida experience than those looking for a highly developed recreational park.

Visitors should be prepared for:

  • Unpaved trails

  • Uneven or natural terrain

  • Wet and muddy conditions following rain

  • Heat and humidity

  • Insects

  • Wildlife

  • Areas with limited development and amenities

Conditions can change depending on weather and trail maintenance. In March 2026, Flagler County recommended that visitors participating in a guided Graham Swamp hike wear long pants and closed-toe shoes and bring water, sunscreen, insect repellent, and weather-appropriate clothing.

A Place for Nature Lovers

Graham Swamp can be particularly appealing to people who enjoy observing Florida's natural environment. The combination of wetlands, forest, marsh habitats, and wildlife creates a setting that feels considerably more remote than many neighborhood parks.

Birdwatchers may also find the preserve appealing. During a March 2026 guided walk, Flagler County highlighted wintering and migrating songbirds as well as unusual plants and spring wildflowers.

For photographers, cyclists, hikers, and anyone interested in Florida's ecosystems, the preserve offers a chance to see landscapes that are increasingly difficult to find close to developed communities.

Why Renters and Residents May Like Graham Swamp

For someone choosing where to live in Palm Coast, proximity to natural areas can be an important lifestyle consideration.

Graham Swamp may appeal to:

  • Cyclists who want access to mountain-bike and multi-use trails

  • Hikers who prefer natural, unpaved trails

  • Birdwatchers and wildlife enthusiasts

  • Photographers and nature lovers

  • Residents who enjoy fishing

  • People who prefer outdoor recreation away from heavily developed parks

The preserve also complements nearby amenities such as Lehigh Trail, giving residents multiple options for outdoor recreation.

Graham Swamp Requires a Little More Preparation

Unlike a paved neighborhood trail, Graham Swamp can require visitors to pay closer attention to conditions. Wetlands naturally change with rainfall and seasonal weather, and some trails can become muddy or uneven.

Before visiting, it is sensible to check current trail conditions and any closure notices. Palm Coast has also reported maintenance and safety work affecting the City of Palm Coast Graham Swamp Trail, including repairs to boardwalks, bridges, and trail surfaces. The city stated in March 2026 that this trail remained closed indefinitely while an expanded rehabilitation project was underway.

That closure concerns the city trail and should not automatically be interpreted as a closure of every recreational area within the broader Graham Swamp Conservation Area. Visitors should check the specific trail or access point they plan to use before heading out.

Graham Swamp offers a very different outdoor experience from Palm Coast's paved neighborhood trails and traditional city parks. With thousands of acres of wetlands and natural communities, it provides opportunities for hiking, mountain biking, fishing, wildlife viewing, and nature exploration.

For renters and residents who value access to authentic Florida landscapes, Graham Swamp can be an important lifestyle advantage. It also connects with the wider trail network, including Lehigh Trail, making it possible to combine different types of outdoor recreation in the Palm Coast area.

If Lehigh Trail is the easier, more accessible option for everyday exercise, Graham Swamp is the place to go when you want to experience a wilder side of Palm Coast.


Washington Oaks Gardens State Park: Gardens, Trails, History, and a Unique Coquina Beach

For Palm Coast residents and visitors, Washington Oaks Gardens State Park offers an experience that is very different from both Lehigh Trail and Graham Swamp. Located at 6400 N. Oceanshore Blvd., between the Atlantic Ocean and the Matanzas River, the state park combines formal gardens, historic landscapes, short hiking and biking trails, mature coastal forest, and a distinctive Atlantic beach lined with coquina rock formations.

It is one of those places where visitors can enjoy several different environments in a single trip. You can walk through gardens in the morning, explore a nature trail, have a picnic beneath large oak trees, and then head to the beach to see the unusual coquina formations.

The Formal Gardens Are the Centerpiece

The formal gardens are one of the main reasons people visit Washington Oaks. Winding paths lead visitors around reflecting ponds and through gardens featuring both native and non-native plants.

The park is particularly known for azaleas, camellias, roses, and birds of paradise. The gardens are surrounded by a mature oak hammock, creating a shaded and peaceful setting that feels very different from the open Atlantic beach nearby.

The rose garden is another highlight. Florida State Parks notes that the garden contains approximately 150 rose bushes, making it a particularly attractive stop for visitors interested in photography, gardening, or simply enjoying a quiet walk.

The Historic Washington Oak

The park's history is closely connected to the Young family.

Owen D. Young purchased the property in the 1930s, and he and his wife, Louise, used it as a winter retreat. The Youngs helped shape the gardens and landscape that visitors see today. Louise Young donated most of the property to the state of Florida after her husband's death, with the gardens to be maintained and improved.

One of the most recognizable features is the Washington Oak, an enormous live oak estimated by Florida State Parks to be between 200 and 300 years old. The tree stands at the center of the gardens and has become one of the park's defining landmarks.

The property also has a deeper history. Before the Youngs owned it, the land was part of the historic Bella Vista Plantation. The area's history therefore extends well beyond the Young family's winter retreat.

Trails at Washington Oaks

Washington Oaks is not simply a garden and beach destination. The park also provides several short opportunities for hiking and bicycling.

The Bella Vista trail system covers approximately 1.7 miles and includes:

  • Timucuan hiking trail

  • Jungle Road hike-and-bike trail

  • Old A1A hike-and-bike trail

These trails travel through mature coastal maritime hammock and coastal scrub, giving visitors an opportunity to experience the park's natural environment away from the formal gardens.

There is also the half-mile Mala Compra hiking loop, which explores the transition between coastal maritime hammock and the estuarine tidal marsh along the Matanzas River.

These trails are relatively short compared with longer regional trail systems, making Washington Oaks particularly appealing to people who prefer combining a short nature walk with other activities rather than spending an entire day on the trail.

The Matanzas River and Atlantic Beach

Washington Oaks occupies a unique position between the Matanzas River and the Atlantic Ocean.

On the river side, visitors can fish from the seawall and enjoy views across the water. The park also offers picnic facilities beneath large oak trees, with a covered pavilion, tables, grills, restrooms, and a children's playground.

On the Atlantic side, the park features approximately 0.75 miles of beach. But this is not an ordinary sandy beach.

The shoreline is famous for its coquina rock formations, which create unusual textures and tidal areas along the coast. Florida State Parks describes the beach as a popular place for beachcombing, exploring tidal pools, and photography.

The rocks are one of Washington Oaks' most distinctive characteristics and give photographers and nature lovers something different from the typical Florida beach experience.

Birding and Wildlife Viewing

Washington Oaks also offers opportunities for birding and wildlife observation. The park is part of the Great Florida Birding and Wildlife Trail, and birds can be observed in several different habitats, including the gardens, Matanzas River, coastal areas, and beach.

Because the park combines gardens, mature trees, coastal hammock, marsh, riverfront, and beach habitats, visitors can experience a surprisingly wide range of environments within a relatively compact area.

Which Palm Coast Outdoor Destination Is Right for You?

Palm Coast has a variety of outdoor spaces, but Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens State Park offer three very different experiences.

Choosing the right one depends largely on what you want to do. Are you looking for an easy paved trail for regular exercise? Do you want to explore Florida wetlands and wildlife? Or would you rather spend a day combining gardens, history, nature, and the beach?

Here is a closer look at what each destination offers.

Choose Lehigh Trail for Easy Outdoor Exercise

If you want an easy, paved outdoor trail, Lehigh Trail is probably the best choice.

The trail follows an old railroad corridor and stretches approximately 6.7 miles, providing a long, relatively accessible route for walking, running, cycling, and other recreational activities. It also parallels the Lehigh Canal for much of its route and travels through the natural landscape around Graham Swamp. (flaglercounty.gov)

The paved surface makes Lehigh Trail particularly practical for people who want to incorporate outdoor exercise into their regular routine.

It may be the best option for:

  • Walkers

  • Runners

  • Road and recreational cyclists

  • Dog owners

  • Families

  • People who prefer paved surfaces

  • Residents looking for a convenient place for regular exercise

The Lehigh Trailhead on Colbert Lane also provides parking, restrooms, community gardens, and separate dog-park areas for small and large dogs. (palmcoast.gov)

Best for: Everyday recreation

If your priority is convenience and accessibility, Lehigh Trail is the clear choice. It works particularly well for someone who wants a trail they can use frequently rather than a destination that requires planning an entire day around the visit.

Choose Graham Swamp for a Wilder Florida Experience

If your goal is to experience Florida's natural wetlands and wildlife, Graham Swamp is the more adventurous option.

The Graham Swamp Conservation Area covers more than 3,000 acres and contains wetlands, forests, marshes, coastal flatwoods, and other natural communities. Flagler County identifies more than 2,500 acres of wetland hardwood swamp within the preserve. (flaglercounty.gov)

The area provides opportunities for hiking, bicycling, fishing, wildlife viewing, and nature study. Unlike Lehigh Trail, many of the recreational routes are unpaved and can feel considerably more natural and remote. (flaglercounty.gov)

Visitors should be prepared for natural trail conditions, including possible mud, uneven terrain, heat, humidity, and insects.

Graham Swamp may be the better choice for:

  • Wildlife enthusiasts

  • Hikers

  • Mountain bikers

  • Birdwatchers

  • Nature photographers

  • People who enjoy less-developed environments

  • Residents who want to explore Florida's native ecosystems

Best for: Nature and adventure

If you would rather trade pavement and convenience for wetlands, wildlife, and natural terrain, Graham Swamp is the stronger choice.

Choose Washington Oaks for a Full Day of Scenery

If you want a scenic day out combining nature, history, gardens, and the beach, Washington Oaks Gardens State Park is difficult to beat.

Located between the Atlantic Ocean and the Matanzas River, the park combines formal gardens, mature oak trees, hiking and biking trails, riverfront scenery, and approximately 0.75 miles of Atlantic beach. (floridastateparks.org)

The formal gardens feature plants such as azaleas, camellias, roses, and birds of paradise. The park is also home to the famous Washington Oak, an enormous live oak estimated to be between 200 and 300 years old. (floridastateparks.org)

Visitors can also explore the Bella Vista trail system and the Mala Compra hiking loop. The park provides opportunities for birding, fishing, wildlife viewing, and picnicking. (floridastateparks.org)

The Atlantic shoreline is especially distinctive because of its coquina rock formations, which create a landscape that looks very different from a typical Florida beach.

  • Visitors who want several activities in one location

Best for: A complete day trip

Exploring Palm Coast's Outdoor Gems: Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens

Palm Coast offers a diverse collection of outdoor destinations, from paved recreational trails to protected wetlands and historic coastal gardens. Three destinations stand out for residents, renters, and visitors looking for different ways to enjoy the outdoors: Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens State Park.

Each offers a different experience. Lehigh Trail is well suited to walking and cycling, Graham Swamp provides a more natural wetland environment, and Washington Oaks combines gardens, history, trails, the Matanzas River, and an unusual Atlantic shoreline.

Lehigh Trail: A Multi-Use Trail for Everyday Recreation

Lehigh Trail follows an abandoned railroad corridor and extends through Palm Coast and Graham Swamp before reaching Colbert Lane. Flagler County describes the trail as occupying approximately 194.95 acres of former railroad corridor and paralleling the Lehigh Canal for much of its route. The county identifies bicycling, hiking and nature trails, pets, and wildlife viewing among the trail's features.

The City of Palm Coast describes the trail as approximately 6.7 miles long, running east-west from the U.S. 1 area through Town Center and Graham Swamp to the Lehigh Trailhead on Colbert Lane. The trail sits on the former railroad spur that once connected the Lehigh Portland Cement Company with the Florida East Coast Railroad.

For residents, one of the biggest advantages is accessibility. The trail can be used for regular walks, recreational cycling, running, and enjoying the surrounding scenery.

Lehigh Trailhead Amenities

The Lehigh Trailhead adds several amenities that make the trail particularly useful for local residents.

The City of Palm Coast lists:

  • Parking

  • Restrooms

  • Trail access

  • Community gardens

  • Small and large dog parks

The trailhead also includes 18 community garden plots, including six elevated ADA-accessible plots. The dog park has separate areas for small and large dogs.

The trailhead is located at 1350 Belle Terre Parkway, while Flagler County lists the Colbert Lane trailhead location at 5875 Colbert Lane. Visitors should check the current information from the relevant agency before visiting because maintenance and temporary closures can affect individual facilities.

Graham Swamp: A More Natural Florida Experience

Graham Swamp offers a very different type of outdoor experience. The Graham Swamp Conservation Area contains extensive wetlands and forested uplands, with more than 2,500 acres covered by wetland hardwood swamp according to Flagler County.

The swamp is also environmentally important. It helps retain and filter stormwater runoff from developments to the west and north while supporting the ecological health of northeast Florida's coastal ecosystems.

For visitors, Graham Swamp provides opportunities for:

  • Hiking

  • Bicycling

  • Mountain biking

  • Fishing

  • Wildlife viewing

  • Nature study

The eastern preserve at 5140 Colbert Lane includes unpaved hiking and nature trails and a mountain-bike trail.

The western preserve, located at 1125 Old Kings Road N., offers unpaved hiking and nature trails, fishing, a fishing dock, a picnic area, and wildlife viewing.

What Makes Graham Swamp Special?

Graham Swamp is a good choice for people who want to experience Florida's natural landscape rather than a highly developed recreational park.

The environment can be more rugged than Lehigh Trail, so visitors should be prepared for natural and potentially wet trail conditions. The reward is the opportunity to explore wetlands and forests while observing birds, mammals, reptiles, and other wildlife.

For Palm Coast residents who enjoy hiking, mountain biking, fishing, or wildlife photography, Graham Swamp can be an especially valuable nearby resource.

Washington Oaks Gardens State Park: Gardens, History, and the Beach

Washington Oaks Gardens State Park provides yet another experience. Located along the Atlantic coast, the park combines formal gardens, mature oak trees, nature trails, the Matanzas River, and a distinctive Atlantic shoreline.

The formal gardens are one of the park's defining features, while the historic landscape adds another layer to the visit. The park is also known for its enormous Washington Oak and its unusual coquina-rock shoreline.

Unlike Lehigh Trail and Graham Swamp, Washington Oaks is as much about scenery and history as it is about outdoor exercise.

Visitors can combine a garden walk with short hikes, wildlife viewing, fishing, picnicking, and a visit to the Atlantic beach.

Planning Your Visit to Palm Coast's Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens

Palm Coast offers several excellent outdoor destinations, but planning ahead can make your visit more enjoyable. Lehigh Trail, Graham Swamp, and Washington Oaks Gardens State Park each have different trail surfaces, amenities, hours, and conditions.

Before heading out, it is a good idea to check the official park or county website for current information, especially after significant weather or when maintenance work may affect access.

Check Conditions Before You Go

Florida weather can change quickly, particularly during the warmer and wetter months. Rain can affect unpaved trails, while storms, maintenance projects, or other circumstances can temporarily affect access to recreational areas.

For Lehigh Trail, Flagler County identifies the trail as a paved multi-use trail and lists hiking, bicycling, pets, and wildlife viewing among its features. The county's current page also provides the Colbert Lane location and Parks and Recreation contact information.

For Graham Swamp, conditions can be more variable because the preserve includes unpaved hiking and nature trails and a mountain-bike trail. Flagler County notes that the conservation area contains extensive wetlands and that visitors can hike, bicycle, fish, and view wildlife.

Checking the current county information before leaving home is especially useful if you are planning to spend several hours on the trails.

Washington Oaks Gardens State Park Hours

Washington Oaks Gardens State Park is currently listed by Florida State Parks as being open 8 a.m. until sunset, 365 days a year.

The park combines formal gardens, nature trails, riverfront areas, and Atlantic beach access, so visitors can spend several hours exploring different parts of the property.

Because state park hours and operating conditions can change, it is still smart to verify the official information shortly before your visit.

Washington Oaks Admission Fees

Florida State Parks currently lists the following admission fees for Washington Oaks Gardens State Park:

  • $5 per vehicle for two to eight people

  • $4 for a single-occupant vehicle

  • $2 for pedestrians and bicyclists

The park uses an honor box for admission payments, and the official state park page notes that visitors should have the correct change.

Florida State Parks also provides an option for visitors to purchase a single-use day pass online before visiting.

Because fees and payment procedures can change, visitors should check the official Florida State Parks website before their trip.

Planning for Lehigh Trail

Lehigh Trail is one of the easier options for visitors who want a paved outdoor route. The trail extends through Palm Coast and Graham Swamp, with the Colbert Lane trailhead located at 5875 Colbert Lane.

The county lists bicycling, hiking and nature trails, pets, and wildlife viewing among the trail's features.

If you are bringing a dog or planning a longer cycling or walking session, bring sufficient water and consider the day's temperature and humidity.

Planning for Graham Swamp

Graham Swamp requires a little more preparation than Lehigh Trail because many of its trails are unpaved.

The eastern preserve at 5140 Colbert Lane includes mountain-bike and unpaved hiking/nature trails, along with fishing and wildlife-viewing opportunities.

Before visiting, consider bringing:

  • Water

  • Comfortable outdoor footwear

  • Sun protection

  • Insect repellent

  • Weather-appropriate clothing

  • A fully charged phone

After heavy rain, natural trails can become wetter or more difficult to navigate, so checking current conditions is particularly important.

Check for Temporary Closures

Even when a park is normally open, individual trails, facilities, roads, or access points can occasionally be affected by maintenance or other temporary conditions.

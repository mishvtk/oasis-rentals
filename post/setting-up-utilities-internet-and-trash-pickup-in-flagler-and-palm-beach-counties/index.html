Setting Up Utilities, Internet, and Trash Pickup in Flagler and Palm Beach Counties

Moving into a new home is exciting, but getting your essential services connected is one of the first priorities. Whether you're renting or buying in Flagler County or Palm Beach County, understanding how to set up utilities, internet, and waste collection will help you settle in quickly and avoid unnecessary delays.

Most new residents quickly discover that one of the first tasks after signing a lease or closing on a home is setting up electricity. Fortunately, the process is straightforward in both Flagler and Palm Beach Counties, and planning ahead can help ensure your home is ready from the moment you move in.



Start with Electricity

For most homes in Flagler County and Palm Beach County, electricity is provided by Florida Power & Light (FPL), one of the largest electric utilities in the United States. FPL offers an easy online account setup process, allowing new customers to arrange service before moving into their new home.

When starting service, you will generally need to provide:

  • Your new service address

  • Your expected move-in date

  • Valid identification information

  • Payment information for any applicable deposit

In many cases, the entire process can be completed online within minutes. If additional verification is required, FPL customer support can assist with completing your account setup.

Schedule Service Before Moving Day

One of the simplest ways to avoid unnecessary stress is to schedule your electric service several days before your move. This helps ensure that electricity is already active when you arrive, allowing you to:

  • Turn on air conditioning immediately, especially during Florida's warm climate.

  • Connect internet equipment and other essential electronics.

  • Keep refrigerators and freezers running from the first day.

  • Enjoy a comfortable first night without unexpected service delays.

Scheduling early is especially important during busy moving seasons, such as summer and the beginning or end of each month, when customer service requests tend to increase.

Helpful Tip for New Florida Residents

After your electricity is connected, consider creating an online FPL account. This allows you to:

  • View and pay bills electronically.

  • Monitor your energy usage.

  • Report outages.

  • Receive service notifications.

  • Set up automatic payments or paperless billing.

Managing your account online makes it easier to track utility costs and helps you stay informed throughout the year.


Water and Sewer Services in Flagler and Palm Beach Counties

After arranging your electricity, the next essential utility to set up is water and sewer service. Unlike electricity, which is largely provided by a single company, water and sewer services in Florida are managed by different providers depending on your city, neighborhood, or community.

Understanding who serves your address before moving can save time and help ensure your water service is activated when you arrive.

Water and Sewer Service in Flagler County

In Flagler County, water and sewer services are typically provided by the local municipality or a regional utility authority.

For example:

  • City of Palm Coast residents generally receive water and wastewater services through the City of Palm Coast Utility Department.

  • Homes located outside Palm Coast may be served by the Florida Governmental Utility Authority (FGUA) or another local utility provider, depending on the property's location.

Because service areas vary, it's a good idea to verify your provider with your landlord, property manager, real estate agent, or the county before scheduling service.

Water and Sewer Service in Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County has multiple water and wastewater providers, with service determined by the municipality or utility district where your home is located.

Depending on your address, your provider may be:

  • A city or town water department

  • A regional utility authority

  • A special utility district serving a specific community

Since neighboring cities can have entirely different utility systems, it's important to confirm your water provider before your move. This information is usually available from your lease documents, closing paperwork, homeowners association, or local municipality.

Set Up Service Before Moving Day

Once you know your utility provider, contact them several days before your move to arrange service activation. You may be asked to provide:

  • Your new service address

  • Your move-in date

  • A government-issued photo ID

  • Contact information

  • A security deposit, if applicable

Scheduling in advance helps ensure water and sewer services are available as soon as you move into your new home.

A Helpful Moving Tip

Keep a record of your utility account numbers and login information after establishing service. Having everything organized will make it easier to pay bills, report service issues, and manage your account online in the future.


Choosing an Internet Provider in Flagler and Palm Beach Counties

Reliable internet service is an essential part of settling into a new home, whether you're working remotely, attending online classes, streaming entertainment, or managing smart home devices. In both Flagler and Palm Beach Counties, internet availability varies by neighborhood, so it's important to check which providers serve your specific address before making a decision.

Common Internet Providers

Several major internet providers serve communities throughout Flagler and Palm Beach Counties, including:

  • AT&T Fiber – Available in many neighborhoods where fiber infrastructure has been installed, offering high-speed symmetrical upload and download speeds.

  • Xfinity (Comcast) – A widely available cable internet provider offering a variety of speed options for households of different sizes.

  • Spectrum – Another popular cable internet provider serving many residential communities with flexible speed plans.

  • Regional Fiber Providers – Some neighborhoods and newly developed communities may also have access to smaller regional fiber providers that offer competitive pricing and high-speed connections.

Since coverage varies by location, always verify service availability using your exact street address before selecting a plan.

What to Compare Before Signing Up

Not all internet plans are the same. Before choosing a provider, consider comparing the following:

  • Download and upload speeds to match your household's needs.

  • Monthly pricing, including promotional rates and regular pricing after introductory periods.

  • Equipment rental fees for modems or Wi-Fi routers.

  • Installation or activation costs, if applicable.

  • Contract requirements, including early termination fees or no-contract options.

  • Fiber availability, which can provide faster and more consistent performance than traditional cable service where available.

Taking a few minutes to compare plans can help you avoid unexpected costs and ensure you get the best value.

Is Fiber Internet Worth It?

If you work from home, participate in video conferences, upload large files, play online games, or stream multiple 4K videos simultaneously, fiber internet is often the best choice when available.

Fiber connections typically offer:

  • Faster download and upload speeds

  • Lower latency for smoother video calls and gaming

  • More reliable performance during peak usage hours

  • Better support for multiple connected devices

While cable internet is sufficient for many households, fiber can provide a noticeable improvement for users with higher bandwidth demands.

Schedule Installation Early

If your new home requires professional installation, schedule your appointment as early as possible—especially during busy moving seasons. Some providers offer self-installation kits, while others may require a technician visit.

Having internet service ready on move-in day can make unpacking, setting up smart devices, and staying connected much easier.



Trash and Recycling Collection in Flagler County

Setting up trash and recycling service is an important part of moving into a new home. In Flagler County, collection services vary depending on whether you live in an incorporated city or an unincorporated area. Knowing who provides your service and when collection takes place can help you avoid missed pickups and keep your household running smoothly.

Trash Collection in Unincorporated Flagler County

Residents who live in unincorporated Flagler County receive residential solid waste collection through Waste Pro under the county's waste management program.

Typical residential services include:

  • Household garbage collection provided by Waste Pro.

  • Recycling collection once each week.

  • Yard waste collection, generally scheduled on Wednesdays for many service areas.

Collection days may differ depending on your neighborhood, so new residents should verify their pickup schedule before placing bins at the curb.

Find Your Collection Schedule

Flagler County offers an online collection schedule that allows residents to look up their designated pickup days by address. Checking the schedule is especially helpful after moving, during holidays, or following severe weather events that may temporarily affect collection services.

Following the correct schedule helps ensure that garbage, recycling, and yard waste are collected on time and reduces the chance of missed pickups.

Residents of Incorporated Cities

If you live within one of Flagler County's incorporated municipalities, your sanitation services are managed separately from the county's program.

This includes residents of:

  • Palm Coast

  • Flagler Beach

  • Bunnell

Each city has its own sanitation or public works department that oversees trash, recycling, bulk waste, and yard waste collection. Pickup schedules, accepted materials, and service guidelines may differ from those in unincorporated areas, so it is important to contact your city directly or visit its official website for the most up-to-date information.

Moving Tip

After moving into your new home, confirm:

  • Your designated collection days

  • Where to place trash and recycling carts

  • Accepted recycling materials

  • Bulk item disposal procedures

  • Holiday collection schedule changes

A few minutes of preparation can help you avoid missed pickups and ensure your household complies with local collection guidelines.


Trash and Recycling Collection in Palm Beach County

One of the final utilities to confirm before moving into a new home is trash and recycling collection. Unlike some counties that use a single provider, Palm Beach County's waste collection services vary by municipality, neighborhood, and residential community. Knowing who manages your service will help ensure your garbage and recycling are collected on the correct schedule.

Who Provides Trash Collection?

The provider for your home depends on where you live. Depending on your location, waste collection may be:

  • Managed by your city or town, with municipal sanitation services handling garbage, recycling, and sometimes yard waste.

  • Provided by a private waste management company under a contract with your neighborhood or local government.

  • Coordinated through a homeowners association (HOA), where waste collection is included as part of community services and residents follow HOA guidelines for pickup days and container placement.

Because Palm Beach County has dozens of incorporated municipalities and many planned communities, neighboring neighborhoods may have different providers and collection schedules.

Confirm Your Waste Collection Before Moving

Before your move-in date, ask who is responsible for trash and recycling service. Good sources of information include:

  • Your landlord

  • Property manager

  • Real estate agent

  • Homeowners association (if applicable)

  • The city or town where the property is located

Confirming this information ahead of time can help you avoid missed collections and ensure you understand any community-specific rules.

What to Ask

When you find out who provides your waste service, it's helpful to ask:

  • Which days are trash and recycling collected?

  • Are garbage and recycling carts provided, or do you need to request them?

  • Is yard waste collected separately?

  • How are bulk items such as furniture or appliances disposed of?

  • Are there special holiday collection schedules?

Knowing these details before you move in will make settling into your new home much easier.

A Helpful Moving Tip

If you're renting in a condominium, gated community, or HOA-managed neighborhood, ask whether trash collection is included in your rent or HOA fees. Some communities provide centralized dumpsters, while others offer curbside pickup with specific rules for container placement and collection times.

Taking a few minutes to verify these details can help you avoid unnecessary fees and keep your new home in compliance with community guidelines.



Check HOA Requirements Before Moving In

If you're moving into a planned community, condominium, or gated neighborhood in Florida, it's important to understand whether your property is governed by a Homeowners Association (HOA). This is especially common in Palm Beach County, where many residential communities have HOA rules that supplement local city or county regulations.

While your city or waste provider determines when trash and recycling are collected, your HOA may establish additional rules about how and when residents must prepare their waste for pickup.

Common HOA Waste Collection Rules

Depending on your community, HOA guidelines may include requirements for:

  • Trash container placement, including where bins should be stored before and after collection.

  • Collection days, with designated times for placing carts at the curb.

  • Recycling requirements, including approved containers and accepted recyclable materials.

  • Bulk pickup procedures for large items such as furniture, mattresses, or appliances.

  • Yard waste disposal, including how branches, grass clippings, and landscaping debris should be bundled or bagged.

These rules are designed to keep neighborhoods clean, safe, and visually appealing while ensuring waste collection runs efficiently.

Why HOA Rules Matter

Failing to follow your community's guidelines can result in:

  • Missed trash or recycling collection

  • Warning notices from the HOA

  • Fines or penalties for repeated violations

  • Complaints from neighbors or property management

Even if your waste provider collects on a certain day, your HOA may require that containers be removed from the curb within a specific time after pickup.

Review Your HOA Documents

Before moving in, ask your landlord, property manager, real estate agent, or HOA representative for a copy of the community's rules and regulations. Pay particular attention to sections covering:

  • Trash and recycling procedures

  • Yard maintenance and waste disposal

  • Bulk item collection

  • Community appearance standards

Understanding these requirements in advance can help you avoid unnecessary fines and make settling into your new neighborhood much smoother.

A Helpful Tip

Keep a copy of your HOA guidelines in an easily accessible place or download a digital version if available. Having the rules on hand makes it simple to check collection schedules and community requirements whenever you need them.


Update Your Mailing Address Before You Move

After arranging your utilities, one of the most important moving tasks is updating your mailing address. Taking care of this before or shortly after your move helps ensure that bills, financial statements, government correspondence, and other important documents arrive at your new home without delays.

Creating a checklist of organizations that need your new address can make the process much easier and reduce the risk of missing important mail.

Who Should Be Notified?

As part of your move, be sure to update your address with:

  • Banks and credit card companies so statements, replacement cards, and account notifications are sent to the correct address.

  • Insurance providers, including homeowners, renters, auto, health, and life insurance companies.

  • Your employer to ensure payroll records, tax documents, and employment correspondence remain accurate.

  • Vehicle registration if your state requires registration records to reflect your current address.

  • Driver's license, if required under your state's laws.

  • The United States Postal Service (USPS) by submitting a mail forwarding request so eligible mail is redirected to your new address during the transition.

Why Updating Your Address Matters

Keeping your address current helps you:

  • Receive bills and financial statements on time.

  • Avoid missed insurance or loan notices.

  • Ensure tax documents are delivered correctly.

  • Maintain accurate government and vehicle records.

  • Reduce the chance of lost or delayed mail during your move.

Updating your information early can also help protect against identity theft by reducing the likelihood that sensitive documents are delivered to your previous address.

Set Up USPS Mail Forwarding

Even if you've notified everyone of your new address, it's a good idea to request USPS Mail Forwarding before your move. Mail forwarding provides an extra layer of protection by redirecting eligible mail from your old address to your new one for a limited period, giving you time to update all of your accounts.

A Helpful Moving Tip

Keep a checklist of every organization you've contacted and note the date each address change was submitted. Reviewing your mail over the first few weeks after moving can help you identify any companies or organizations that still need to be updated.

Completing these tasks before or soon after moving day helps make the transition smoother and ensures that important correspondence reaches your new home without interruption.


Create a Move-In Checklist for a Smooth Transition

Moving into a new home involves more than packing boxes. A little preparation before moving day can help you avoid unnecessary stress and ensure that your home is ready from the moment you arrive. Creating a move-in checklist is one of the easiest ways to stay organized and avoid overlooking important utility and service arrangements.

By confirming a few key items in advance, you can focus on settling into your new home instead of dealing with unexpected service interruptions.

Your Essential Move-In Checklist

Before moving day, make sure you have completed the following:

  • Electricity activated so power is available when you arrive.

  • Water and sewer service scheduled with the correct utility provider.

  • Internet installation appointment booked or self-installation equipment ready if applicable.

  • Trash collection information confirmed, including pickup days and your waste service provider.

  • Recycling bins received or instructions provided for obtaining them, if applicable.

  • Utility account numbers saved in a secure location for easy reference.

  • Emergency contact numbers for your utility providers in case you need to report an outage or service issue.

Why a Checklist Matters

Preparing these essentials ahead of time can help you:

  • Avoid delays in utility activation.

  • Stay connected for work, school, or entertainment.

  • Keep food refrigerated from your first day.

  • Understand your neighborhood's waste collection schedule.

  • Quickly contact service providers if problems arise.

  • Reduce the stress that often comes with moving.

A simple checklist also makes it easier to keep track of completed tasks and identify anything that still needs attention before move-in day.

Keep Important Information Together

Consider storing your utility account numbers, login credentials, confirmation emails, and customer service phone numbers in one secure place. Whether you use a folder, a notebook, or a digital notes app, having everything organized can save valuable time if you need assistance after moving in.

Try to complete your utility setup and service appointments several days before your move whenever possible. Allowing extra time helps accommodate unexpected delays and gives you peace of mind knowing your essential services will be ready when you arrive.

With a well-planned move-in checklist, you'll be able to settle into your new home more comfortably and spend less time handling last-minute details.

Setting up utilities is one of the first steps toward feeling at home in Flagler or Palm Beach County. Planning ahead ensures that electricity, water, internet, and waste services are ready when you move in. By contacting providers early and understanding local collection schedules, you can avoid service interruptions and enjoy a stress-free move.

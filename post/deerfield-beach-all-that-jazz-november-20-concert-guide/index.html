Deerfield Beach All That Jazz: November 20 Concert Guide

All That Jazz returns to Deerfield Beach on Friday, November 20, 2026, with a free outdoor performance by The Jazz Collective at Villages of Hillsboro Park. Music is scheduled from 7:00 to 9:00 p.m. Bring your own seat, check the city's event notice before leaving, and arrive early enough to sort out parking before the opening set.

The location is convenient for renters in western Deerfield Beach. You can spend Friday night close to the neighborhood without crossing the Intracoastal, paying beach parking rates, or planning the evening around the oceanfront.

What Deerfield Beach has confirmed

Essential event details

The City of Deerfield Beach announcement identifies November 20 as the return of All That Jazz. The city's special-events calendar also lists the concert for that Friday.

  • Event: All That Jazz
  • Performer: The Jazz Collective
  • Date: Friday, November 20, 2026
  • Time: 7:00 to 9:00 p.m.
  • Location: Villages of Hillsboro Park, 4111 NW 6th Street, Deerfield Beach
  • Admission: Free, with no registration listed
  • Seating: Attendees should bring a lawn chair or blanket
  • Food: A food truck is expected, and guests may bring a picnic

A dated local report on the city announcement includes the same details. Since the concert is outdoors, use the city page as the final authority for any same-day changes.

What to check before leaving

The announcement does not cover every logistical question a renter may have. Before leaving home, confirm that the location and performance time remain unchanged. Check as well for city notices about weather, overflow parking, pets, coolers, alcohol, or lawn restrictions.

Rules from a beach event may not apply at Villages of Hillsboro Park. If a policy affects your plans, contact Deerfield Beach Parks and Recreation through the city website instead of relying on a social-media comment or an older concert listing.

Why the location works for Friday night

A western Deerfield Beach location

Villages of Hillsboro Park is west of Interstate 95, near the Hillsboro Boulevard and Powerline Road side of the city. The trip is different from heading to a concert near the pier. You will not need to route everyone toward A1A or contend with the usual Friday beach traffic.

For renters around Century Village, Independence Bay, Natura, the Villages of Hillsboro, or homes near Powerline Road, the park is on the same side of the city. Residents near Parsons Way, including the area around this three-bedroom Deerfield Beach home, can use local streets instead of making an east-west trip to the ocean.

Leave time for arrival

I would aim to reach the park area around 6:15 or 6:30 p.m. This is planning advice, not an announced gate time. The extra time allows you to park legally, carry chairs to the lawn, visit the food truck, and find a spot before the scheduled 7:00 p.m. start.

If you are meeting friends, agree on one obvious rendezvous point before anyone starts driving. Park entrances are poor meeting places once cars begin arriving. A shared map pin works better than a string of "Where are you?" messages.

Transportation and parking checks

Driving and legal parking

The city announcement names the venue but does not provide an event-specific parking map or promise a particular number of spaces. Nearby shopping centers, private communities, residential drives, and fire lanes are not concert overflow unless signs or event personnel explicitly authorize their use.

Use this parking routine:

  1. Enter the park name in your navigation app before leaving, not just "All That Jazz."
  2. Follow temporary signs and staff directions when you arrive.
  3. Read every posted restriction before walking away from the vehicle.
  4. If the immediate area is full, ask an event worker for an approved alternative instead of choosing a private lot yourself.
  5. Photograph your parking location or save a pin, particularly if you arrive after sunset.

The city's general parking page mainly explains municipal parking rules and beach-area zones. It does not set the plan for this park concert, so follow the event notice and instructions at the site.

Shuttle, rideshare, and rail limitations

Deerfield Beach operates free community shuttles, but the city says the fixed routes run six days a week and use scheduled stops. Review the current route map, operating hours, and last trip on the city's transportation page before depending on a shuttle for the trip home after a 9:00 p.m. concert.

The Deerfield Beach Tri-Rail station has commuter parking and connections, according to the station information page, but the park is separate from the station. A rail rider still needs a confirmed first-mile and last-mile plan. Check the actual Friday timetable rather than assuming a train will match the final song.

For an app-based ride, place the pickup pin away from the busiest entrance if staff permit it. Confirm the vehicle and driver in the app, wait in a lighted public area, and allow for a short pickup delay when the concert ends.

Ask accessibility questions early

The city describes its transportation services as compliant with Americans with Disabilities Act guidelines. That description does not specify the surface, viewing arrangement, or drop-off procedure for this concert. A guest who uses a wheelchair, walker, or other mobility aid should ask Parks and Recreation about the accessible route from parking, designated spaces, restrooms, and an appropriate passenger drop-off point.

Bring the valid placard or plate if your party needs accessible parking, and display it as required. An early arrival can help because accessible spaces, like other convenient spaces, may fill.

What to bring to the lawn

A practical concert kit

Seating is on the lawn rather than in assigned seats. Pack for the walk from the car as well as the time on the grass, and keep the load light enough to carry in one trip.

  • A low lawn chair or blanket for each person
  • A small flashlight or charged phone for the walk back
  • Insect repellent
  • A light layer for anyone who gets cool after sunset
  • Water, plus a picnic only if it complies with the current event rules
  • A compact poncho if showers are possible
  • Any essential medication
  • A small trash bag so the group can leave the lawn clean

Leave large equipment at home unless the city specifically allows it. Oversized umbrellas, wagons, tents, amplified speakers, and tall chairs may block sightlines or conflict with event rules. Check before packing glass containers, alcohol, grills, pets, or anything that must be staked into the ground.

Keep food plans simple

The event information says a food truck is expected and guests may bring a picnic. If food service matters to your group, have another option besides the truck. Popular items can sell out, lines may build before the music, and dietary choices could be limited.

You can also pick up dinner along Hillsboro Boulevard before turning toward the park. Keep cold food chilled, skip a spread that requires a large table, and pack only what you can carry out afterward.

Weather and event-change planning

Do not rely on the early-November forecast

A forecast issued on November 5 is not reliable for November 20. The current National Weather Service outlook covers only the near term, so it cannot promise the conditions on concert night.

Start checking the National Weather Service Miami office during concert week. On Friday, review the hourly forecast, radar, rain probability, wind, and any hazardous-weather messages for Broward County.

Three weather checks

Use these checks instead of depending on the weather icon in a phone app:

  1. The morning check: Review the city event page and the National Weather Service forecast.
  2. The late-afternoon check: Look at radar before packing the car.
  3. The departure check: Reopen the city announcement for a postponement, cancellation, or location update.

A passing shower does not necessarily mean the outdoor concert is canceled. Wait for the organizer's decision about whether the performance will proceed. Silence on an unofficial event page does not confirm that the lawn is open.

Rain and flood awareness

November is still within the Atlantic hurricane season, but the actual local forecast and city guidance will determine the plan on November 20. Never drive through standing water to save a few minutes or reach a particular parking space.

If recent rain has softened the ground, closed-toe shoes and a waterproof seat layer will be more useful than dress shoes. A blanket may absorb moisture from the grass even if no rain falls during the performance.

A simple local itinerary

Before the music

For an easier evening, place a food order before the dinner rush or eat at a Deerfield Beach business along Hillsboro Boulevard or Powerline Road. Keeping the meal near the venue avoids crossing the city twice.

  • About 5:30 p.m.: Eat nearby or collect a picnic.
  • About 6:15 p.m.: Reach the park area and follow event parking directions.
  • Before 7:00 p.m.: Set up your chair, use the restroom, and visit the food truck.
  • 7:00 to 9:00 p.m.: Enjoy The Jazz Collective.
  • After 9:00 p.m.: Pack your belongings, check the lawn, and walk back with your group.

These arrival suggestions are separate from the official program. They provide some breathing room around the city's published performance time.

After the final set

If your group wants dessert or a late bite, choose the business before the concert and confirm its Friday hours directly. You will not have to search from the parking lot while concert traffic is leaving.

Drivers should secure chairs and coolers before joining the exit flow. Rideshare users can request the car once the group is packed and ready, then check the plate and driver details before getting in.

Final renter checklist

Make the small decisions at home and save this checklist for November 20:

  • Recheck the city announcement and special-events calendar.
  • Confirm the venue in your navigation app.
  • Review the current weather and radar.
  • Charge phones and share the meetup point.
  • Pack seats, water, repellent, a light layer, and a small trash bag.
  • Verify any accessibility arrangements before departure.
  • Use only approved parking and obey temporary signs.
  • Confirm the last shuttle, train, or rideshare plan if you are not driving.
  • Leave valuables at home or out of sight.
  • Carry everything off the lawn at the end of the night.

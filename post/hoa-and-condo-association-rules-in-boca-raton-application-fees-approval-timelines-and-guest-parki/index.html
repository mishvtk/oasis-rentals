HOA and condo association rules in Boca Raton: application fees, approval timelines, and guest parking

Renting or buying a home in Boca Raton can involve more than signing a lease or purchase contract. Many communities are governed by a homeowners association (HOA) or condominium association, and those associations may have their own application, approval, parking, and guest policies.

For renters, understanding these rules before applying can help prevent delays, unexpected fees, or problems with move-in.


Check the Association Rules Before Applying for a Boca Raton Condo or HOA Property

If you are planning to rent a condo or home in Boca Raton, checking the association rules should be one of the first steps before submitting an application. A property may look like a good fit, but the condominium association or homeowners association (HOA) can have its own application process, documentation requirements, fees, and approval procedures.

Before paying an application fee or signing a lease, ask the landlord, seller, property manager, or association for the current governing documents and application requirements.

What Documents Might You Need?

Depending on the community, a rental or occupancy application may require some or all of the following:

  • A completed association application

  • Government-issued identification

  • Proof of income or employment

  • Background or credit information

  • A copy of the proposed lease

  • Vehicle information

  • Emergency contact information

  • Application, screening, or processing fees

  • Additional documents requested by the association

Not every Boca Raton community will require the same information. Some associations may have a detailed screening process, while others may have a simpler registration procedure.

Why You Should Review the Governing Documents

Florida condominium and HOA communities operate under governing documents that can establish rules for how the property and community are managed. For condominiums, Florida law provides for governing documents such as the declaration, articles of incorporation, and bylaws.

For homeowners associations, Florida law also gives associations powers and responsibilities that operate together with their governing documents.

This means renters should not assume that a rule from one Boca Raton condo or HOA applies to another community.

For example, one community may require association approval before a tenant moves in, while another may have different registration or screening procedures. Some communities may also have specific rules concerning leases, vehicles, parking, pets, occupancy, or the length of a rental.

Ask About Fees Before Applying

Application and processing fees can also vary by community and by the type of approval required.

For condominium associations, Florida law addresses transfer fees and provides specific limits when an association is required to approve a sale, lease, sublease, or other transfer and the governing documents authorize the fee.

Because the rules can depend on the property's governing documents and the type of transaction, prospective renters should ask for the exact fee schedule in writing before submitting an application.

Confirm the Approval Process

It is also important to find out who actually handles the application.

The process might involve:

  1. The landlord or property manager submitting the application.

  2. The association or management company reviewing the documents.

  3. Background or other screening being completed.

  4. The association providing approval or requesting additional information.

  5. The tenant receiving confirmation before moving in.

Ask how long the process normally takes and whether the lease can begin before association approval is issued. If approval is required, avoid making assumptions about the move-in date until the association confirms the application has been approved.

Ask for the Rules Before You Commit

A smart Boca Raton renter should request the association's current documents and rules before paying nonrefundable fees or committing to a lease.

In particular, ask about:

  • Rental restrictions

  • Minimum lease terms

  • Association approval requirements

  • Application and screening fees

  • Parking and vehicle rules

  • Guest restrictions

  • Pet policies

  • Occupancy limits

  • Move-in and move-out requirements

  • Required deposits

  • Rules for renewing or extending a lease

Getting this information early can help prevent delays and unexpected costs.

Every Boca Raton condo or HOA community can have its own requirements, so the safest approach is to check the association rules before applying.

Ask for the current governing documents, application package, fee schedule, and approval requirements. Then compare those rules with your rental plans before submitting an application.

Doing this early can help you avoid applying for a property that does not meet your needs and can make the rental process much smoother.


Application Fees: Ask What You Are Paying For

When renting a condo or HOA property in Boca Raton, application fees can become an important part of your move-in budget. In addition to the landlord's application or screening costs, a community may have its own association application, transfer, registration, or move-in charges.

Before submitting an application, ask for a written breakdown of exactly what each fee covers.

What Florida Law Says About Condo Transfer Fees

For Florida condominium associations, state law places specific limits on certain fees connected with the approval of a sale, lease, sublease, or other transfer.

Under Florida Statutes Section 718.112, a condominium association generally cannot charge a transfer-approval fee unless the association is required to approve the transfer and the fee is authorized by the declaration, articles of incorporation, or bylaws.

The statute currently provides that an authorized transfer fee may not exceed $150 per applicant, subject to statutory CPI adjustments. For purposes of calculating the fee, spouses and parents with dependent children are treated as one applicant. A fee also cannot be charged when the lease or sublease is being renewed with the same tenant.

This does not mean every Boca Raton condominium charges $150. The actual authorized fee may be lower, and the association's governing documents determine whether the applicable transfer-approval fee is permitted.

HOA Fees Can Be Different

Homeowners associations operate under a different statutory framework, including Florida's Chapter 720. HOA communities may have their own governing documents establishing requirements, charges, and procedures.

For that reason, renters should not assume that the fee rules for a Boca Raton condominium automatically apply to an HOA property.

If you are renting a single-family home, townhouse, or other property governed by an HOA, ask the landlord or property manager for a written list of all association-related charges before paying.

Questions to Ask Before Paying

Before submitting an application, consider asking:

  • What is the association application fee?

  • What specifically does the fee cover?

  • Is the fee refundable if the application is denied?

  • Is there a separate background-check or screening fee?

  • Is the fee charged per person or per household?

  • Are there separate vehicle or parking registration fees?

  • Are there move-in or move-out fees?

  • Is an elevator reservation fee required for a condominium building?

  • Is a security deposit required by the association?

  • Is that security deposit separate from the landlord's security deposit?

  • Are there any other registration or administrative charges?

Getting the answers in writing can help you avoid unexpected expenses later.

Association Fees vs. Landlord Fees

It is also important to distinguish between landlord or property-management fees and association fees.

For example, you could potentially have:

Landlord or property manager costs

  • Rental application fee

  • Tenant screening fee

  • Security deposit

  • First month's rent

  • Pet-related charges, if applicable

Association-related costs

  • Association application or transfer fee

  • Background screening

  • Vehicle registration

  • Parking registration

  • Move-in or elevator reservation fee

  • Association-required security deposit, where authorized

Not every property will have all of these charges. The goal is to identify every potential cost before you commit.

Ask About Association Security Deposits

Florida condominium law also allows an association, when authorized by the declaration, articles, or bylaws, to require a prospective tenant to place a security deposit into an association escrow account. The statutory provision limits that deposit to the equivalent of one month's rent and specifies that it is intended to protect against damage to common elements or association property.

This is different from the security deposit you may be required to pay to the landlord under the lease.

If an association requires a separate deposit, ask who holds the money, what it covers, when it can be used, and how it will be returned.

Calculate the Total Move-In Cost

When comparing Boca Raton rentals, don't look only at the advertised monthly rent.

A property advertised at a certain monthly price may have additional upfront costs associated with the rental and association approval.

Before applying, calculate:

First month's rent + security deposit + landlord fees + association fees + screening costs + parking/vehicle fees + other required move-in costs

This gives you a more realistic picture of how much cash you will need before moving in.

Get the Fee Schedule Before You Apply

The best approach is simple: ask questions before paying.

Request the current association application package and fee schedule. Confirm whether each charge is refundable, who receives the payment, what the fee covers, and whether additional charges could apply.

For condominium properties, compare the requested transfer-related fee with the requirements in the association's governing documents and the applicable Florida condominium statute. For HOA properties, review the community's governing documents and ask the association or property manager to explain the applicable charges.

A few minutes of research before applying can help you avoid unexpected costs and make it easier to compare Boca Raton rental properties accurately.


Approval Timelines Can Affect Your Move-In Date

One of the biggest mistakes renters can make when applying for a Boca Raton condo or HOA property is assuming that an approved lease automatically means they can move in immediately.

In an association-managed community, there may be a second step after the landlord approves your rental application: approval by the condominium association or homeowners association (HOA).

If association approval is required before occupancy, the tenant may need to complete the association's application process before receiving permission to move in.

Why Association Approval Can Take Time

The processing time can depend on the individual community and its procedures.

For example, the application may need to go through:

  • Application submission and document review

  • Payment of association fees

  • Background or screening checks

  • Management-company review

  • Board or committee review, where applicable

  • Verification of employment or other information

  • Correction of missing or incomplete documents

  • Written association approval

  • Vehicle or parking registration

  • Move-in scheduling

Because communities have different governing documents and administrative procedures, there is not one universal statewide approval deadline for every ordinary condo or HOA rental application.

For that reason, renters should ask the association or property manager about the expected processing time before signing a lease with a tight move-in date.

A Special 7-Day Rule for Servicemembers

Florida does provide a specific rule for prospective tenants who qualify as servicemembers under Florida law.

Under Florida Statutes Section 83.683, if a condominium association, cooperative association, or homeowners association requires a prospective servicemember tenant to complete a rental application before occupying the property, the association must process the application within 7 days after submission.

The association must also notify the servicemember in writing within that 7-day period whether the application is approved or denied. If the application is denied, the association must provide the reason for the denial.

If there is no timely denial, the association must allow the owner to lease the property to the servicemember, and the landlord must lease it to the servicemember, provided the other application and lease requirements are satisfied. The statute also states that these provisions cannot be waived or modified by agreement.

This is a specific statutory protection for qualifying servicemembers. It should not be interpreted as a general 7-day approval requirement for every Boca Raton rental applicant.

Don't Confuse Lease Approval With Move-In Approval

Suppose a landlord approves your application on August 20 and signs a lease beginning September 1.

That does not necessarily mean you can automatically move in on September 1 if the community requires association approval first.

The association may still need to complete its own review and issue approval before occupancy.

This is particularly important if you are:

  • Moving from another city or state

  • Booking movers in advance

  • Arranging temporary accommodation

  • Scheduling utility transfers

  • Shipping furniture

  • Taking time off work

  • Coordinating a family move

A delay of several days can create additional costs if your moving arrangements are already fixed.

Ask These Questions Before Signing

Before committing to a rental with association approval requirements, ask the landlord or property manager:

1. Does the association need to approve my application?

Find out whether association approval is mandatory before occupancy.

2. How long does approval normally take?

Ask for the community's typical processing timeframe rather than relying on a general estimate.

3. When should I submit the application?

Some communities may recommend submitting the paperwork well before the lease start date.

4. Does the application require a background or credit check?

Additional screening can affect processing time.

5. Are there specific board or committee review dates?

If an application must be reviewed during a scheduled meeting, timing could affect the move-in date.

6. What happens if documents are missing?

An incomplete application may not be considered fully submitted.

7. Will I receive written approval?

Ask for confirmation that the association has approved the application before scheduling the final move-in.

Build Extra Time Into Your Moving Schedule

If you are planning to rent a Boca Raton condo or HOA property, give yourself some breathing room.

A practical timeline might include:

  1. Submit the association application

  2. Pay required fees

  3. Provide all requested documents

  4. Complete background or other screening

  5. Allow time for association review

  6. Receive written approval

  7. Complete vehicle or parking registration

  8. Schedule the building or community move-in

  9. Confirm the final move-in date

The more requirements a community has, the more important it becomes to start the process early.

Don't Schedule Everything Around the Lease Start Date

A lease that begins on the first of the month does not necessarily mean the association will finish its approval process by the first.

Before scheduling movers or making nonrefundable travel arrangements, confirm that the association has approved your application and that the property is cleared for occupancy.

If you are working with a property manager or real estate agent, ask them to confirm the association's requirements and expected timeline in writing.

Association approval can be an important part of renting a Boca Raton condo or HOA property. The landlord's approval and a signed lease may not be the final step if the community requires separate approval.

For most renters, the timeline depends on the individual association's governing documents and procedures. Qualifying servicemembers have a specific statutory 7-day processing and notification rule under Florida law.

The safest strategy is to start the association application early, submit complete documentation, ask about the expected processing time, and obtain written approval before finalizing your move-in arrangements.


Guest Parking Can Be More Complicated Than It Looks

When renting a condo or HOA property in Boca Raton, renters often focus on the monthly rent, application process, and amenities. Parking may seem straightforward, especially when a community has apparently empty spaces.

However, guest parking can be one of the more complicated parts of living in an association-managed community.

An empty parking space does not necessarily mean it is available for your guest. Condominiums and HOAs can have specific rules governing resident spaces, guest spaces, permits, overnight parking, registration, and towing.

Guest Parking Rules Can Vary by Community

Every community can have different parking procedures. Depending on the property, guest parking rules may address:

  • Guest parking permits

  • Digital guest registration

  • Physical guest passes

  • Overnight parking

  • Maximum guest-stay periods

  • The number of guest vehicles allowed

  • Reserved parking spaces

  • Towing enforcement

  • Parking decals or passes

  • Commercial vehicles

  • Oversized vehicles

  • Street parking

  • Resident versus guest spaces

Some communities may allow guests to park without advance registration during the day but require registration for overnight stays. Others may require residents to register every guest vehicle through an online system or obtain a temporary parking pass.

Before moving in, ask the property manager or association for the current parking rules and guest-registration procedures.

Don't Assume an Empty Space Is Available

One common mistake is assuming that an unoccupied space is available simply because nobody is using it.

A space could be:

  • Assigned to another resident

  • Reserved for a specific unit

  • Designated for visitors under certain conditions

  • Restricted during certain hours

  • Reserved for emergency or service use

  • Subject to a permit requirement

Parking in the wrong space could result in a warning, violation, or towing, depending on the community's rules and enforcement procedures.

If your guests are visiting for the first time, make sure they know exactly where they are allowed to park.

Overnight Guest Parking May Have Different Rules

Overnight parking is especially important for renters who regularly have family or friends staying with them.

A community may limit how long a guest vehicle can remain in a guest space. For example, the association may require registration after a certain number of hours or prohibit overnight parking without prior authorization.

Before a guest stays overnight, ask:

Does the vehicle need to be registered?

Is a guest parking pass required?

How many consecutive nights can a guest park?

Is there a limit on the number of overnight guests or vehicles?

Does the guest need to move the vehicle during certain hours?

These details can prevent unnecessary parking violations.

Florida Law Also Addresses HOA Parking Restrictions

Florida law places limits on certain homeowners association parking restrictions.

Under Florida Statutes Section 720.3075, HOA governing documents generally may not prohibit a property owner or a tenant, guest, or invitee from parking a personal vehicle in an area where that person has a right to park under applicable state, county, or municipal regulations.

The statute also addresses certain restrictions involving work vehicles and provides specific conditions and exceptions. (leg.state.fl.us)

This does not mean that guest parking in an HOA community is unrestricted.

The law applies to specific circumstances, and the property's governing documents, parking configuration, and applicable state, county, and municipal regulations still matter.

For example, a community may have rules governing where a vehicle can park, whether a particular space is assigned, and whether certain vehicles qualify as personal vehicles or work vehicles under the statute.

Condo Parking Rules Can Be Different

Condominium parking is governed under a different statutory framework from HOA communities.

Florida's Condominium Act and the individual condominium's declaration, bylaws, rules, and regulations can establish requirements concerning the use of common elements and limited common elements.

This is one reason renters should not assume that a parking rule from one Boca Raton condominium applies to another.

The same is true when comparing a condo with an HOA townhouse or single-family home.

Ask About Towing Before Your Guests Arrive

Towing policies are another important consideration.

Ask the association or property manager:

  • What circumstances can result in towing?

  • Are guest vehicles required to display a permit?

  • Is digital registration required?

  • How long can a guest remain parked?

  • Are overnight guests permitted?

  • Where are visitors allowed to park?

  • Are there designated guest spaces?

  • What happens if a guest parks in a resident's assigned space?

  • Is street parking permitted?

  • Are there restrictions on commercial or oversized vehicles?

Understanding the rules in advance is much easier than dealing with a towing problem after it happens.

Keep Guest Parking Information Handy

If you frequently have visitors, save the community's parking instructions on your phone.

Keep track of:

  • Guest registration website or app

  • Parking permit instructions

  • Guest space locations

  • Overnight parking requirements

  • Maximum parking periods

  • Management or security contact information

  • Towing information

You can then quickly register a visitor without having to search for the rules every time someone comes to visit.

Guest parking may look simple, but HOA and condo communities can have detailed rules about where visitors can park, how long they can stay, and whether they need a permit or registration.

Florida law does provide protections concerning certain HOA parking restrictions, including provisions involving personal vehicles and certain work vehicles. However, those protections do not eliminate community-specific parking rules. (leg.state.fl.us)

Before renting a Boca Raton condo or HOA property, ask for the current guest parking rules and find out exactly how visitors should register and where they are permitted to park.

A few minutes of preparation can help prevent parking violations, towing, and unnecessary conflicts with your neighbors or association.

What Happens If Parking Rules Are Violated?

Parking rules may seem like a minor part of renting a Boca Raton condo or HOA property, but violations can lead to warnings, fines, towing, or other enforcement measures.

The consequences depend on the community's governing documents, the type of violation, and applicable Florida law. Renters should understand the rules before allowing themselves, family members, or guests to park on the property.

Parking Violations Can Have Consequences

A parking violation could involve:

  • Parking in a resident's assigned space

  • Using a guest space without authorization

  • Failing to register a guest vehicle

  • Parking overnight when overnight parking is prohibited

  • Exceeding the permitted guest-stay period

  • Parking a commercial or oversized vehicle where it is prohibited

  • Failing to display a required parking pass or decal

  • Parking in a restricted or reserved area

  • Violating community parking or vehicle-registration rules

Depending on the association's rules, an initial violation might result in a warning. Repeated or more serious violations could result in additional enforcement action.

HOA Fines Are Subject to Florida Law

Florida's HOA statute allows an association to levy reasonable fines against a parcel owner or the parcel's tenant, guest, or invitee for violations of the declaration, bylaws, or reasonable rules of the association.

However, the association must follow statutory procedures.

Under Florida Statutes Section 720.305, a fine generally cannot exceed $100 per day for a single violation, and the total fine for a continuing violation generally cannot exceed $1,000, unless the governing documents provide for a higher amount. The statute also establishes notice and hearing requirements before certain fines can become effective. (leg.state.fl.us)

The law also provides that an association may be able to suspend certain use rights, including common-area use, under specified circumstances. (leg.state.fl.us)

This does not mean that every parking violation automatically results in a $100 fine. The actual consequences depend on the association's governing documents, the specific violation, and the applicable law.

Renters Should Understand the Owner May Be Involved

If you are renting a property, a parking violation involving your vehicle or your guest's vehicle may not remain solely between you and the parking enforcement company.

The association's relationship is generally with the property owner and the community's governing documents may establish enforcement procedures involving the owner, tenant, guest, or invitee.

For example, if a guest repeatedly parks in a restricted area, the property owner may receive a notice concerning the violation even though the owner was not driving the vehicle.

That is why renters should take association parking rules seriously.

What If Your Guest Receives a Warning?

If your guest receives a parking warning, don't ignore it.

Instead:

  1. Read the notice carefully.

  2. Identify the specific parking rule involved.

  3. Check whether the vehicle needs to be moved.

  4. Contact the property manager or association if you are unsure what to do.

  5. Keep documentation of your communications.

  6. Make sure future guests understand the parking requirements.

If the notice threatens towing, treat the situation as urgent.

Do not assume that an empty parking space is available simply because no vehicle is currently occupying it.

What If the Vehicle Is Threatened With Towing?

If you receive a towing warning, contact the association, property manager, security office, or other designated contact promptly.

Ask:

  • Why is the vehicle subject to towing?

  • What rule was allegedly violated?

  • Does the vehicle need to be moved immediately?

  • Is a guest registration or permit missing?

  • Where can the vehicle legally be parked?

  • Is there a process for disputing the violation?

  • Who should you contact if the vehicle has already been towed?

Keep photographs of the vehicle, parking space, signs, permits, and any notices you receive. Documentation can be useful if there is a disagreement about what happened.

Depending on the applicable governing documents and Florida law, rules can address the conduct of owners, tenants, guests, and invitees.

Florida HOA law specifically references violations involving a parcel owner or the parcel's tenant, guest, or invitee when addressing certain fines. (leg.state.fl.us)

Therefore, if you are renting a Boca Raton property, make sure your guests understand the basic parking rules.

For condominium associations, Florida law addresses certain fees associated with approval of sales, leases, subleases, and other transfers. The governing documents must authorize the applicable fee, and statutory limits and conditions may apply. (leg.state.fl.us)

For an HOA, the applicable rules can be different. That is why renters should request the actual fee schedule instead of relying on assumptions about what an association is allowed to charge.

Ask How Long Approval Takes

A signed lease does not necessarily mean you can move in immediately.

If association approval is required, the application may need to go through the property manager, management company, committee, or board before occupancy is permitted.

Ask:

  • How long does approval normally take?

  • When should the application be submitted?

  • Are applications reviewed on specific dates?

  • Does the association require a board meeting?

  • What happens if documents are missing?

  • Will approval be provided in writing?

If your lease has a firm start date, build extra time into your schedule for association processing.

Florida law provides a specific seven-day application processing and notification requirement for qualifying servicemember applicants under certain circumstances, but that is a special statutory rule and should not be treated as a general approval deadline for all renters. (leg.state.fl.us)

Review the Parking Rules

Parking is another area where community rules can make a significant difference.

Before signing, confirm:

  • Which parking spaces are assigned to the unit

  • Whether the unit has a designated parking space

  • Where guests are permitted to park

  • Whether guest registration is required

  • Whether overnight guest parking is allowed

  • How long a guest vehicle can remain

  • Whether parking permits or decals are required

  • Whether commercial or oversized vehicles are restricted

  • What circumstances could result in towing

Florida law places certain limits on HOA parking restrictions, including provisions concerning personal vehicles and certain work vehicles. However, this does not mean community parking is unrestricted. Applicable laws and the association's governing documents still need to be considered. (leg.state.fl.us)

Ask About Move-In Rules

Moving into a condo or HOA community may involve more than simply receiving the keys.

Some communities require advance scheduling for:

  • Moving trucks

  • Elevators

  • Loading areas

  • Building entrances

  • Move-in dates

  • Security deposits

  • Certificates of insurance

  • Approved moving hours

For a high-rise condominium, for example, the building may require you to reserve a service elevator several days in advance.

A community may also restrict the hours during which movers can access the property.

Ask about these requirements before booking your moving company.

Review the Documents Before Paying

Ideally, prospective renters should have an opportunity to review the applicable association rules before becoming financially committed to the property.

Ask the landlord or property manager for the current:

  • Declaration

  • Bylaws

  • Rules and regulations

  • Rental restrictions

  • Application package

  • Fee schedule

  • Parking rules

  • Move-in procedures

  • Guest policies

The exact documents you receive will depend on whether the property is governed by a condominium association, HOA, or another type of community association.

A Boca Raton rental should be evaluated based on more than the monthly rent and the condition of the property.

If the property is located in a condo or HOA community, the association's governing documents can affect your rental experience, including how long you can rent, how you apply, what you pay, where you and your guests can park, and how you move in.

Before signing, request the current association documents and get important requirements in writing.

Taking the time to review these rules before committing can help you avoid unexpected fees, application delays, parking problems, and move-in complications.


Boca Raton Renters Should Budget for More Than Rent

When comparing rental properties in Boca Raton, it is easy to focus on the advertised monthly rent. However, the monthly rent is only one part of the total cost of moving into a home.

For renters considering a condo or HOA property, upfront costs and association requirements can make a significant difference in the amount of money needed before move-in.

Before choosing a property, calculate the complete move-in budget rather than comparing rent prices alone.

Your Initial Move-In Costs May Include

Depending on the property and community, you may need to budget for:

  • First month's rent

  • Security deposit

  • Landlord application fee

  • HOA or condo application fee

  • Background or credit screening fee

  • Association registration fees

  • Pet-related charges

  • Parking-related charges

  • Moving expenses

  • Utility deposits

  • Renters insurance

  • Other property-specific charges

Not every rental will have all of these expenses. The exact costs depend on the lease, property owner, management company, utility providers, and association rules.

First Month's Rent and Security Deposit

The largest upfront expenses are often the first month's rent and security deposit.

Florida law regulates residential security deposits and advance rent under Florida Statutes Section 83.49. Depending on how the landlord holds the deposit, different statutory requirements apply concerning deposits, notices, and the return of funds at the end of the tenancy. (leg.state.fl.us)

Before signing, confirm:

  • How much is the security deposit?

  • Is additional advance rent required?

  • Who will hold the deposit?

  • What conditions apply to getting the deposit back?

  • Are there separate deposits required by the association?

Understanding these costs helps you determine how much cash you will need at the beginning of the lease.

Landlord and Association Application Fees

A rental application may involve more than one application.

The landlord or property manager may charge an application or screening fee, while a condominium or HOA community may have a separate association application process.

For condominium associations, Florida law addresses certain fees associated with approval of leases and other transfers and places statutory conditions and limits on authorized transfer-approval fees. (leg.state.fl.us)

For HOA properties, applicable requirements can differ.

Before applying, ask for a written list of:

  • Landlord application fees

  • Background-check fees

  • Association application fees

  • Registration fees

  • Transfer-related fees

  • Move-in fees

  • Other required charges

Pet Costs Can Add Up

If you have a pet, check both the lease and association rules before signing.

Potential expenses may include:

  • Pet deposits

  • Pet fees

  • Pet rent

  • Association registration fees

  • Pet-related screening charges

The association may also have its own rules concerning the number, size, or type of pets permitted.

Don't assume that approval by the landlord automatically means the pet is approved by the community.

Parking Can Affect Your Budget

Parking is another expense renters sometimes overlook.

Depending on the property, you may encounter:

  • Additional parking fees

  • Vehicle registration fees

  • Guest parking requirements

  • Parking permits or decals

  • Garage fees

  • Move-in parking requirements

Before signing, find out exactly how many parking spaces come with the rental and whether additional charges apply.

If you regularly have visitors, also ask whether guest parking registration or overnight parking involves additional requirements or fees.

Moving Expenses

Moving costs can vary significantly depending on the property.

For a condominium, you may need to account for:

  • Moving company charges

  • Truck rental

  • Elevator reservations

  • Moving deposits

  • Building access requirements

  • Required insurance documentation

  • Loading-area reservations

Some buildings restrict moving hours or require advance scheduling.

Ask the property manager or association about move-in procedures before booking movers so that you don't discover an unexpected requirement on moving day.

Utility Deposits and Connection Costs

Utilities can also affect your initial budget.

Depending on the provider and your account history, you may need to pay deposits or connection-related charges for services such as:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Internet

  • Other property-specific services

Also confirm which utilities are already included in the rent or covered by the association.

A property with a slightly higher monthly rent could potentially have lower out-of-pocket utility responsibilities, while another property may require the tenant to establish several accounts.

Don't Forget Renters Insurance

Renters insurance is another cost to consider.

A landlord or association may require proof of renters insurance before move-in. Even when it is not required, coverage can help protect your personal belongings and provide liability coverage depending on the policy.

Ask whether the lease specifies minimum coverage requirements and whether the association has any additional insurance requirements for tenants.

Calculate the Total Cost Before Comparing Rentals

Instead of asking only:

"What is the monthly rent?"

ask:

"How much will I need to spend to move in, and what will my total monthly housing cost be?"

For example, your initial budget could look like:

**First month's rent

  • Security deposit

  • Application and screening fees

  • Association fees

  • Pet charges

  • Parking charges

  • Moving expenses

  • Utility deposits

  • Renters insurance

    = Estimated move-in cost**

Then calculate your ongoing monthly expenses separately.

This approach gives you a more realistic comparison between properties.

A Lower Rent Does Not Always Mean a Lower Cost

Suppose one Boca Raton property has a lower advertised rent but requires significant association application, registration, parking, or move-in costs.

Another property may have a higher monthly rent but fewer upfront requirements.

Looking only at the advertised rent could make the first property appear cheaper when the actual move-in cost is considerably higher.

That is why renters should compare total housing costs, not just the headline rental price.

Ask for a Complete Cost Breakdown

Before applying, ask the landlord or property manager to provide an estimate of all required upfront costs.

A useful question is:

"Can you provide a complete breakdown of all costs I will need to pay before move-in, including landlord, association, parking, pet, and other required fees?"

Then verify the information with the association if necessary.

Getting the information in writing can make it easier to compare properties and avoid surprises.

Boca Raton renters should budget for more than the advertised monthly rent.

Your actual move-in cost may include the first month's rent, security deposit, application and screening fees, HOA or condo charges, parking costs, pet expenses, moving expenses, utility deposits, and renters insurance.

For condo and HOA properties, association requirements can add another layer of costs and procedures.

Before choosing a rental, calculate both the total upfront move-in cost and the expected monthly housing cost. This gives you a much clearer picture of whether the property truly fits your budget.

HOA and condo association rules can have a major impact on renting in Boca Raton. Application fees, approval procedures, and guest parking rules are not necessarily the same from one community to another.

Before signing a lease, ask for the association's current rules in writing, confirm all fees, find out how long approval is expected to take, and understand exactly how guest parking works.

The goal is simple: know the association rules before you commit to the property.

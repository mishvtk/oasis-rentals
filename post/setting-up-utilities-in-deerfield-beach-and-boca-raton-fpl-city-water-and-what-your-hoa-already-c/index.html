Setting up utilities in Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton: FPL, city water, and what your HOA already covers

Moving into a rental, condo, townhouse, or single-family home in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton means more than signing a lease or closing on a property. You also need to determine which utilities you must set up yourself and which services may already be included through your landlord, condominium association, or HOA.

The biggest mistake renters and buyers can make is assuming that every property handles utilities the same way. In South Florida, responsibility can vary by property, service area, and HOA documents.

Here is what to know before moving in.


Electricity: Start With FPL When Moving to Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton

Electricity is one of the first utilities renters and homeowners should arrange when moving to Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, Florida. Before your move-in date, confirm which electric provider serves the specific property and arrange service so that power is available when you receive the keys.

For many South Florida properties, Florida Power & Light (FPL) is the electric utility. However, the provider can vary by location, so it is important to verify service for your exact address rather than assuming FPL is automatically responsible for the property.

What You Need to Start Electric Service

If your new home is served by FPL, setting up an account can generally be completed online. FPL says customers establishing personal electric service need:

  • The service address

  • The requested service start date

  • Identification information, such as a Social Security number or driver's license number, when applicable

  • A valid email address for creating an FPL.com user ID

If you do not have a Social Security number, FPL states that another form of identification, such as a driver's license or passport number, may be used.

Schedule Service Before Your Move-In Date

Do not wait until moving day to arrange electricity if you can avoid it. When you know your lease or purchase closing date, contact the electric provider and request service for the date you expect to take possession.

FPL allows customers to select a future service-start date. This can make it easier to coordinate electricity with your lease start date or closing.

FPL also states that it can typically connect service the same day when an order is received by 12 p.m., Monday through Saturday. Orders received after that time are generally connected by the following business day. However, property-specific conditions can sometimes require additional time.

For that reason, renters should not assume that placing an order at the last minute will always result in immediate service.

Check Whether the Property Already Has Electricity

Before setting up a new account, ask the landlord, property manager, seller, or HOA whether electricity is currently active.

A rental property may have power available when you move in, but that does not necessarily mean electricity is included in your rent. In many rental situations, the tenant is responsible for establishing an account and paying the electric bill separately.

Ask these questions before signing the lease:

  1. Is electricity included in the rent?

  2. Which company provides electricity to the property?

  3. Is the electric account currently active?

  4. When should the tenant transfer or start service?

  5. Are there any property-specific requirements for establishing service?

  6. Are there any HOA rules concerning access to meters or electrical equipment?

Getting these details in writing can help prevent confusion during move-in.

What About Moving From Another Florida Address?

If you already have FPL service at another address, you may be able to transfer your service rather than opening a completely new account.

FPL says customers can transfer service to another location as long as the new property is within an FPL-served area. You will need the new service address and the date you want service to begin.

If the new property is not served by FPL, you will need to stop your existing FPL service and establish service with the appropriate electric provider for the new address.

Budget for Electricity Separately

When calculating the real monthly cost of renting in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, remember that advertised rent may not include electricity.

Your monthly housing budget may also need to cover:

  • Electricity

  • Water and sewer

  • Trash service, depending on the property

  • Internet

  • Renters insurance

  • Parking or other property fees

  • HOA-related charges, where applicable

Electricity can also vary significantly from month to month. Air conditioning is particularly important to consider in South Florida, where cooling can be a major part of household electricity usage.

When comparing rental properties, ask whether the landlord can provide previous utility information or an estimated monthly range. This can give you a better idea of the property's total housing cost.

Do Not Assume the Utility Provider

One of the most important steps is verifying the electric provider for the exact property address.

FPL provides an online service-area process that allows customers to determine whether a location is served by FPL. If the address is outside FPL's service territory, you will need to identify and contact the appropriate provider instead.

This is especially important when moving between different Florida cities or neighborhoods because utility service boundaries do not necessarily follow city or county boundaries.

A Simple Move-In Strategy

For renters moving to Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, a practical approach is:

1. Confirm the provider.Ask the landlord or property manager and verify the provider using the utility's address-checking tools.

2. Confirm who pays.Make sure your lease clearly explains whether electricity is included or paid separately by the tenant.

3. Gather your information.Have your service address, requested start date, identification information, and email address ready.

4. Schedule service early.Request service for your move-in date rather than waiting until you arrive.

5. Keep your confirmation.Save your confirmation number, requested start date, and account information.

6. Check the property after move-in.Make sure the electricity is operating and contact the utility promptly if there is a problem.

Electricity should be one of the first utilities on your move-in list when renting or buying in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton. FPL is an important starting point, but always verify the provider for your specific address.

If FPL serves your property, you can generally establish, stop, or transfer service online. FPL says you will need the service address and preferred start date, along with applicable identification information. Same-day connection is typically available for orders received by noon Monday through Saturday, although individual property conditions may affect timing.

Planning your electricity service before moving day can help ensure that your new home is ready when you arrive and can also give you a clearer picture of your true monthly housing costs.


Water and Sewer in Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton: What Renters Need to Know

Water and sewer are two utilities renters should confirm before moving into a home, condo, or townhouse in South Florida. Unlike electricity, where the provider is often easier to identify, water service can depend on the property's exact location, service area, and how the community handles utility billing.

For renters in Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton, the first step is to determine who provides the water service and who is responsible for paying the bill.

Water and Sewer in Deerfield Beach

Water service in Deerfield Beach can be a little more complicated because not every property inside the city is served by the City of Deerfield Beach.

The City of Deerfield Beach states that properties in the city are served by either the City of Deerfield Beach Water System or Broward County Water and Wastewater Services. The city provides an interactive water-service lookup tool that allows residents to check which provider serves a specific address.

This is particularly important when renting a condo, townhouse, apartment, or property located near a utility service-area boundary.

Check Your Water Provider Before Moving In

Before setting up an account, check the property's address through the city's water-service lookup.

If the property is served directly by the City of Deerfield Beach, a new residential account generally requires:

  • A completed application for service

  • A signed lease for tenants or closing statement for owners

  • A driver's license or government-issued photo ID

  • A security deposit

The city currently states that the security deposit is based on meter size and starts at $185. The deposit must be paid before service begins.

Don't Assume Water Is Automatically Your Responsibility

Renters should also check the lease and ask the landlord or property manager whether water and sewer are billed directly to the resident.

Some condominium and HOA communities may have water arrangements that differ from a typical single-family rental. Depending on the property and association, water may be included in association expenses, billed through the association, or handled separately.

Before opening a water account, ask:

"Is water and sewer individually billed to my unit, or is it included in the HOA or association fees?"

Getting the answer before moving in can help prevent an unnecessary account or unexpected utility charges.

What About Sewer?

Sewer charges are often connected to the property's water and wastewater service rather than being treated as a completely separate utility that renters arrange independently.

For that reason, when you confirm the water provider, also ask who bills for sewer and whether sewer is included in your monthly rent, HOA charges, or utility account.

If you have a water or sewer emergency in Deerfield Beach, the city provides after-hours water/sewer contacts on its official website.

Water and Sewer in Boca Raton

Boca Raton operates its own Utility Services Department and provides water and wastewater services to customers in the city as well as portions of the surrounding Boca Raton area. The city says it provides water service to approximately 28,000 city residents and 6,500 unincorporated residents.

However, having a Boca Raton mailing address does not necessarily mean the property is inside the City of Boca Raton. Some areas with Boca Raton addresses are located in unincorporated Palm Beach County, so renters should confirm the property's actual utility service area.

Setting Up a Boca Raton Water Account

When moving into a residence served by the City of Boca Raton, water service is generally established in the name of the owner or occupant unless the landlord has specified otherwise for a rental property.

To open an account, customers generally need to complete an application and provide documentation such as:

  • A signed lease for a rental property

  • A home settlement statement for a purchase

  • Required account information and identification

The city says applications should be submitted at least 24 hours before service is needed.

Boca Raton Water Deposits

Boca Raton's residential water deposits depend on the meter size and account location.

For typical residential accounts, the city's service policies list deposits of approximately:

  • Owners: $100 to $125

  • Renters: $300 to $375

The deposit is billed when the account is opened and is generally due within 30 days. The city also states that deposits earn interest annually and are refundable when the account is closed, subject to applicable requirements.

Water Bills Can Include Sewer Charges

One important point for renters is that a Boca Raton utility bill is not necessarily just a water bill.

The city states that its bimonthly bill can include water, sewer, and garbage charges. The bill includes usage-based water charges as well as fixed charges such as customer, water-capacity, and sewer charges.

That means renters should look at the entire utility bill when estimating their monthly housing costs rather than budgeting only for water consumption.

HOA and Condo Residents Should Ask Before Opening an Account

If you're renting a condo or townhouse in either Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, don't assume that every utility works the same way as it would in a single-family home.

Before moving in, ask the landlord, leasing agent, HOA, or property manager:

  1. Who provides the water?

  2. Who provides wastewater/sewer service?

  3. Is the unit individually metered?

  4. Will I receive a utility bill directly from the provider?

  5. Is water or sewer included in the HOA or association fees?

  6. Is garbage included with the water bill?

  7. Are there any utility charges included in my rent?

  8. Do I need to transfer the account into my name?

Getting these answers in writing can make your move-in budget much more accurate.

Budget for More Than Just Rent

When comparing rentals in Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton, don't calculate affordability using rent alone.

Your monthly housing expenses may include:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Garbage

  • Internet

  • Renters insurance

  • HOA or association-related charges, if applicable

  • Parking fees

  • Pet fees

  • Other property-specific utility charges

Water and sewer can also vary depending on the property, meter, billing structure, and consumption.

For a rental in Deerfield Beach, first determine whether the property is served by the City of Deerfield Beach or Broward County Water and Wastewater Services. The city's water-service lookup can help you identify the provider for the specific address.

For a rental in Boca Raton, confirm whether the property falls within the city's utility service area and determine whether the landlord or tenant is responsible for establishing the water account. Boca Raton's utility bill can include water, sewer, and garbage charges, so renters should understand the complete billing arrangement before signing a lease.

The simplest rule is: never assume water and sewer are included or excluded. Ask before you move in, check the lease, and confirm the provider for the exact property address.

What is included depends on the individual community, its governing documents, the association's budget, and the terms of your lease or purchase agreement.

HOA-Covered Water Versus Individually Metered Water

When renting or buying a condominium or townhouse in Deerfield Beach, Boca Raton, or another South Florida community, one of the most important utility questions is who is responsible for the water bill.

You might assume that because you pay an HOA or condominium association fee, water is automatically included. That is not always the case.

A community may have a master water meter serving multiple units, or it may have individual water meters for each residence. The billing arrangement can be completely different from one community to another.

Understanding the difference can help you avoid unexpected utility charges and make a more accurate monthly housing budget.

What Is HOA-Covered Water?

A condominium or townhouse community may use a master water meter that measures water consumption for multiple units or the entire property.

In this arrangement, the utility provider may send the water bill to the association rather than directly to individual residents.

The association then pays the bill using association funds. The cost may effectively be shared among owners through their regular HOA or condominium assessments.

For a renter, this can mean that you do not need to open a separate water account.

However, that does not automatically mean the water is free.

The cost of water may already be reflected indirectly in the association's operating expenses and, for a rental property, the landlord may account for association expenses when setting the rent.

What Is Individually Metered Water?

An individually metered property has a separate water meter associated with the individual unit.

When this arrangement is used, the homeowner or tenant may be responsible for establishing an account with the appropriate water provider and paying charges based on the unit's usage and applicable service fees.

For renters, the lease should clearly state whether the tenant is responsible for opening and maintaining the water account.

Depending on the location, the property could be served by a municipal utility, county utility system, or another authorized provider.

Why the Difference Matters

The difference between master-metered and individually metered water can have a significant effect on your monthly expenses.

With a Master Meter

The arrangement may look something like this:

Utility provider → HOA/condominium association → Association budget → Owners' assessments

The association receives and pays the water bill, and the expense is handled through the association's finances.

With Individual Meters

The arrangement may instead look like:

Utility provider → Individual unit → Homeowner or tenant

The resident is responsible for the account and receives the bill directly.

Neither arrangement is automatically better. The important thing is knowing which system applies to the property you are considering.

Master Meter Does Not Always Mean Everyone Pays the Same

Even when a community has a master water meter, the association may have different ways of allocating the expense.

The water cost could be treated as a general association expense, or the governing documents could establish another method of allocating utility costs.

Some communities may also use submetering or other systems to track individual consumption even though the primary utility account is maintained by the association.

Because these arrangements vary, renters and buyers should not assume that all residents are charged for water in exactly the same way.

Individual Meters Can Make Usage Easier to Track

With an individual meter, residents can generally see how their own water consumption affects their bill.

This can make it easier to identify unusual increases caused by:

  • A running toilet

  • A leaking faucet

  • Irrigation use

  • Plumbing problems

  • Increased household consumption

However, an individual meter also means the resident is directly responsible for understanding the utility's rates, billing schedule, deposits, and account requirements.

What Renters Should Ask Before Signing a Lease

If you're renting a condo or townhouse, ask the landlord or property manager:

  1. Does my unit have its own water meter?

  2. Is water included in the HOA or condominium fees?

  3. Who receives the water bill?

  4. Do I need to open a water account in my name?

  5. Is sewer billed separately or together with water?

  6. Are water charges included in my rent?

  7. Can I see a recent utility bill for the unit?

  8. Are there any additional water or utility reimbursement charges?

Getting these answers before moving in can prevent confusion later.

Check the Lease and Association Documents

The physical meter arrangement is only part of the answer.

The lease, condominium documents, HOA documents, and utility billing policies can determine who is financially responsible for the water expense.

For example, a unit could be located in a community with a master meter, but the lease could require the tenant to reimburse the landlord for a specified portion of the water expense.

Conversely, a property with an individual meter may have a rental agreement under which the landlord pays the water bill.

The key is to determine who is legally and contractually responsible for the bill, rather than relying solely on the type of meter.

Why This Matters in Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton

Water service arrangements can vary even between properties located in the same city.

In Deerfield Beach, properties may be served by different water providers depending on the property's service area. The city provides a water-service lookup tool that can help residents determine the provider for a particular address.

Boca Raton also has its own municipal water and wastewater service area, while properties with Boca Raton mailing addresses can have different governmental or utility arrangements depending on their location.

For that reason, the safest approach is always to verify the exact property address and then confirm the billing arrangement with the landlord, HOA, condominium association, or utility provider.

HOA-covered water and individually metered water are two very different billing arrangements.

With a master meter, the association may receive and pay the community's water bill, with the cost handled through association finances. With individual meters, each unit may have its own account and the resident may be responsible for paying the utility provider directly.

The setup can be completely different from one community to another, even within the same city.

Before signing a lease or purchasing a condo or townhouse, find out:

Who provides the water? Who receives the bill? Who pays it? And is the cost included in your HOA fees, rent, or individual utility account?

Knowing the answer before move-in can help you avoid unexpected bills and build a more accurate housing budget.

What Renters Should Confirm With the Landlord Before Signing a Lease

Renting a home, condo, or townhouse in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton involves more than comparing monthly rent. One of the most important steps before signing a lease is understanding which utilities and property-related expenses you will actually be responsible for paying.

A rental advertised at a certain monthly price can become significantly more expensive if the tenant is also responsible for electricity, water, sewer, trash, internet, landscaping, or other services.

For that reason, renters should ask the landlord or property manager for a written breakdown of utilities and other recurring expenses before signing the lease.

Ask for a Written Utility Breakdown

Don't rely only on a verbal statement such as "utilities are not included."

Ask specifically which services are included in the rent and which ones must be paid separately.

Your lease should clearly explain the financial responsibilities of both the landlord and tenant.

At a minimum, confirm responsibility for:

  1. Electricity

  2. Water

  3. Sewer

  4. Trash

  5. Internet

  6. Landscaping, if renting a house

  7. Irrigation

  8. Pest control

  9. Gas or propane, if applicable

  10. HOA or condominium fees

Understanding each item before move-in can help prevent unexpected bills.

1. Electricity

Electricity is often one of the largest variable utility expenses for a Florida renter.

Ask:

  • Do I need to open an account in my name?

  • Which company provides electricity to the property?

  • Is there an existing account that needs to be transferred?

  • Is electricity included in the rent?

  • Are there any special billing arrangements?

If you are responsible for electricity, ask the landlord for the current provider and, if available, previous average bills.

Florida's hot climate means air-conditioning use can have a significant impact on monthly electricity costs.

2. Water

Water responsibility can vary significantly between properties.

A single-family home may require the tenant to establish an individual water account, while a condominium or townhouse community may have water handled through the association.

Ask:

"Do I open the water account myself, or is water billed through the landlord or association?"

Also confirm whether water charges include sewer.

3. Sewer

Don't assume that sewer is automatically included with water.

In many communities, water and wastewater charges appear together on the same utility bill. However, the billing arrangement can vary by property.

Ask:

  • Is sewer included with the water bill?

  • Is sewer included in the rent?

  • Is sewer billed by the HOA or condominium association?

  • Will I receive a separate bill?

Knowing the answer helps you calculate your actual monthly housing cost.

4. Trash

Trash collection may be handled by the city, HOA, condominium association, landlord, or a private provider.

Ask:

  • Who provides residential garbage collection?

  • Is the service included in the rent?

  • Is it included in HOA or condominium fees?

  • Do I need to establish an account?

  • Where should garbage containers be placed?

  • Is recycling included?

This is particularly important in communities where trash service is centrally arranged.

5. Internet

Internet service is frequently the tenant's responsibility, but some apartment, condo, and HOA communities may include internet or cable through a bulk service agreement.

Ask:

  • Is internet included?

  • Which provider services the property?

  • Is there a community-wide internet agreement?

  • What internet speed is included?

  • Are equipment fees included?

  • Can I choose another provider?

Remote workers should pay particular attention to this expense because reliable high-speed internet can be an essential part of the monthly budget.

6. Landscaping

If you're renting a single-family house, determine who is responsible for maintaining the yard.

The lease should explain whether landscaping is:

  • Included in the rent

  • Paid by the landlord

  • Paid by the tenant

  • Handled by an HOA

  • Shared between the landlord and tenant

If the tenant is responsible, clarify whether that includes mowing, trimming, tree maintenance, fertilization, or other services.

7. Irrigation

Irrigation can be easy to overlook when calculating rental expenses.

Ask who pays for:

  • Irrigation water

  • Irrigation system maintenance

  • Repairs to sprinkler systems

  • Timer or controller problems

In some communities, irrigation may be connected to a separate system or may be covered as part of community landscaping.

Don't assume that responsibility for ordinary water automatically tells you who pays for irrigation.

8. Pest Control

Florida properties may require regular pest-control services, particularly single-family homes.

Ask whether pest control is:

  • Included in the rent

  • Provided by the landlord

  • Included through the HOA

  • The tenant's responsibility

Also clarify whether routine pest control and treatment for an infestation are handled differently.

Getting this information in writing can prevent disagreements later.

9. Gas or Propane

Some Florida homes use natural gas or propane for appliances, water heating, pools, grills, or other equipment.

If the property uses gas or propane, ask:

  • Which appliances use gas?

  • Who pays the gas bill?

  • Is the service already connected?

  • Who arranges propane deliveries?

  • Who pays for tank rental, if applicable?

  • Who is responsible for maintaining the equipment?

These costs may not apply to every property, but they should be identified before signing the lease if they do.

10. HOA or Condominium Fees

Renters should also understand whether an HOA or condominium association is involved.

In many rental situations, the property owner pays the association's assessment, but the tenant may still be responsible for certain costs or services under the lease.

Ask:

  • Is there an HOA or condominium association?

  • Who pays the regular association fees?

  • Are water, sewer, trash, internet, or landscaping included through the association?

  • Are there separate tenant-related fees?

  • Are there move-in or elevator reservation fees?

  • Are there community rules that affect the tenant?

Don't assume that because an expense is paid by the association it is automatically included in your rent.

Put Everything in Writing

A written agreement is much easier to rely on than a verbal promise.

Before signing, review the lease and make sure it clearly identifies:

Landlord-paid expenses

and

Tenant-paid expenses

If the landlord says, for example, "I'll take care of the water," ask that the arrangement be reflected in the lease or another written document.

Ask About Average Utility Costs

If you are responsible for utilities, consider asking the landlord for recent average costs.

"Could you provide the approximate monthly electricity and water costs for the previous tenant?"

Utility bills can vary based on household size, usage, season, and weather, so previous bills are not guarantees of future expenses. However, they can provide a useful starting point for budgeting.

Don't Forget Move-In and Account Deposits

Some utility providers may require deposits or fees when a new account is established.

Before moving in, ask whether you will need to budget for:

  • Utility deposits

  • Connection fees

  • Transfer fees

  • Activation charges

  • HOA move-in fees

  • Elevator reservation fees

  • Internet installation fees

These are one-time expenses, but they can add significantly to the cost of moving.

Create Your True Monthly Housing Budget

When comparing rental properties in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, look beyond the advertised rent.

Your actual monthly housing cost may look more like:

Rent + electricity + water + sewer + trash + internet + insurance + other tenant-paid services

If you are renting a house, you may also need to account for landscaping, irrigation, pest control, or pool maintenance depending on the lease.

A property with slightly higher rent could actually be more affordable if several utilities and services are included.

A Simple Pre-Lease Question

Before signing a lease, ask the landlord:

"Can you provide a written list showing every utility and recurring property expense, and whether it is paid by the landlord, tenant, HOA, or condominium association?"

This gives you a clear starting point and reduces the chance of discovering unexpected expenses after moving in.

If you're renting in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, don't evaluate a property based on rent alone.

Before signing the lease, confirm responsibility for electricity, water, sewer, trash, internet, landscaping, irrigation, pest control, gas or propane, and HOA or condominium-related expenses.

The most important rule is simple:

If you are expected to pay it, make sure you know about it before signing the lease.

A clear written breakdown of utilities can help you compare rental properties more accurately, prepare for move-in costs, and avoid disputes with the landlord later.


What Homebuyers Should Check Before Closing

Buying a home in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton involves more than reviewing the purchase price and mortgage payment. Before closing, buyers should understand the property's ongoing utility costs, HOA responsibilities, and community services.

This is especially important when purchasing a condominium or townhouse. A property with a relatively low HOA fee may still require the owner to pay water, sewer, trash, internet, and other utilities separately. Conversely, a higher HOA fee does not automatically mean that all utilities are included.

Before closing, take the time to verify exactly what you will be responsible for paying.

Ask for Current Utility Bills

One of the most useful things a buyer can request is a history of the property's utility expenses.

Ask the seller or real estate agent for recent bills for services such as:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Gas or propane, if applicable

  • Internet or cable, if there is a community arrangement

Recent bills can help you estimate the property's actual operating costs.

Remember that previous utility bills are not guarantees of future expenses. Electricity use can vary significantly depending on household size, thermostat settings, occupancy, and seasonal weather.

However, a bill history can provide a much better starting point than simply guessing.

Review the HOA Documents

If the property is located in an HOA or condominium community, review the association documents before closing.

Depending on the type of property, relevant documents may include:

  • Declaration of covenants

  • Articles of incorporation

  • Bylaws

  • Rules and regulations

  • Association budget

  • Financial statements

  • Current assessment information

  • Maintenance responsibilities

  • Insurance information

  • Recent meeting information

Florida law provides specific disclosure and document requirements for condominium and homeowners' associations, so buyers should carefully review the documents applicable to the property.

Check the HOA Budget

The HOA budget can provide important clues about what your association dues actually pay for.

Look for expenses related to:

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash collection

  • Landscaping

  • Irrigation

  • Pool maintenance

  • Common-area electricity

  • Insurance

  • Exterior maintenance

  • Security

  • Cable or internet

  • Other community services

This can help you determine whether a service is being paid through association assessments or whether individual owners are expected to establish separate accounts.

Don't Judge an HOA by the Monthly Fee Alone

A $300 monthly HOA fee is not necessarily cheaper than a $600 fee.

The lower-fee community might require homeowners to pay water, sewer, landscaping, exterior maintenance, or other expenses separately.

The higher-fee community might include several of these services in its operating budget.

The important question is not simply:

"How much is the HOA fee?"

Instead, ask:

"What exactly does the HOA fee cover?"

A Simple Utility Setup Strategy for Deerfield Beach and Boca Raton

Setting up utilities before moving into a new home can feel overwhelming, especially when different properties have different providers and HOA or condominium arrangements.

A simple plan can help you avoid service interruptions, duplicate bills, and unexpected move-in expenses.

Whether you're renting or buying in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton, use the following five-step strategy to organize your utilities before you move.

Step 1: Ask the Landlord, Seller, or HOA

Start by finding out exactly which utilities and services you are responsible for.

Don't assume that the previous owner or tenant handled everything the same way you will.

Ask for a written breakdown covering:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Internet

  • Cable

  • Landscaping

  • Irrigation

  • Pest control

  • Gas or propane, if applicable

  • HOA or condominium-related charges

If you're renting, check that the information matches your lease.

If you're buying, review the HOA or condominium documents and association budget.

The goal is to identify which expenses are yours and which are already covered through rent, association fees, or another arrangement.

Step 2: Confirm the Water Provider

Water is one of the utilities you should verify using the property's exact address.

Deerfield Beach

The City of Deerfield Beach explains that properties in the city may be served by either the City of Deerfield Beach water system or Broward County Water and Wastewater Services.

Use the city's water-service information and address lookup to determine which provider serves the property.

If the City of Deerfield Beach provides the service, check the requirements for establishing a new account, including identification, documentation, and any applicable deposit.

Boca Raton

For Boca Raton properties, review the city's Utility Services information and confirm whether the property is within the city's water and wastewater service area.

Don't rely solely on a Boca Raton mailing address. Confirm the actual utility provider for the property.

Ask About the Meter

Also determine whether the property is:

  • Individually metered

  • Master metered

  • Submetered

  • Billed through an HOA or condominium association

This can determine whether you need to open your own account.

Step 3: Set Up Electricity

Electricity should be one of the first utilities you arrange before moving in.

If the property is served by Florida Power & Light (FPL), arrange your service start date before moving day.

Ideally, electricity should be active when you receive the keys.

This is particularly important in South Florida because air conditioning is an essential part of maintaining a comfortable home.

Before setting up service, have the necessary information available, such as:

  • Property address

  • Service start date

  • Identification

  • Contact information

  • Lease or ownership information, if required

If you're unsure whether FPL serves the property, confirm the provider before opening an account.

Step 4: Confirm HOA-Covered Services

This is one of the most important steps for condo and townhouse residents.

Don't pay for a utility twice.

An HOA or condominium association may already arrange certain community services through association fees.

Depending on the community, these could potentially include:

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Landscaping

  • Irrigation for common areas

  • Cable

  • Internet

  • Pest control

  • Common-area electricity

  • Other community services

However, every association is different.

Before opening accounts, ask:

"Which utilities and services are already included in my HOA or condominium fees?"

Then confirm whether you need to establish individual accounts for anything else.

Check Bulk-Service Agreements

Some communities have bulk agreements for services such as internet, cable, trash, or other utilities.

If a bulk agreement exists, ask whether:

  • The service is mandatory

  • The cost is included in your association assessment

  • You receive the service automatically

  • You can upgrade the service

  • Additional fees apply

This information can prevent unnecessary accounts and unexpected charges.

Step 5: Keep Copies of Everything

Once your utilities are arranged, keep a record of the setup.

Save:

  • Confirmation emails

  • Account numbers

  • Service start dates

  • Deposit receipts

  • Connection-fee receipts

  • Utility provider contact information

  • Move-in meter readings

  • HOA utility information

  • Your first utility bills

Taking photographs of the water and electricity meters on move-in day can also be useful, particularly for renters.

If there is a dispute later about when responsibility for utility usage began, having documentation can make it easier to establish the starting point.

Create a Simple Move-In Utility File

You don't need a complicated system.

Create one digital folder or physical file containing:

Property address

Electricity provider: ______

Water provider: ______

Sewer provider: ______

Trash provider: ______

Internet provider: ______

Electric service start date: ______

Water service start date: ______

Account numbers: ______

Deposits paid: ______

Move-in meter readings: ______

Having this information in one place makes it easier to manage your first month in the property.



Start Services Before Moving Day

Don't wait until the day you move in to arrange utilities.

Some providers may require advance notice, deposits, documentation, or account verification.

Whenever possible, schedule services several days before your move-in date.

This gives you time to resolve problems if:

  • The address is listed incorrectly

  • The property has a different utility provider

  • A previous account has not been closed

  • A deposit is required

  • HOA information is missing

  • A service request needs additional documentation

Your Utility Setup Strategy at a Glance

The process can be summarized in five simple steps:

1. Ask: Determine which utilities you are responsible for.

2. Confirm: Verify the water and electricity providers for the exact property.

3. Set up: Arrange electricity and any other individual services before move-in.

4. Check: Confirm which services are already covered through the HOA or condominium association.

5. Document: Save account information, confirmations, deposits, and meter readings.

Setting up utilities doesn't have to be complicated.

Before moving into your Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton home, start by asking the landlord, seller, or HOA exactly which services you are responsible for. Then confirm the water provider, arrange electricity, check HOA-covered services, and keep documentation of every account you establish.

The most important rule is:

Verify first, set up second, and document everything.

A few minutes of preparation before move-in can help prevent duplicate bills, missed services, and unexpected utility costs during your first month in your new home.

Setting up utilities in Deerfield Beach or Boca Raton is relatively straightforward once you understand who is responsible for each service.

The key is not to assume that every property works the same way.

Electricity will generally require its own account when it is the resident's responsibility. Water and sewer depend on the property's service area and billing arrangement. HOA and condominium communities may also include certain services in association fees, but the exact coverage depends on the community's governing documents and agreements.

Before moving day, confirm every utility in writing. A few phone calls to the landlord, HOA, property manager, and utility provider can help you avoid unexpected bills and make your move much smoother.

Palm Coast Adds 264 Homes: What Renters Should Know

Palm Coast renters got a substantial new option on September 30, 2026, when Hillpointe announced the grand opening of Pointe Grand Town Center, a garden-style community with 264 two-bedroom apartments. The additional supply should give renters more choices and may prompt nearby properties to compete through concessions. One community, though, is not large enough to determine rents across Palm Coast.

Anyone shopping this fall should compare the new apartments with established communities and privately managed homes. Calculate the full monthly cost, and ask each leasing office to put its incentives and fees in writing.

What opened in Palm Coast

Pointe Grand Town Center at a glance

Hillpointe’s September 30 grand-opening report names the development Pointe Grand Town Center. The property is on Town Center Boulevard in ZIP code 32164, near Central Park, Imagine School at Town Center, and the commercial area around State Road 100.

The development has 264 garden-style apartments. Each residence is described as a two-bedroom, two-bathroom home with 1,170 square feet. Reported features include full-size washers and dryers, covered patios or balconies, smart-home technology, quartz counters, stainless-steel appliances, and digital keyless entry.

The report lists a pool, fitness center, business center, clubhouse, grilling pavilion, fire pit, dog park, pet spa, electric-vehicle charging stations, community Wi-Fi, and valet trash. These amenities matter in a price comparison because some may carry mandatory monthly charges instead of being included in the advertised base rent.

The project’s path from approval to opening

The Palm Coast City Council approved the project’s technical site plan on May 21, 2024. The approved plan called for apartments in 10 three-story buildings extending north from Central Avenue along Town Center Boulevard.

The approval discussion covered traffic near Lake Avenue and Imagine School as well. The developer agreed to provide turn-lane improvements, and the council tied their completion to certificates of occupancy being issued for half of the development. If you are considering this part of Town Center, test the school-day drive instead of judging the route during a quiet weekend tour.

How the new homes compare with Palm Coast rent benchmarks

August rent figures for the two main ZIP codes

The latest month in the supplied Zillow Observed Rent Index ZIP-code file is August 2026. The typical observed market rent was $1,823.92 in ZIP 32164 and $1,956.34 in ZIP 32137.

ZIP 32164 includes Town Center and many inland neighborhoods. ZIP 32137 covers much of northern Palm Coast and areas closer to the Intracoastal and coast.

What ZORI does and does not measure

ZORI is neither the asking rent for Pointe Grand Town Center nor a quote for every available home. Zillow defines it as a smoothed measure of typical observed market-rate rent, weighted to represent the rental housing stock instead of only the listings available on a given day.

The index works as a market reference, but it cannot replace a property-by-property comparison. A new apartment with a pool, pet facilities, bundled services, and a leasing concession can have a base rent above or below the ZIP benchmark while carrying a very different effective monthly cost.

What renters may notice in the near term

More comparable tours

The most immediate effect is added choice. Someone looking for a two-bedroom apartment can tour several new floor plans in one community, then compare them with established apartment properties, duplexes, townhomes, and single-family homes elsewhere in 32164.

The differences may be easier to judge in person. Town Center may suit someone who values access to State Road 100, Central Park, and I-95. A house or duplex in Lehigh Woods, Seminole Woods, Pine Lakes, or Indian Trails may have a driveway, yard, different storage, or fewer shared amenities.

Our Palm Coast renter guides can help you compare those sections before arranging tours. You can also review the broader rental inventory and use a local home, such as this Palm Coast rental, to compare space, parking, privacy, and total cost.

Leasing incentives may become easier to find

New communities often offer concessions while leasing their first apartments. At the time reviewed, the community’s official contact page advertised up to 10 weeks of free base rent on qualifying longer leases. Shorter qualifying terms had a different promotion. The page says restrictions apply and that fees and add-ons are still due.

That offer shows why it pays to ask for the effective rent instead of relying on the largest promotional headline. An incentive may expire, depend on the move-in date, cover only selected units, or have to be repaid if the resident ends the lease early.

Nearby landlords have another competitor

A large group of newly available apartments gives renters another stop before they make a decision. Nearby managers may adjust availability, improve service, refresh units, or introduce concessions of their own. Some may hold firm because their homes appeal to a different renter, such as a household looking for a garage, fenced yard, or additional bedroom.

In the near term, qualified applicants are likely to see more competition for their business. A citywide rent decline is not guaranteed. Properties competing directly with a new two-bedroom apartment in Town Center are likely to feel the opening most.

Why one development will not set citywide rents

Palm Coast is not a uniform rental market

Choosing Town Center is a different location decision from looking near Matanzas Woods Parkway, Pine Lakes Parkway, Palm Harbor, Belle Terre Parkway, or the Flagler Beach bridge. Housing type, age, flood exposure, school commute, lawn responsibilities, pet rules, and access to I-95 all influence what households will pay.

The August data moved in opposite directions, with 32164 declining month over month while 32137 increased. That difference is a reminder not to attribute Palm Coast’s next rent movement to one opening.

Supply is one part of the equation

Additional supply can give renters more options, particularly when many units begin leasing at the same time. Citywide rents also respond to household growth, employment, mortgage costs, seasonal demand, insurance and operating expenses, construction completions, and the number of current tenants who renew instead of moving.

Florida’s winter migration begins by late fall, which can complicate seasonal demand. Renters should follow actual availability and concessions rather than assume that a grand opening will make every part of Palm Coast cheaper.

The clearest evidence will come after leasing begins

Watch occupancy, how long advertised incentives remain, and the responses from competing properties. If specials disappear quickly, leasing demand may be absorbing the new supply. If concessions remain or grow, renters may gain negotiating room within the same apartment category.

“Attainable” does not mean income-restricted affordable housing

Hillpointe and the grand-opening report describe the apartments as “attainable.” Here, the developer is using the term to position housing intended for working households. The report does not call the development a subsidized property or say that the apartments have income qualifications or regulated rent limits.

Legally restricted affordable-housing programs generally operate differently. For example, HUD program rules connect affordable units to household-income tests and rent restrictions. The word “attainable” does not by itself create an income cap, rent ceiling, voucher program, or below-market price.

Ask the leasing office whether any unit is covered by an income limit, regulatory agreement, or affordability period. If none is, treat the apartment as market-rate housing and compare its complete lease cost with other market-rate options.

Use the complete monthly cost, not the headline rent

Build a side-by-side worksheet

Begin with the base rent and request a written list of every required charge. The Pointe Grand Town Center website currently discloses an Essential Services Package Fee of $199 per month and says utilities may apply too. It also lists a one-time application fee of no more than $100 and an administrative fee of no more than $275. Credit screening may result in another move-in charge.

These are disclosures for this property, not norms across Palm Coast. They show how an apartment advertised near the ZIP benchmark may cost more once mandatory services, utilities, parking, pets, and insurance are included.

  • Base rent: Write down the amount due in a regular month before any concession.
  • Effective rent: Divide the total base rent payable across the full lease term, keeping recurring fees separate.
  • Recurring charges: Note service packages, trash, pest control, internet, amenity charges, parking, garages, and pet rent.
  • Utilities: Find out which accounts you open directly and which services management allocates or bills.
  • Move-in money: Count application and administration fees, deposits, pet charges, and any credit-based fee.
  • Exit costs: Read the clauses on early termination, notice, cleaning, damage, and concession repayment.

Compare the lease term carefully

A large concession tied to a longer lease is not always the best deal. Think about how long you expect to stay in Palm Coast, whether your work or school arrangements could change, and what you would owe if you had to leave early.

Have the leasing agent show you the regular base rent, each month that receives a credit, the total rent payable over the term, and any amount owed if the concession must be repaid. Keep the signed concession addendum with your lease.

Tour the neighborhood as carefully as the apartment

Test Town Center Boulevard at the right time

Pointe Grand Town Center is across from Imagine School at Town Center, and traffic came up during the city approval process. The approved access plan centered on Town Center Boulevard and Lake Avenue and included turn-lane work.

If school arrival or dismissal overlaps with your commute, drive the route at that time. Test the trip from the property to State Road 100 and I-95 too, then compare it with the route from each inland house or duplex on your shortlist.

Check the details of daily life

  1. Stand inside the proposed unit with the air conditioning and appliances off. Listen for noise from the road, hallway, pool, and neighboring units.
  2. Walk from the unit to the assigned or available parking area after dark.
  3. Confirm where guests, work vehicles, trailers, and moving trucks are allowed to park.
  4. Ask which internet providers serve the exact building and whether community Wi-Fi replaces or supplements a private account.
  5. Get the pet requirements for breed, weight, count, waste, and leashes in writing.
  6. Before applying, check whether your furniture fits through the bedrooms, patio door, stairways, and elevators.

What Palm Coast renters should monitor next

Occupancy and concessions

Save screenshots of the property’s promotions on the day you inquire. For the actual agreement, rely on the written offer attached to your application and lease. Over the next leasing cycles, watch whether free-rent offers shrink, remain in place, or expand.

Compare the number of floor plans available for immediate move-in as well. A property listing many move-in dates may give applicants more flexibility. Limited availability may leave less room to negotiate.

Other Town Center construction and approvals

Pointe Grand is one of several multifamily projects discussed for this corridor. During the approval process, the Palm Coast Observer reported that The Legacy at Palm Coast was planned for 300 apartments on the other side of Town Center Boulevard. Future completions, delays, or later phases will have more bearing on competition across the area than this opening by itself.

Check Palm Coast development agendas and local reporting for certificates of occupancy, roadway changes, and newly approved communities. During your own search, repeat the rent-and-fee comparison whenever another group of units begins leasing.

FAQ

What is Pointe Grand Town Center?

Pointe Grand Town Center is a newly opened garden-style apartment community on Town Center Boulevard in Palm Coast. Hillpointe’s September 30, 2026 report says it has 264 two-bedroom, two-bathroom apartments, each with 1,170 square feet.

Will 264 new apartments make Palm Coast rents fall?

Not necessarily. The added supply may give renters more choices and encourage concessions at directly competing properties. Citywide rents also depend on demand, renewals, operating costs, housing type, location, and other construction.

What were Palm Coast’s latest ZIP-code rent benchmarks?

Zillow’s August 2026 ZORI values were $1,823.92 for ZIP 32164 and $1,956.34 for ZIP 32137. ZORI measures typical observed market rent and is not a quote for a particular apartment.

Does “attainable housing” mean income-restricted affordable housing?

No. In this announcement, “attainable” is the developer’s market positioning. The report does not describe the apartments as subsidized or subject to regulated rents and household-income limits.

How should renters compare a leasing special with regular rent?

Ask for the regular base rent, the exact months receiving credits, all mandatory recurring fees, total rent payable over the lease, and any concession-repayment clause. Compare the complete monthly cost instead of the promotional headline.

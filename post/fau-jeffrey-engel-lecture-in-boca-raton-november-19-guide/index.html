FAU Jeffrey Engel Lecture in Boca Raton: November 19 Guide

Presidential historian Jeffrey Engel will present "Seeking Monsters to Destroy: How America Goes to War" at Florida Atlantic University in Boca Raton on Thursday, November 19, 2026. The program runs from 4 to 5:30 p.m. in the Barry and Florence Friedberg Auditorium. The official listing shows a $25 ticket price for members and nonmembers.

The lecture is for residents and newcomers interested in how presidents lead the country toward armed conflict. Buy your ticket in advance, check the parking and security instructions before leaving home, and allow time for the book signing announced by FAU.

The event details at a glance

When, where, and how much

This annual John O'Sullivan Memorial Lecture is part of FAU's history programming. The official FAU course catalog currently lists these details:

  • Date: Thursday, November 19, 2026
  • Time: 4 to 5:30 p.m.
  • Venue: Barry and Florence Friedberg Auditorium
  • Campus address: 777 Glades Road, Boca Raton, Florida 33431
  • Admission: $25 for members and nonmembers
  • Afterward: A book signing is scheduled to follow the presentation

The catalog lists the lecture as a live Boca Raton program. The local event announcement also directs ticket buyers to FAU Events or the box office at 561-297-6124. Event arrangements can change, so confirm availability through one of those channels before planning the rest of your day.

Who is likely to enjoy it

The subject may interest more than regular history readers. Veterans, educators, students, foreign-policy followers, book clubs, and residents who want to place current debates in a longer historical frame may all find something here.

No academic background is required. FAU describes the program as an examination of the historical forces and presidential decisions that send the United States into conflict. That gives the audience a clear question to consider during the lecture.

What "How America Goes to War" is likely to examine

The decision before the battlefield

The title points to decisions made before troops deploy. Presidents must define a threat, explain it to the public, consult advisers, deal with Congress, and decide whether force is necessary. The lecture therefore focuses on presidential judgment, political pressure, diplomacy, and the stories leaders tell about national interest more than battlefield chronology.

FAU says Engel will draw from his latest work, Seeking Monsters to Destroy: How America Goes to War. The university's description promises a close look at the historical forces and presidential choices that draw the country into conflict, with no attempt to survey every American war.

Why presidential choices matter

Presidents do not make war decisions in empty rooms. Intelligence assessments, alliances, domestic politics, earlier commitments, public fear, economic interests, and beliefs about America's place in the world can shape their choices.

A historian can help an audience separate the immediate trigger from the longer path that made intervention possible. Listen for the patterns Engel finds and the exceptions to them. One useful question is why a war began. Another is which assumptions made one option seem more reasonable than the others.

Questions worth taking with you

  • How has the balance between presidential initiative and congressional authority changed?
  • Which past decisions became templates for later presidents?
  • How do leaders build public support when the costs of conflict remain uncertain?
  • When do analogies to earlier wars clarify a crisis, and when do they distort it?
  • What evidence can historians use to separate a leader's public explanation from private reasoning?

Write down one or two questions beforehand. Even without an audience question period, those prompts can help you organize your notes.

Who Jeffrey Engel is

A specialist in presidents and foreign policy

Engel is a professor of history at Southern Methodist University and the David Gergen Director of its Center for Presidential History. His official SMU biography says he founded the center in 2012 and studies the presidency, diplomacy, and American engagement with the world.

FAU says he has authored or edited 15 books. The university points to When the World Seemed New, his study of George H.W. Bush and the end of the Cold War, and Impeachment: An American History. His published work also includes books about the Iraq surge, presidential crises, Franklin Roosevelt's Four Freedoms, the Gulf War, and American foreign relations.

What his background adds

Engel's training covers presidential history and international affairs. SMU lists previous academic appointments at Texas A&M University, the University of Pennsylvania, and Yale University. He also holds a doctorate in American history from the University of Wisconsin-Madison.

His background suggests that he will work from records, institutions, and comparisons among administrations. Expect historical context from a presidential perspective instead of campaign commentary or a step-by-step military lecture.

What to expect in the room

The official program lasts 90 minutes, followed by a book signing. Bring a small notebook and arrive early enough to clear entry screening. If you hope to join the signing line, avoid scheduling a firm commitment immediately after the published end time.

Anyone hoping to have a book signed should ask the ticket office about on-site sales and signing restrictions. Every title may not be stocked, and the venue may not automatically accept outside copies.

Finding Friedberg Auditorium on the Boca Raton campus

Use the campus destination, not just FAU

FAU's Boca Raton campus is large, so "meet me at FAU" is not a useful navigation plan. The university gives the Friedberg venue's address as 777 Glades Road. The auditorium and Ely Meyerson Continuing Education Hall are in the CEH complex. FAU's calendar places the Friedberg Lifelong Learning Center off West University Drive.

Drivers coming from Interstate 95 can use Glades Road and enter at West University Drive. The official OLLI directions sheet identifies Lot 15, across from the auditorium, as the primary parking area.

Parking rules are unusually specific

The current OLLI parking page directs patrons to designated OLLI lots on class days. Vehicles must be parked front-in. University lots without OLLI markings are reserved for students and faculty.

Do not choose a space solely because it is close or back into a marked space. The university says patrons parked correctly in an OLLI-designated lot should not risk a citation. FAU Parking and Transportation handles citation payments and appeals.

A sensible arrival plan

Try to reach the campus entrance well before the 4 p.m. start, especially on your first visit. Campus traffic, locating the correct lot, walking from Lot 15, completing entry screening, and finding your seat all take time.

Save the venue page and parking map to your phone before leaving. A screenshot can help if cellular service slows when many attendees arrive at once.

Your before-you-go checklist

Confirm the essentials

  1. Open the FAU listing and verify that the date, start time, and venue have not changed.
  2. Buy the $25 ticket through the official channel or call the box office at 561-297-6124.
  3. Ask whether your ticket is mobile, printable, or held at will call.
  4. Review the current OLLI parking instructions and identify Lot 15 on the campus map.
  5. Confirm accessibility arrangements if anyone in your party needs an accessible space, assisted listening, or step-free seating.
  6. Ask about book availability and signing rules if the signing is important to you.

Pack lightly for security

The local event announcement reports that FAU screens patrons and bags at the entrance. It recommends bringing a clear bag or a small hand-sized bag.

Bring only what you need, such as identification, your ticket, a phone, keys, a pen, and perhaps a slim notebook. Check the event page for the latest prohibited-items policy before leaving home. An older rule or instructions for another FAU venue may not apply.

Plan for accessibility directly with FAU

Arrange accessibility needs before event day. Contact the organizer or box office with a specific request, such as wheelchair seating, a companion seat, an accessible route from parking, or hearing assistance.

OLLI Boca lists 561-297-3185 and olliboca@fau.edu on its official directions page. An early call gives staff time to explain the correct drop-off point and seating procedure.

Turn the lecture into a practical Boca Raton evening

Keep the geography tight

The lecture starts late enough for an early meal and finishes early enough for dinner afterward. Staying near the Glades Road corridor is easier than crossing the city during rush hour. You could choose a restaurant west of Interstate 95 before the program or wait for campus traffic to clear before heading east toward downtown.

If you are meeting another attendee, agree on one gathering point and arrive in one car when practical. This can prevent one person from circling a campus lot while the other waits at the auditorium.

Use a simple timing rule

For your first campus visit, work backward from the 4 p.m. start. Set aside time for the Glades Road approach, parking, the walk, screening, and seating. The scheduled conclusion is 5:30 p.m., but that may not be your departure time because FAU has announced a book signing afterward.

Leave some room before any dinner reservation instead of booking the earliest available table. A flexible evening near campus will be easier to manage than a broad tour of Boca Raton attractions.

Check the forecast close to the date

November weather is generally better suited to an afternoon campus walk than peak summer weather. Still, a forecast issued in September cannot predict conditions on November 19. Check the weather that morning and bring a compact umbrella only if the venue's current security policy allows it.

Wear shoes suitable for the walk between Lot 15 and the auditorium. If heavy rain is expected, ask FAU for the appropriate passenger drop-off location instead of improvising beside a busy campus road.

The short version

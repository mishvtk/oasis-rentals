Deerfield Beach Rents Rose 8.5% in Three Years

Typical observed rent in Deerfield Beach ZIP 33441 reached $2,566 in August 2026, up 3.23% from August 2025 and 8.5% from August 2023, according to Zillow's ZIP-level Observed Rent Index. The longer view is useful because the index fell during parts of 2025 and early 2026, then rebounded sharply in summer 2026.

This does not mean every Deerfield Beach home now costs $2,566. For renters, the useful point is that the market's typical asking-rent level moved about $201 per month higher in three years, with several potential negotiating windows along the way.

Comparing the same month each year reduces the distraction of ordinary seasonal movement. The table below uses the August value for each year in Zillow's smoothed, monthly ZORI series for ZIP 33441.

What the headline does and does not say

The three-year result shows a clear increase, though the climb was uneven. A renter looking only at August 2025 might have concluded that rents had leveled off. Someone looking only at the July 2026 jump might have expected every new listing to rise at the same speed.

Neither conclusion is dependable. ZIP 33441 contains different housing types and locations, including units closer to the beach and The Cove and homes west of Federal Highway. The index describes the broader market. An individual lease still depends on the home's size, condition, parking, association rules, furnishings and exact location.

The uneven path through 2025

A spring decline interrupted the longer rise

The softness in 2025 was substantial enough for renters to notice. Zillow's ZIP series moved from $2,468.65 in January 2025 to $2,393.34 in April, a decline of about $75 over those three monthly observations.

The index recovered to $2,485.80 by August 2025. That rebound was uneven as well. October registered $2,430.52 before December finished at $2,510.71.

Why a softer month can be useful

A declining index does not guarantee a price cut on the exact townhouse or condo you want. It is still a reason to widen the comparison set and test the advertised terms instead of assuming the asking price is firm.

During a softer stretch, compare homes on both sides of US 1. Check how parking and utilities affect the total cost, and ask how long each unit has been available. Start with the Deerfield Beach rental guides, then build a small set of genuinely comparable homes. Mixing beachside condos with inland single-family houses will not produce a useful comparison.

Early 2026 brought another reversal

Winter strength gave way to spring softness

The index began 2026 at $2,534.07 in January. It slipped to $2,487.43 in February, $2,469.36 in March and $2,440.40 in April. After a brief May recovery, it fell again to $2,432.47 in June.

That June value was about $102 below January. Based only on the first half of the year, you could reasonably have described the local trend as cooling. The next two observations changed the picture.

The summer rebound was sharp

ZIP 33441 ZORI jumped from $2,432.47 in June 2026 to $2,554.52 in July, an increase of approximately 5.02% in one month. It edged another 0.45% higher to $2,566 in August.

A housing budget should not rest on one monthly change. A sharp move may reflect real market pressure, and Zillow smooths ZORI with a three-month moving average. Examine several months, compare the same month across years and verify what current landlords are actually offering.

A practical rule: Use the three-year trend to set your budget, the latest three to six months to judge momentum, and current comparable homes to evaluate a specific lease.

What the $201 increase means for a household

Translate the index into a budget line

The difference between August 2023's $2,364.91 and August 2026's $2,566 is $201.09 per month. If that full difference appeared in an individual household's rent, it would equal approximately $2,413.08 over a twelve-month lease.

That annual figure is a planning illustration. It is not a forecast or a claim that every tenant received the same increase. A renewing renter may be below the market index, while a newly renovated or furnished home near the ocean may be above it.

Budget the effective rent, not just the headline

When comparing two homes, create one monthly total that includes every recurring obligation disclosed in the lease. Ask for the charges in writing before deciding that a lower advertised rent is the better deal.

  • Base monthly rent.
  • Required resident, amenity or association charges.
  • Utilities assigned to the tenant.
  • Parking, pet and storage charges.
  • Lawn, pool or pest-control obligations for a house or townhouse.
  • The monthly value of any genuine concession.

Divide a concession across the full lease term before comparing it with a lower base rent. Also confirm whether the concession must be repaid after an early termination or payment default. The lease language controls that calculation, regardless of the promotional headline.

How to use the trend when renewing

Bring matching comparisons

If your renewal arrives above the 3.23% year-over-year ZIP trend, it is not automatically unreasonable. Your home may have features the index cannot isolate. Even so, the August 2025-to-August 2026 change provides a neutral starting point for a conversation.

Bring three to five current comparisons with the same general property type, bedroom count and location. A condo near Hillsboro Boulevard should not be compared casually with a larger house farther west. Review available rental homes and other professionally presented inventory. For each home, record the rent, included services, deposit, association process and availability date.

Ask about terms besides base rent

If the owner will not change the monthly figure, ask about terms with measurable value. These may include a longer rate lock, a different start date, professional cleaning, a repair that improves daily use, or clarification about which recurring services are included.

Keep the request concrete: "The proposed renewal is this amount, these comparable homes are in this range, and I can commit by this date if we agree on these terms." A short written comparison is more useful than saying the rent simply feels too high.

Lease timing before winter season

Start the comparison in October

Florida's snowbirds are generally present from November through April. A City of Deerfield Beach public-safety study also identifies November through April as the beachfront tourism district's highest-activity period.

This does not prove that every annual rental becomes more expensive in November. For an October renter, it is still sensible to leave enough time to learn about association approval, deposits, pet restrictions and move-in scheduling.

Use a simple timing checklist

  1. Ask your current landlord for renewal terms before you submit notice, if your lease permits that sequence.
  2. Compare the effective monthly cost of at least three similar homes.
  3. Confirm the application and association-approval steps in writing.
  4. Check the exact availability date and whether the home can be delivered on time.
  5. Price moving, overlapping rent and storage before chasing a small monthly saving.
  6. Read concession repayment language before signing.

A nearby Deerfield Beach layout, such as the three-bedroom Parsons Way townhouse profile, can help you identify relevant features to compare, even when a particular home is not currently available. Match bedrooms, bathrooms, parking and outdoor maintenance responsibilities before comparing price.

What renters should watch next

Do not treat $2,566 as a quote

Zillow defines ZORI as a smoothed measure of the typical observed market-rate rent. Its housing-data documentation says the index is weighted to represent the rental housing stock, instead of covering only homes currently advertised.

The figure works well as a market benchmark, though it is not an appraisal of a particular address. A unit's renovation level, lease length, furniture, water proximity, insurance requirements and association rules can all affect its actual asking rent.

Watch the direction across several releases

For the next update, I would ask three questions. Did the index remain above the July level? Did the year-over-year rate accelerate or slow? Did current comparable homes change their prices or begin offering concessions?

If the index declines for several observations while comparable homes remain available, renters may gain room to negotiate. If it stays near its August high and well-matched homes disappear quickly, delaying solely in hope of a lower month may carry more risk.

Methodology and reading the numbers correctly

How Zillow constructs ZORI

Zillow says ZORI measures changes in asking rents while controlling for changes in the quality of available rental stock. Its methodology uses repeated observations of the same rental units, Census-based weighting and a three-month simple moving average.

The dollar-denominated value is tied to the mean asking rent among the middle portion of recent observations. It is then chained backward using the index's monthly changes. This makes ZORI different from a simple average of the listings that happen to be online during one week.

How much did ZIP 33441 rent increase over three years?

The index rose from $2,364.91 in August 2023 to $2,566 in August 2026, an increase of $201.09 per month, or 8.5%.

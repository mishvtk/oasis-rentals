What to Look for in a Furnished Rental Property

Furnished rental properties offer a convenient solution for individuals who are relocating, traveling for business, or simply looking for a hassle-free move-in experience. However, not all furnished rentals are created equal. To ensure you choose a property that fits your lifestyle, budget, and comfort needs, here are some key factors to consider:

1. Quality and Condition of Furniture in a Furnished Rental Property

When renting a furnished property, the furniture becomes more than just decor—it’s a key factor in your comfort, daily living, and even the value you get for your rent. While it might be tempting to assume everything is in top shape because it’s labeled “furnished,” that’s not always the case. Taking the time to inspect the quality and condition of furniture can help you avoid discomfort and costly disputes down the line.

Why Furniture Quality Matters

Furniture isn’t just about aesthetics—it impacts your sleep, productivity, and relaxation. A sagging mattress or a broken chair can make daily life frustrating. More importantly, subpar furniture may be a red flag that the property isn’t well-maintained in general.

In contrast, clean, sturdy, and modern furnishings reflect a landlord who takes pride in the property and is more likely to respond promptly to maintenance requests. Well-chosen furniture also enhances the property's overall feel, helping tenants feel more at home from day one.

What to Inspect Before Signing the Lease

Before committing to a furnished rental, take time to examine every major furniture item. Here’s a checklist:

  • Couches and Chairs: Sit on them to test for comfort and support. Check for stains, rips, or loose legs.

  • Beds and Mattresses: Lie down to check for sagging or creaking. Ask if the mattress has been replaced or cleaned recently.

  • Tables and Desks: Ensure they are stable and level. Wobbling can be a sign of age or poor assembly.

  • Dressers and Cabinets: Open and close drawers and doors to check for sticking or misalignment.

  • Appliances: Test major appliances like the refrigerator, microwave, washing machine, and air conditioning. These should be clean and in working order.

Photographing the furniture during your walkthrough can serve as documentation if any issues arise later.

Signs of Well-Maintained Furniture

Look for the following indicators that furniture is in good condition:

  • Smooth surfaces without visible damage or peeling

  • Clean upholstery with no odor or pet hair

  • Functional hardware (knobs, handles, hinges)

  • Balanced furniture with no instability

  • Electronics or appliances that operate quietly and efficiently

If the furniture is branded or clearly of high quality (e.g., IKEA, West Elm, etc.), that's often a sign of a well-thought-out rental investment.

What to Do If You Find Issues

If you notice damage or excessive wear, address it before signing the lease. Ask the landlord to repair or replace the item, or have it noted in the lease or condition report. In some cases, you might negotiate a rent reduction or request the ability to bring your own furniture.

A well-furnished rental should feel like a home—not a compromise. By taking time to evaluate the furniture’s quality and condition, you protect your comfort and ensure you’re getting fair value from your rental experience.

2. Inventory List: A Crucial Step Before Signing a Furnished Rental Lease

When renting a furnished property, knowing exactly what comes with the unit is just as important as checking the rent or lease length. One of the most essential documents in a furnished rental agreement is the inventory list—a comprehensive record of all items included in the home, from large furniture to small kitchen utensils. This list not only defines what you’re entitled to use during your stay, but it also helps avoid conflicts and confusion down the road.

What Is an Inventory List?

An inventory list is a detailed document provided by the landlord or property manager that outlines every item in the rental unit. It’s commonly used in short-term rentals, corporate housing, and furnished apartments where multiple furnishings and household items are provided.

The inventory typically includes:

  • Large furniture: Beds, sofas, dining tables, dressers, desks, and chairs

  • Appliances: Refrigerator, oven, microwave, washer, dryer, toaster, etc.

  • Electronics: TV, remote controls, Wi-Fi router, lamps

  • Household items: Curtains, rugs, wall art, mirrors

  • Kitchenware: Plates, glasses, cutlery, pots and pans, cooking utensils

  • Bedding and linens: Sheets, pillows, blankets, towels (if provided)

Some landlords even include brand names and item conditions to ensure accuracy and transparency.

Why an Inventory List Matters

  • Prevents disputes: If an item goes missing or is damaged, the inventory serves as proof of its existence and condition at move-in.

  • Protects your deposit: Without a clear list, landlords might deduct charges for items you never used or that were never provided.

  • Clarifies expectations: It ensures you and the landlord have the same understanding of what is included in the rental.

  • Helps with move-out: You’ll know exactly what needs to be left behind and in what condition, avoiding last-minute confusion.

Tips for Tenants

  1. Request a Copy: If one isn’t offered, ask for a detailed inventory list before you sign the lease.

  2. Check Items Yourself: Walk through the property with the list in hand. Inspect each item for damage or wear, and make notes or take photos.

  3. Request Updates: If items are added or removed before you move in, ensure the inventory list is updated accordingly.

  4. Sign and Save: Once both parties agree, sign the inventory list and keep a copy for your records.

What Happens Without One?

Renting a furnished property without an inventory list opens the door to potential disagreements, especially at move-out. You could be held responsible for damages you didn’t cause, or lose part of your security deposit over items you never used or broke. An inventory list sets expectations and serves as legal documentation if needed.

A clear and thorough inventory list is your safety net in a furnished rental agreement. It’s a small step that provides big protection—ensuring that your stay is worry-free and that both you and your landlord are treated fairly.

3. Included Appliances and Utilities: Know What You're Getting in a Furnished Rental

When searching for a furnished rental, it’s easy to focus on furniture and décor—but don’t overlook the importance of included appliances and utilities. These essentials can have a significant impact on your day-to-day comfort, as well as the overall cost of living. Before signing a lease, be sure you understand exactly what's included so you can budget accurately and avoid surprises.

Why Appliances Matter

Appliances can make or break the convenience of your rental experience. While most furnished units come with the basics, the specific appliances provided can vary widely.

Here’s what to look for:

  • Kitchen appliances: Stove, oven, refrigerator, microwave, toaster, blender, and dishwasher

  • Laundry appliances: In-unit washer and dryer, or access to a shared laundry room

  • Heating and cooling: Central air, ceiling fans, space heaters, or portable AC units

  • Small conveniences: Vacuum cleaner, coffee maker, iron, and ironing board

Check each appliance to ensure it’s in working condition. If the listing claims these items are included, verify during your walk-through and ask for repairs or replacements in writing if something is broken or missing.

Utilities: What’s Covered?

Utilities are another key area that can affect your monthly expenses. In furnished rentals, some or all utilities may be included in the rent—especially in short-term or corporate housing. Here are the main ones to confirm:

  • Electricity and gas: Check if you’ll be responsible for these, especially during high-usage months.

  • Water and sewer: These are sometimes included, but not always.

  • Internet and cable: Fast, reliable internet is especially important for remote workers and students.

  • Trash and recycling pickup: These services are often included in apartment complexes, but not guaranteed in standalone homes.

Always ask for clarification and request that utility responsibilities be clearly outlined in the lease.

How It Affects Your Budget

A rental that includes appliances and utilities may appear more expensive at first glance, but it can actually save you money—and stress—in the long run. Not having to set up separate utility accounts or purchase essential appliances reduces your upfront costs and simplifies the move-in process.

Before choosing between two rentals, compare not just the rent prices but also the hidden costs of excluded utilities or missing appliances. A unit with an in-unit washer and dryer and utilities included may end up being the better deal overall.

Checklist for Renters

  • ✅ Ask for a full list of appliances

  • ✅ Test each appliance during your walk-through

  • ✅ Confirm which utilities are included and which are not

  • ✅ Get everything in writing in your lease agreement

  • ✅ Factor appliance condition and utility coverage into your budget decision

Understanding what appliances and utilities are included in your furnished rental can save you time, money, and frustration. Clarify everything upfront, and you’ll enjoy a smoother, more cost-effective rental experience.

4. Lease Terms and Flexibility: Matching the Agreement to Your Lifestyle

Furnished rental properties are popular for their convenience and ready-to-live appeal—but just as important as the furniture inside is the flexibility of the lease. Whether you're relocating for work, traveling, or in between permanent homes, a lease that aligns with your timeline and lifestyle is key to a smooth rental experience.

Understand the Lease Duration

Most furnished rentals are designed for short-term use, but that can mean different things depending on the property and the landlord. Some offer:

  • Month-to-month leases: Ideal for travelers, contract workers, or those testing out a new city.

  • Fixed-term leases: Typically range from 3 to 12 months and may be required for discounts or in seasonal markets.

  • Vacation or seasonal rentals: Often offered weekly or monthly, especially in tourist destinations.

Before signing, make sure the lease term aligns with your intended stay. If you plan to extend your stay, discuss renewal options ahead of time.

Check for Early Termination Clauses

Life is unpredictable—jobs change, plans shift, and emergencies happen. That’s why it’s critical to read the lease for early termination clauses. These clauses outline what happens if you need to leave before the agreed-upon end date. You might encounter:

  • Early termination fees

  • Loss of deposit

  • Requirement to give advance notice (e.g., 30 or 60 days)

  • Options to sublet or find a replacement tenant

Understanding these terms protects you from costly surprises if your situation changes.

Look for Flexibility Options

Furnished rentals often cater to flexible lifestyles, but that doesn’t mean all landlords offer the same level of adaptability. Ask about:

  • Extension options: Can you stay longer if needed? At what rate?

  • Shorter-term leases: Is there a premium for fewer months?

  • Transitioning to long-term: Can the rental become permanent if you decide to settle?

Some landlords may even offer incentives for longer stays, such as reduced rent or utility coverage.

Watch for Penalties and Hidden Fees

Always read the fine print. Some leases include penalties for:

  • Breaking the lease early

  • Not giving proper notice for moving out

  • Failing to maintain furniture or household items

Also, be wary of administrative fees or charges for early renewals or holding the unit for extra days.

Why It Matters

Flexible lease terms can save you stress, money, and time. They’re especially important if you:

  • Are unsure about how long you’ll stay in one place

  • Are waiting to close on a home or secure a job

  • Need a temporary solution while transitioning between cities or countries

Key Questions to Ask Your Landlord

  • What is the minimum and maximum lease term?

  • Is the lease renewable, and under what conditions?

  • What happens if I need to leave early?

  • Are there any fees for changes in lease terms?

A furnished rental should support—not complicate—your lifestyle. By paying close attention to lease terms and asking the right questions, you can choose a home that offers not just comfort, but true flexibility.


5. Location and Safety: Choosing the Right Environment for Your Furnished Rental

When searching for a furnished rental, it’s easy to focus on what’s inside the unit—comfortable furniture, stylish decor, and included amenities. But what’s outside matters just as much. Location and safety are two of the most critical factors in determining whether your rental experience will be comfortable, convenient, and secure.

Why Location Is Key

The location of your furnished rental can directly impact your daily routine, commute, and overall quality of life. Since many furnished rentals are chosen for short-term or transitional stays, being in a convenient, accessible neighborhood is even more important.

Consider the following when evaluating location:

  • Proximity to work or school: Shorter commutes save time and money.

  • Access to public transportation: Buses, trains, and bike lanes can be essential, especially in urban areas.

  • Nearby essentials: Grocery stores, pharmacies, banks, and hospitals should be within easy reach.

  • Dining and entertainment: Restaurants, cafes, parks, and gyms can make your stay more enjoyable.

  • Walkability and parking: Walkable neighborhoods add convenience, and available parking may be crucial if you have a vehicle.

Choosing the right location helps ensure that your temporary home feels integrated into your daily life—not isolated or inconvenient.

Safety: A Non-Negotiable Priority

A great rental in a bad neighborhood isn’t a great deal. Before signing the lease, make sure the property is located in a safe and secure area.

Steps to evaluate safety:

  1. Research local crime ratesUse online tools like NeighborhoodScout, CityProtect, or local police websites to view recent crime statistics and safety reports.

  2. Visit the area at different timesIf possible, walk or drive through the neighborhood during the day, evening, and late night. Observe lighting, activity, and overall cleanliness.

  3. Ask locals or current tenantsNeighbors or existing tenants can provide insights into how safe and welcoming the area truly feels.

  4. Check building securityLook for secure entry systems, working locks, surveillance cameras, and well-lit entrances. In apartments or condos, inquire about on-site security staff or controlled access.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Boarded-up buildings or vandalism nearby

  • Poorly lit streets and stairwells

  • Lack of pedestrian activity or empty sidewalks

  • High turnover or vacancy rates in the area

  • Ongoing police activity or repeated news reports of crime

These indicators don’t always mean a neighborhood is unsafe, but they warrant closer examination.

Balancing Cost and Safety

Desirable locations often come with higher rent, but the added security and convenience can be well worth the investment. If you're on a budget, look for up-and-coming areas with improving infrastructure and safety measures—just make sure you're comfortable with the current state of the neighborhood.

Location and safety should never be afterthoughts when renting a furnished property. They influence everything from your daily routine to your peace of mind. By doing your research and visiting in person when possible, you’ll be better equipped to choose a rental that not only looks good but feels right, too.

6. Storage Space: Don't Overlook Practicality in a Furnished Rental

While furnished rentals offer convenience and a move-in-ready experience, they often prioritize aesthetics over functionality—especially when it comes to storage space. Sleek, minimalist designs may look great in photos, but if there’s nowhere to put your clothes, luggage, or day-to-day essentials, your rental can quickly become cluttered and uncomfortable.

Why Storage Space Matters

Even if you're only staying for a few months, having enough room to organize and store your belongings is essential to feeling at home. Without adequate storage, your living area can become messy, reducing comfort and efficiency. This is particularly important for:

  • Long-term stays: Accumulated belongings require more organization.

  • Remote workers: Files, tech gear, and office supplies need designated spots.

  • Families or roommates: Shared living requires more space management.

What to Look for in Terms of Storage

When touring a furnished rental or reviewing photos and floor plans, pay attention to the following:

Closets

  • Is there a dedicated closet in each bedroom?

  • Are they spacious or limited to a single wardrobe?

  • Are there built-in shelves or hanging space?

Dressers and Drawers

  • Are dressers or chests of drawers provided in bedrooms?

  • Are kitchen drawers and cabinets sufficient for utensils, cookware, and pantry items?

Kitchen and Bathroom Cabinets

  • Look under the sink, above counters, and in pantry areas for food and cleaning supply storage.

  • In bathrooms, check for medicine cabinets or linen closets.

Under-Bed and Hidden Storage

  • Beds with drawers or space for storage boxes underneath can make a big difference.

  • Ottomans, benches, or coffee tables with built-in storage can help reduce clutter.

Additional Features

  • Consider wall-mounted shelves, hooks, or entryway cubbies.

  • A rental with a garage, basement, or outdoor storage unit adds great value, especially for long stays.

Questions to Ask the Landlord or Property Manager

  • Is there additional storage outside the unit (e.g., in the building or garage)?

  • Can furniture be rearranged or removed to create more space?

  • Are tenants allowed to bring in their own storage solutions (e.g., shelves or wardrobes)?

Tips to Maximize Limited Storage

  • Use stackable storage bins or vacuum-sealed bags for off-season clothes or bedding.

  • Install over-the-door hooks or organizers for accessories and toiletries.

  • Opt for dual-purpose furniture like storage benches, ottomans, or fold-away desks.

Don’t let a beautifully furnished rental distract you from the basics. Storage space is key to maintaining order and comfort, especially if you're planning to stay for more than a few weeks. By ensuring there’s enough room for your belongings, you can enjoy both the look and functionality of your temporary home.

7. Internet and Cable Access: A Modern Necessity in Furnished Rentals

In today’s connected world, reliable internet is more than just a convenience—it’s a necessity, especially for digital nomads, remote workers, students, and content creators. When considering a furnished rental, internet and cable access should be among the top priorities on your checklist. Whether you’re attending virtual meetings, streaming content, or managing a business online, the right connectivity can make or break your rental experience.

Why Internet Matters

High-speed internet is no longer optional for many renters. With the rise of remote work and online lifestyles, having dependable Wi-Fi is crucial for:

  • Video conferencing and remote collaboration

  • Streaming entertainment, including movies, games, and music

  • Online education or digital learning tools

  • Cloud-based work and file sharing

  • Smart home devices that require connectivity

A furnished rental that includes high-speed internet removes the hassle of setting up service contracts and waiting for installation—perfect for short-term or transitional living.

What to Check Before You Sign

Before committing to a furnished rental, confirm these key points:

1. Is Internet Included?

Some rentals offer all-inclusive packages where Wi-Fi is part of the deal. Others may require you to set up and pay for your own service. Make sure the lease clearly states whether internet access is provided and what speed is guaranteed.

2. What’s the Speed and Bandwidth?

For video conferencing, streaming, and multi-device usage, you'll want download speeds of at least 25 Mbps, though 100 Mbps or higher is ideal for households or heavy users. Ask the landlord for speed test results or the name of the internet service provider (ISP).

3. Is There Reliable Coverage Throughout the Home?

Dead zones—areas where Wi-Fi drops or is slow—can be frustrating. Ask if the home has been tested for consistent signal strength in all rooms, especially in workspaces like offices or bedrooms.

4. Are There Limits on Usage?

In rare cases, shared rentals or corporate housing may have data caps. Make sure the internet plan allows for unlimited usage if you rely on it heavily.

What About Cable TV?

While many people now stream their favorite shows online, cable TV is still a valued amenity for some renters. If having live channels or access to sports, news, and local programming matters to you, check:

  • If cable service is included in the rental

  • Which provider is used and what channels are available

  • Whether you can upgrade or modify the plan at your own expense

For renters who don’t need cable, access to a smart TV or streaming device (e.g., Roku, Fire Stick, or Apple TV) can be a great alternative.

Tips for Renters

  • Do a speed test during your tour, if possible, using an app like Speedtest by Ookla.

  • Bring your own router or Wi-Fi extender if you're unsure about the quality of the connection.

  • Ask about tech support—if there’s an issue, will the landlord handle it or will you be responsible?

Fast, stable internet is now as essential as electricity or running water—especially if you're working remotely or spending a lot of time at home. Verifying internet and cable access before you move in ensures a seamless, stress-free living experience in your furnished rental.

8. Cleanliness and Pest Control: Ensuring a Healthy Start in Your Furnished Rental

One of the biggest advantages of choosing a furnished rental is the promise of a move-in ready space. However, that promise extends beyond stylish furniture and stocked cabinets—it should also include a clean, hygienic, and pest-free environment. Cleanliness and pest control aren’t just about appearances; they directly affect your health, comfort, and overall satisfaction during your stay.

Cleanliness: More Than Just Looks

A furnished rental should be thoroughly cleaned and sanitized before you move in. Since the furniture, appliances, and even utensils have been used by others, it’s essential to confirm that proper cleaning standards have been followed.

What to Look For:

  • Freshly cleaned floors, carpets, and upholstery

  • Dust-free surfaces, baseboards, and window sills

  • Clean bathrooms and kitchens, including inside cabinets and drawers

  • Sanitized appliances, especially refrigerators, microwaves, and washing machines

  • No lingering odors, mold, or visible dirt

During a walkthrough, take note of hidden or hard-to-reach areas, which often reveal whether the cleaning was surface-level or thorough.

Questions to Ask:

  • When was the property last professionally cleaned?

  • Is the cleaning done by a professional service between tenants?

  • Are linens and bedding freshly laundered and replaced regularly?

Pest Control: A Silent Dealbreaker

Even the most luxurious furnished rental can become unlivable if it has pest problems. Insects, rodents, or other unwanted visitors not only pose health risks but also indicate a lapse in property maintenance.

Common Signs of Pest Issues:

  • Droppings or insect remains in drawers or corners

  • Musty or foul smells in cabinets or closets

  • Chewed furniture, cords, or baseboards

  • Small holes or nests near windows, doors, or walls

What to Confirm Before Moving In:

  • Is there a pest control service in place?

  • How frequently are inspections and treatments done?

  • Are there recent reports or complaints from previous tenants?

A landlord who invests in regular pest control is likely to be proactive about other maintenance issues as well.

Your Rights as a Renter

In many areas, landlords are legally required to provide a safe and habitable environment, which includes cleanliness and pest control. If issues arise during your tenancy, report them immediately in writing and request prompt resolution. Documenting any problems with photos or videos is helpful for follow-up or in case of disputes.

Maintaining Cleanliness During Your Stay

While it’s the landlord’s responsibility to provide a clean home at move-in, keeping it that way is a shared effort. Simple steps include:

  • Regularly emptying trash and cleaning surfaces

  • Avoiding food spills and crumbs that attract pests

  • Notifying your landlord at the first sign of an infestation

A clean and pest-free furnished rental isn’t a luxury—it’s a basic expectation. Taking the time to ask the right questions and inspect the space before moving in ensures a safe, healthy, and pleasant stay from day one.

9. Style and Comfort: Finding a Furnished Rental That Feels Like Home

When choosing a furnished rental, it’s easy to focus on practical features like location, appliances, and lease terms. But one often-overlooked factor that can significantly affect your quality of life is style and comfort. Even if functionality tops your priority list, the design and atmosphere of your living space play a big role in how relaxed, productive, and happy you feel day to day.

Why Style and Comfort Matter

A furnished rental isn’t just a place to sleep—it becomes your personal space, whether you're staying for a few weeks or several months. The right style can uplift your mood, reduce stress, and make your stay more enjoyable. A poorly designed or visually cluttered space, on the other hand, can feel impersonal or even depressing.

Benefits of a well-styled and comfortable space:

  • Improved mental well-being through aesthetics and organization

  • Increased productivity, especially for remote workers or students

  • Easier transition to a new city or life phase by making the space feel like “home”

  • Positive guest experience when friends or family visit

What to Look for in Style and Design

When touring a furnished rental or reviewing listing photos, assess whether the design elements align with your personal preferences and lifestyle needs.

Lighting

  • Is there natural light through windows or skylights?

  • Are artificial light sources (lamps, ceiling lights) warm and well-placed?

Color Palette

  • Are the walls and furnishings painted or covered in calming, neutral, or coordinated tones?

  • Do the colors help create a peaceful and welcoming vibe?

Furniture Arrangement

  • Is the furniture arranged to allow for easy movement and conversation?

  • Does the layout suit your daily routine, such as dining, relaxing, or working from home?

Decor and Finishing Touches

  • Are there thoughtful touches like artwork, plants, or stylish textiles (rugs, pillows, curtains)?

  • Does the space reflect a cohesive design style (modern, rustic, minimalist, etc.)?

Comfort Isn’t Just About Looks

Style is visual, but comfort is physical. Make sure the furniture provided isn’t just attractive but also functional and cozy.

Comfort Essentials:

  • Supportive sofa and chairs for lounging or watching TV

  • A good-quality mattress that suits your sleep needs

  • Adequate dining and work surfaces that are the right height and size

  • Quiet ambiance with soundproofing or location away from noise

If possible, test furniture during a visit. Sit on the couch, lie on the bed, and imagine your daily activities in the space.

Personalization Options

Some landlords may allow you to bring in small decor items or rearrange furniture to make the space your own. Adding a throw blanket, some framed photos, or a favorite pillow can go a long way in making a temporary rental feel more like home.

A furnished rental that’s both stylish and comfortable creates a positive environment where you can relax, focus, and feel truly at ease. While it may not be your forever home, choosing a space that reflects your taste and supports your lifestyle can turn any stay into an enjoyable experience.

10. Insurance and Liability: Protecting Yourself in a Furnished Rental

When renting a furnished property, it’s easy to get caught up in the aesthetics and convenience—but don’t overlook the importance of insurance and liability. Understanding what’s covered—and what’s not—can help protect you from unexpected costs and legal issues. Whether you’re staying for a few months or a year, knowing your responsibilities and having the right protection in place is essential.

What the Landlord’s Insurance Covers

Most landlords carry property insurance, but it typically covers only the structure of the building and the landlord’s own belongings, such as the furniture and appliances included in the rental.

However, this insurance does not cover:

  • Your personal belongings (clothing, electronics, jewelry, etc.)

  • Accidental damage you may cause to the property or furnishings

  • Liability for accidents (like a guest getting injured inside your rental)

That means if a fire, theft, or water leak damages your laptop or suitcase, the landlord’s insurance won’t reimburse you. Similarly, if you accidentally spill wine on the couch or break a lamp, you may be held financially responsible.

Why You Should Consider Renters Insurance

Renters insurance is a smart and affordable way to protect yourself in a furnished rental. It typically includes:

  • Personal property coverage: Protects your belongings from fire, theft, vandalism, and certain natural disasters.

  • Liability coverage: Covers legal and medical costs if someone is injured in your rental unit or if you accidentally damage someone else's property.

  • Loss of use (or additional living expenses): Pays for hotel stays or temporary housing if the rental becomes uninhabitable due to a covered event.

In many cases, renters insurance costs less than $20 per month—and it could save you thousands in the event of a loss.

Check Your Lease and Ask Questions

Before signing, carefully review the lease agreement for clauses related to insurance and liability:

  • Are you required to carry renters insurance?

  • What happens if you damage the furniture or appliances?

  • Are there penalties for accidental damage, or is normal wear and tear excluded?

If anything is unclear, ask the landlord or property manager for clarification.

Additional Tips for Renters

  • Document the property’s condition at move-in with photos or videos, especially of furniture and appliances.

  • Keep receipts or an inventory of your personal items for easier insurance claims.

  • Compare renters insurance providers to find a policy that suits your needs and budget.

Furnished rentals offer convenience, but also come with added responsibility—especially when it comes to protecting both the property and yourself. By understanding the limits of the landlord’s insurance and investing in renters insurance, you’ll enjoy greater peace of mind and be better prepared for the unexpected.

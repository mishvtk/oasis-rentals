Boca Raton Advances Police Bond and Approves Downtown Club

Boca Raton approved The Sanborn, an eight-story private club beside Sanborn Square, and moved a proposed police-facilities bond of up to $125 million closer to the March 2027 ballot. The development approval for the club is in place, though permitting and construction remain ahead. Voters have not authorized the bond, and the city says it has not approved a final police site, design, or funding plan.

Renters have some practical questions to consider: how The Sanborn will manage cars on a compact downtown block, whether construction will disrupt nearby streets, and how a future public-safety bond could affect services and housing costs. The decisions made in October 2026 leave several details unsettled, especially for anyone preparing to sign or renew a lease.

What Boca Raton approved and what remains proposed

The Sanborn received development approval

On October 13, 2026, Boca Raton's Community Redevelopment Agency voted 5-0 to approve The Sanborn at Northeast First Avenue and East Boca Raton Road, directly across from Sanborn Square. According to the Boca Post account of the hearing, the approval covers an approximately 54,681-square-foot, eight-story private membership club on a 0.30-acre site.

The vote grants an individual development approval. The club is not open, and construction does not begin automatically. An approved plan must still go through demolition, construction planning, permits, inspections, financing, and the issuance of a certificate of occupancy before the club can operate.

The police bond is still a proposal

The City Council also moved the revised police-facilities proposal forward for possible consideration by voters in March 2027. The current concept calls for general obligation bonds of up to $125 million, financed for no more than 30 years. The city's Police Services Complex project page says voters have not approved a bond and the city has not determined the final location, design, or total project cost.

Council's direction allows planning, public outreach, financing analysis, and preparation of a possible ballot question to continue. It does not authorize the city to borrow the entire amount today.

What The Sanborn could change downtown

A destination beside Sanborn Square

The approved plans put a members-only club on a small corner parcel between established and planned downtown buildings. Reported uses include a spa, gym, game room, multipurpose space, restaurants, bars, a library, a card room, and an open-air roof deck. The existing two-story building is expected to be demolished.

The change for nearby renters goes beyond the addition of another tall building. The club's busiest periods may overlap with dinner, nightlife, weekend events, and activity at Sanborn Square. If the club is built, residents near East Boca Raton Road, Northeast First Avenue, Federal Highway, and Mizner Park should expect different arrival and departure patterns on the block.

Parking depends partly on another project

The approved shared-parking analysis calls for 183 spaces. The Sanborn's two-level automated underground garage would hold 85 cars. As many as 98 more spaces would come from the Aletto garage approximately 60 feet away across East Boca Raton Road, according to the reported CRA presentation.

The estimated need for off-site parking changes by period: no spaces during weekday daytime, 81 on weekday evenings, 13 during weekend days, and 98 on weekend nights. The parking agreement is a condition of approval. Project representatives also said The Sanborn could not receive a certificate of occupancy without approved parking if the Aletto garage were unavailable.

Renter takeaway: A noon visit on a Tuesday will not tell you much about downtown parking at its busiest. Visit a prospective building on a Friday evening and during a Sanborn Square event. Ask how the building separates resident, guest, delivery, and rideshare traffic.

Street and sidewalk conditions may change

The approved work includes seven live oaks and would widen the Northeast First Avenue sidewalk by roughly three feet, bringing it to eight feet, according to the CRA coverage. The wider sidewalk could eventually make the frontage more comfortable for pedestrians. During demolition and construction, renters may encounter trucks, noise, dust, lane interruptions, and changed pedestrian routes.

The material reviewed for this article did not include a construction schedule. Renters should consider the timing an open question instead of assuming disruption will begin immediately after approval.

Parking, traffic, and neighborhood activity to monitor

Watch the curb, alley, and valet operation

The automated garage received particular attention because cars will enter a constrained downtown site. The applicant told the CRA that retrieval should take two to three minutes and that the system would have a backup generator. The project also includes two car elevators, although only one is required. Renters will be able to compare those design claims with actual operations after the club opens.

If you live nearby, watch for vehicle queues along Northeast First Avenue, East Boca Raton Road, or the alley. Pay attention to whether valet staging, food deliveries, rideshare pickups, and service vehicles compete for the same curb space.

Operating restrictions are part of the parking plan

Certain club areas must close during specified periods to keep simultaneous demand within the shared-parking analysis. Reported conditions include closing the spa from midnight to 6 a.m. on weekdays and limiting the game room and main restaurant during weekday mornings and afternoons. According to Boca Daily News, the project must also provide transit passes to on-site employees and contribute $9,165 annually to downtown transit programs.

These conditions provide more detail than a general promise that the parking plan will work. Nearby renters can ask the CRA how it will monitor compliance and where they should direct complaints about queuing, noise, or operating hours.

Neighborhood activity may vary by block

A new dining and social destination can bring foot traffic and support nearby businesses. It may also generate evening activity that neighbors can hear or see from their homes. The effect will vary from one block and unit to another. A unit facing a courtyard may experience the project differently from a unit facing the club, its roof deck, or a valet route.

Follow the Boca Raton renter updates as construction and operating details become clearer. Before making a leasing decision, check the conditions in person as well.

How the proposed police bond would work

Voters would authorize the debt

A general obligation bond allows a city to borrow for a major capital project and repay principal and interest through property-tax revenue. Boca Raton is considering authorization of up to $125 million for a term that would not exceed 30 years. A majority of participating city voters would have to approve the referendum before the city could issue the proposed debt.

The current proposal is $50 million below the $175 million measure rejected in March 2026. According to the city's project comparison, the city also reduced the working facility concepts from approximately 165,000 square feet to roughly 90,000 to 100,000 square feet.

Two location concepts remain under evaluation

The city is studying a two-story central headquarters on city-owned property near Spanish River Boulevard and Broken Sound Boulevard, along with a downtown police station. The alternative is a six-story headquarters beside the existing downtown location on a 1.94-acre site.

The Police Department and city staff recommend the central-headquarters-plus-downtown-station concept, though both concepts remain under public evaluation. The city says officers would continue working throughout its 15 patrol zones under either option. The headquarters address therefore would not determine the location of day-to-day patrol coverage.

Why the city says new facilities are needed

The current headquarters opened in 1986 at 100 Northwest Boca Raton Boulevard. The city reports that Boca Raton's population increased from 58,821 in 1986 to 103,062, while police personnel increased from 145 to 331. The city also says the existing headquarters is not hurricane-rated and must be evacuated during major storms.

Those facts explain the city's proposal, but the appropriate design, location, borrowing amount, and tax impact remain open questions. Renters and homeowners should examine those details before the referendum.

Why renters should follow the financing

Housing carries property costs

The city estimates that a property with a taxable value of $475,000 would pay approximately $93 per year, or about $7.75 per month, under the current bond assumptions. The actual cost would depend on the taxable value, interest rates, and amount ultimately issued.

The property owner, rather than the tenant, would receive that property-tax assessment. Over time, landlords consider taxes along with insurance, association charges, maintenance, repairs, and market conditions when setting renewal terms. The bond could create pass-through pressure, though it would not establish a dollar-for-dollar rent increase. No one can responsibly predict a specific rent change from the bond alone.

Service improvements are part of the equation

The city says both concepts would provide a hurricane-rated headquarters and consolidate command, communications, investigations, evidence, records, and specialized operations. Renters would share in any improvement to continuity during a major storm or coordination during an emergency, just as property owners would.

Approval of the borrowing would not guarantee a particular operational result. Voters should look for a defined project scope, independent cost estimates, debt assumptions, construction contingencies, and a clear account of what would happen if bids exceeded the available proceeds.

Compare the total housing cost

When comparing Boca Raton homes, account for base rent, required parking, association fees charged to tenants, utilities, renter's insurance, commuting, and recurring service fees. You can compare current rental options and review the layout of a three-bedroom Boca Raton home as a reference, even when a particular home is not currently available.

Downtown renters should also account for paid guest parking and the practical cost of keeping a second vehicle. A building with dependable assigned parking may cost slightly more in rent yet remain less expensive overall than a lower-priced unit accompanied by regular garage charges and uncertain parking.

A checklist for downtown renters

Before signing a lease

  1. Visit at peak hours. Walk the block on a weekday evening and a weekend night. Note valet activity, restaurant traffic, street noise, and access to your building.
  2. Get the exact parking arrangement. Confirm the assigned space, guest rules, towing policy, garage hours, electric-vehicle restrictions, and procedures during nearby events.
  3. Check the unit's orientation. Identify windows and balconies facing Northeast First Avenue, East Boca Raton Road, Sanborn Square, alleys, loading areas, or future construction.
  4. Ask about planned work. Request written disclosure of known nearby construction, façade projects, road work, and building renovations.
  5. Read the noise language. Review quiet hours, complaint procedures, balcony rules, and any limits on lease remedies during outside construction.
  6. Compare the full monthly cost. Include parking, utilities, insurance, association application charges, and commute costs.

During construction and after opening

  • Keep dated photos and a written log of recurring access, dust, noise, or parking problems.
  • Report problems specific to your building through management's traceable portal or email.
  • For public streets, approvals, and operating conditions, contact the city or CRA and reference The Sanborn approval, CRP-25-01.
  • Stay out of construction areas and avoid confronting valet staff. Document the location, time, and effect, then use the appropriate reporting channel.
  • Visit the block again before renewal. Conditions may differ substantially from those in place when you signed the original lease.

Dates and official channels to follow

For The Sanborn

Check Boca Raton CRA agendas, development-review records, permit activity, and public notices for construction milestones or amendments to the approved plan. The final site plan, parking conditions, construction-management plan, and any later request to change operating restrictions will provide the most useful details.

For the police proposal

Check the city's Police Services Complex page and City Council agendas for the final site recommendation, cost estimate, financing assumptions, referendum resolution, and exact ballot wording. Before March 2027, confirm the election date and eligibility through official city and Palm Beach County election channels instead of relying on an older news story.

FAQ

Has The Sanborn private club been approved?

Yes. Boca Raton's Community Redevelopment Agency approved its individual development application by a 5-0 vote on October 13, 2026. Permitting, demolition, construction, inspections, and a certificate of occupancy still remain.

Did Boca Raton approve the $125 million police bond?

No. Voters have not authorized a bond. The city is evaluating and advancing a proposal for up to $125 million in general obligation bonds, with a possible referendum targeted for March 2027.

Where would the new police headquarters be built?

The city has not approved a final site. It is evaluating a central headquarters near Spanish River and Broken Sound boulevards with a downtown station, along with a separate six-story downtown-headquarters concept.

Will the police bond raise a renter's monthly rent?

There is no automatic or predictable rent increase. Property owners would pay any associated property tax, and some may consider that cost at renewal alongside insurance, maintenance, association charges, and market conditions.

What parking should downtown renters watch near The Sanborn?

The approved plan uses 85 underground spaces and as many as 98 off-site spaces in the Aletto garage. Renters should watch evening valet queues, rideshare pickups, alley access, guest parking availability, and compliance with operating-hour restrictions.

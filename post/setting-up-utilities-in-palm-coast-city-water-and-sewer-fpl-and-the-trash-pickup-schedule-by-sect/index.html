Setting up utilities in Palm Coast: city water and sewer, FPL, and the trash pickup schedule by section


Moving into a rental property in Palm Coast means taking care of several important utilities before move-in day. For properties located within the City of Palm Coast, one of the first steps renters should take is arranging municipal utility service.

The City of Palm Coast provides utility services that include water, wastewater (sewer), and residential solid-waste services. The City’s customer-service system allows residents to establish new utility service, transfer service, or stop service when moving.



Start With City of Palm Coast Water, Sewer, and Trash Service

For a rental property within Palm Coast city limits, renters should first determine how utilities are handled under their lease. Some rental agreements may require the tenant to place certain utilities in their own name, while others may include particular services in the rent.

The City of Palm Coast provides a New Service Application through Palm Coast Connect. Residents must create an account or sign in before submitting a new-service request. The online system can also be used to manage other utility-related requests.

What Renters Should Have Ready

Before applying for service, renters should have the following information available:

  • The complete rental property address

  • The date they want utility service to begin

  • Identification and account information, when requested

  • Their lease or other documentation if required

  • A payment method for utility charges or any applicable deposits

Having this information ready can make the setup process easier and help avoid delays.

Water and Sewer Service

City utility service includes both water and wastewater. Palm Coast maintains a municipal water-distribution system and wastewater collection system serving properties throughout the city.

Renters should confirm that water and sewer service will be active when they take possession of the property. It is especially important to clarify responsibility for the utility account with the landlord or property manager before moving in.

Tenants should also review their lease to determine whether they are responsible for all utility charges or only certain services.

Palm Coast's utility billing structure includes charges for water and wastewater, and current utility rates are published by the City.

Residential Trash and Recycling

Trash service is another important part of setting up a Palm Coast rental.

The City's residential solid-waste collection program includes services such as garbage, recycling, yard waste, bulk trash, and white-goods removal. Residential collection services are provided under the City's contract with FCC Environmental.

Renters should check their property's scheduled collection days and learn the City's requirements for placing garbage, recycling, and yard waste at the curb.

Palm Coast Connect also provides tools for checking trash-day information and reporting issues such as missed garbage or recycling pickups.

Set Up Service Before Move-In Day

One of the easiest ways to avoid problems on moving day is to arrange utility service before the lease begins.

Ideally, renters should submit their utility request early enough to allow time for the City to process the account. This helps ensure that essential services such as water are available when the tenant takes possession of the property.

Before moving in, renters should also ask the landlord or property manager:

  1. Which utilities are included in the rent?

  2. Which utilities must be placed in the tenant's name?

  3. Is there an existing utility account for the property?

  4. Is a deposit required?

  5. When should the tenant's responsibility for utilities begin?

Getting these details in writing can help prevent confusion about bills and responsibility after move-in.

Palm Coast Utility Customer Service

If you have questions about establishing or transferring utility service, the City of Palm Coast lists its Customer Service number as:

Palm Coast Customer Service:(386) 986-2360

The City lists customer-service hours as Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

Renters can also use Palm Coast Connect to access new utility service and other utility-related services.

Final Checklist for Palm Coast Renters

Before moving into a Palm Coast rental, make sure you:

☐ Review the lease for utility responsibilities

☐ Confirm the property is within the City of Palm Coast service area

☐ Gather your property address and move-in date

☐ Create or sign into your Palm Coast Connect account

☐ Apply for water and sewer service if required

☐ Confirm trash and recycling collection information

☐ Ask about deposits or other account requirements

☐ Keep confirmation of your utility-service request

☐ Save the City's Customer Service number

Taking care of utilities before move-in can make the transition into a Palm Coast rental much smoother and help ensure that water, sewer, and trash services are properly arranged from the beginning.


Understanding Palm Coast Water and Sewer: What Renters Should Know

Water and sewer costs are an important part of a renter's monthly housing budget. In Palm Coast, understanding how the City's water and wastewater systems work, how sewer charges are calculated, and how irrigation can affect the bill can help renters avoid unexpected utility expenses.

Palm Coast's Water and Wastewater Systems

The City of Palm Coast operates central water and wastewater systems serving properties throughout the community. The City's Water Distribution Division maintains water infrastructure, including water lines, fire hydrants, isolation valves, water meters, and backflow-prevention devices. The City also has a dedicated wastewater collection system that transports wastewater from homes to treatment facilities.

For renters, the first step is to confirm how the particular property receives and pays for water and sewer service. Before signing a lease, ask the landlord or property manager whether the home is connected to the City's water and wastewater system and whether any additional services, such as irrigation, are separately metered or billed.

Understanding Your Water and Sewer Bill

Palm Coast utility bills can include more than basic water consumption. The City's residential utility information explains that a typical utility bill may include charges for water, wastewater, garbage, and stormwater, with irrigation charges applicable where appropriate.

This means renters should not assume that their water usage alone represents the entire monthly utility cost.

When reviewing a rental property's previous bills, it can be useful to ask the landlord or property manager for an estimate of typical monthly utility expenses. This can provide a more realistic picture of the property's total monthly housing cost.

Watch Your Sewer Charges

One important change for Palm Coast renters took effect on April 1, 2025.

The City previously capped sewer charges at 8,000 gallons of water usage. That cap was removed beginning April 1, 2025. According to the City, water passing through the property's main water meter is now subject to sewer charges.

This can be particularly important for homes with substantial lawn irrigation.

If irrigation water runs through the property's main water meter, that water can also result in sewer charges. The City recommends a separate irrigation meter as a way to avoid sewer charges on water used specifically for irrigation.

For renters, this is worth discussing with the property owner before signing a lease, especially if the home has a large lawn or extensive landscaping.

Ask About Irrigation Before Renting

A rental house may have irrigation that operates separately from normal household water use. The way the irrigation system is metered can affect the property's utility costs.

Before signing a lease, renters should ask:

  • Is irrigation connected to the main water meter?

  • Is there a separate irrigation meter?

  • Who pays for irrigation water?

  • Are irrigation charges included in the monthly utility bill?

  • What were the property's typical water and sewer costs during the previous year?

These questions can be especially useful for single-family homes where outdoor watering may represent a significant portion of total water consumption.

Questions to Ask Before Signing a Lease

Understanding who is responsible for utilities can make it easier to calculate the true cost of renting a home in Palm Coast.

Before signing a rental agreement, consider asking the landlord or property manager:

Is Water Included in the Rent?

Some rental agreements may include certain utilities, while others require the tenant to establish and pay for the utility account directly. Review the lease carefully so you know exactly which expenses are your responsibility.

Is Sewer Included?

Water and wastewater charges may appear together on the City's utility bill. Confirm whether the landlord pays these charges or whether they are transferred to the tenant.

Is Irrigation Separately Metered?

This can be particularly important for properties with extensive landscaping. A separate irrigation meter can prevent irrigation water from being subject to sewer charges, according to the City.

Who Pays the Monthly Utility Bill?

Do not assume that the person responsible for the water bill is automatically responsible for every utility charge. Confirm the arrangement in the lease.

Is There a Utility Deposit?

The City provides information about utility deposits and explains that deposits are held on file for 23 months. Customers with a qualifying payment history may request that the deposit be returned or applied to their utility bill.

Renters should confirm with the City whether a deposit applies to their specific account and circumstances.

What Was the Property's Typical Monthly Utility Cost?

Ask for recent utility bills or a reasonable estimate of typical costs. Keep in mind that usage can vary based on household size, weather, irrigation, landscaping, and other factors.

Why Utility Questions Matter for Your Rental Budget

Rent is only one part of the cost of renting a home.

For a Palm Coast rental, your monthly housing budget may also need to account for:

  • Water

  • Wastewater/sewer

  • Garbage

  • Stormwater charges

  • Irrigation, if applicable

  • Electricity

  • Internet and other services

Understanding these costs before signing a lease can help renters compare properties more accurately.

A home with a slightly higher monthly rent could potentially have different utility expenses than another property with a lower rent. Looking at the total monthly housing cost, rather than rent alone, can therefore provide a better basis for comparing rental options.

Palm Coast renters should pay particular attention to how water, sewer, and irrigation are billed. The City's April 2025 change removing the former 8,000-gallon sewer-use cap makes understanding the property's meter configuration even more important.

Before signing a lease, confirm who pays for water and sewer, whether irrigation is separately metered, whether a utility deposit applies, and what the property's typical utility costs have been.

A few questions before move-in can make monthly expenses much more predictable and help you build a more accurate Palm Coast rental budget.


Setting Up Electricity With FPL: A Guide for Palm Coast Renters

Electricity is a separate utility from the City of Palm Coast's water and wastewater services. For Palm Coast rental properties served by Florida Power & Light Company (FPL), renters generally need to establish an electric account when they become responsible for the property's electricity.

Setting up electric service before moving in can help ensure that the home has power when you receive the keys and can make the transition into your new rental much easier.

Setting Up FPL Electricity Service

FPL provides online options for customers to start, stop, or move electric service. When starting residential service, FPL says customers will need information such as the service address, requested service-start date, and identification information. Customers also create an FPL.com User ID during the process and need a valid email address for the account login.

For Palm Coast renters, the service address is particularly important. Make sure the address entered on the FPL application exactly matches the rental property's address shown on the lease.

When Should You Start Electricity Service?

Renters should generally schedule the electricity start date to coincide with their move-in date, unless the landlord or property manager has made another arrangement.

Before submitting the request, confirm with the landlord:

  • When does the landlord's responsibility for electricity end?

  • When does the tenant's responsibility begin?

  • Will electricity remain on between tenants?

  • Does the property manager require the tenant to establish service before receiving the keys?

The timing can vary from one rental property to another, so it is better to confirm the arrangement rather than assume that power will automatically be available.

FPL allows customers to select a preferred future service-start date. FPL also states that service can often be connected the same day when an order is received by noon Monday through Saturday, although circumstances at the property can require additional time.

Before Starting FPL Service

Having the necessary information ready can make the account setup process easier.

Renters should have:

  • Rental property address

  • Requested service-start date

  • Identification information

  • Valid email address

  • Payment information

  • Previous FPL account information, if transferring or referencing an existing account

FPL says identification may include information such as a Social Security number or driver's license number. If a Social Security number is not available, FPL indicates that another form of identification, such as a driver's license or passport number, may be used.

Moving From Another FPL Property?

If you are already an FPL customer and are moving to another property within FPL's service area, FPL provides an online move or transfer service.

FPL says customers transferring service will need information including their current account credentials, the date service should stop at the old address, the date service should begin at the new address, and a mailing address for bills.

This can be more convenient than closing an account and starting from scratch when moving between two FPL-served properties.

However, renters should still verify that the new property is within FPL's service territory. If the new property is served by another electric provider, the renter will need to arrange service with that provider instead.

What About Electricity Between Tenants?

Some rental properties may have arrangements that keep electricity active between tenants.

For example, FPL offers a property-manager program under which qualifying rental properties can have electric service automatically connected in the property manager's name when a tenant disconnects service. This is an arrangement between the property manager and FPL and does not mean every rental property automatically keeps electricity active between tenants.

For this reason, renters should ask the landlord or property manager exactly how electricity is handled before moving in.

Check the Lease Before Setting Up Service

The lease should clearly explain which utilities the tenant is responsible for paying.

Before signing, look for information about:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer or wastewater

  • Trash service

  • Irrigation

  • Internet or other optional services

If electricity is not included in the rent, ask whether you are expected to establish an FPL account in your own name.

It is also useful to ask whether the property has historically had unusually high electricity costs. Florida's hot climate can make air-conditioning a significant part of a household's electricity use, so reviewing previous bills, when available, can help renters build a more realistic monthly budget.

Plan Electricity Alongside Your Other Utilities

Electricity is only one part of the utility budget for a Palm Coast rental.

Depending on the property and lease, renters may also be responsible for:

  • City water

  • Wastewater/sewer

  • Trash and recycling

  • Stormwater-related charges

  • Irrigation

  • Internet

  • Other property-specific services

Setting up each service before move-in can help prevent surprises and make it easier to estimate the property's total monthly housing cost.

FPL Electricity Setup Checklist

Before moving into your Palm Coast rental, make sure you:

☐ Confirm who is responsible for electricity

☐ Check whether the property is served by FPL

☐ Have the complete service address

☐ Choose the electricity service-start date

☐ Have identification information available

☐ Have a valid email address

☐ Have payment information ready

☐ Provide previous FPL account information if transferring service

Setting up electricity should be one of the first tasks on a Palm Coast renter's move-in checklist. FPL provides online options to start, stop, or move residential electric service, making it possible to arrange service before moving day.

The most important step is to coordinate the service-start date with the landlord or property manager. Confirm who is responsible for the electricity, whether power will remain active before move-in, and whether you need to establish your own FPL account.

Taking care of electricity in advance can help ensure that your new Palm Coast rental is ready when you move in and can also help you create a more accurate monthly utility budget.


Palm Coast Trash Pickup: How the Schedule Works

Trash collection is an important part of setting up a new rental home in Palm Coast. Unlike some communities where every neighborhood follows the same collection day, Palm Coast uses property-specific collection schedules based on the street and service route.

Residential waste collection is provided through the City's contracted waste hauler, FCC Environmental Services. Services include household garbage, recycling, yard waste, bulk waste, and white-goods collection. (City of Palm Coast)

For renters moving into Palm Coast, knowing the property's exact collection schedule can help prevent missed pickups and avoid putting waste at the curb on the wrong day.

Palm Coast Does Not Have One Universal Trash Day

One of the most important things new renters should know is that there is not one trash-collection day for the entire City of Palm Coast.

Collection days vary depending on the property's street and assigned service route.

This means that even two homes located relatively close to one another can have different garbage collection schedules.

Instead of relying on information from a neighbor or assuming that a particular neighborhood has a specific trash day, renters should use the City's official address-based schedule lookup.

How to Find Your Palm Coast Trash Schedule

The City of Palm Coast provides an online tool that allows residents to look up their collection schedule.

When searching for a property, the City instructs residents to enter the street name without the house number. (City of Palm Coast)

For example, if the property is located on "Eric Trail," the search may require entering Eric rather than the full street address.

If the street does not appear in the search results, the City recommends trying the street name without the street suffix.

Using the official lookup is the most reliable way for a new renter to determine when garbage, recycling, and other materials should be placed at the curb.

Trash Pickup by Section or Route

Palm Coast's collection system is organized around individual service routes rather than a single citywide schedule.

For a typical residential property, the collection pattern includes regular household garbage twice per week. According to the City's collection information:

  • First collection day: Regular household garbage

  • Second collection day: Regular household garbage plus eligible bulk waste

  • Recycling: Collected once per week

  • Yard waste: Collected on Wednesdays throughout the City

Because collection schedules are route-specific, the exact garbage and recycling days depend on the property's location. (City of Palm Coast)

Example of How the Schedule Can Differ

A renter living in one section of Palm Coast might have garbage collection on Monday and Thursday, while another property nearby could have a different pair of collection days.

The important point is that renters should check the schedule for their specific street rather than relying on a general neighborhood schedule.

Recycling Collection

Recycling is collected once per week for residential properties.

New renters should check the City's schedule to determine their property's recycling day and review the City's recycling guidelines before placing materials at the curb.

Not every item that appears recyclable can necessarily be placed in the residential recycling container. Following the City's accepted-material guidelines can help reduce contamination and collection problems. (City of Palm Coast)

Yard Waste Collection

Yard waste is collected on Wednesdays throughout Palm Coast, according to the City's residential collection information. (City of Palm Coast)

This service can be particularly useful for single-family rental homes with lawns, landscaping, trees, or other vegetation.

Renters should still follow the City's requirements for preparing and placing yard waste for collection.

Bulk Trash and White Goods

Palm Coast's residential waste program also provides collection for eligible bulk waste and white goods.

Bulk items can include larger household items that do not fit into a regular garbage container. White goods generally include large household appliances.

Before placing a large item at the curb, renters should check the City's current requirements because collection rules, preparation requirements, and eligible items can vary by category. (City of Palm Coast)

Renters should also confirm whether the landlord or property manager has specific rules regarding removal of large furniture or appliances.

When Should You Put Trash at the Curb?

Knowing the collection day is only part of the process. Renters should also follow the City's requirements for when containers and other waste materials may be placed at the curb.

The City's residential collection information provides guidelines for curbside placement and collection. Checking these requirements before moving in can help prevent missed service or problems with improperly placed materials. (City of Palm Coast)

If you live in a homeowners association or planned community, there may also be additional community rules regarding when containers can be placed at the curb and when they should be removed.

What New Palm Coast Renters Should Do

Before moving into a rental, add these tasks to your move-in checklist:

  1. Look up the property's official collection schedule.

  2. Enter the street name without the house number when using the City's lookup tool.

  3. Confirm the property's regular garbage days.

  4. Check the weekly recycling day.

  5. Remember that yard waste is collected on Wednesdays.

  6. Review the rules for bulk waste and white goods.

  7. Check with the HOA or property manager for additional community requirements.

  8. Save the collection schedule on your phone for easy reference.

Palm Coast's trash collection system is organized by street and service route, so renters should not assume that every neighborhood or section has the same collection days.

A typical residential property receives garbage collection twice per week, recycling once per week, and yard-waste collection on Wednesdays. Bulk and white-goods collection are also available under the City's residential waste program.

The safest approach for a new renter is simple: look up the property's exact schedule using the City of Palm Coast's official collection-schedule tool before putting anything at the curb.

Taking a few minutes to check the schedule can help prevent missed pickups and make settling into your new Palm Coast rental much easier.


When Should You Put Trash Out in Palm Coast?

Knowing when to put trash out is an important part of living in a Palm Coast rental. The City's residential collection system operates according to scheduled collection days, but residents should not expect their garbage to be picked up at a specific time of day.

For renters, understanding the timing rules can help prevent missed collections and avoid conflicts with HOA or community requirements.

Have Your Trash Ready Before 6:00 a.m.

Palm Coast residents should have their garbage, recycling, and other eligible curbside materials ready for collection before 6:00 a.m. on the scheduled collection day.

Collection routes can change throughout the day, so waiting until later in the morning to put containers out could result in a missed pickup.

The City recommends having materials properly prepared and accessible before collection begins. (City of Palm Coast)

You Can Put Containers Out the Evening Before

Residents can generally place their containers and eligible materials at the curb as early as 6:00 p.m. on the evening before collection.

This can be particularly convenient for renters who leave home early for work or school. Putting the container out the night before means you do not have to worry about remembering to do it before 6:00 a.m.

However, renters should check whether their HOA, condominium association, or property manager has stricter rules about when containers can be placed at the curb.

Collection Can Take Place Throughout the Day

Trash collection does not necessarily occur at the same time every week.

Collection vehicles may arrive at different times depending on the route, traffic, weather, operational conditions, and the number of properties being serviced.

Residents should therefore plan for collection to take place during the City's designated collection window rather than expecting a specific pickup time.

Having waste at the curb before the beginning of the collection period is the safest approach.

Why Timing Matters for Renters

Timing can be especially important for renters living in:

  • HOA communities

  • Planned residential developments

  • Townhome communities

  • Condominiums

  • Properties with limited curb space

  • Neighborhoods with specific container-placement rules

Some communities may require residents to remove garbage containers from the curb relatively soon after collection. Others may restrict how long containers can remain outside.

For this reason, renters should review both the City's collection requirements and the property's community rules.

What to Do Before Moving In

Before moving into a Palm Coast rental, add these questions to your move-in checklist:

  1. What are the property's garbage collection days?

  2. What day is recycling collected?

  3. When should containers be placed at the curb?

  4. Are there HOA or property-management rules about container placement?

  5. When should containers be removed after collection?

  6. Where should containers be placed if the property has limited curb space?

Knowing the answers ahead of time can make your first few weeks in the rental much easier.

A Simple Palm Coast Trash Routine

Once you know your property's schedule, create a simple routine:

The evening before:Place your garbage or recycling container out after the permitted time.

Before 6:00 a.m. on collection day:Make sure everything is properly prepared and accessible for collection.

During the collection day:Keep in mind that the truck may arrive at different times depending on the route.

After collection:Follow your HOA or community rules regarding when containers should be brought back from the curb.

Palm Coast renters should focus on the scheduled collection day, not a specific pickup time.

The safest approach is to have eligible waste and containers ready before 6:00 a.m. on collection day and, where permitted, place them at the curb beginning at 6:00 p.m. the evening before.

If you live in an HOA or another managed community, always check the property's additional rules. Community requirements may affect where and when containers can be placed outside.

A few minutes of planning can help prevent missed collections and keep your rental property in compliance with both City and community requirements.


Recycling, Yard Waste, and Bulk Items in Palm Coast

Palm Coast renters receive more than regular household garbage collection. The City's residential waste program also includes recycling, yard waste, bulk waste, and white-goods collection.

Understanding the rules for each type of material is important because different items may have different preparation requirements, collection days, size limits, and fees.

Before putting anything at the curb, renters should check the City's current requirements and, if applicable, their HOA or property-management rules.

Recycling in Palm Coast

Palm Coast provides weekly residential recycling collection through a single-stream recycling program.

Single-stream recycling means residents generally place accepted recyclable materials together in the designated recycling container rather than separating different recyclable materials into separate containers.

However, not everything that appears recyclable belongs in the recycling bin.

Residents should follow the City's current list of accepted materials and keep the recycling container free of contamination. Items that are not accepted or materials contaminated with food or other waste can interfere with the recycling process.

For renters, a good practice is to review the City's recycling guidelines when moving into a new property rather than relying on recycling habits from another community.

Recycling Tips for Renters

Before putting out recycling:

  • Check the City's current list of accepted materials.

  • Keep prohibited items out of the recycling container.

  • Follow the City's container and placement requirements.

  • Avoid putting ordinary household garbage in the recycling container.

  • Check your property's weekly recycling collection day.

The City's recycling information provides the most current guidance for Palm Coast residents. (City of Palm Coast)

Yard Waste Collection

Yard waste is another regular component of Palm Coast's residential collection program.

According to the City's collection information, yard waste is collected on Wednesdays throughout Palm Coast. (City of Palm Coast)

This can be especially useful for renters living in single-family homes with lawns, trees, landscaping, or other vegetation.

However, yard waste must meet the City's collection requirements. The City places limits on the size and amount of yard waste that can be collected through the regular residential service.

Oversized piles, improperly prepared materials, or material that exceeds the City's requirements may not qualify for normal collection and could potentially result in additional charges.

Before Putting Out Yard Waste

Renters should:

  • Check the City's current yard-waste preparation requirements.

  • Keep yard waste separate from household garbage and recycling.

  • Avoid creating oversized piles.

  • Make sure individual pieces meet the City's size requirements.

  • Place materials in the appropriate collection area.

If you hire a landscaping company, confirm whether the company removes the yard waste or whether it will be collected through the City's residential service.

Bulk Trash Collection

Palm Coast's residential waste program also provides collection for eligible bulk items.

Bulk waste can include larger household items such as:

  • Furniture

  • Mattresses

  • Large household items

  • Certain appliances

  • Other eligible oversized household materials

For residential properties, eligible bulk waste is generally collected on the second scheduled garbage collection day. The City's residential collection program provides for up to 3 cubic yards of bulk waste under the regular service. (City of Palm Coast)

Renters should check the City's current requirements before putting large items at the curb because not every oversized item automatically qualifies as regular bulk collection.

Moving Out? Check Bulk-Waste Rules

Bulk collection can be particularly important when moving out of a rental.

Instead of leaving unwanted furniture or other large items behind for the landlord, renters should determine whether the items qualify for Palm Coast's bulk collection program.

If an item is not eligible for regular collection, the renter may need to make other arrangements for disposal.

This is particularly important at the end of a lease because improperly discarded furniture or other materials could potentially create problems with the landlord or property manager.

White Goods and Large Appliances

Palm Coast also provides collection services for eligible white goods, which generally refers to large household appliances.

Examples can include:

  • Refrigerators

  • Freezers

  • Washing machines

  • Dryers

  • Water heaters

  • Other qualifying large appliances

Some appliances may require special preparation or arrangements before collection.

For example, the City's guidance states that refrigerators and freezers must have their doors removed before collection. (City of Palm Coast)

Because large appliances can have specific safety and preparation requirements, renters should check the City's current instructions before placing one at the curb.

Don't Assume Everything Can Go to the Curb

One of the most common mistakes renters can make is assuming that anything they no longer want can simply be placed outside for the next garbage pickup.

Palm Coast has different rules for:

  • Regular household garbage

  • Recycling

  • Yard waste

  • Bulk waste

  • White goods

  • Other specialty materials

The collection requirements can also depend on the item's size, type, and preparation.

When in doubt, check the City's official waste-collection information or contact the City's customer-service team before putting the item out.

Palm Coast uses a single-stream recycling system with weekly collection. The City advises residents to rinse recyclable containers and place only accepted recyclable materials in the recycling bin.

If a recycling container is missing or needs replacement, the City provides a process for requesting one.

☐ Ask About Irrigation and Whether It Has a Separate Meter

If the rental has lawn irrigation, ask whether the irrigation system has its own meter.

Palm Coast changed its sewer-billing structure on April 1, 2025, removing the previous 8,000-gallon sewer-use cap. Water passing through the property's main meter can now be subject to sewer charges. The City recommends a separate irrigation meter to avoid sewer charges on water used for irrigation.

This can be an important question for renters with larger lawns or extensive landscaping.

☐ Confirm Utility Deposits and Activation Requirements

Ask the City or utility provider whether a deposit or other activation requirement applies to your account.

Palm Coast states that utility deposits are held for 23 months and that customers with a qualifying payment history may request the deposit be returned or applied to their utility bill.

Requirements can vary depending on the account and circumstances, so confirm the details when establishing service.

Budget for Utilities Separately From Rent in Palm Coast

When calculating the total monthly cost of renting in Palm Coast, it is important to look beyond the advertised rent. The amount shown in a rental listing may not include the utilities and other recurring expenses that you will be responsible for each month.

A better approach is to estimate the total monthly housing cost, including rent, utilities, insurance, and any applicable property or community fees.

What Should You Include in Your Budget?

Depending on the lease and property, a Palm Coast renter's monthly housing budget may include:

  • Water

  • Sewer or wastewater

  • Electricity

  • Trash

  • Internet

  • Irrigation costs, if applicable

  • Renters insurance

  • HOA or community-related fees, if applicable

  • Seasonal changes in electricity usage

Not every renter will pay all of these expenses separately. Some landlords may include certain utilities in the rent, while others require tenants to establish their own accounts.

Always review the lease and ask the landlord or property manager exactly which expenses are included.

Water and Sewer Costs

Palm Coast operates municipal water and wastewater systems, and the City publishes current utility rates for water, wastewater, irrigation, and related services.

Your water and sewer bill can therefore be different from another rental property's bill depending on factors such as household size, water consumption, irrigation, and the property's meter configuration.

If you are comparing several rental homes, ask whether recent water and sewer bills are available. Looking at actual bills from the property can provide a more useful estimate than using a general average.

Electricity Can Change From Month to Month

Electricity is another major expense to consider when budgeting for a Palm Coast rental.

Monthly electricity costs can vary based on:

  • Home size

  • Number of occupants

  • Air-conditioning use

  • Thermostat settings

  • Appliance efficiency

  • Insulation

  • Seasonal weather

  • Pool equipment, if applicable

  • Other household electricity usage

Because air conditioning can be an important household expense in Florida, renters should avoid assuming that one month's electric bill represents the property's typical annual cost.

If the landlord or property manager can provide recent electricity bills, reviewing several months can help you understand how costs change throughout the year.

Don't Forget Trash and Other Charges

Depending on how the rental agreement is structured, tenants may also be responsible for garbage and other municipal charges.

Palm Coast's utility information and billing resources provide current information about the City's utility services and rates.

Ask whether trash, stormwater-related charges, or other municipal services are included in the rent or billed separately.

Irrigation Can Affect Your Monthly Budget

A rental home with a lawn or extensive landscaping may have irrigation expenses that an apartment or smaller property does not.

Palm Coast publishes separate irrigation rates, so renters should ask whether the property has an irrigation system and how it is metered.

Before signing a lease, consider asking:

  • Does the property have irrigation?

  • Is irrigation separately metered?

  • Who pays the irrigation bill?

  • Is irrigation included in the rent?

  • What have the property's recent water and irrigation costs been?

These questions can help you compare rental homes more accurately.

Palm Coast Water and Sewer Rates Are Being Adjusted

Palm Coast has been implementing phased water and sewer rate increases as part of its long-term utility infrastructure program.

The City's 2025 Utility Strategic Plan states that the rate adjustments included an 8% increase in April 2025, another 8% increase in October 2025, and annual adjustments based on the Consumer Price Index (CPI) beginning in October 2026.

The City says the increases are intended to help address aging infrastructure, rising operating costs, population growth, and the need to maintain and expand water and wastewater capacity.

For renters planning to stay in a property for several years, this is worth considering when creating a longer-term housing budget. Current rates should always be checked directly with the City because utility charges can change.

Ask for Recent Utility Bills

One of the most useful steps before signing a lease is to ask the landlord or property manager for recent utility bills, when available.

Ideally, ask for several months of bills rather than relying on one statement. This can help reveal seasonal differences and provide a better picture of the property's typical costs.

When comparing rental homes, consider asking for:

  • Recent water and sewer bills

  • Recent electricity bills

  • Irrigation costs, if applicable

  • Information about trash charges

  • Any other recurring utility-related expenses

Actual property-specific bills can be especially helpful when comparing homes with different sizes, landscaping, pools, or energy-efficiency features.

Calculate the Total Monthly Housing Cost

Instead of comparing properties based only on rent, create a simple monthly estimate:

Monthly rent+ Water and sewer+ Electricity+ Trash and other utility charges+ Internet+ Irrigation, if applicable+ Renters insurance+ Applicable HOA or community fees= Estimated total monthly housing cost

This approach gives you a clearer picture of what it may actually cost to live in each property.

Build a Cushion for Variable Expenses

Utility bills can change from month to month. For that reason, renters may want to leave some room in their budget for higher-than-expected expenses.

Electricity may increase during periods of heavier air-conditioning use, while water costs can also change based on household usage and irrigation.

A rental that fits your budget based on rent alone may become more expensive once these variable costs are added.

Questions to Ask Before Signing a Lease

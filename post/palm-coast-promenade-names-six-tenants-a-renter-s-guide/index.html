Palm Coast Promenade Names Six Tenants: A Renter’s Guide

Palm Coast’s Promenade at Town Center has confirmed six commercial tenants: Fleet Feet, 6th Street Deli, Enchanted Medical Aesthetics, The SIM Lounge Golf & Sport, AdventHealth Sports Rehab and Grandpa Joe’s Candy Shop. Nearby renters could gain easier access to dining, recreation and health services, though construction and added traffic may affect the area around Central Avenue and Bulldog Drive.

The announcement gives renters a clearer picture of the development. It does not mean every business is open, and it does not establish that the Promenade will raise nearby rents. Lease status, buildout, opening dates and future tenant announcements can all change.

What the six-tenant announcement confirms

The first announced businesses

The Promenade’s October 1, 2026 announcement names businesses covering retail, food, personal services, recreation and health care:

  • Fleet Feet, offering running footwear, apparel and fitness products, with planned group runs, walks and educational events.
  • 6th Street Deli, planning bagels, deli sandwiches, coffee, desserts, indoor seating and patio dining.
  • Enchanted Medical Aesthetics, offering medical-aesthetics and wellness services.
  • The SIM Lounge Golf & Sport, an indoor golf-simulator and multisport venue.
  • AdventHealth Sports Rehab, providing physical therapy and sports-rehabilitation services.
  • Grandpa Joe’s Candy Shop, a specialty candy retailer.

Together, the businesses could let a renter combine breakfast, a rehabilitation appointment, a shoe fitting and indoor recreation in one development. The list still falls short of a complete everyday-errand stop because it has no announced supermarket, pharmacy, bank or general household-goods store.

Why the location matters

The Promenade is at the southwest corner of Central Avenue and Bulldog Drive, across from Central Park and City Hall. Its announcement describes 204 apartments above approximately 53,000 square feet of street-level commercial space.

Customers will come from more than the surrounding neighborhoods. Residents above the storefronts, City Hall visitors, park users and people arriving from State Road 100 can add activity at different times of day.

What remains uncertain

Signed tenants are not the same as open doors

A commercial lease is a meaningful commitment, but each tenant may still need interior construction, inspections, equipment installation, hiring and final approvals. The announcement describes the businesses’ plans without giving individual opening dates.

Renters comparing homes now should keep that distinction in mind. Do not pay higher rent today because a leasing pitch says a favorite business is “coming soon.” Ask what is open on the day of your tour and consider everything else a possible future benefit.

The schedule has already moved

When the project broke ground, its shops, restaurants and apartments were expected to open in May 2026. A September 22, 2026 development report said that target had been missed and that six commercial leases had been secured out of a potential 12 to 16 spaces.

A missed target does not show that the project is failing. It does give renters a reason to verify milestones instead of relying on an old completion date. Check the development’s website, look for active storefront buildouts during an in-person visit and ask individual businesses to confirm their opening plans.

Do not confuse this project with westward expansion

The Promenade is a Town Center project. It is separate from the proposed planning area west of U.S. 1. Palm Coast says Raydient Palm Coast withdrew the westward-expansion applications on August 21, 2026, and that proposal is no longer under city consideration.

The city’s Matanzas Woods Parkway Loop Road is also a separate infrastructure project. Keeping the projects straight will help renters avoid drawing conclusions about the Promenade from unrelated west-side planning news.

How daily errands could change

Town Center renters gain the clearest convenience

People living near Central Avenue, Bulldog Drive and Belle Terre Parkway are likely to see the most immediate benefit. The deli could shorten a breakfast or lunch trip. Fleet Feet and the golf simulator could make a shopping stop part of a planned activity. Sports rehabilitation could reduce cross-town travel for some appointments.

The development is beside Central Park, so the planned Fleet Feet walks and runs could build on an existing recreation destination. The Promenade’s announcement specifically notes access to Central Park, the lake, and nearby hiking and biking trails.

Convenience depends on the errand

The first tenant group is concentrated in food, fitness, wellness and leisure. Renters will still need to drive for groceries, prescriptions, school pickup and many household purchases. It makes more sense to map recurring weekly trips than to count nearby businesses.

My test is simple: list the five places you visit most often, then measure the routes from the rental at the times you would actually travel. A deli you visit twice a month should not outweigh a difficult daily route to work, school or child care.

What could follow

More commercial spaces remain possible, though the current announcement does not confirm the identities or timing of future tenants. Restaurants, offices or personal services could make the development useful for more errands. Each type of business could also bring its own parking and traffic peaks.

Watch for signed-tenant announcements instead of relying on rumors. A letter of intent, a concept discussed online and an executed lease are different stages of the process.

Traffic and commute implications

Construction-phase conditions

Tenant buildouts may bring delivery vehicles, contractor parking and temporary changes around entrances. Visit a prospective rental on a weekday morning and again near the evening peak. Look for truck staging, blocked sightlines and construction lighting, and listen for noise that carries toward the home.

If the unit faces an active work area, ask the landlord what they know about construction hours. The answer may be incomplete. Observe the conditions yourself and put any important representation in writing before signing.

After the businesses open

The tenants may draw customers at different times. The deli could be busiest in the morning and at lunch, while rehabilitation appointments may be spread throughout the workday. Fleet Feet events and the indoor sports venue could add evening or weekend trips. These are reasonable planning possibilities based on the announced uses, not published traffic projections.

If you commute by State Road 100, test the full trip through the Central Avenue and Bulldog Drive area. A map’s off-peak estimate will not tell you what the usual drive is like. Make the trip on the same weekday and at the same time you normally leave home.

Look beyond the Promenade entrance

Town Center access is only one part of a commute. Renters traveling north toward Palm Coast Parkway may encounter a separate resurfacing project. The city says the Palm Coast Parkway project covers the corridor from U.S. 1 to Hammock Beach Bridge, with work that includes resurfacing and turn-lane extensions.

Check Palm Coast Progress before signing a lease or planning a new commute. Temporary work zones can affect the drive even when a home’s permanent location appears convenient.

Which rental areas may feel the effects

Town Center and nearby 32164

Renters closest to Town Center may have the easiest access to the six businesses, Central Park and events around City Hall. They are also the most likely to notice construction, deliveries and customer traffic. That makes proximity a tradeoff rather than an automatic advantage.

When comparing a Town Center apartment with a house or duplex elsewhere in 32164, weigh usable space, parking, yard responsibility and commute direction along with retail access. You can review broader Palm Coast renter guides and current rental availability before deciding what that convenience is worth.

Lehigh Woods, Seminole Woods and the U Section

These southern and western parts of 32164 may benefit from additional places to stop in Town Center while traveling along Belle Terre Parkway or State Road 100. The value will depend on the exact address. Palm Coast’s lettered sections are large enough for two homes in the same ZIP to have noticeably different routes.

For an example of the kind of three-bedroom rental that competes on space instead of living above retail, compare your shortlist with Palm Coast Paradise Unit A. It can serve as a comparison point, though it does not prove that one location is better.

32137 and northern Palm Coast

Renters in 32137 may visit the Promenade as a destination, but most will not have it as a walkable daily amenity. If your regular trips already lead toward Palm Coast Parkway, Matanzas Woods Parkway or the Hammock, occasional Town Center convenience may not make up for a longer commute.

Route testing is especially useful here. Compare the home-to-work trip first, then consider optional Promenade stops. A new development receiving attention should not reverse those priorities.

August 2026 rent context

What the ZIP-level data show

The latest figures supplied for this article are from Zillow’s ZIP-level Observed Rent Index, or ZORI, for single-family homes and condominiums. The August 2026 dataset puts typical observed rent at $1,823.92 in ZIP 32164 and $1,956.34 in ZIP 32137.

At the time, 32137 was $132.42 higher per month. That works out to about $1,589 over 12 months before utilities, commute fuel, insurance, deposits or fees.

How to read those figures

ZORI is an index that measures typical market rent. It is not a quote for every property. The index combines rental observations for the covered housing types and should not be read as the average asking price of the homes you personally tour.

A renovated waterfront home, a new apartment, an older duplex and a larger detached house may each sit far from the ZIP-level figure. Compare similar properties by looking at bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, age, condition, pet rules, included services and lease length.

What the Promenade does not prove about future rents

A completed mixed-use project could make nearby homes more appealing to some renters. The additional apartments could increase housing supply, while construction or congestion could make certain locations less attractive to others. The tenant announcement does not establish the direction or size of any effect on rent.

The August figures also show the two ZIPs moving in different recent directions. Citywide development news does not move every submarket together. Avoid paying a “future amenity” premium unless the amenity is open, useful to you and worth the total monthly cost.

A practical checklist before you sign

Test the location

  1. Drive the work or school route during your actual weekday peak.
  2. Repeat the route after dark and on a weekend.
  3. Enter and leave through the streets you would normally use, not just the route selected by a navigation app.
  4. Walk the immediate block and listen for construction, loading activity and event noise.
  5. Check Palm Coast Progress for active road work before making a commute assumption.

Verify the promised convenience

  • Separate open businesses from announced tenants.
  • Ask each business directly about its expected opening rather than relying on a residential leasing statement.
  • Check whether the service replaces a regular drive or is only an occasional extra.
  • Confirm parking rules, guest parking and any garage or assigned-space charge.

Compare the full housing cost

Start with monthly rent, then add utilities, internet, renter’s insurance, pet charges, parking, lawn or pest responsibilities and the estimated cost of your commute. If one home costs $100 more but removes a recurring drive, calculate the actual difference. If it offers only the promise of future convenience, leave that unproven benefit out of your budget.

Decision rule: Pay for the home and location that exist today. Consider unopened businesses and unannounced tenants a possible bonus rather than part of the lease value.

What renters should watch next

Four developments will provide more useful information than another rendering: opening dates for individual businesses, permits or visible interior buildouts, additional signed tenants and changes to vehicle access around Central Avenue and Bulldog Drive. The tenant mix matters as well. A grocery store, pharmacy or major restaurant would affect daily routines differently from another appointment-based service.

Follow city road notices along with the Promenade’s updates. The development and Palm Coast’s other transportation projects operate on separate schedules, and either one can affect a route without changing the other.

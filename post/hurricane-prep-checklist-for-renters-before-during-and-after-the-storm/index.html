Hurricane prep checklist for renters: before, during, and after the storm

Living in hurricane-prone areas comes with unique responsibilities—especially for renters who may not control the property they live in. While landlords are typically responsible for the building structure, tenants must take proactive steps to protect their belongings, safety, and financial well-being. This checklist will help renters stay prepared before, during, and after a hurricane.



Before the Hurricane: A Renter’s Guide to Smart Preparation

When a hurricane is approaching, preparation becomes your strongest defense. For renters, taking proactive steps before the storm can significantly reduce damage, protect personal belongings, and ease stress during an already challenging time. Here’s how to get ready effectively.

  1. Review Your Insurance Coverage: A Critical First Step for Renters

When preparing for a hurricane or severe storm, one of the most important steps renters can take is to carefully review their insurance coverage. While many tenants carry renters insurance, there is often a misunderstanding about what these policies actually protect—and what they leave out.

A standard renters insurance policy typically covers personal belongings against risks like fire, theft, and certain types of water damage. However, one major exclusion surprises many policyholders: flood damage. Flooding, which is a common and costly consequence of hurricanes, is not included in most basic renters insurance plans. This means that if rising water damages your furniture, electronics, or personal items, you could be left paying out of pocket.

For renters living in coastal or flood-prone areas, purchasing a separate flood insurance policy is a smart and often necessary investment. Flood insurance is usually available through national programs or private insurers and can provide financial protection against losses that standard policies won’t cover.

In addition to understanding your coverage, documenting your belongings is another essential step. Take clear photos or videos of your possessions, including furniture, electronics, clothing, and valuables. Store these records in a secure, cloud-based location so they remain accessible even if your device is lost or damaged. Having this visual proof can significantly speed up the claims process and improve your chances of receiving fair compensation.

Ultimately, reviewing your insurance coverage isn’t just about checking a box—it’s about ensuring you’re fully prepared for the financial realities of a disaster. Knowing your policy details, filling in coverage gaps, and keeping a record of your belongings can make a stressful situation far more manageable.


Create an Emergency Kit: Be Ready When It Matters Most

In the face of a hurricane or severe storm, access to basic necessities can quickly become limited. Power outages, flooded roads, and disrupted supply chains may leave you without access to food, clean water, or medical supplies. That’s why creating a well-prepared emergency kit is essential for every renter.

An emergency kit helps ensure that you can remain safe, comfortable, and self-sufficient for at least three days. This is the critical window during which emergency services may be delayed and stores may be closed or inaccessible.

Your kit should include the following essentials:

  • Bottled water: Aim for at least one gallon per person per day for drinking and basic hygiene.

  • Non-perishable food: Stock up on ready-to-eat items such as canned goods, energy bars, and dried foods.

  • Flashlights and extra batteries: These are safer and more reliable than candles during power outages.

  • A first aid kit: Include bandages, antiseptics, pain relievers, and any necessary prescription medications.

  • Portable phone chargers or power banks: Staying connected is crucial for receiving updates and contacting help.

  • Important documents in waterproof containers: Protect identification, insurance policies, and emergency contacts from water damage.

It’s also important to store your emergency kit in a location that is easy to access at a moment’s notice. In an evacuation or sudden emergency, you won’t have time to gather supplies—having everything ready to grab can make a significant difference.

You may also consider customizing your kit based on your personal needs, such as adding pet supplies, extra clothing, hygiene items, or special medical equipment.

Preparing an emergency kit is a simple but powerful step toward resilience. When a storm hits, having the right supplies on hand can reduce stress, improve safety, and give you greater peace of mind during uncertain conditions.

  1. Secure Your Living Space: Reduce Risks Before the Storm

Taking the time to secure your living space before a hurricane or severe storm can significantly reduce potential damage and help keep you safe. Strong winds and heavy rain can turn everyday items into hazards, especially if they are left unsecured around your home.

Start by inspecting your outdoor areas. Bring inside or firmly secure items such as patio furniture, potted plants, decorations, and trash bins. In high winds, these objects can become dangerous projectiles, potentially damaging windows, walls, or nearby property.

Next, ensure that all windows and doors are properly closed and locked. Even small openings can allow wind and rain to enter, increasing the risk of interior damage. If your residence includes storm shutters, impact-resistant windows, or other protective features, make sure they are ready for use. Renters should communicate with their landlord or property manager ahead of time to confirm responsibilities and timing for installing these protections.

It’s also a good idea to move valuable or fragile items away from windows and place them in safer, more secure areas of your home. If flooding is a concern, consider elevating important belongings off the floor to reduce the risk of water damage.

Securing your living space is a proactive step that can make a major difference during a storm. By minimizing potential hazards and reinforcing vulnerable areas, you not only protect your belongings but also create a safer environment for yourself and others.

  1. Know Your Evacuation Plan: Plan Ahead for a Safer Escape

When a hurricane approaches, time becomes one of your most valuable resources. Waiting until the last minute to decide where to go can lead to confusion, traffic congestion, and unnecessary risk. That’s why having a clear evacuation plan in place is essential for every renter.

Start by researching local evacuation routes in your area. Many communities have designated paths designed to move residents safely away from high-risk zones. Familiarize yourself with these routes in advance, and consider identifying more than one option in case your primary route becomes blocked or overcrowded.

Next, locate nearby emergency shelters or safe zones. Local authorities often provide updated information on shelter locations during storm season. If possible, arrange to stay with friends or family members who live outside the affected area. Having a confirmed destination can make the evacuation process far less stressful.

Transportation is another critical factor. Keep your vehicle fueled and in good working condition, especially during hurricane season. Gas shortages and long lines are common when evacuation orders are issued, so it’s wise to fill up your tank early rather than waiting until the last moment.

It’s also helpful to prepare a small “go bag” with essentials such as clothing, medications, important documents, and your emergency kit items. This ensures you can leave quickly without scrambling to gather necessities.

Planning ahead not only reduces panic but also increases your chances of a safe and orderly evacuation. By knowing where to go, how to get there, and what to bring, you can respond quickly and confidently when it matters most.

  1. Protect Important Documents: Safeguard What Matters Most

In the midst of a hurricane or severe storm, physical belongings can often be replaced—but important documents may be much harder to recover. Losing identification, legal paperwork, or medical records can create long-term challenges, especially when filing insurance claims or accessing essential services. Taking steps to protect these documents ahead of time is a crucial part of emergency preparedness.

Start by gathering all critical paperwork in one place. This may include identification (such as passports or ID cards), lease agreements, insurance policies, medical records, bank information, and emergency contact lists. Having these documents organized makes them easier to secure and access when needed.

To protect against water damage, store your documents in waterproof bags, sealed plastic containers, or specialized document protectors. Keep them in a location that is both safe and easy to access in case you need to evacuate quickly.

For an added layer of security, consider creating digital backups. Scan or photograph your important documents and upload them to a secure cloud storage service or encrypted digital platform. This ensures that even if your physical copies are lost, stolen, or destroyed, you can still access the information from anywhere with an internet connection.

You may also want to store copies on a password-protected USB drive kept in your emergency kit. Redundancy is key—having both physical and digital copies greatly increases your chances of recovering quickly after a disaster.

Protecting important documents may seem like a small step, but it can make a significant difference during recovery. With proper preparation, you can avoid unnecessary stress and ensure that critical information remains safe and accessible when you need it most.

Preparing before a hurricane is not just about protecting property—it’s about safeguarding your well-being and peace of mind. By reviewing your insurance, assembling essential supplies, securing your home, planning evacuation routes, and protecting critical documents, you can face the storm with greater confidence and control.



During the Hurricane: Staying Safe When the Storm Hits

When a hurricane is actively impacting your area, your focus should shift entirely to safety. Conditions can change rapidly, and even well-prepared renters must stay alert and cautious. Knowing what to do during the storm can help protect you from serious harm and reduce unnecessary risks.


  1. Follow Official Instructions: Trust Reliable Guidance During the Storm

During a hurricane or severe storm, accurate and timely information can make a life-saving difference. Conditions can change rapidly, and decisions made by emergency management officials are based on the latest data, forecasts, and safety assessments. For renters and residents alike, following official instructions is one of the most important steps to staying safe.

Make it a priority to stay informed through reliable sources. Monitor updates from local authorities using battery-powered radios, mobile alerts, or trusted news outlets. Many governments also provide emergency notifications through text alerts or dedicated apps, ensuring you receive real-time information even if other communication channels are disrupted.

Emergency management agencies issue guidance based on the storm’s path, intensity, and potential impact. This may include instructions on sheltering in place, evacuation routes, or safety precautions. It’s important to listen carefully and act promptly on these recommendations.

If evacuation orders are announced, take them seriously and respond without delay. These orders are not given lightly—they are issued to protect lives in areas at high risk of flooding, storm surge, or structural damage. Waiting too long can lead to dangerous situations, including heavy traffic, limited fuel availability, or being trapped in unsafe conditions.

Equally important is avoiding misinformation. In times of crisis, rumors and unverified updates can spread quickly, especially on social media. Rely only on official channels and trusted organizations to guide your decisions.

By staying informed and following official instructions, you can reduce uncertainty, act with confidence, and prioritize your safety during even the most unpredictable situations.

  1. Stay Indoors and Away from Windows: Prioritize Safety During the Storm

When a hurricane or severe storm is in full force, staying indoors is critical to your safety. Venturing outside, even briefly, can expose you to dangerous winds, flying debris, and sudden changes in weather conditions. Remaining inside a secure structure significantly reduces your risk of injury.

The safest place to take shelter is in an interior room on the lowest level of your building, away from windows and glass doors. Areas such as bathrooms, closets, or hallways are often ideal because they are typically surrounded by more walls and have fewer openings, offering better protection from wind and debris.

Windows are one of the most vulnerable parts of any structure during a storm. Strong winds can shatter glass, allowing rain and debris to enter your home at high speed. For this reason, it’s essential to stay clear of windows at all times, even if they appear intact. Closing curtains or blinds can provide an additional layer of protection by helping contain shattered glass if breakage occurs.

Another important precaution is to avoid using elevators during the storm. Power outages and mechanical malfunctions are common in severe weather, and using an elevator could leave you trapped. Instead, rely on stairs if movement within the building is necessary.

By choosing a safe indoor location and avoiding high-risk areas like windows and elevators, you can greatly reduce your exposure to hazards. Staying put and protected is one of the simplest yet most effective ways to remain safe until the storm passes.

  1. Use Emergency Supplies Wisely: Make Your Resources Last

During a hurricane or severe storm, your emergency kit becomes your primary source of support. With power outages, limited access to stores, and potential delays in emergency response, it’s essential to use your supplies carefully and responsibly. Managing your resources wisely can help ensure your safety and comfort for the duration of the crisis.

Start by rationing your food and water. Even if you have prepared a three-day supply, conditions may require you to stretch those resources longer than expected. Drink water in measured amounts and avoid unnecessary waste. Choose non-perishable foods that provide energy and nutrition while requiring little to no preparation.

Equally important is managing your electronic devices. Your phone is a vital tool for receiving updates, contacting emergency services, and staying connected with loved ones. Keep it charged as much as possible and limit usage to essential communication. Avoid activities that quickly drain battery life, such as streaming videos or playing games.

If you have portable chargers or power banks, use them strategically. Rather than charging devices frequently, wait until battery levels are low to maximize efficiency. If possible, rotate usage among devices to ensure at least one remains powered at all times.

You should also use other supplies—such as flashlights, batteries, and first aid items—only when necessary. Turning off lights when not in use and conserving medical supplies can make a significant difference during extended emergencies.

Using your emergency supplies wisely is about planning, discipline, and awareness. By stretching your resources and prioritizing essential needs, you can better navigate the uncertainties of a storm and remain prepared until normal services are restored.

  1. Avoid Floodwaters: Stay Clear of Hidden Dangers

Flooding is one of the most hazardous and unpredictable consequences of hurricanes and severe storms. While it may be tempting to move through standing water to reach safety or assess damage, doing so can put you at serious risk. Avoiding floodwaters altogether is one of the safest decisions you can make during and after a storm.

Even shallow water can be far more dangerous than it appears. Just a few inches of moving water can knock a person off their feet, while a foot or two can carry away a vehicle. The depth of floodwater is often difficult to judge, especially when roads and sidewalks are no longer visible beneath the surface.

In addition to the risk of strong currents, floodwaters often conceal hidden hazards. These may include sharp debris, broken glass, displaced objects, open drains, or damaged infrastructure. There is also a significant danger from downed power lines, which can electrify the water and create life-threatening conditions.

Another critical concern is contamination. Floodwater can contain sewage, chemicals, oil, and other harmful substances that pose serious health risks. Contact with contaminated water can lead to infections, skin irritation, or more severe illnesses, especially if you have cuts or open wounds.

The safest approach is simple: stay away from floodwaters entirely. Do not attempt to walk, swim, or drive through flooded areas under any circumstances. Wait until local authorities confirm that it is safe to move around and that the area has been properly assessed.

By avoiding floodwaters, you protect yourself from both visible and hidden dangers. Patience and caution during these situations can prevent injuries and save lives.

During a hurricane, the best decisions are often the simplest—stay informed, stay inside, and avoid unnecessary risks. By following official guidance, taking shelter properly, managing your supplies, and steering clear of floodwaters, you give yourself the best chance of staying safe until the storm passes.


After the Hurricane: What Renters Should Do Next

The storm may have passed, but the risks don’t end when the skies clear. The aftermath of a hurricane can be just as dangerous and stressful, especially for renters who must rely on both landlords and insurance providers to recover. Taking the right steps quickly—and safely—can make a major difference in how smoothly you bounce back.

  1. Return Only When It’s Safe

In the aftermath of a hurricane or severe storm, the urge to return home and assess the damage can be overwhelming. However, safety must always come first. Authorities such as the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the National Weather Service strongly advise residents to wait until an official “all clear” is issued before going back to affected areas.

Even when the storm has passed, hidden hazards can remain. Structural damage may not always be visible, and weakened roofs, walls, or foundations can collapse without warning. Additionally, downed power lines may still be live, posing a serious risk of electrocution. Gas leaks are another potential danger, as they can lead to fires or explosions if not properly handled.

Floodwaters can also leave behind contaminated surfaces, debris, and unstable ground conditions. Roads may be washed out or blocked, making travel unsafe. Entering too soon not only puts your life at risk but can also hinder emergency responders who are still working to secure the area.

When authorities confirm it is safe to return, proceed with caution. Inspect your surroundings carefully, avoid entering buildings that appear damaged, and report any hazards to local officials immediately. Taking a patient and cautious approach can help prevent further injury and ensure a safer recovery process.

  1. Document Any Damage

Once authorities confirm it is safe to return, one of the most important steps you can take is to carefully document any damage to your home and belongings. This process plays a crucial role in recovering financial losses and ensuring a smoother insurance or rental claim.

Start by taking clear, high-quality photos and videos of all affected areas. Capture wide shots of each room as well as close-ups of specific damage to walls, floors, ceilings, furniture, and personal items. Be thorough—what may seem minor at first could become more significant later. Visual evidence provides a reliable record of the condition of your property immediately after the disaster.

Organizations like the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Insurance Information Institute emphasize the importance of documentation when filing insurance claims. This evidence helps verify the extent of your losses and can speed up the claims process.

In addition to photos and videos, make a written inventory of damaged items, including descriptions, approximate values, and purchase dates if possible. Keep receipts for any emergency repairs or replacements, as these may be reimbursable under your policy. If you are renting, promptly report the damage to your landlord and share your documentation to support any necessary repairs.

The more detailed and organized your records are, the stronger your claim will be. Taking the time to document everything properly can make a significant difference in how quickly and successfully you recover after a disaster.

  1. Prevent Further Damage

After documenting the initial impact of a storm, the next priority is to prevent additional damage—if it is safe to do so. Taking quick, temporary measures can help protect your home and belongings from worsening conditions while you wait for professional repairs.

Start by addressing the most vulnerable areas. Broken windows can be covered with plastic sheets or boards to keep out rain and debris. Leaks in the roof or ceiling should be managed by placing buckets or containers underneath to collect water and reduce interior damage. If possible, move furniture, electronics, and other undamaged belongings away from affected areas to keep them safe from moisture or contamination.

According to the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Insurance Information Institute, these temporary fixes—often referred to as “mitigation efforts”—are not only practical but also encouraged by insurance providers. Acting promptly can help minimize losses and demonstrate that you took reasonable steps to protect your property.

It is important, however, to avoid making permanent repairs until your insurance company has assessed the damage. Focus only on safe, short-term solutions that stabilize the situation. Be sure to keep all receipts for materials such as tarps, boards, or cleaning supplies, as well as any services you pay for. These expenses are often reimbursable under many insurance policies.

By taking simple, proactive steps, you can limit further damage and support a more efficient recovery process—both structurally and financially.

  1. Contact Your Landlord

After ensuring your safety and documenting any damage, it is essential to contact your landlord as soon as possible. Prompt communication helps initiate the repair process and ensures that any safety hazards are addressed quickly.

Inform your landlord of all structural damage and potential risks, such as broken windows, leaking roofs, electrical issues, or signs of instability. Providing clear photos and videos can help them better understand the extent of the damage and prioritize necessary repairs. Early notification also creates a record of the incident, which may be important for both legal and insurance purposes.

In most cases, landlords are responsible for maintaining and repairing the structure of the building, including walls, roofing, plumbing, and electrical systems. Renters, on the other hand, are typically responsible for their personal belongings. Organizations like the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Insurance Information Institute highlight the importance of understanding these responsibilities when dealing with post-disaster recovery.

Be sure to ask your landlord about the expected timeline for repairs and whether the property is still safe to live in. If the damage is severe, discuss the possibility of temporary relocation. Some lease agreements or renters insurance policies may cover additional living expenses if the unit becomes uninhabitable.

Maintaining clear and timely communication with your landlord can help reduce confusion, speed up repairs, and ensure that you take the right steps toward recovery.

  1. File Insurance Claims Promptly

After a disaster, acting quickly on your insurance claim can make a significant difference in how fast you recover financially. Time is critical, and most insurance providers encourage policyholders to report damage as soon as possible.

Begin by contacting your insurance company immediately to start the claims process. Provide all necessary documentation, including photos and videos of the damage, receipts for any temporary repairs or replacements, and a detailed inventory of affected items. The more complete and accurate your submission, the smoother and faster your claim is likely to be processed.

Organizations such as the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Insurance Information Institute stress the importance of timely filing and thorough documentation. Delays in reporting can sometimes lead to complications or even reduced compensation, depending on your policy terms.

Staying organized throughout the process is equally important. Keep a record of all communications with your insurance provider, including emails, phone calls, claim numbers, and the names of representatives you speak with. Tracking follow-ups and deadlines will help prevent unnecessary delays and ensure that your claim stays on track.

Filing promptly and maintaining clear records not only speeds up the recovery process but also gives you greater control during what can be a stressful time.

Recovering after a hurricane can feel overwhelming, but taking these steps helps protect your safety, your finances, and your peace of mind. Being proactive and organized during this phase is key to getting back to normal as quickly as possible.

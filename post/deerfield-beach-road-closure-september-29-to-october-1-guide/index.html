Deerfield Beach Road Closure: September 29 to October 1 Guide

SW 10th Street is temporarily closed between SW 11th Avenue and SW 12th Avenue from Tuesday, September 29, through Thursday, October 1, 2026. The City of Deerfield Beach notice tells motorists to follow the detour signs and traffic-control devices at the site. It does not give closure hours or a turn-by-turn detour online.

If this segment lies between your rental and work, school, I-95, a delivery destination or a service call, plan as though it is unavailable until the city confirms otherwise. Follow the signs at the site, do not cut through residential driveways or parking lots, and allow more time during school and commuter traffic.

Where and when the closure applies

The affected segment

According to the city’s September 29 announcement, the closure is limited to SW 10th Street between SW 11th Avenue and SW 12th Avenue. Although the segment is short, SW 10th Street carries traffic between local streets, I-95 and destinations farther west. The effects can reach beyond the households next to the work.

The city says its Environmental Services team is completing traffic-calming improvements. The scheduled work period runs from September 29 through October 1, but the notice does not say the road will reopen each evening or give a reopening time for October 1.

What the notice does not establish

The online notice has no map of the exact barricade positions and does not name a formal sequence of streets for the detour. It also gives no delay estimate in minutes. A navigation app may still direct drivers toward the closed block while crews and signs are in place.

Treat the dates as the work window, without assuming that every lane will reopen at a particular time. Weather, equipment movement or conditions found during the work can change a municipal schedule.

Who is most likely to encounter delays

Drivers using the local east-west connection

Drivers who normally enter or leave SW 10th Street through SW 11th Avenue or SW 12th Avenue will feel the most direct effect. People approaching on nearby streets may also find temporary queues where vehicles turn away from the barricades.

SW 10th Street carries more than neighborhood traffic. The city’s Complete Streets Guidelines identify the corridor between I-95 and SW 11th Way as a six-lane minor arterial. They identify SW 11th Way between SW 10th Street and SW 15th Street as a city collector. Because of that street hierarchy, a geographically small closure can shift traffic onto nearby intersections.

School runs, work trips and appointments

Families may find the closure most inconvenient during morning drop-off and afternoon pickup, when departure times are less flexible. Commuters traveling toward I-95 should check which side of the closed segment they are starting from before accepting a route from an app.

Home-health workers, cleaners, repair technicians and other visitors may not know the neighborhood. Send them the affected cross streets before they leave. "SW 10th Street closure between SW 11th and SW 12th avenues" gives them more useful information than saying only that construction is nearby.

The official detour and sensible backup routes

What counts as the official detour

The city’s only published instruction is to follow the posted detour signs and traffic-control devices. Signs at the work zone take priority over the suggestions below, directions from a navigation application or a familiar shortcut. The city’s notice page does not identify an official turn-by-turn detour.

If a proposed route conflicts with a barricade, flagger or temporary sign, do not enter the block. Continue along the marked detour even if your phone still shows SW 10th Street as open.

Nearby route options to evaluate

SW 15th Street is the clearest nearby east-west corridor to consider when the posted traffic pattern allows it. The city’s street guidelines identify SW 15th Street as a minor arterial between Powerline Road and Federal Highway. Because SW 11th Way connects SW 10th Street with SW 15th Street, that corridor may work for traffic approaching the closure from the west.

Hillsboro Boulevard may work better as a wider-area alternative when your trip begins north of SW 10th Street or ends well beyond the closed segment. A planned larger loop is usually preferable to reaching the barricade and improvising on small residential streets.

These options can help with planning, but they do not replace the city’s marked detour. Check your direction of travel, temporary turn restrictions and current traffic before selecting either corridor.

How renters should adjust daily plans

Work commutes and school runs

Try your revised route before your most important trip. A short drive during a quieter part of the day can show where the barricades begin, which turns remain available and whether you can still reach your building’s usual entrance.

  • Save the city notice on your phone in case you need to show an employer, school office or caregiver why your route changed.
  • Set an earlier household departure time because the city has not supplied a precise delay estimate.
  • Have one family member check the route before everyone leaves, which can prevent conflicting directions from different navigation apps.
  • If a child rides a bus, monitor the school or transportation provider’s usual communication channel for stop changes. The city notice does not announce any school-bus revisions.

Deliveries and rides

Add this short access note to grocery, meal and parcel instructions: "SW 10th Street is closed between SW 11th and SW 12th avenues. Follow posted detour." Do not tell a driver to pass a barricade because local access appears open from your side.

For a rideshare pickup, put the pin at a lawful point that the driver can reach safely. Do not ask a driver to stop beside cones, in an active travel lane or where a turning queue limits visibility. If your complex has multiple gates, check that the alternate gate permits visitor entry.

Maintenance and service appointments

Send the closure notice to plumbers, air-conditioning technicians, pest-control teams and home-health providers before their appointment window. Include your gate name, building number and best reachable entrance. When they arrive, drivers should still follow instructions from traffic-control personnel.

If uncertain access could lead to a missed appointment fee, ask the provider to note the municipal closure on the work order. Renters should promptly tell their property manager when the route affects access for an urgent repair.

Planning for one-car and remote-work households

One-car households

Combine errands when practical and decide who needs the vehicle before the morning begins. If one household member must get to work while another handles school pickup or a medical appointment, plan the handoff at an accessible meeting point. The normal route may not remain usable.

Gather medications, school materials, work credentials and charging cables the night before. A detour is easier to handle when nobody has to make another trip home for a forgotten item.

Remote workers

Working remotely reduces commuting, though the closure can still affect food deliveries, equipment shipments and scheduled repair visits. Add your phone number and the cross-street warning to delivery notes, and stay reachable as a driver approaches.

If construction noise or vehicle activity interferes with a call, use headphones and move away from the street-facing side of the home. The city describes the project as traffic-calming work but does not publish daily operating hours, so there is no confirmed quiet period around which to schedule meetings.

A renter’s evening checklist

  1. Review the latest city notice and live traffic map.
  2. Confirm the next day’s first destination and accessible exit.
  3. Message caregivers, guests and scheduled contractors.
  4. Move any time-sensitive pickup to a safe, reachable location if necessary.
  5. Keep the Environmental Services contact number available for questions the online notice does not answer.

Rain, visibility and changing traffic conditions

Why conditions deserve another check

South Florida rain can make cones, temporary signs and unfamiliar turns harder to see. Congestion on an alternate route may also become less predictable. Check current conditions shortly before leaving instead of relying on the route you reviewed the previous evening.

For congestion and incidents on state roads, consult FL511, Florida’s official real-time traffic service. Its map covers traffic speeds, closures, incidents, construction zones and weather-related road information. Because a local city closure may not appear immediately, use FL511 with the Deerfield Beach notice and the signs at the site.

Do not treat the closure as permission to improvise

A flooded road edge, construction vehicle or moved barricade can make a turn that worked yesterday unsuitable today. Do not drive around cones, follow a work truck into the closure or cross private commercial property as a bypass. If access to your rental appears completely blocked, stop somewhere lawful and contact the property manager or the city instead of entering the work zone.

Where to check for changes or early reopening

City updates

Begin with the city’s mobile-friendly closure notice and the Deerfield Beach news feed. A lane that looks open does not confirm that the project is finished. Crews may temporarily move barriers for equipment or controlled access.

For details not answered online, the notice directs residents to Environmental Services at 954-250-4307. Ask whether the closure remains active, whether local access is being maintained for your destination or whether the scheduled completion has changed.

What to verify before resuming your normal route

  • The city has posted an update, or all closure signs and barricades have been removed.
  • Your navigation service shows a current timestamp instead of cached directions.
  • The entrance you normally use is open for your direction of travel.
  • No temporary restriction blocks the turn needed to reach your home, school or workplace.

Palm Coast Tree Lighting 2026: November 28 Visitor Guide

Palm Coast’s 15th Annual Tree Lighting Ceremony is scheduled for Saturday, November 28, 2026, from 6 to 9 p.m. at Central Park in Town Center, 975 Central Avenue. Admission is free. Plans for the evening include the tree lighting, music, family activities, food trucks, Santa’s arrival on a Palm Coast fire engine, and the opening night of the Fantasy Lights Festival.

Renters will have an easier evening if they arrive before the 6 p.m. start, choose a meeting and pickup point before entering the park, and wear comfortable shoes with a light layer. Check the city’s event announcement and the day-of forecast before leaving home.

When and where the Palm Coast tree lighting takes place

The confirmed schedule

The City of Palm Coast’s official announcement sets the ceremony for Saturday, November 28, from 6 to 9 p.m. This is the city’s 15th annual ceremony and the formal start of Palm Coast’s holiday-season programming.

The venue is Central Park in Town Center, at 975 Central Avenue, Palm Coast, Florida 32164. When using a navigation app, make sure the destination is Central Park in Town Center and not another park with a similar name.

What admission covers

The city says admission to the Fantasy Lights Festival is free. Donations are appreciated for the Rotary Club of Flagler County’s community-service programs. The city has not announced a general admission ticket or advance reservation requirement for the tree-lighting ceremony.

Food trucks are expected, and food purchases are a separate household expense. Setting a spending limit before you arrive can help, especially when several children want their own meals or treats.

What is planned for the 15th annual ceremony

The evening’s main activities

The program extends beyond the tree lighting. According to the city, Central Park will have festive lights, music, food, family activities, children’s crafts, and Santa’s Village. Children will be able to write and send letters to Santa and take photos with him.

Santa is scheduled to arrive aboard a Palm Coast fire engine, and the Palm Coast City Council is expected to participate. The city has not published a minute-by-minute program. Unless a more detailed schedule is released, avoid promising children that Santa, the lighting, or another activity will happen at an exact time.

Opening night of Fantasy Lights

The ceremony also launches the Rotary Club of Flagler County’s 21st Anniversary Fantasy Lights Festival. The city describes the seasonal attraction as dozens of illuminated displays accompanied by music.

Opening night will probably draw people interested in both the civic ceremony and the first walk through the displays. November 28 will therefore differ from an ordinary visit later in the season. Attend the ceremony for the community kickoff. If you only want to see the lights at a quieter pace, review the Rotary schedule for another evening.

Getting to Central Park in Town Center

Plan for a busy arrival window

Central Park is a major event venue in Palm Coast’s designated downtown. The city lists parking as a park amenity, although its tree-lighting announcement does not promise a reserved event lot, shuttle service, or particular traffic pattern.

A parking symbol on the park page does not mean a nearby space will still be open close to the 6 p.m. start. Plan to walk from the legal space you find instead of circling for a front-row spot.

A renter-friendly arrival plan

  1. Save the destination: Enter 975 Central Avenue before leaving your rental and check the final destination name.
  2. Allow extra time: Try to be parked and walking before the official start instead of reaching the park entrance at 6 p.m.
  3. Use legal spaces only: Follow temporary signs, cones, accessible-space markings, fire lanes, and restrictions at nearby businesses or residential areas.
  4. Photograph your location: Take a picture of the nearest sign, building, or row marker. Holiday lights can make an unfamiliar area look different after dark.
  5. Choose a reunion point: Pick a fixed, well-lit landmark inside the event in case members of the group become separated.

Drivers arriving from different parts of Palm Coast should meet at the park instead of trying to keep a convoy together through event traffic. Share arrival updates only after the driver has parked.

Pickup and rideshare planning

If someone is dropping off an older relative, a child, or a visitor with limited stamina, agree on the return pickup location before separating. The instruction to "meet near the lights" is too vague because the park covers several acres and a crowded curb can be hard to describe by phone.

The city’s announcement does not identify a rideshare zone. Follow temporary traffic directions when you arrive, and do not stop in a travel lane or fire-access area. If the closest curb is congested, choose a legal location farther away and walk the last segment together.

How to use the Central Park setting

Know the park’s layout and amenities

The official park page lists parking, restrooms, a water fountain, walking facilities, and trail access. It also says a half-mile brick path circles the central fountain, while a concrete amphitheater extension supports performances and ceremonies.

The park works well for a large outdoor celebration, though visitors may spend more time standing and walking than expected. Closed-toe walking shoes are more practical than footwear chosen only for holiday photographs.

Select a practical home base

Families should choose a recognizable gathering point soon after entering. Pick somewhere stationary, easy to describe, and away from moving vehicles. A food truck is a poor sole landmark because vendors may look similar, and a line may hide the truck from view.

Keep phones charged without depending on phone service as the entire reunification plan. Crowds, noise, low batteries, and distracted children can complicate a simple call. If a child is too young to recite a caregiver’s phone number reliably, write it on a card and place it in the child’s pocket.

What Palm Coast renters should bring

A compact outdoor-evening kit

There is no need to load a wagon with everything from the rental. A small, hands-free bag holding these items should cover the most common needs:

  • A light jacket, sweater, or wind-resistant layer
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • A charged phone and compact battery pack
  • Water, subject to any posted event rules
  • A small amount of money or another payment method for food
  • Any required medication
  • A small poncho if rain remains possible
  • A reusable bag for children’s crafts or personal items

Late-November Florida evenings can feel cooler after sunset, particularly when wind moves across an open park. Wear layers that are easy to carry instead of one bulky item that becomes inconvenient if the evening stays mild.

What to leave at the rental

Leave oversized chairs, large coolers, sports equipment, and anything that could create a tripping hazard at the rental unless the city specifically confirms it is appropriate. The announcement does not include a detailed prohibited-items list, so check the event page shortly before attending.

Think carefully before bringing a pet. Lights, music, food lines, children, and a fire engine can overwhelm an otherwise calm animal. Unless the city posts clear pet guidance and your dog is comfortable in dense public gatherings, a quiet evening at home may be kinder.

Comfort plans for families, older visitors, and newcomers

Families with young children

Photograph each child at the rental before departure so you have a current record of what the child is wearing. Give children one simple instruction: if separated, stop moving and ask a uniformed event worker or public-safety official for help.

Discuss food, crafts, Santa photos, and the departure time in advance. Santa’s Village and children’s activities are confirmed, but the city has not guaranteed unlimited capacity or published individual wait times.

Older adults and visitors with limited mobility

Use the park’s paved and brick walking surfaces when possible. Keep in mind that a half-mile loop may be tiring for someone with limited stamina. Decide whether the priority is the ceremony, Santa, food, or the light displays, and conserve energy for that part of the evening.

Drivers cannot count on finding an open accessible parking space. If a passenger needs close access, monitor city updates for event-specific accessibility or drop-off instructions. Palm Coast Parks & Recreation lists its main line as 386-986-2323 for questions that need an official answer.

New Palm Coast residents

If you recently moved into the P, R, W, C, or another lettered section, this event offers an introduction to Town Center without turning the evening into a citywide sightseeing trip. Concentrate on learning one destination, where you parked, and the route you will take home.

Weather and schedule checks before leaving

Do not treat an early forecast as a promise

A reliable event-night forecast is not available weeks ahead. Check the National Weather Service through weather.gov when November 28 enters the short-range forecast, and check it again on the afternoon of the event.

Temperature tells only part of the story. Rain timing, wind, humidity, and the after-sunset "feels like" conditions will affect comfort in an open park. If rain is possible, a poncho is easier to manage than a wide umbrella that may block views or become awkward in a crowd.

A final-check decision rule

  • The day before: Review the city announcement for a cancellation, postponement, parking notice, or revised program.
  • Before getting dressed: Check the hourly forecast for the full 6-to-9-p.m. event window.
  • Before leaving: Look at current radar and the city’s official communication channels.
  • If conditions become unsafe: Follow city instructions instead of relying on an old social-media screenshot.

The city’s November announcement establishes the planned date and schedule, although outdoor events can change. Check an official source instead of asking a neighborhood group to interpret the weather or event policy.

A simple plan for a comfortable evening

Before you lock the rental

  1. Confirm that the event remains scheduled for November 28.
  2. Check the evening forecast and current radar.
  3. Save 975 Central Avenue in your navigation app.
  4. Charge phones and pack one light layer per person.
  5. Set a food budget and departure time.
  6. Choose a meeting point and pickup strategy.
  7. Photograph where you park.

Apartment and duplex renters should also bring the correct gate, building, or front-door access method. After a long outdoor evening, you do not want to find that the only working entry credential is sitting on the kitchen counter.

If the event shapes your housing search

Town Center events can show newcomers how much they value access to civic gatherings, but one pleasant evening should not determine a lease. Before choosing a location, compare the everyday trips to work, groceries, schools, and medical care.

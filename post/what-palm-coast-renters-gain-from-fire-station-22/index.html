What Palm Coast Renters Gain From Fire Station 22

Palm Coast renters now have an active, modern fire station near Palm Coast Parkway and Colbert Lane. The new building gives firefighters better facilities and space for modern apparatus. It adds to the city’s emergency-service network, though it cannot guarantee a particular response time, make every nearby home equally safe, or automatically reduce renters-insurance premiums.

For renters, Fire Station 22 is one factor to weigh alongside road access, the rental’s condition, evacuation plans, commute convenience, and price.

What Fire Station 22 means for renters

A modern base in an established part of Palm Coast

The city celebrated the opening of Fire Station 22 on October 8, 2026. The station is at Palm Coast Parkway NE and Colbert Lane in the Palm Harbor area. It replaces Palm Coast’s original station, built in 1977.

The original building was not designed to serve as the long-term home of a modern fire department. Its replacement has space for current apparatus and today’s work and training requirements. It also gives crews who spend long shifts there a healthier working environment.

What the opening does not prove

A station’s location cannot tell you exactly how long an ambulance or fire engine will take to reach a rental. Units may already be responding to another call, traffic can slow the route, and dispatchers may send resources from a different station. The type and seriousness of an incident also determine which personnel and equipment respond.

A useful rule: Treat the station as a neighborhood advantage rather than a promise. Check the rental’s safety features and access before giving proximity too much weight.

What opened and why the city replaced the old station

The new station was built for current operations

The city describes the replacement as a 10,000-square-foot prototype fire station. Land clearing began in April 2025, and the facility was already active by the grand-opening ceremony. Station tones sounded for emergency calls during the ceremony itself.

The city says the department expects to handle more than 18,700 calls during 2026. With that citywide workload, Palm Coast needs suitable stations, room for apparatus, adequate crew facilities, and a response network spread across the growing city.

The building has efficiency features

According to the city, the LEED-certified station produces approximately 136% of the energy it consumes. Water-saving systems are expected to reduce usage by more than 30%. The building also has a certified indoor-air-quality designation.

These features mainly concern municipal operations and firefighters’ working conditions. Renters should not read them as evidence of lower rent, lower taxes, or direct savings on household utilities.

The original station has a planned second life

The city says it will preserve the historic station and turn it into a community space where visitors can learn about Palm Coast’s history and fire service. This plan is separate from the new station’s operational benefits. It also keeps the older public building in community use.

Neighborhoods and travel routes to examine

Start with Palm Harbor and the east side

Station 22 is most relevant to the established Palm Harbor side of the city and nearby east-side areas. If you are considering a rental around Palm Harbor Parkway, Club House Drive, Florida Park Drive, Old Kings Road, Palm Coast Parkway, or Colbert Lane, add the station to your route map. Then inspect the streets that actually connect the home to those roads.

Sharing a ZIP code with the station does not necessarily make a home convenient. Palm Coast’s canals, cul-de-sacs, divided parkways, bridge crossings, and limited neighborhood exits can make two seemingly close addresses work quite differently.

Check ordinary traffic and emergency access

Drive the route around the time you usually leave for work. Palm Coast Parkway is one of the city’s principal east-west routes, and Colbert Lane connects destinations along the eastern side. Pay attention to turning movements at the intersection, school-hour traffic, construction, and the route you would take if your usual exit were blocked.

Look at the rental from a responder’s point of view, too. A home set far back from the road, a faded curb number, a narrow driveway, a locked gate, low tree limbs, or cars blocking the address can cost precious time even with a station nearby.

Keep hurricane evacuation planning separate

Fire Station 22 is part of the city’s public-safety infrastructure, but renters still need to know their evacuation zone and destination. Palm Coast’s public-safety page directs residents to Flagler County Emergency Management and ALERTFlagler for emergency information.

During hurricane season, decide where you would go, how you would cross the Intracoastal Waterway if necessary, and which alternate route would avoid a flooded or closed street. Save the plans offline. At the height of a storm, emergency personnel cannot make every inaccessible rental accessible.

How public-safety infrastructure fits a rental decision

Use several measures of safety

Living closer to a station can be reassuring, especially for households with older adults, young children, mobility limitations, or serious medical conditions. Even so, the condition and layout of the home often deserve more attention than its straight-line distance from a firehouse.

During a tour, I recommend checking these items:

  • Working alarms: Ask to test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms where applicable, and find out who replaces the batteries.
  • Visible address: Stand at the street after dark and check whether you can read the house number from a vehicle.
  • Clear exits: Make sure bedroom windows open and exterior doors are unobstructed.
  • Fire-extinguisher plan: Ask whether an extinguisher is supplied. Household members should also know how and when to use it.
  • Emergency access: Inspect the driveway, review gate instructions and parking rules, and note any access code responders may need.
  • Medical information: Palm Coast’s public-safety resources link to Flagler County’s File of Life program for medications, allergies, and emergency contacts.

Use the city’s safety service

The Palm Coast Fire Department provides free home safety inspections to Palm Coast residents. Renters should coordinate entry and scheduling with the property manager or owner first. An inspection can offer practical safety guidance, although the city notes that residents remain responsible for keeping their homes safe.

The same page gives the Palm Coast Fire Department’s non-emergency main line as 386-986-2300. Call 911 during an active emergency instead of using the administrative line.

Questions to ask before signing a lease

Questions about the home

  1. When were all smoke alarms last tested, and is the test documented?
  2. Are any alarms hardwired, interconnected, or equipped with backup batteries?
  3. Can the address be seen clearly from both travel directions at night?
  4. Do all bedrooms have a usable exit, and do the windows open without special tools?
  5. Who is responsible for maintaining alarms, extinguishers, gates, and exterior lighting?
  6. Does the lease restrict where vehicles, grills, propane cylinders, or e-bikes may be stored?

Questions about the street and neighborhood

  1. Is there more than one practical way out of the neighborhood?
  2. Do parked vehicles routinely narrow the street?
  3. Could a gate, bridge, canal, or construction zone complicate access?
  4. Is there a hydrant nearby, and is it unobstructed?
  5. Where is the household meeting point after an evacuation?
  6. Which route will you use during a hurricane evacuation or prolonged power outage?

These questions apply across the city. Keep them handy while reading the broader Palm Coast renter guides or comparing homes on the current rental page.

Why the station may not lower your insurance premium

Insurers consider several rating factors

The Florida Department of Financial Services explains that renters-insurance rates can reflect the property’s city, county, and ZIP code; the amount of coverage selected; the fire-protection class; construction type; building age; prior coverage; and company-specific credits or surcharges.

A fire department’s capability can be relevant, but a new station does not automatically change a territory’s protection class or every carrier’s rate. Even if an underlying classification changes, it may not affect rates immediately or produce the same quote from every insurer.

Ask for quotes for the exact address

Give the agent the rental’s full address, its construction type if known, the requested personal-property limit, the deductible, the liability limit, and information about any safety devices. Ask which credits are included and whether the quote covers replacement cost or actual cash value for your belongings.

Florida’s insurance guide says renters coverage generally protects personal property against covered perils and may cover events such as fire, smoke, theft, windstorm, or certain water losses. Contracts vary. Flood coverage may require an endorsement or a separate policy, and Station 22 does not change that distinction.

For your budget: Record the actual written premium in your comparison sheet. Do not estimate a discount just because one home seems closer to the station.

Apply a simple scoring method

Rate each rental from poor to strong for commute, emergency access, evacuation options, home safety, insurance cost, and total monthly housing cost. Then weight the categories for your household. Someone with a time-sensitive medical condition may place more weight on emergency access. A daily commuter may care more about the route to Interstate 95.

Do not give a home the highest safety score solely because it is near Station 22. A home farther away may be the stronger choice if it has clear access, visible numbering, maintained alarms, two usable exits, and a better-prepared household.

A practical renter checklist

Before the tour

  • Place the rental, Station 22, your workplace, and your regular destinations on one map.
  • Identify the neighborhood’s main exit and at least one alternative.
  • Request an insurance quote using the exact address.
  • Check Flagler County emergency information and register for ALERTFlagler.

During the tour

  • Test alarms with permission and inspect bedroom exits.
  • Read the street number from the road.
  • Look for obstacles involving the gate, driveway, tree limbs, or parking.
  • Ask who maintains safety devices and how emergency repairs are reported.
  • Check the electrical panel area, cooking area, garage, and outdoor grill location for obvious hazards.

Before signing

  • Put maintenance responsibilities in writing.
  • Save the property manager’s emergency-maintenance procedure.
  • Confirm your renters-insurance coverage and effective date.
  • Create a household escape plan and choose a meeting place.
  • Store lease, insurance, medication, and emergency-contact records somewhere accessible away from the home.

FAQ

Where is Palm Coast Fire Station 22?

The new station is at 350 Palm Coast Parkway NE, at Palm Coast Parkway and Colbert Lane in the Palm Harbor area.

Does living near Fire Station 22 guarantee a faster response?

No. Proximity may help, but response depends on unit availability, traffic, incident type, dispatch decisions, and access to the specific property.

Will Fire Station 22 lower my renters-insurance premium?

Not automatically. Florida insurance guidance says rates can reflect location, coverage amount, fire-protection class, construction, building age, prior coverage, and insurer-specific factors. Request a quote for the exact address.

What safety features should I check in a Palm Coast rental?

Test smoke and applicable carbon-monoxide alarms, make sure bedroom exits work, check address visibility and driveway or gate access, and ask who maintains alarms, extinguishers, and exterior lighting.

Can Palm Coast renters request a home safety inspection?

The Palm Coast Fire Department offers free home safety inspections to city residents. Renters should coordinate access with the property manager or owner before scheduling.

How should I compare a rental near Station 22 with one elsewhere?

Compare actual rent, written insurance quotes, work-trip routes, street access, evacuation alternatives, alarm and exit condition, address visibility, and your household’s medical or mobility needs.

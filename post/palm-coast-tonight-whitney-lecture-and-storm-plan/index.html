Palm Coast Tonight: Whitney Lecture and Storm Plan

The Evenings at Whitney lecture is scheduled for Thursday, September 10, 2026, at 6 p.m., but check the host’s event page before leaving in case weather changes the plan. Palm Coast has a chance of afternoon and evening thunderstorms. Allow extra travel time, have an indoor place to wait and secure your rental home before heading toward Marineland.

The National Hurricane Center’s September 10 Atlantic outlook says tropical cyclone formation is not expected during the next seven days. An ordinary Florida thunderstorm can still bring lightning and heavy rain.

What is happening at Whitney tonight

The lecture and its local connection

The University of Florida’s Whitney Laboratory for Marine Bioscience is presenting “Natural Products That Alter Sensory Perception and Their Use to Treat Chronic Pain.” According to the official September 10 lecture announcement, the speaker is Dr. Kevin J. Tidgewell, an assistant professor of pharmaceutical sciences at the University of Kentucky.

The subject brings together natural-products chemistry, neuroscience and the search for compounds with analgesic potential. Programs like this give Palm Coast and Flagler residents a chance to hear working scientists explain their research in plain language and answer questions in the same room.

The program is scheduled for 6 p.m. on Thursday, September 10, 2026, in Lohman Auditorium at the Whitney Laboratory, 9505 Ocean Shore Boulevard in St. Augustine. The official announcement says the lecture is free and includes a live Zoom option. The date, time, location and online registration link all come directly from the university’s event page.

Why “near Palm Coast” needs some context

The laboratory sits beside State Road A1A in the Marineland area, north of Palm Coast and outside the Palm Coast city limits. For renters coming from Indian Trails, the B Section, Pine Lakes or Lehigh Woods, this is an A1A-area evening outing rather than a meeting at Town Center.

The difference matters when rain is passing through. Weather near your home may not match the conditions along the oceanfront approach. The exposed coastal roadway can also feel quite different from Belle Terre Parkway or Palm Coast Parkway. Check the entire route, instead of relying only on the weather icon for your ZIP code.

Confirm these details before you leave

Use the host’s page as the final authority

Begin with the Whitney Laboratory event announcement. Confirm that the in-person program is still going ahead, review any arrival instructions and save the laboratory’s contact information. The announcement lists 904-461-4000 and wh-whitneylab@ufl.edu for questions.

Whitney’s lecture-series page says its public talks normally run from 6 to 7 p.m., with free admission and free parking. It also says reservations are generally unnecessary. A notice for a particular event can supersede the general series information, so check both pages instead of relying on an old calendar entry or social post.

A practical verification list

  • Reopen the official event page shortly before departure and check for a cancellation, postponement or online-only notice.
  • Confirm the 6 p.m. start and Lohman Auditorium location on the September 10 listing.
  • If you would rather avoid the drive in unsettled weather, use the registration link for the live Zoom option on the official announcement.
  • Save the event confirmation, map and host telephone number somewhere you can reach without searching while parked.
  • Check current radar and alerts as well as the morning forecast.
  • Tell someone at home whether you are attending in person or changing to the online program.

The registration details can be confusing. The in-person series normally does not require reservations, while the online option uses a registration link. Verify which format you plan to use before getting in the car.

Palm Coast’s September 10 weather window

Afternoon storms may overlap with departure

The Palm Coast forecast supplied for September 10 calls for a 50% chance of showers and thunderstorms after 1 p.m. Otherwise, daytime conditions are mostly sunny, with a high near 85°F, southeast wind from 1 to 10 mph and gusts as high as 16 mph. Possible rainfall is listed between one-tenth and one-quarter inch.

Tonight’s forecast calls for a 40% chance of showers and thunderstorms before 10 p.m., followed by partly cloudy conditions and a low near 76°F. These percentages apply across the forecast area. They do not mean that rain will fall for half the afternoon or that every Palm Coast neighborhood will get a storm.

How to use those percentages

With a 6 p.m. event, the likely drive falls within the forecast thunderstorm period. Base the decision to go or wait on current radar, lightning information and official alerts near departure time. Sunshine outside your lanai does not prove that A1A is clear farther north.

Leave enough time for a passing cell so you do not have to hurry. If the safest choice makes you late, accept the delay or join online. A lecture is not worth driving through standing water, poor visibility or frequent lightning.

A storm-smart arrival plan

If thunder is audible, stay inside

The City of Palm Coast Emergency Management page states that if you can hear thunder, you are within striking distance of lightning. When thunder is audible, remain inside a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle.

Do not wait beneath a tree, an open pavilion or an overhang that leaves you exposed. An umbrella can keep rain off your clothes, but it is not a lightning shelter. If a storm is directly over the parking area when you arrive, stay in your vehicle in a legal space and follow any instructions from venue staff.

Driving through a heavy shower

  • Turn on your headlights, reduce your speed and leave more space between vehicles.
  • Avoid sudden lane changes on A1A, particularly where water is collecting.
  • Do not enter a flooded roadway or drive around a closure or barricade.
  • If visibility becomes poor, pull into a safe parking area away from trees and traffic lanes.
  • Keep your phone available for alerts, but do not handle it while driving.

If lightning or heavy rain stops your trip, contact the venue from a safe parked location. The online option on the official event page provides a practical fallback. Return home or stop somewhere safe and join remotely instead of trying to outrun the weather.

Choose your decision points in advance

Before backing out of the driveway, decide what conditions would make you postpone departure. Those conditions might include an active warning covering your route, frequent lightning near Marineland, water across a road or visibility too poor for comfortable driving.

Set another checkpoint for arrival. If you hear thunder after parking, remain in your vehicle and reassess. Deciding on these rules ahead of time can keep you from continuing simply because you have already left home.

Thunderstorms and tropical weather are different risks

What the NHC outlook tells us

The National Hurricane Center’s Atlantic Tropical Weather Outlook, issued on the morning of September 10, says tropical cyclone formation is not expected during the next seven days across the North Atlantic, Caribbean Sea and Gulf of America. That is reassuring regional information for hurricane-season planning.

The outlook does not clear tonight’s drive of weather risks. A local thunderstorm can bring lightning, intense rainfall, gusty wind and ponding on roads without any connection to a tropical cyclone. Use the NHC outlook for tropical conditions and local weather alerts when making an immediate decision.

Keep hurricane-season habits in place

September falls within Atlantic hurricane season, so renters should keep their usual preparedness system ready even on a quiet tropical-weather day. Palm Coast’s emergency page links residents to its disaster-preparedness guide and ALERTFlagler, a no-cost notification service that includes severe-weather alerts.

Review those resources before an emergency begins. Know where you keep medications, identification, lease and insurance records. If your rental has a Pretreatment Effluent Pumping tank, Palm Coast advises limiting water use during a power outage and heavy rain. Because the system is electrically powered, those conditions can otherwise create a risk of wastewater backup.

Prepare your rental for an evening away

A five-minute departure check

  1. Close and latch windows. Check the bedrooms, bathrooms, kitchen and any sliding doors that open to a patio or lanai.
  2. Bring in lightweight outdoor items. Cushions, empty planters, toys and small decorations can blow around during an ordinary thunderstorm.
  3. Protect electronics. Save your active work, charge your phone and use surge protection where your lease and equipment instructions allow it.
  4. Check doors and drainage. Lock exterior doors and make sure nothing is blocking a patio drain.
  5. Leave alerts enabled. Keep your phone charged, audible and set to receive emergency notifications.
  6. Plan for pets. Bring animals inside and provide a storm-sensitive pet with a secure interior resting place.

This is a checklist for an ordinary evening. It is not permission to install shutters, climb onto a roof or make exterior alterations. Under your lease, those jobs may be the responsibility of the owner, association or property manager. Report a loose screen, roof leak or drainage problem through the usual maintenance channel instead of attempting a risky repair.

Use the same standard for any rental type

The checklist applies to a duplex, townhouse or detached home. If you are comparing Palm Coast rentals, ask during the tour where outdoor items can be secured and how storm-related maintenance should be reported. A local three-bedroom layout, such as this Palm Coast example, can remind you to inspect every bedroom window, exterior door and patio area instead of stopping at the front room.

Renters should also know who receives an after-hours report of water intrusion. Save that contact before leaving. A small leak is easier to document and address when it is reported promptly than after a long delay.

What to carry and what to leave behind

Pack for a short walk in uncertain weather

  • A light rain layer that leaves both hands free.
  • An umbrella for rain only, never for lightning protection.
  • A small dry bag or sealed pouch for your phone, keys and event information.
  • Water for the drive and any weather-related wait.
  • The saved event page, venue contact and Zoom confirmation if you registered online.
  • Closed, slip-resistant shoes that are suitable for a wet parking surface.

Leave loose papers and anything you do not want exposed to humidity at home. A large bag may also make it harder to get into the building efficiently in the rain.

A simple decision rule

If the route is merely wet, slow down and allow time. If thunder is audible, lightning is frequent, visibility is poor or water covers the road, wait indoors or use the online option.

This conservative rule separates an inconvenience from a danger and gives you an alternative if conditions change quickly.

Local resources for renters

Save these before the next storm

Bookmark Palm Coast’s emergency page and consider enrolling in ALERTFlagler through the link provided there. Keep your property manager’s maintenance procedure with your insurance contact and lease. For neighborhood-specific guidance, the Palm Coast renter resource page has more information about local roads, utilities and seasonal routines.

If an immediate emergency threatens life or safety, call emergency services. For a non-emergency rental problem, follow the reporting method in your lease so the time, condition and photographs are documented.

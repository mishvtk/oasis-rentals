Setting up utilities in Palm Coast: city water and sewer, FPL, and the trash pickup schedule by section

Moving into a home or apartment in Palm Coast means setting up several essential services before or shortly after move-in day. The main utilities most renters and homeowners need to understand are city water and sewer, electricity through Florida Power & Light (FPL), and residential trash collection.

The good news is that Palm Coast makes it possible to manage water, sewer, and trash through the city utility system, while electricity is established separately with FPL. Understanding how each service works can help you avoid delays, unexpected charges, or missed trash collections.


Palm Coast Water and Sewer: What Renters Need to Know Before Moving In

When moving into a rental property in Palm Coast, Florida, setting up utilities should be one of the first items on your move-in checklist. For properties served by the City of Palm Coast, the city's utility system provides water, wastewater services, and residential solid waste services.

The City of Palm Coast Customer Service portal allows residents to establish new utility service and also provides options for transferring or canceling service when moving.

Setting Up Palm Coast Water, Sewer, and Trash

Before establishing service, renters should have the new property's address and any required account information available. The city's online system can be used to start new utility service for water, sewer, and trash.

However, renters should first review their lease. Not every rental arrangement handles utilities in the same way. Your lease should clearly identify which services you are responsible for paying and whether the landlord keeps any utilities in their name.

The City of Palm Coast's utility billing operations cover the city's water and sewer utility, solid waste services, and stormwater management fees.

What Can Be Included on a Palm Coast Utility Bill?

Depending on the property and account, a monthly utility bill may include charges related to:

  • Water

  • Wastewater/sewer

  • Residential solid waste or trash

  • Stormwater or other applicable municipal utility charges

Palm Coast publishes its current utility rates online, including separate rate information for water, wastewater, and reclaimed water services.

One important point for renters is that sewer billing can be affected by overall water consumption. The city states that, since April 1, 2025, the previous 8,000-gallon sewer billing cap was removed. This means water passing through the property's main meter can be subject to sewer charges. If irrigation is connected to the main water meter, that usage can also affect sewer charges.

Online Utility Management

Palm Coast provides online tools that make it easier for residents to manage their accounts. Customers can pay utility bills, set up automatic payments, review previous bills, and access billing information through the city's online portal.

The city's utility resources also provide information about water consumption, conservation, billing questions, water quality, and service issues.

For renters, having online access can be particularly useful because it allows you to monitor your water usage and identify unusually high consumption. A sudden increase may be worth investigating for leaks or other problems.

Utility Deposits for Renters

A utility deposit can be another upfront expense when moving into a Palm Coast rental.

The City of Palm Coast states that residential deposits are held for 23 months. Customers with a good payment history, specifically no late fees within the previous two years, may request that the deposit be returned or applied to their utility bill, subject to the city's requirements.

If you move out, the city states that any remaining deposit is automatically applied to the final utility bill. If a credit remains after the final bill, a check can be mailed.

This makes the utility deposit an important item to include when calculating the cash needed to move into a Palm Coast rental.

Budget for Utilities Separately From Rent

When comparing rental properties in Palm Coast, don't look only at the advertised monthly rent.

Your actual monthly housing expenses may include:

  • Rent

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Electricity

  • Internet

  • Renter's insurance

  • Possible irrigation-related costs

  • Utility deposits or other account setup costs

Utility costs can vary depending on the size of the property, household water consumption, irrigation setup, and other factors.

What Renters Should Do Before Move-In

Before moving into a Palm Coast rental, consider taking these steps:

  1. Read your lease carefully. Confirm which utilities you are responsible for paying.

  2. Ask the landlord or property manager who currently provides each service.

  3. Confirm whether the property is served by the City of Palm Coast.

  4. Set up water, sewer, and trash service before your move-in date if required.

  5. Ask about any required utility deposit.

  6. Create your online utility account so you can manage payments and monitor bills.

  7. Check the property's irrigation setup, particularly whether irrigation uses the main water meter.

  8. Keep confirmation of your service start date for your records.

  9. Budget for the first utility bill separately from your rent and security deposit.

Setting up water, sewer, and trash is an important part of moving into a Palm Coast rental. Understanding your lease, knowing which utilities you are responsible for, and budgeting for potential deposits can help prevent unexpected expenses during the move.

The City of Palm Coast provides online resources for starting, transferring, and canceling utility service, managing bills, monitoring water use, and requesting assistance with utility-related questions.


Setting Up Electricity With FPL in Palm Coast

When moving into a rental property in Palm Coast, electricity is handled separately from the city's water, sewer, and trash services. If your new home receives electric service from Florida Power & Light (FPL), you will need to establish or transfer your electricity account directly with FPL.

Taking care of electricity before moving day can help ensure that the lights, air conditioning, appliances, and other essential systems are operating when you take possession of the property.

How to Start FPL Electricity Service

FPL provides an online process for customers who need to start, stop, or move electric service. New customers can use the service to establish electricity at a new address, while existing FPL customers can transfer service when moving from one property to another.

When requesting residential service, you should be prepared to provide information such as:

  • The complete service address

  • Your requested service start date

  • Identification information

  • A valid email address for your online account

  • Other information FPL may require to establish service

Because requirements can vary depending on the account and property, renters should check FPL's current instructions before submitting their service request.

When Should You Start FPL Service?

Ideally, arrange electricity before your lease begins or before you take possession of the property.

Don't wait until moving day if you can avoid it. Setting up service ahead of time gives you an opportunity to resolve account or identification issues before you arrive.

For example, if your lease begins on August 20, you should confirm with the landlord or property manager whether your electricity service should begin on August 20 or on another specified date.

Questions to Ask Before Setting Up Electricity

Before transferring electricity into your name, renters should confirm several details with the landlord or property manager.

1. What Is the Exact Move-In Date?

Your electric service start date should generally correspond with the date you become responsible for the property, according to the terms of your lease.

Make sure the date on your FPL account matches your lease and any instructions from the property manager.

2. Is Electricity Currently Active?

Ask whether electricity is already connected at the property.

In some cases, electricity may remain active between tenants. In other situations, the previous tenant may have already scheduled a disconnect.

Knowing the current status can help prevent confusion about when your account needs to begin.

3. Do You Need to Provide Proof of Service?

Some landlords or property managers may ask tenants to provide confirmation that electricity has been transferred into the tenant's name.

If this is a requirement of your rental, keep your FPL confirmation and account information after completing the setup.

4. Is an Electric Deposit Required?

Ask FPL whether a deposit or other payment is required for your specific account.

The amount and requirements can depend on individual circumstances, so renters should confirm the current terms directly with FPL rather than assuming that every new account will have the same requirements.

A possible utility deposit is another reason to include electricity setup costs in your move-in budget.

Moving From Another Home?

If you are already an FPL customer and are moving to another property, FPL provides a Move Service option.

This can make it easier to coordinate the end of service at your current home with the start of service at your new Palm Coast rental.

Before moving, confirm:

  • The address where service is currently active

  • Your old property's stop date

  • Your new property's start date

  • The new service address

  • Any required account or identification information

  • Whether you need to provide additional information for the new property

Carefully checking the dates can help prevent accidentally ending electricity too early at your current home or starting service too late at your new rental.

Check for Pool and Irrigation Equipment

Palm Coast has many homes with features that can affect electricity consumption. Before moving into a house, ask whether the property has equipment such as:

  • Pool pumps

  • Pool heaters

  • Irrigation pumps

  • Well pumps

  • Outdoor lighting

  • Electric water heaters

  • Other large electrical equipment

These systems can contribute to the property's electricity usage and may be important when estimating your monthly utility budget.

If the rental has a swimming pool or irrigation system, ask the landlord or property manager who is responsible for operating and maintaining the equipment.

Budget for Electricity Separately From Rent

Electricity can become one of the largest variable household expenses, particularly during Florida's hot weather when air conditioning may run frequently.

When calculating your total monthly rental cost in Palm Coast, consider budgeting for:

  • Monthly rent

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Internet

  • Renter's insurance

  • Possible utility deposits

  • Other property-specific expenses

The actual electricity cost will depend on the home's size, insulation, air-conditioning system, appliances, thermostat settings, occupancy, and other factors.

Final FPL Move-In Checklist

Before taking possession of your Palm Coast rental, make sure you:

  1. Confirm the exact lease and move-in date.

  2. Ask whether electricity is currently active.

  3. Confirm that FPL serves the property.

  4. Set up or transfer your FPL account.

  5. Select the appropriate electricity start date.

  6. Provide the required identification and account information.

  7. Ask whether a deposit is required.

  8. Save your confirmation after completing the request.

  9. Check whether the property has a pool, irrigation pump, or other major electrical equipment.

  10. Keep your FPL account information for future billing and service needs.

Setting up electricity should be treated as a separate step from establishing Palm Coast water, sewer, and trash service. If Florida Power & Light serves your rental, arrange your FPL service before you take possession of the property and make sure the start date matches your rental arrangements.

Planning ahead can help ensure that your new Palm Coast home has electricity when you arrive and can also help you avoid unexpected utility-related expenses during the move.


Palm Coast Trash Pickup: What Renters Need to Know About Garbage Collection

Trash collection is an important part of setting up utilities when moving into a Palm Coast rental. Unlike electricity, which is handled separately through the electric provider, residential garbage, recycling, yard waste, bulk trash, and certain appliance collections are part of the City's municipal solid-waste service.

The City of Palm Coast currently contracts with FCC Environmental Services for residential garbage collection, recycling, yard waste, bulk trash, and white-goods removal. FCC began providing residential collection services in Palm Coast on June 1, 2023.

How Often Is Trash Collected in Palm Coast?

Palm Coast residents generally have two garbage collection days each week.

The city's current service structure provides:

  • First collection day: Regular household garbage

  • Second collection day: Regular household garbage plus eligible bulk items

  • Recycling: Collected weekly

  • Yard waste: Collected weekly, currently on Wednesdays

  • White goods: Collected separately with the required arrangements

The city notes that collection routes can operate anytime between 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. on the scheduled service day. Residents should have their items ready before 6 a.m.

Trash Pickup by Palm Coast Section

Palm Coast is made up of numerous neighborhoods and community sections, including:

  • Indian Trails

  • Pine Lakes

  • Palm Harbor

  • Cypress Knoll

  • Quail Hollow

  • Seminole Woods

  • Matanzas Woods

  • Lehigh Woods

  • Belle Terre

  • Pine Grove

  • Woodlands

  • Palm Coast Plantation

  • Grand Haven

  • Hammock Beach

  • Ocean Hammock

  • Tidelands

  • Toscana

  • The Conservatory

  • The Hammock

  • Matanzas Shores

  • Other designated Palm Coast communities

However, renters should not assume that every property in the same neighborhood has the same trash collection days.

The City of Palm Coast provides an address or street-specific schedule lookup. The city's garbage collection page allows residents to search by street name to find the property's scheduled pickup days.

This is especially important when moving into a new rental because collection routes and schedules can change.

Use the Palm Coast Trash Widget

Palm Coast also provides a Trash Widget through Palm Coast Connect. After connecting your property or account, the widget can show your scheduled pickup days and holiday trash schedule.

This can be a convenient way for renters to keep track of their collection schedule without relying on memory.

What About Trash Cans?

Residents are responsible for purchasing their own garbage receptacles.

The city's current garbage collection information states that trash containers should generally:

  • Hold between 10 and 35 gallons

  • Not exceed 60 pounds when collected

  • Be limited to four receptacles on a service day

  • Have bagged items placed inside the cans

Renters should check with their landlord or property manager before purchasing containers if the rental property already has trash cans available.

Bulk Trash Pickup

Bulk collection is particularly useful when moving into or out of a Palm Coast home.

Eligible bulk items can include things such as:

  • Furniture

  • Mattresses

  • Televisions

  • Dishwashers

  • Stoves

  • Washers and dryers

Bulk items are generally collected on the second scheduled trash day of the week, with a limit of up to 3 cubic yards. The city says no separate case is needed for eligible bulk pickup within the applicable limits.

Construction and demolition materials are not considered normal bulk trash and are not picked up by FCC under the city's regular bulk service.

Recycling Collection

Palm Coast's residential recycling program uses a single-stream system and is collected once per week.

Residents should rinse recyclable containers such as cans and bottles to help prevent contamination. The city also provides a process for residents to request a replacement recycling bin or an initial bin if they are new to Palm Coast.

For renters, it is a good idea to check whether the property already has a recycling container when completing your move-in inspection.

Yard Waste Collection

Yard waste has its own collection requirements.

The City of Palm Coast currently lists Wednesday as the regular yard-waste collection day. Yard waste must meet size and weight requirements, including limits on the size of loose piles, branches, and individual pieces.

If you are renting a house with a lawn, ask the landlord or property manager who is responsible for landscaping and yard-waste preparation.

The city also notes that waste generated by a landscaping contractor, tree service, or similar contractor must be removed by the contractor that generated it.

White-Goods Pickup

Large household appliances, sometimes called white goods, have a separate collection process.

Examples include:

  • Refrigerators

  • Freezers

  • Washers

  • Dryers

  • Water heaters

For refrigerators and freezers, the city requires the doors to be removed for safety. Residents should also allow several days for collection after making the appropriate request.

When Should You Put Trash Out?

Palm Coast's garbage information states that containers should be placed in the collection area before 6 a.m. on the scheduled collection day.

The city also has rules concerning when containers can be placed at the curb. Under Palm Coast Code Chapter 41, waste containers generally may be placed out no earlier than 6 p.m. the evening before collection and should be removed no later than 6 a.m. the day after they are emptied.

For renters, following these rules can help prevent missed pickups and avoid leaving trash containers at the curb longer than necessary.

Where Should Trash Be Placed?

The city states that collection items should be placed in the designated collection area, generally within five feet of the driveway.

Before moving in, check the property to determine where the previous occupants placed garbage and recycling containers. If you are uncertain, ask your landlord or property manager.

What If Your Trash Is Missed?

If your garbage was not collected, Palm Coast provides an online service request system through Palm Coast Connect.

Before reporting a missed pickup, make sure the garbage was placed at the curb by the required time. The city states that garbage needs to be out by 6 a.m. on the service day.

Palm Coast Connect can also be used for issues involving recycling, yard waste, bulk pickup, and other garbage-related concerns.

How Trash Costs Appear on Your Utility Bill

Residential solid-waste service is part of Palm Coast's municipal utility system. The city's FCC contract includes garbage, recycling, yard waste, bulk trash, and white-goods removal.

For renters, the lease should specify whether the tenant or landlord is responsible for the municipal utility bill. If you are responsible for utilities, confirm whether the residential solid-waste charge will appear on your Palm Coast utility statement.

Final Trash Pickup Checklist for Palm Coast Renters

Before moving into a Palm Coast rental, make sure you:

  1. Confirm who is responsible for the trash portion of the utility bill.

  2. Check your property's exact garbage collection schedule.

  3. Connect your property to Palm Coast Connect if you want to use the Trash Widget.

  4. Confirm your two weekly garbage collection days.

  5. Check your weekly recycling day.

  6. Remember that yard waste is currently collected on Wednesdays.

  7. Learn the rules for bulk items before placing large furniture at the curb.

  8. Check whether the rental already has garbage and recycling containers.

  9. Put trash out before 6 a.m. on your collection day.

  10. Follow the city's rules for when containers can be placed and removed.

  11. Contact Palm Coast Connect if you have a missed pickup or collection problem.

Understanding Palm Coast's trash schedule is an important part of planning your move and managing your monthly utility expenses. The city provides twice-weekly household garbage collection, weekly recycling and yard-waste services, and regular bulk collection for eligible items.

Because collection schedules are assigned by route, renters should always check the specific address or street rather than assuming that every home in a particular Palm Coast neighborhood has the same pickup days.

Using the city's schedule lookup and Trash Widget can make it easier to keep track of garbage, recycling, and holiday collection changes.


A Simple Utility Setup Timeline for Palm Coast Renters

Moving into a new rental home in Palm Coast involves more than signing a lease and picking up the keys. Renters may also need to arrange water, sewer, electricity, trash, and recycling services before moving day.

Creating a simple utility timeline can make the process easier and help prevent surprises during the first month. It also gives you a record of when your responsibility for each utility begins and ends.

Before Moving In

The best time to organize utilities is before you take possession of the property.

Start by reviewing your lease and confirming which utilities you are responsible for. Some landlords may include certain services in the rent, while other rental agreements require the tenant to establish and pay for utilities directly.

Contact the City of Palm Coast

If the property is served by the City of Palm Coast and you are responsible for municipal utilities, contact the city to:

  • Establish water and sewer service if required.

  • Confirm your utility account start date.

  • Ask whether a utility deposit applies.

  • Confirm which municipal utility services will be included on your account.

  • Confirm that residential trash service is associated with the property.

  • Ask how to access your online utility account.

The City of Palm Coast provides online resources for establishing, transferring, and canceling utility service. The city also provides information about utility billing, water, wastewater, solid waste, and other municipal services.

Before completing the setup, make sure the service start date matches the date specified in your lease or by your property manager.

Contact FPL

Electricity is separate from the City's water and sewer account. If the property receives electric service from Florida Power & Light, arrange service directly with FPL.

Before moving in:

  • Start or transfer electric service.

  • Choose the correct service start date.

  • Provide the identification and account information requested by FPL.

  • Confirm whether a deposit or other payment is required.

  • Save your service confirmation.

If you are transferring electricity from another home, coordinate the stop date at your old address with the start date at your Palm Coast property.

On Move-In Day

Once you receive the keys, take a few minutes to verify that your essential utilities are working properly.

Check the Water

Turn on several faucets and confirm that water is running normally.

Also check for obvious problems such as:

  • No water pressure

  • Leaking faucets

  • Running toilets

  • Water around appliances

  • Unusual water discoloration

  • Signs of leaks under sinks

If you notice a problem, report it promptly to the landlord or property manager and document the issue with photographs.

Check the Electricity

Confirm that electricity is active throughout the home.

Check:

  • Interior lights

  • Air conditioning

  • Major appliances

  • Electrical outlets

  • Garage equipment

  • Pool equipment, if applicable

  • Irrigation equipment, if applicable

You should also confirm that the electric account is in the correct name and that the service start date corresponds with your rental agreement.

Check Trash and Recycling

Make sure the property has the appropriate garbage and recycling containers.

Palm Coast's residential collection schedule can vary by service route, so don't assume that every property in the same neighborhood has identical pickup days.

Check your property's address-specific schedule and save the information for future reference.

The City of Palm Coast also provides online tools that can help residents access garbage collection information and holiday schedule changes.

Why Utility Records Matter When You Move Out

Utility records are not only useful when moving in. They can become especially important when you eventually move out.

When your lease ends, you may need to demonstrate the date your responsibility for utilities ended. Keeping your original service confirmation and final cancellation or transfer confirmation can provide a useful record.

For example, keep documentation showing:

Old property: Utility service start date → monthly bills → final service date

New property: Utility service start date → monthly bills → eventual final service date

This creates a clear timeline of your utility responsibility.

What Palm Coast Renters Should Budget For Beyond Monthly Rent

When comparing rental properties in Palm Coast, Florida, it is important to look beyond the advertised monthly rent. Two homes with similar rents can have very different total monthly costs once utilities, insurance, and other property-related expenses are added.

Understanding these additional costs before signing a lease can help renters create a more realistic housing budget and avoid unexpected expenses after moving in.

Your Total Housing Cost Is More Than Rent

The advertised rent is usually only one part of your monthly housing expenses.

Depending on the rental agreement and property, your budget may need to include:

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Trash

  • Electricity

  • Internet

  • Renters insurance

  • Optional irrigation-related costs

  • HOA or community fees, if the lease passes them to the tenant

  • Utility deposits or connection charges, if applicable

Not every renter will pay all of these expenses directly. Some landlords include certain utilities in the rent, while others require tenants to establish their own accounts.

Always review the lease carefully to determine which expenses are your responsibility.

Water and Sewer Costs

For properties served by the City of Palm Coast, water and sewer are part of the city's municipal utility system.

Your monthly utility bill can include water, wastewater/sewer, residential solid waste, and other applicable municipal charges.

Water consumption can vary considerably from one household to another. A larger household may naturally use more water, while outdoor irrigation can also increase consumption.

Palm Coast has also changed its sewer billing structure. The city states that the previous 8,000-gallon sewer billing cap was removed beginning April 1, 2025. This means renters should pay attention to water consumption, particularly at properties where irrigation is connected to the main water meter.

When comparing homes, ask whether irrigation is included and how the system is metered.

Electricity Can Be a Major Variable Expense

Electricity is another important cost to consider when comparing Palm Coast rentals.

Monthly electric bills can fluctuate depending on factors such as:

  • Home size

  • Number of occupants

  • Air-conditioning usage

  • Thermostat settings

  • Insulation

  • Age and efficiency of the HVAC system

  • Appliances

  • Pool equipment

  • Irrigation pumps

  • Seasonal temperatures

A smaller apartment and a larger single-family home may have very different electricity costs even if their monthly rents are similar.

For homes with pools, large yards, older air-conditioning systems, or other energy-intensive equipment, ask additional questions before signing the lease.

Ask About Recent Utility Bills

One of the most useful questions a prospective renter can ask is whether the landlord or property manager can provide recent utility information.

If available, ask about previous bills for:

  • Electricity

  • Water

  • Sewer

  • Irrigation

  • Other recurring property-related utility costs

Actual bills from the property can provide a better indication of potential expenses than a general estimate.

Keep in mind that previous bills are not guarantees of future costs. Utility usage depends on the occupants, weather, thermostat settings, water consumption, and other factors.

However, recent billing history can still help you compare two properties more accurately.

Don't Forget Internet

Internet service is another expense that is often left out of the initial rental calculation.

Before choosing a property, check:

  • Which internet providers serve the address

  • Available service speeds

  • Monthly pricing

  • Installation or equipment fees

  • Whether the property already has equipment

  • Whether the landlord or HOA has any restrictions

This can be particularly important for people who work from home, stream entertainment, attend online classes, or need reliable connectivity.

Renters Insurance

Renters insurance is another expense to include in your housing budget.

A landlord's insurance policy generally protects the building, not the tenant's personal belongings. Renters insurance can provide coverage for eligible personal property and may also include liability coverage, depending on the policy.

Your lease may require proof of renters insurance before or shortly after move-in.

Because insurance costs vary by location, coverage, deductible, insurer, and other factors, obtain an actual quote for the property you are considering rather than assuming a fixed monthly amount.

Irrigation Costs

Some Palm Coast homes have irrigation systems, particularly single-family properties with larger yards.

Ask:

  • Is irrigation included in the lease?

  • Who is responsible for the irrigation bill?

  • Is irrigation connected to the property's main water meter?

  • Who pays for irrigation repairs?

  • Is there a separate irrigation meter?

  • Is the system operated automatically?

These details can affect both water usage and your overall monthly housing expenses.

HOA or Community Fees

Some Palm Coast rental properties are located in planned communities or neighborhoods with homeowners' associations.

In many rental arrangements, the landlord pays HOA fees. However, a lease may pass certain community-related charges to the tenant.

Before signing, ask whether you are responsible for:

  • HOA fees

  • Community amenity fees

  • Application fees

  • Parking fees

  • Gate or access-related charges

  • Other recurring community expenses

Make sure any tenant-paid fees are clearly identified in the lease.

Utility Deposits and Connection Charges

Moving into a rental can also require upfront utility expenses.

Depending on the provider and your account, you may encounter:

  • Utility deposits

  • Connection fees

  • Account setup charges

  • Required advance payments

These costs are different from your recurring monthly bills and should be included in your initial move-in budget.

For example, you may need enough money to cover your security deposit, first month's rent, renters insurance, and utility deposits at approximately the same time.

Questions to Ask Before Signing a Lease

Before committing to a Palm Coast rental, consider asking the landlord or property manager:

  1. Which utilities are included in the rent?

  2. Which utilities must the tenant put in their own name?

  3. What company provides electricity?

  4. Is the property served by City of Palm Coast water and sewer?

  5. Who pays for trash service?

When budgeting for a Palm Coast rental, the monthly rent should be your starting point, not your final number.

Water, sewer, trash, electricity, internet, renters insurance, irrigation, community fees, and utility deposits can all affect the real cost of renting a home.

Electricity and water expenses can also vary significantly from property to property. For that reason, prospective renters should ask for recent utility information whenever possible and consider the home's size, air-conditioning system, irrigation setup, pool equipment, and other features.

A rental that costs slightly more in monthly rent may ultimately be more affordable if its utility costs are lower. Looking at the total monthly housing cost rather than rent alone can help Palm Coast renters make a more informed decision.


What If You Have a Problem With Your Utility Service in Palm Coast?

Utility problems can be frustrating, especially when you have just moved into a new rental. A missed garbage pickup, billing question, water problem, or service issue can quickly become an inconvenience if you do not know where to report it.

For Palm Coast renters, the first step is to identify which provider is responsible for the problem. City water, sewer, and solid-waste services are handled through the City of Palm Coast, while electricity is provided separately through the electric utility serving the property, such as Florida Power & Light.

Understanding how to report common utility problems can help you resolve issues more efficiently and keep a record of your request.

Palm Coast Connect for City Utility Problems

The City of Palm Coast provides Palm Coast Connect, an online customer-service system that residents can use to report and track various city service issues.

Depending on the problem, residents can use the system for matters involving:

  • Utility billing

  • Water service

  • Sewer service

  • Garbage collection

  • Recycling collection

  • Yard waste

  • Bulk waste

  • Other city services

If you have a problem with a city utility, submitting a service request can create a record of the issue and make it easier to follow up if the problem is not resolved.

What to Do If Your Garbage Was Not Collected

A missed garbage pickup does not necessarily mean the collection company failed to service your property. Before reporting the problem, check whether the trash was prepared according to the city's collection requirements.

Confirm that:

1. The Trash Was Out Before 6:00 a.m.

Palm Coast's garbage collection information states that residential waste should be placed at the collection location before 6:00 a.m. on the scheduled service day.

If the container was placed out after the collection vehicle had already passed, the missed pickup may not qualify for a return collection.

2. It Was in the Correct Collection Area

Make sure the garbage container was placed in the property's designated collection area.

The city generally requires containers and collection materials to be positioned appropriately for collection, including placement near the driveway or designated service location.

3. It Was the Correct Collection Day

Palm Coast has address-specific collection routes.

Don't assume that a neighboring property has the same collection schedule simply because it is located in the same neighborhood.

Check your property's garbage schedule using the city's online collection information or Trash Widget.

4. The Materials Met Collection Requirements

Make sure the items placed out for collection were eligible for the type of service scheduled that day.

Different rules apply to:

  • Household garbage

  • Recycling

  • Yard waste

  • Bulk items

  • Large appliances or white goods

Items that do not meet collection requirements may not be collected.

What If Everything Was Correct?

If your garbage or recycling was properly prepared, placed in the correct location, and put out on the correct day before the required time, but it was still missed, you can report the problem through Palm Coast's customer-service system.

When submitting a request, provide as much information as possible, including:

  • Property address

  • Type of collection that was missed

  • Scheduled collection date

  • Description of the items

  • Any relevant photographs

  • Other information requested by the city

Keeping a copy or confirmation of the service request can be useful if you need to follow up.

Water or Sewer Problems

Water and sewer issues should also be reported promptly.

If you experience problems such as:

  • No water

  • Low water pressure

  • Suspected leaks

  • Sewer-related problems

  • Unexpected utility charges

  • Questions about your water bill

start by determining whether the issue is related to your property or the municipal service.

Renters should also notify the landlord or property manager when a problem involves plumbing, fixtures, appliances, irrigation equipment, or other parts of the rental property that may be the landlord's responsibility.

For example, if you discover a leaking toilet that is causing unusually high water consumption, notify the property manager promptly rather than waiting for the next utility bill.

Utility Billing Problems

If your utility bill seems unusually high, review the statement and compare it with previous billing periods.

A higher bill could be related to:

  • Increased water consumption

  • Irrigation use

  • A plumbing leak

  • Seasonal changes

  • Increased household occupancy

  • Higher electricity usage

  • Other changes at the property

For a Palm Coast municipal utility bill, contact the city if you have questions about water, sewer, trash, or other charges appearing on the municipal account.

If the issue concerns electricity, contact your electric provider separately.

Keep Your Records

Whenever you report a utility problem, keep records of the communication.

Save:

  • Service request numbers

  • Confirmation emails

  • Screenshots

  • Utility bills

  • Photographs

  • Dates and times of reported problems

  • Names or departments contacted

  • Communications with your landlord or property manager

This documentation can be especially helpful if the issue affects your bill or continues for an extended period.

What Renters Should Do When Reporting a Problem

A simple process can help keep utility problems organized:

Step 1: Identify the service involved.

Step 2: Check whether the problem is the city's responsibility, the utility provider's responsibility, or the landlord's responsibility.

Step 3: Check your lease for maintenance and utility responsibilities.

Step 4: Document the problem with photographs or written notes.

Step 5: Report the problem to the appropriate provider or property manager.

Step 6: Save the confirmation or case number.

Step 7: Follow up if the problem is not resolved.

This approach creates a clear record of what happened and when you reported it.

Electricity Problems Are Handled Separately

Remember that Palm Coast's municipal utility services and electricity are separate.

If the problem involves electricity, such as an outage or account issue, contact the electric provider serving your property.

If your electricity is provided by Florida Power & Light, FPL provides its own customer-service and outage-reporting resources.

Renters should also notify their landlord or property manager when an electrical problem appears to involve the home's wiring, outlets, appliances, air-conditioning system, or other property equipment.

Setting up utilities should be one of the first tasks on your Palm Coast moving checklist. City water, sewer, and solid-waste services are handled through the City of Palm Coast, while electricity is established separately through the electric provider serving your property.

The most important thing to remember about trash service is that collection schedules are address-specific. Palm Coast provides online tools for checking your property's pickup information, and residents should have collection materials ready before 6:00 a.m. on the scheduled collection day.

If a collection is missed, first confirm that the container was placed out on time, in the correct location, on the correct collection day, and that the material met the city's requirements.

By arranging water, sewer, electricity, and trash service before moving in, and by knowing how to report problems when they occur, renters can reduce unnecessary interruptions and maintain better records throughout their tenancy.

Just as importantly, understanding your utility responsibilities can help you calculate the true monthly cost of renting a Palm Coast home.

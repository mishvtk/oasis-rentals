Palm Coast Planning Board Reset: What Renters Should Watch

Palm Coast’s reconstituted planning board will review development proposals that can shape where housing is built, how traffic enters a site, and what infrastructure comes with that growth. The five appointments do not approve construction or cause rents to rise or fall. Still, the board’s votes are worth following because they are an early, public checkpoint for projects that may eventually affect rental choices and daily life.

Renters should follow proposals near a current or prospective home from application through final approval, permitting, construction, and leasing. Each is a separate stage. A favorable planning vote does not promise that apartments, townhomes, shops, roads, or advertised amenities will appear on a particular schedule.

What changed on the Palm Coast planning board

Four regular members and one alternate were appointed

The Palm Coast City Council appointed four regular members and one alternate after an unusual five-seat turnover, according to a September 21, 2026, report from FlaglerLive. The new regular members are Jeff Adams, Dawid Blaszkiewicz, Andrew Dodzik, and Adolphus Evans. Tyler Warner was appointed as an alternate.

Regular members hold voting seats on the seven-member Planning and Land Development Regulation Board. An alternate participates when a regular member cannot attend, which helps the board maintain enough members to conduct its work.

Why so many seats opened together

The turnover went well beyond replacing a single departing volunteer. Charles Lemon had resigned for health reasons, while Suzanne Nicholson and Dave Ferguson left as their terms were ending. Hung Hilton’s term also ended, and alternate Larry Gross completed his term. That left four regular seats and one alternate position open at roughly the same time.

The newcomers join Chair Sandra Shank, James Albano, and Dana Mark Stancel as regular members. Gary Decker remains an alternate. According to the same appointment report and the city’s development-review rules, board members serve four-year terms and may serve no more than two consecutive terms.

Experience changes, but the rules do not

The incoming members have backgrounds in construction, engineering, public works, housing, and financial analysis. Their experience may shape the questions they ask. Each application, however, must still be measured against Palm Coast’s Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, staff analysis, and evidence presented during the public process.

It is too early to describe the new board as favorable or unfavorable to growth. How its members handle specific applications will tell renters more than the appointments alone.

What the board actually does

It is an early public checkpoint

The board generally meets monthly to consider land-development regulations, rezonings, comprehensive-plan questions, and development applications. It often sends recommendations to the City Council. The council retains final authority over many major land-use decisions and can hear appeals from certain board decisions.

Some applications can end at the board level. FlaglerLive reports that the board can make final decisions on commercial projects below 100,000 square feet. Palm Coast’s official technical material also names the board as the approval authority for certain subdivision master plans involving more than 100 residential units. The agenda for each case should state whether the requested action is a recommendation or a final decision.

Its review reaches beyond the building footprint

Palm Coast’s Land Development Code covers subdivisions, land and water use, flood-prone property, environmentally sensitive land, landscaping, signs, stormwater management, and public-facility impacts. A planning board packet can therefore be useful even to a renter with no interest in zoning terminology.

Site plans and staff reports may show proposed entrances, internal roads, drainage areas, buffers, sidewalks, parking, or connections to public utilities. Those details may have more bearing on your routine than the project’s marketing name.

A planning vote is not a building permit

A board recommendation may still need City Council action. After a land-use approval, a project may go through engineering review, utility coordination, construction planning, permitting, financing, and contractor scheduling. Completed units must then be marketed and leased.

When a project is described as “approved,” check what was approved and which body approved it. A recommendation to rezone land differs from final site-plan approval. Neither means bulldozers will arrive the following morning.

Which proposals should renters watch?

Housing and mixed-use applications

Start with proposals for apartments, duplexes, townhomes, build-to-rent houses, subdivisions, or residential space above or beside shops. Palm Coast’s official Planning Projects map lets residents see how proposals relate geographically to established areas such as Town Center, Lehigh Woods, Seminole Woods, Indian Trails, Pine Lakes, and Palm Harbor.

Mixed-use and master-planned applications warrant a close look because they can combine homes with retail, offices, recreation, and new street connections. Read the application itself before assuming every component in a concept plan has final approval.

Projects near the roads you use every day

Watch development around Palm Coast Parkway, Belle Terre Parkway, State Road 100, Seminole Woods Boulevard, Matanzas Woods Parkway, Old Kings Road, and U.S. 1. A proposal does not automatically create a traffic problem. Its transportation materials may, however, identify access points, turn lanes, intersection work, sidewalks, or other conditions that could affect a commute.

If you are considering a home in the city’s lettered sections, look beyond straight-line distance. A nearby development accessed from another collector road may have little effect on your route. A more distant project using your usual intersection could be more noticeable.

Services and amenities that change a neighborhood routine

Commercial, medical, school, recreation, and utility-related proposals can affect renters without adding housing. A new service may shorten an errand. Construction around an entrance could temporarily make access more difficult.

The city’s map helps with location, while the meeting packet provides the detail. Search for references to water and sewer, stormwater, emergency access, landscape buffers, pedestrian connections, and road improvements. These are useful points to compare with promises made in a rental advertisement.

How development may affect rental supply

More approved homes can expand choices, eventually

An approved residential project can add to the local housing inventory if it is financed, permitted, built, and offered for rent. The housing types may suit different households, ranging from apartments for one-person households to townhomes or detached houses for renters who need bedrooms, a garage, or outdoor space.

That possibility is not a rent forecast. Asking rents depend on the number and type of completed units, household demand, operating expenses, concessions, location, and competition from existing homes. The planning board does not set private rents.

Count stages, not announcements

Use this development sequence when evaluating a headline:

  1. Application: A developer asks the city to review a proposal.
  2. Public review: Staff and the appropriate board examine the request.
  3. Final land-use decision: The authorized board or City Council approves, denies, or conditions the application.
  4. Engineering and permits: Detailed plans must satisfy applicable requirements.
  5. Construction: Site work and vertical building begin.
  6. Completion and leasing: Finished homes enter the usable rental supply.

A project can change, pause, or stop between any two stages. When weighing future options, give more weight to visible construction and issued permits than to an early conceptual presentation.

Compare the proposed homes with what you need

A large unit count will not matter equally to every renter. Check the bedroom mix, accessibility, parking, pet policies, expected lease length, and whether the proposal is for rental or for-sale housing. To compare those plans with homes currently being offered, browse Palm Coast information in the local rental guide and the broader property listings.

If you need three bedrooms in the 32164 area, for example, compare any proposed development with an existing option such as Palm Coast Paradise Unit A. One property cannot predict a future project’s pricing. The useful comparison is among usable space, location, lease terms, and availability, since announced units are not interchangeable.

Construction can affect daily life before it adds anything

Temporary disruption usually arrives first

Site clearing, grading, utility installation, concrete deliveries, and building work can bring noise, dust, truck traffic, and temporary changes in access. Rainy periods can alter a contractor’s schedule, so an estimated completion date remains an estimate rather than a guarantee.

If you are looking at a rental beside undeveloped land, visit on a weekday morning and afternoon. Check for survey stakes, posted notices, construction fencing, cleared vegetation, heavy equipment, and temporary traffic-control signs.

Traffic effects depend on the location

Vehicle counts are only part of the traffic picture. Ask where vehicles will enter, which intersections will carry them, whether turn lanes or signals are proposed, and when road work would occur.

A renter commuting along Belle Terre Parkway may care more about an entrance configuration than architectural drawings. Someone near Town Center may be watching pedestrian connections and event-day circulation. A household near U.S. 1 may put more weight on safe highway access and the timing of off-site improvements.

Completed projects can change convenience

After construction, a project may provide homes, shops, medical offices, sidewalks, drainage improvements, or new road connections. It may also bring more activity to a previously quiet edge of a neighborhood.

Renters will weigh those changes differently. Some value shorter errands and more housing options. Others prefer low traffic and fewer nearby businesses. Project documents let you consider that tradeoff using the plans for the actual site.

How to follow a Palm Coast proposal

Use the map to identify the case

Open the Planning Projects map and zoom in on the neighborhood, road, or vacant parcel you care about. Record the project name, application or case number, applicant, project type, and status if displayed. Take a dated screenshot because project information can change as the review moves forward.

Match it to an agenda and packet

Next, open Palm Coast’s Agendas and Minutes page. It keeps separate records for the Planning and Land Development Regulation Board, City Council, and public notices. Open the meeting packet as well as the short agenda, then search for the project name or case number.

Read the staff recommendation, requested action, location map, proposed uses, access plan, and listed conditions. Check whether the board will make a recommendation or a final decision, and whether another public hearing is expected before the City Council.

Build a one-page tracking note

  • Project name and case number
  • Parcel location and closest major road
  • Requested zoning, land-use change, or development approval
  • Proposed housing and nonresidential uses
  • Planned entrances and off-site road work
  • Drainage, utility, sidewalk, and buffer commitments
  • Board vote and attached conditions
  • Next hearing, permit, or review stage

After the meeting, return to the city page for the minutes or video and verify the motion that passed. Conditions added during the discussion may matter more to a nearby renter than the headline vote.

Questions to ask before signing near a project

Ask the property manager or owner

  • Is construction active or expected on an adjoining parcel?
  • Has the owner received any notice about road, utility, drainage, or access work?
  • Could work affect the driveway, guest parking, mailbox, trash collection, or usual route?
  • What lease language covers access interruptions, noise, or changes outside the owner’s control?
  • Are any nearby amenities being advertised before final approval or completion?

Ask the city record

A verbal description of “future shops” or “a planned park” is not enough. Look for the approved plan, staff report, conditions, and hearing record. If the documents are unclear, use the contact or case-submission options on the city website and include the case number and parcel location.

Use a practical signing rule

If nearby construction would materially affect your sleep, commute, remote work, or children’s routine, do not sign until you know the project’s current stage, approved access points, and best available schedule.

No one can guarantee when a privately developed project will finish. You can still tell the difference between an early application and permitted construction, then decide how much uncertainty your household can accept.

Keep the board reset in perspective

Appointing five people changes the city’s governance. It does not create rental units, authorize a particular development, or determine a future asking rent.

For renters, the useful information will come from the public record as the new board reviews individual cases. Follow the map, read the packets, identify who makes the decision, and track any conditions through the later stages. That record is more useful than trying to predict a market outcome from the appointees’ résumés.

FAQ

Did Palm Coast appoint five regular planning board members?

No. The City Council appointed four regular members, Jeff Adams, Dawid Blaszkiewicz, Andrew Dodzik, and Adolphus Evans, plus Tyler Warner as an alternate.

Can the planning board approve construction by itself?

Its authority depends on the application. It recommends action to the City Council on many land-use matters and can make final decisions in certain cases. Development approval remains separate from detailed engineering, permits, construction, and leasing.

Will the new appointments make Palm Coast rents rise or fall?

The appointments alone do not determine rents. Future rental conditions will depend on which projects proceed, what housing is completed, demand, location, operating costs, and competition among available homes.

Where can renters find upcoming development votes?

Use Palm Coast’s Agendas and Minutes page, select the Planning and Land Development Regulation Board, and open the full meeting packet. Search by the project name or case number found on the city’s Planning Projects map.

What should I check before renting beside a proposed project?

Verify the proposal’s current stage, approved entrances, expected construction schedule, planned road or utility work, and whether advertised amenities have final approval. Check the public record instead of relying only on a verbal description.

Does an approved rezoning mean construction is about to start?

No. Rezoning is a land-use step. A project may still require other approvals, engineering review, permits, financing, contractor scheduling, construction, and final inspections before homes or businesses can open.

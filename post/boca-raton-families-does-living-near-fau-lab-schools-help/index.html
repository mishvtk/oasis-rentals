Boca Raton Families: Does Living Near FAU Lab Schools Help?

Living near FAU Lab Schools does not guarantee admission. A.D. Henderson University School uses a lottery-based admissions process instead of a conventional neighborhood attendance boundary. An invitation to continue the process does not guarantee enrollment.

The newly announced $10 million gift supports educational research, teacher development, facilities, equipment, and workforce initiatives. It does not add neighborhood preference, guarantee seats for nearby residents, or replace the published admissions process.

What the $10 million gift is intended to support

The named center and its broader mission

Florida Atlantic University announced the gift from the Carl Angus DeSantis Foundation on November 20, 2026. The money supports the Laboratory Schools’ Center for Educational Strategy and Innovation, which will be renamed the Carl A. DeSantis Center for Educational Strategy and Innovation, along with the Lab Schools’ research program. BocaNewsNow also covered the announcement locally.

According to FAU’s official announcement, the center will train teachers and school leaders, expand research-based teaching strategies, develop STEM and workforce preparation, and share successful practices with other schools. The university says the gift also supports student research pathways, laboratory equipment and facilities, and a healthcare workforce pipeline.

What the announcement does not say

The announcement does not say that the gift changes admissions rules, guarantees enrollment for children living near campus, or establishes a residential attendance zone around FAU. It does not announce a new transportation entitlement for nearby or distant students either.

Keep that distinction in mind when comparing rentals around Glades Road, Spanish River Boulevard, Military Trail, Boca Raton Boulevard, and Interstate 95. A shorter trip may make daily logistics easier, but families should not treat it as an admissions advantage unless the school publishes a policy saying so.

Why FAU Lab Schools are not a conventional zoned-school assignment

A separate public laboratory-school district

FAU Lab Schools include A.D. Henderson University School and Florida Atlantic University High School. A.D. Henderson describes itself as a public developmental laboratory school in the Florida Atlantic University Lab School District. Its mission includes researching, demonstrating, and evaluating educational methods. It does not simply serve every child in the surrounding neighborhood.

Its current admissions page says the school uses a lottery-based selection process. That process is designed to keep class sizes consistent with state guidelines and maintain the student-body diversity required of Florida laboratory schools. Openings are limited, and the school expressly warns that it cannot accommodate every applicant.

How an ordinary Boca Raton address works

For conventional Palm Beach County public schools, families can enter a home address in the district’s Find My School tool to identify the assigned elementary, middle, and high schools. The district registration page directs new families to use that result and contact the assigned school about registration documents.

That address-based process is separate from the FAU Lab Schools lottery. A lease near the FAU campus may put a child within the attendance boundary of a Palm Beach County school, but it does not make the Lab Schools the child’s zoned assignment.

Practical rule: Consider proximity to FAU Lab Schools a commute benefit only. Admission remains unresolved until the family receives and completes a final enrollment offer.

Admissions details to verify before signing a lease

Start with the published timeline

The current A.D. Henderson admissions page lists September 1 as the application opening date and December 1 as the deadline. The lottery is conducted in April and May, with decisions beginning in spring and continuing as seats become available. Dates can change between admission cycles, so check the page for the year your child would enter.

The same page says lottery selection and enrollment invitations depend on the family providing the required documents and the school reviewing them. It explicitly states that an invitation does not guarantee enrollment. Renters should keep every backup school plan in place after receiving a preliminary email.

Questions to send directly to admissions

Before making housing contingent on FAU Lab Schools, email coepk12@fau.edu, the address provided on the admissions page. Ask for clear, written answers to these questions:

  • Which grades are accepting applications for the upcoming year?
  • Must a new application be submitted if a child was not admitted previously?
  • Does the child’s current county or ZIP code affect eligibility or selection?
  • What documents are required after a lottery selection?
  • When should families expect final enrollment confirmation?
  • How are siblings, wait-listed applicants, and midyear openings handled?
  • Which admissions policy applies to the child’s grade, especially for FAU High School?

Policies for younger students and high-school applicants may differ. Use the school’s Parent Portal policy links to find the current A.D. Henderson and FAU High School admissions policies. An old social-media post or another parent’s experience may no longer match them.

Transportation and pickup logistics across Boca Raton

Build the commute around the actual bell schedule

For the 2026-2027 school year, the school’s attendance policy states that the instructional day begins at 8:00 a.m. Elementary dismissal is at 2:30 p.m., and middle- and high-school dismissal is at 2:45 p.m. A student arriving after the morning bell is tardy. Test the route during a weekday school commute, not on a quiet Sunday afternoon.

From east Boca, the route may funnel toward Glades Road or Spanish River Boulevard. From central Boca, Military Trail and St. Andrews Boulevard can become part of the calculation. Families in West Boca need to allow for a much longer eastbound trip and possible congestion around Interstate 95 and the FAU entrances.

Do not assume a school bus will solve the trip

The school’s August 2026 arrival guidance discusses car riders, authorized walkers, bicyclists, and students using public transportation. It directs families to record transportation authorization in the FOCUS system. Before signing a lease, ask the school directly what transportation, if any, is available for the address.

For car riders, the school uses SchoolPass for approved pickup contacts. Its 2026 arrival and dismissal guidance warns that campus traffic can be heavier during university move-in and that car-line procedures may take longer during the opening weeks. According to its published handbook, the school also offers before care beginning at 7:00 a.m. and aftercare through 6:00 p.m., subject to registration and capacity.

Test more than one campus entrance

FAU has approaches from Glades Road, Northwest 20th Street, and Spanish River Boulevard. A November 2026 traffic advisory gives families a reason to know the alternatives: an FAU football game was expected to delay dismissal traffic even though all three approaches remained open.

Drive the morning and afternoon routes from each serious rental candidate. When reviewing the current rental inventory, save the address in a map application and schedule a real test drive. An archived Boca Raton listing such as the Helsinki Circle Lakeview Home can also show how a West Boca starting point differs from living immediately around the university.

Coordinate lease timing with admissions and backup schools

Do not let a lease deadline outrun the school decision

The timing can be awkward. Applications currently close on December 1, while lottery activity occurs in April and May. Decisions may continue as seats open. South Florida rental competition typically increases during the November-through-April snowbird season, while family moves often cluster around summer.

If a landlord wants a signed lease before the school provides a final answer, evaluate the decisions separately. Ask whether the home still works for your child’s zoned Palm Beach County school, a district choice option, or another acceptable backup. If it does not, the rental carries more educational risk than its location suggests.

Verify the zoned fallback for each exact address

Do not infer school assignment from a neighborhood name, ZIP code, or the school attended by a child across the street. Palm Beach County publishes attendance-boundary maps and the Find My School tool, and boundaries can differ by grade level.

Enter the complete rental address and save the result. Then call the identified school to ask what proof of residence is required, when registration can begin, and what happens if the move occurs after classes start. The district’s admissions team lists 561-434-8197 and admissions@palmbeachschools.org for enrollment questions.

Use a two-track calendar

  1. Before December: Confirm the current Lab Schools deadline and submit a complete application.
  2. During the rental search: Check the zoned elementary, middle, and high school for every candidate address.
  3. Before paying nonrefundable fees: Ask the school whether the child has a final enrollment offer or remains at an intermediate stage.
  4. Before lease execution: Test morning drop-off and afternoon pickup routes on regular weekdays.
  5. Before move-in: Assemble proof-of-residence and registration documents for the selected school and the backup plan.

Make the housing decision after the school facts are clear

A home near FAU may be a sensible choice because it can shorten a confirmed student’s commute and provide access to central Boca Raton. It may also suit a family comfortable with the address’s regular Palm Beach County assignment. The risk comes from paying a location premium based on the assumption that distance creates admission rights.

Renting in Florida Without a Car: A Guide to Transit-Friendly Living

Living in Florida without a car presents unique challenges—but when you choose the right city and modes of transit, you can thrive. This guide explores top cities, practical transit modes, and tips for seamless, car-free living.


Top Florida Cities for Car-Free Renters

Florida may be known for its car culture and sprawling highways, but several cities are becoming increasingly welcoming to residents who prefer to live without owning a vehicle. With growing transit networks, walkable neighborhoods, and modern rail connections, car-free renters can still enjoy the Sunshine State’s vibrant lifestyle.

Miami–Fort Lauderdale Corridor

The Miami–Fort Lauderdale corridor stands out as the most transit-friendly area in Florida.

Miami: Florida’s largest urban hub offers multiple public transit options. The Metrorail, a 25-mile rapid transit line, connects neighborhoods to downtown and the airport. Within central areas like Brickell and Downtown, renters benefit from the Metromover, a free automated system that loops through business and residential districts. In addition, the Metrobus network and city trolley system provide extensive coverage, especially convenient for residents of Miami Beach, where parking can be limited and expensive.

Brightline: For those commuting beyond Miami, the Brightline rail offers a modern, high-speed connection linking Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton, and West Palm Beach. With recent expansion toward Orlando, the service makes car-free intercity travel not only possible but practical. Brightline stations are centrally located, allowing renters to live comfortably near transit hubs and avoid daily driving.

Fort Lauderdale: Just north of Miami, Fort Lauderdale complements the region’s car-free lifestyle. The city operates a Sun Trolley system, local buses, and bike-share programs, making short-distance travel around downtown and its scenic waterways easy. Its connection to Brightline ensures renters have both local and regional travel options.

For renters who prioritize convenience, affordability, and freedom from car ownership, the Miami–Fort Lauderdale corridor is Florida’s premier destination. With expanding rail lines, walkable neighborhoods, and innovative transit systems, this region is reshaping urban living in the state.


Orlando: Transit-Friendly Living in Central Florida

While Orlando is often associated with theme parks and tourists, the city has also been developing transit options that make it increasingly possible to live without a car. For renters seeking a car-free lifestyle, Orlando offers commuter rail, extensive bus service, and a reliable downtown circulator.

SunRail: Connecting the Region

The backbone of Orlando’s commuter system is SunRail, a 61-mile commuter rail line with 17 stations. Recently extended to DeLand, SunRail connects downtown Orlando with surrounding communities including Winter Park, Altamonte Springs, and Kissimmee. This makes it ideal for renters who want to live in suburban towns while still commuting into Orlando without relying on a car. The service has also encouraged transit-oriented development around its stations, giving renters more walkable living options.

LYNX Bus & LYMMO BRT: Urban Mobility

Orlando’s primary bus system, LYNX, operates around 68 routes across Orange, Osceola, and Seminole counties. Covering both residential and commercial areas, the network makes commuting to work, school, and attractions possible without a vehicle.

For renters living in downtown Orlando, the LYMMO Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) is especially valuable. This free circulator runs on dedicated lanes and offers high-frequency service, making it easy to navigate Orlando’s central business district without worrying about parking. With routes serving key areas such as Parramore, Thornton Park, and Lake Eola, LYMMO is a convenient option for short-distance trips.

A Growing Transit-Friendly City

Although Orlando is still evolving as a car-free city, its expanding rail system, bus coverage, and free downtown circulator give renters flexible options. For those who want to enjoy Central Florida’s energy without the hassle of driving, Orlando offers a balance of connectivity and convenience.


Gainesville: A College Town Built for Car-Free Living

Gainesville, home to the University of Florida, stands out as one of Florida’s most accessible cities for renters without cars. With its compact layout, strong student population, and dedicated public transit system, Gainesville offers convenient options for moving around the city without relying on personal vehicles.

RTS: Regional Transit System

The heart of Gainesville’s car-free lifestyle is its Regional Transit System (RTS). Operating 40 city routes and 10 campus routes, RTS is designed to serve both university students and local residents. The system connects key zones of the city—including downtown, residential neighborhoods, and shopping areas—making it easy for renters to get where they need to go.

For students, RTS is particularly efficient, offering frequent service throughout the University of Florida campus. The campus-specific routes allow quick trips between classes, housing, and nearby amenities, reducing the need for cars in a bustling academic environment.

Car-Free Benefits in Gainesville

Gainesville’s relatively small size, coupled with its extensive bus coverage, makes it one of the most practical places in Florida for living without a vehicle. Many renters choose housing within biking or walking distance of bus routes, ensuring easy access to both campus and the city’s cultural spots. With RTS as the backbone of transit, Gainesville offers an affordable and eco-friendly way to live in Central Florida.


Jacksonville: Expansive Transit in Florida’s Largest City

Jacksonville is the largest city by land area in the continental United States, which can make living without a car challenging. However, the city has invested in public transportation options that give renters alternatives to driving, especially in and around downtown. With its elevated monorail and wide-ranging bus network, Jacksonville is steadily improving its accessibility for car-free living.

Skyway Monorail

At the center of Jacksonville’s downtown mobility is the Skyway, an automated monorail system. Covering 8 stops, the Skyway links key downtown hubs including business districts, residential areas, and entertainment venues. For renters living in or near downtown, the Skyway provides a convenient and free way to move quickly through the city’s core without traffic or parking hassles.

JTA Bus, Ferry & Shuttle Services

Beyond the monorail, the Jacksonville Transportation Authority (JTA) operates an extensive bus network that connects neighborhoods across the city. This system serves as the backbone of transit, giving renters access to shopping centers, schools, and employment hubs.

For added convenience, JTA also runs a St. Johns River Ferry, providing a scenic and practical link between the city’s north and south banks. In addition, parking shuttle services help bridge gaps for commuters, enhancing the overall accessibility of Jacksonville without requiring a car.

Car-Free Living Potential

While Jacksonville’s size and spread-out layout mean it’s less walkable than other Florida cities, its growing transit infrastructure offers real options for car-free renters. Between the Skyway, buses, and ferry, residents can navigate downtown and key corridors without relying solely on cars, making Jacksonville a city with unique car-free potential.


Boca Raton: Regional Rail Access in South Florida

Boca Raton, positioned between Miami and West Palm Beach, is one of South Florida’s most commuter-friendly cities for renters who prefer not to own a car. While the city itself is not as transit-heavy as Miami or Fort Lauderdale, its direct connection to Brightline makes it a highly practical choice for those who rely on regional rail for work, school, or leisure.

Brightline: Fast and Modern Connectivity

The Brightline rail system is the primary transit option serving Boca Raton. With its centrally located station, Brightline offers renters convenient, fast, and comfortable travel across South Florida. Commuters can easily reach Miami, Fort Lauderdale, and West Palm Beach, while the recent expansion toward Orlando further enhances Boca Raton’s regional connectivity.

For renters, this means living in Boca Raton provides access to a quieter residential environment without sacrificing the ability to reach major urban centers. The modern trains feature reliable schedules, comfortable seating, and onboard amenities, making daily commuting or weekend trips both efficient and enjoyable.

Car-Free Advantages in Boca Raton

While local transit in Boca Raton is more limited compared to larger cities, Brightline’s presence makes it an attractive option for those who want to live in South Florida without relying on a personal vehicle. For renters working in Miami or visiting Orlando, the city’s rail access offers a strong balance of suburban living and regional mobility.


Pros & Cons: What’s the Reality?

Living in Florida without a car is possible, but it comes with both advantages and drawbacks depending on where you live and how you prefer to travel. While many Floridians rely on their own vehicles, the state’s evolving transportation network offers more alternatives than ever.

Advantages

Fast and Reliable Rail OptionsFlorida’s growing rail system is one of its strongest transportation advantages. Rail corridors like Brightline and SunRail provide quick, dependable service between major hubs, making commuting between cities such as Miami, Orlando, and West Palm Beach far easier than ever before.

Walkable Urban AreasIn dense pockets like downtown Miami or near the University of Florida campus in Gainesville, walkability becomes a real perk. These neighborhoods feature nearby bus routes, grocery stores, and entertainment within short walking distance, reducing the need for constant car use.

Flexible Mobility ServicesFor areas where fixed-route transit is limited, ride-hailing platforms like Lyft and Uber, as well as regional shuttle services, help fill in the transportation gaps. These options allow residents to maintain flexibility without the cost and responsibility of car ownership.

Disadvantages

Limited Statewide CoverageDespite improvements, public transit coverage across much of Florida remains sparse. Outside of urban centers, it can be difficult to rely solely on buses, trains, or shuttles.

Longer Travel TimesPublic transit routes may take significantly longer than driving, especially in areas with limited frequency or indirect bus lines. This makes everyday commuting less convenient for people living outside core transit zones.

Costs Can Add UpWhile cheaper than owning a car in the long run, frequent rides on Brightline, Uber, or shuttles can still add up. Budget-conscious renters may find that the convenience comes at a price.

Florida offers promising transit solutions for those willing to live in the right areas. Rail corridors, walkable districts, and ride-hailing services create a realistic alternative to car ownership—but the experience varies greatly by location. For renters and residents who prioritize convenience, choosing neighborhoods near strong transit connections is essential.


How to Choose the Right Spot

For renters in Florida looking to live without a car, location is everything. Choosing the right spot can make the difference between a seamless transit lifestyle and daily frustrations. Here are the key factors to consider:

Focus on Transit Hubs

The most car-free-friendly living options are in downtown Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Orlando’s central areas, Gainesville near the University of Florida, and downtown Jacksonville. These hubs serve as major anchors for rail lines, bus routes, and local shuttles, offering a strong foundation for car-free living.

Rent Near Frequent Stops

Convenience is about proximity. Apartments within walking distance to Brightline, SunRail, or high-frequency bus routes ensure easier daily commuting. Living near a main stop minimizes the need for long walks in Florida’s heat and reduces reliance on expensive rideshares.

Mix in Alternative Options

Even in the best-connected areas, you’ll sometimes need an extra boost. Rideshare, bike-share programs, and community shuttles are essential “last-mile” solutions to bridge gaps between transit stops and your final destination.

Match Your Lifestyle

Not every location suits everyone equally. Students thrive in Gainesville’s well-connected campus routes, while professionals may prefer living near Miami’s or Fort Lauderdale’s train corridors for quicker regional access. Aligning your housing choice with your daily patterns is key to a smoother lifestyle.

Stay Aware of Climate Realities

Florida’s hot, humid climate is a practical factor in going car-free. Look for neighborhoods with shaded walking paths, bus shelters, or indoor waiting areas to make transit use more comfortable. Flexible schedules also help avoid peak heat hours.

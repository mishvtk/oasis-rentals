Move-in inspection checklist for Florida renters: what to photograph before you unpack

Moving into a new rental in Florida can feel exciting—but before you start unpacking boxes and settling in, it’s crucial to document the condition of your unit. A thorough move-in inspection, backed by clear photos and videos, can protect your security deposit and prevent disputes with your landlord later.

Florida’s climate—humidity, storms, and potential water damage—makes documentation even more important. Here’s a complete checklist of what to inspect and photograph before you unpack.



Why Move-In Documentation Matters

Signing a lease is more than just securing a place to live—it’s also taking on responsibility for the condition of the property. From the moment you receive the keys, you may be held accountable for any damage found in the unit, unless you can clearly prove it existed before you moved in. This is where move-in documentation becomes essential.

Protecting Yourself from Unfair Charges

One of the most common disputes between tenants and landlords involves repair costs. Without proper documentation, landlords may assume that scratches on the floor, stains on the carpet, or dents in the walls occurred during your tenancy. Thorough move-in records—photos, videos, and written notes—give you concrete evidence to show the true condition of the property before you settled in. This can prevent you from being charged for damage you didn’t cause.

Safeguarding Your Security Deposit

Your security deposit is often a significant amount of money, and getting it back in full depends largely on the property’s condition at move-out. Detailed documentation acts as your protection. By comparing move-in and move-out records, you can clearly demonstrate that you’ve maintained the unit properly and that any pre-existing issues were not your responsibility.

Creating a Clear Record of Pre-Existing Damage

Even well-maintained properties can have minor wear and tear. Chips in paint, loose fixtures, or aging appliances are normal—but they should be noted before you move in. A comprehensive move-in checklist, supported by time-stamped photos or videos, creates a reliable baseline. This record ensures that both you and your landlord have a shared understanding of the property’s initial condition.

Making Dispute Resolution Easier

If disagreements arise at the end of your lease, having organized documentation can significantly speed up the resolution process. Instead of relying on memory or verbal claims, you’ll have visual and written proof to support your case. This not only strengthens your position but also helps avoid lengthy back-and-forth discussions or legal complications.

Building a Professional Tenant-Landlord Relationship

Taking the time to document the property at move-in shows responsibility and transparency. It signals to your landlord that you take your obligations seriously, which can foster a more positive and professional relationship throughout your tenancy.

Move-in documentation is a simple step that can save you time, money, and stress later on. By carefully recording the condition of your rental at the start, you protect your rights, your finances, and your peace of mind. In any rental situation, especially in competitive markets, being proactive is always better than dealing with problems after they arise.



General Tips Before You Start Your Move-In Documentation

Before you begin unpacking boxes or arranging furniture, take time to properly document your rental’s condition. A few simple steps can make a major difference in protecting your security deposit and avoiding disputes later. With today’s technology, creating a reliable record is easier than ever—you just need to do it thoroughly and thoughtfully.

Use Your Phone to Take High-Resolution Photos and Videos

Your smartphone is your best tool for move-in documentation. Use the highest resolution settings available to capture clear, detailed images. High-quality visuals make it easier to spot and prove small issues like scratches, cracks, or stains that could otherwise be overlooked or disputed later.

Enable Date and Time Stamps

Whenever possible, turn on date and time stamps in your camera settings or ensure your files are automatically timestamped. This provides a clear record of when the documentation was created, which is especially useful if there’s ever a disagreement about when damage occurred.

Capture Wide Shots and Close-Ups

Start with wide-angle shots of each room to show the overall condition, then move in for close-ups of specific areas. Focus on floors, walls, ceilings, appliances, windows, and fixtures. This combination creates a complete visual record—from general layout to detailed imperfections.

Narrate Your Videos

When recording video, speak as you walk through the property. Point out and describe any visible issues, such as “small crack in the wall near the window” or “water stain on the ceiling.” This real-time narration adds context that photos alone may not fully capture.

Save Everything in a Cloud Backup

Once you’ve taken your photos and videos, store them safely in a cloud service like Google Drive or iCloud. This ensures your documentation won’t be lost if your phone is damaged, lost, or replaced. Organized folders labeled by room or date can make retrieval easier if needed later.

Share Copies with Your Landlord or Property Manager

Transparency is key. Send a copy of your documentation to your landlord or property manager soon after move-in. This creates a shared record and helps establish mutual agreement on the property’s initial condition. It can also prevent misunderstandings down the line.

Preparing your move-in documentation properly sets the tone for your entire tenancy. By using clear visuals, secure storage, and open communication, you create a strong layer of protection for yourself. A little effort at the beginning can save you from costly and stressful issues when it’s time to move out.



Exterior & Entry Areas: Documenting the First Impression of Your Rental

When completing your move-in inspection, start with the exterior and entry areas. These are the first parts of the property you interact with—and often the most exposed to weather, wear, and environmental damage. Carefully documenting these spaces helps establish a clear baseline before you even step inside.

Front Door and Entryway

Begin with the front door, as it’s one of the most frequently used features of the home. Take clear photos of the entire door, then zoom in on any scratches, dents, chipped paint, or rust. Don’t forget to check the locks and handles—make sure they function properly and show no signs of damage.

Also, inspect the door frame and seals. Look for cracks, gaps, or worn weather stripping, which can lead to drafts, moisture intrusion, or pest issues if left unaddressed.

Outdoor Lighting and Mailbox

Next, check all exterior lighting fixtures near the entry. Photograph their condition and test whether they are working. Broken or non-functional lights can be both a safety and security concern.

If your rental includes a mailbox, document its condition as well. Look for dents, rust, or issues with the lock. Even minor damage should be recorded to avoid responsibility later.

Patio or Balcony Areas

If your rental has a patio or balcony, give it a thorough inspection. Capture wide shots of the entire space, then focus on details like flooring, railings, and any built-in fixtures. Look for cracks in concrete, rust on metal surfaces, loose or unstable railings, and signs of wear. These areas are especially important for safety and should be documented carefully.

Florida Tip: Watch for Moisture and Humidity Damage

In a climate like Florida, humidity and heavy rain can take a toll on exterior structures. Pay close attention to signs of water intrusion, such as warped wood, swollen door frames, mold spots, or peeling paint. Catching and documenting these issues early is crucial, as they can worsen over time and lead to costly disputes if not properly recorded.

The exterior and entry areas set the tone for your entire rental experience—and your documentation process. By starting here and capturing both overall and detailed images, you create a strong foundation for your move-in inspection. Taking a few extra minutes to record these spaces can protect you from being held responsible for damage you didn’t cause.



Living Room & Common Areas: Documenting High-Traffic Spaces

Living rooms and shared spaces are some of the most frequently used areas in any rental, which means they’re also the most prone to wear and tear. Because of this, it’s especially important to document their condition thoroughly at move-in. A detailed record ensures you won’t be held responsible for damage that was already there.

Floors: Scratches, Stains, and Warping

Start from the ground up by photographing the floors. Capture wide shots of the entire room, then zoom in on any scratches, stains, dents, or signs of warping. Pay close attention to corners, areas near furniture placement, and high-use pathways. Different flooring types—wood, tile, or carpet—can show damage in different ways, so document anything that looks worn or uneven.

Walls: Holes, Scuffs, and Chipped Paint

Next, inspect the walls carefully. Take photos of each wall from a distance, then capture close-ups of any holes, nail marks, scuffs, or peeling paint. Even small imperfections should be recorded, as they are often points of disagreement during move-out inspections.

Ceilings: Water Stains and Cracks

Don’t forget to look up. Ceilings can reveal hidden issues, especially related to leaks or structural concerns. Photograph any discoloration, water stains, bubbling paint, or cracks. These signs may indicate past or ongoing problems that should be noted before you move in.

Windows and Screens

Windows are another key area to inspect. Take photos showing the condition of the glass, frames, locks, and screens. Look for cracks, gaps, broken seals, or torn screens. Also, note whether windows open and close properly, as functionality is just as important as appearance.

Light Fixtures and Ceiling Fans

Finally, check all light fixtures and ceiling fans. Photograph each one and confirm they are working properly. Turn them on while recording video if possible to show their condition in real time. Look for missing covers, flickering lights, unusual noises, or dust buildup that may indicate poor maintenance.

Living rooms and common areas see daily use, making them critical to document with care. By capturing both the overall condition and specific problem areas, you create a clear and reliable record. This not only protects your security deposit but also helps ensure a smoother move-out process when your lease ends.



Kitchen: Documenting One of the Most Important Areas

The kitchen is one of the most heavily used spaces in any rental—and one of the most common sources of security deposit deductions. Because of the number of appliances, surfaces, and plumbing fixtures involved, it’s essential to document this area thoroughly at move-in. A detailed record can protect you from being held responsible for pre-existing damage or maintenance issues.

Appliances (Inside and Outside)

Start by photographing all kitchen appliances, including the refrigerator, oven, microwave, and dishwasher. Take both exterior and interior shots. Open doors to capture shelves, trays, and seals, and look for stains, odors, rust, or broken components. If possible, take a quick video showing that each appliance powers on and functions properly.

Sink and Faucet

Next, inspect the sink and faucet. Take clear photos of the basin, drain, and fixtures. Check for leaks under and around the sink, rust spots, water stains, or mineral buildup. You may also want to record a short video while running the water to show water pressure and drainage.

Cabinets and Drawers

Open every cabinet and drawer, photographing both the interiors and exteriors. Look for misalignment, broken hinges, peeling surfaces, or water damage. Don’t forget to check corners and undersides, as these areas often show hidden wear.

Countertops

Carefully document the condition of all countertops. Capture wide shots and close-ups of any chips, cracks, burns, or stains. Even small imperfections should be noted, as countertops are expensive to repair or replace and often lead to disputes.

Garbage Disposal (If Applicable)

If your kitchen includes a garbage disposal, document its condition as well. Take a photo of the unit and consider recording a short clip while it’s running to show that it works properly. Note any unusual noises, leaks, or odors.

Florida Tip: Check for Mold and Mildew

In a humid climate like Florida, kitchens are especially vulnerable to moisture-related issues. Pay close attention to the area under the sink and around plumbing fixtures. Look for signs of mold, mildew, or water damage such as dark spots, musty smells, or warped materials. Documenting these early can help avoid being blamed for long-term issues caused by humidity.

The kitchen requires extra attention during your move-in inspection due to its complexity and frequent use. By carefully documenting appliances, surfaces, and plumbing, you create a strong record that protects both your finances and your peace of mind. Taking the time to do it right at the beginning can save you from costly surprises at move-out.



Bedrooms: Don’t Overlook the Details

Bedrooms may seem simple compared to kitchens or living areas, but they still require careful documentation during your move-in inspection. Even minor damage in these private spaces can lead to deductions from your security deposit if not properly recorded. Taking the time to capture every detail ensures you’re protected from being held responsible for pre-existing issues.

Carpets or Flooring

Start by documenting the condition of the bedroom floors. Whether it’s carpet, hardwood, or tile, take wide photos of the entire space and close-ups of any stains, discoloration, scratches, or signs of wear. Pay special attention to high-traffic areas, corners, and spots near closets or windows where damage is more likely to occur.

Closet Interiors

Closets are often overlooked, but they can hide damage that becomes an issue later. Open each closet and photograph the interior thoroughly. Capture shelves, hanging rods, walls, and flooring. Look for broken or loose rods, sagging shelves, stains, or signs of moisture damage.

Doors and Handles

Next, inspect the bedroom door and hardware. Take photos of both sides of the door, focusing on scratches, dents, or chipped paint. Check that handles and locks work properly, and document any looseness or damage.

Windows and Blinds

Finally, document the windows and any window coverings. Photograph the glass, frames, locks, and screens. Look for cracks, drafts, or difficulty opening and closing. For blinds or curtains, check for broken slats, stains, or missing parts, and capture these clearly in your photos.

Bedrooms may not have as many fixtures as other areas, but that doesn’t make them less important. Small issues—like a stained carpet or a broken closet rod—can quickly turn into costly deductions if undocumented. By carefully recording every detail, you ensure a smooth move-out process and protect your deposit.



Bathrooms: A Critical Area to Document

Bathrooms are one of the most sensitive areas in any rental property—especially in humid climates like Florida. Constant exposure to water and moisture makes them more vulnerable to damage, mold, and wear. Because of this, bathrooms are also a frequent source of disputes over security deposits. Thorough documentation at move-in is essential to protect yourself.

Shower and Tub

Start with the shower or bathtub. Take wide photos of the entire area, then focus on grout lines, tiles, and surfaces. Look for cracks, discoloration, missing grout, or signs of mold and mildew. Even small spots should be documented, as they can spread quickly over time.

Toilet

Next, inspect the toilet carefully. Photograph the entire unit and check that it flushes properly. Look around the base for any signs of leaks, water stains, or loose fittings. These issues can worsen over time and may not always be obvious at first glance.

Sink and Vanity

Move on to the sink and vanity area. Capture images of the sink basin, faucet, and the cabinet below. Check for water damage, swelling wood, rust, or plumbing leaks under the sink. Bathrooms are high-moisture zones, so even minor damage here should be recorded.

Mirrors

Mirrors may seem minor, but they can still lead to deductions if damaged. Photograph each mirror and look for chips, cracks, or black spots caused by moisture damage behind the glass. Discoloration along the edges is a common issue in humid environments.

Exhaust Fan

Finally, check the exhaust fan. This is a key feature in preventing moisture buildup and mold growth. Take a photo of the fan and, if possible, record a short video showing that it turns on and works properly. Note any unusual noise or lack of airflow.

Florida Tip: Watch for Mold Growth

In a humid state like Florida, mold can develop quickly—sometimes in just a short period. Carefully inspect corners, grout lines, ceilings, and areas under sinks for any signs of mold or mildew. If you see even small spots or detect a musty smell, document it immediately. Early evidence is crucial in proving that the issue existed before your tenancy.

Bathrooms require extra attention during your move-in inspection due to their constant exposure to moisture. By documenting every detail—from grout lines to ventilation—you create a strong record that can protect your deposit and prevent future disputes. A few extra minutes spent here can save significant time, money, and stress later.


Electrical & Safety Features: Don’t Overlook the Essentials

While cosmetic details often get the most attention during a move-in inspection, electrical and safety features are just as important—if not more. These systems affect not only your comfort but also your safety. Properly documenting their condition at move-in helps ensure everything is functional and protects you from being held responsible for pre-existing issues.

Light Switches and Outlets

Start by checking every light switch and electrical outlet in the unit. Turn switches on and off to confirm they work properly, and plug in a small device (like a phone charger) to test outlets. Photograph any that appear damaged, discolored, loose, or non-functional. Burn marks, cracks, or outdated outlets should be clearly documented.

Smoke Detectors

Smoke detectors are critical for fire safety and are typically required by law in most rental properties. Test each detector using the test button and listen for the alarm. Take a photo of each unit and note whether it is functioning. If batteries are low or missing, document that immediately.

Carbon Monoxide Detectors (If Applicable)

If your rental includes gas appliances or an attached garage, carbon monoxide detectors may be installed. Test these devices just like smoke detectors and photograph their condition. If one is missing where it should be, make a note and inform your landlord.

Circuit Breaker Panel

Locate the circuit breaker panel and take a clear photo of it, including any labels. This panel controls the electrical system in your unit, so it’s important to document its condition and organization. Look for signs of damage, corrosion, or missing labels, and ensure it is easily accessible.

Electrical and safety features may not always be visible at first glance, but they play a crucial role in your daily living experience. By testing and documenting these systems at move-in, you not only protect your security deposit but also ensure your new home is safe and functional. Taking a few extra minutes to check these essentials can provide long-term peace of mind.


HVAC & Air Quality: Ensuring Comfort and Clean Air

In a hot and humid climate like Florida, your HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) system is one of the most important features in your rental. A properly functioning air conditioning system isn’t just about comfort—it also plays a major role in controlling humidity, preventing mold growth, and maintaining healthy indoor air quality. That’s why documenting this area at move-in is essential.

AC Unit (Visible Condition)

Start by locating the air conditioning unit—this could be a central system, wall unit, or split-type AC. Take clear photos of the unit’s exterior and surrounding area. Look for visible damage such as rust, leaks, dirt buildup, or unusual wear. If accessible, check for any signs of water pooling or blocked drainage lines.

Air Vents (Dust and Mold)

Next, inspect all air vents throughout the property. These vents distribute air from the HVAC system, so their condition directly affects air quality. Photograph each vent and look for excessive dust, debris, or signs of mold. Dirty vents can indicate poor maintenance and may impact both airflow and health.

Thermostat Functionality

The thermostat controls the entire HVAC system, so it’s important to confirm it’s working properly. Take a photo of the thermostat and, if possible, record a short video while adjusting the temperature. Ensure the system responds correctly—cooling when set lower and turning off when adjusted. Note any delays, inaccurate readings, or unresponsive controls.

Florida Tip: Watch for Humidity Issues

In a humid environment like Florida, HVAC systems play a key role in moisture control. Poor performance can lead to excess humidity, which encourages mold growth and musty odors. If you notice damp air, condensation, or mold near vents or the AC unit, document it immediately and notify your landlord.

Your HVAC system directly impacts your comfort, health, and overall living experience. By carefully documenting the AC unit, vents, and thermostat at move-in, you create a clear record of their condition and performance. This not only protects your security deposit but also ensures that any existing issues are addressed early—before they become bigger problems.



Laundry Area: Don’t Forget These Hidden Risk Spots

If your rental includes a washer and dryer, the laundry area is another important space to document during your move-in inspection. While it may not seem as critical as a kitchen or bathroom, laundry equipment and connections can easily develop issues like leaks, rust, or mechanical problems—many of which can lead to costly repairs if not documented early.

Machines (Inside and Outside)

Start by photographing both the washer and dryer from the outside. Capture the overall condition, including dents, scratches, rust, or signs of wear. Then open each machine and take photos of the الداخل (drum). Look for residue, mold, lint buildup, or damage inside.

If possible, run a quick cycle and record a short video to show that both machines are functioning properly at move-in.

Connections and Hoses

Next, inspect the water supply lines and drainage hoses connected to the washer. Take close-up photos of all connections. Look for cracks, loose fittings, or signs of wear. These components are common sources of leaks and should be carefully documented.

Signs of Leaks or Rust

Finally, check the surrounding area—floors, walls, and behind the machines—for any signs of water damage. Look for stains, warped flooring, mold, or rust. Even small signs of past leaks should be photographed, as they may indicate ongoing or recurring issues.

The laundry area may be easy to overlook, but it plays a key role in your rental’s overall condition. By documenting the machines, connections, and any signs of damage, you protect yourself from being held responsible for issues you didn’t cause. A few extra minutes spent here can help prevent unexpected repair costs and ensure a smoother move-out process.


Plumbing Check: Test Everything Before You Settle In

Plumbing issues can be some of the most frustrating—and expensive—problems in a rental. That’s why it’s important to actively test all water sources during your move-in inspection, not just visually check them. Running water throughout the unit helps you identify hidden issues early and ensures you won’t be held responsible for problems that already existed.

Water Pressure

Turn on every faucet in the kitchen, bathrooms, and laundry area to check water pressure. The flow should be steady and consistent—not too weak or excessively forceful. Low pressure may indicate clogged pipes or buildup, while overly high pressure can strain plumbing systems over time. Record a short video to clearly show the water flow if anything seems off.

Drain Speed

Let the water run for a minute, then observe how quickly it drains. Sinks, tubs, and showers should empty efficiently without pooling water. Slow drainage can signal clogs, buildup, or deeper plumbing issues. Capture photos or videos if you notice standing water or delayed draining.

Unusual Noises

Listen carefully while using water fixtures. Strange sounds like banging (often called “water hammer”), gurgling, or whistling can indicate air in the pipes, pressure problems, or blockages. These issues may not be visible but are still important to document.

Leaks Under Sinks

Open all cabinets under sinks and inspect the pipes and connections. Look for moisture, drips, rust, mold, or water stains. Even small leaks can worsen over time and lead to damage. Take clear photos of any problem areas, especially if there are signs of previous repairs or ongoing issues.

A proper plumbing check goes beyond just looking—it requires testing. By running all water sources and documenting pressure, drainage, sounds, and leaks, you create a complete record of the system’s condition at move-in. If any issues appear, be sure to photograph or record them and notify your landlord promptly. This simple step can protect you from future repair costs and ensure a smoother rental experience.



Final Walkthrough Video: Your Complete Move-In Record

Before you start unpacking or moving furniture into place, take a few minutes to record a full walkthrough video of your rental. This step ties together all your photos and notes into one continuous, timestamped record of the property’s condition at move-in. It’s one of the most effective ways to protect yourself from disputes and provide clear, undeniable evidence if issues arise later.

Start at the Entrance

Begin your video at the front door or main entrance. Show the condition of the door, locks, and surrounding area before stepping inside. This establishes a clear starting point and helps verify that the video reflects the unit exactly as it was when you first entered.

Move Room by Room

Walk through the property slowly and methodically, covering every room and area. Follow a consistent path—such as moving clockwise through each space—so nothing is missed. Include living areas, bedrooms, kitchen, bathrooms, closets, and any outdoor spaces. This structured approach ensures your video is organized and easy to review later.

Narrate Any Visible Issues

As you record, describe what you see out loud. Point out damage, wear, or anything that looks unusual—such as stains, cracks, or malfunctioning fixtures. For example, you might say, “small scratch on the floor near the window” or “water stain on the ceiling.” This narration adds context and makes your documentation more detailed and useful.

Why This Step Matters

A walkthrough video captures the full condition of your rental in real time, combining visuals, movement, and audio explanation. Unlike photos alone, it shows how everything connects—from room layout to the condition of fixtures and finishes. Most importantly, it provides a timestamped record that can be used as strong evidence if disagreements arise at move-out.

Think of your walkthrough video as your final layer of protection. By recording the entire unit before unpacking, you create a complete and reliable snapshot of the property’s condition on day one. It’s quick, simple, and can save you from major headaches—and expenses—later.


After Documentation: What to Do Next

Once you’ve finished photographing, recording videos, and documenting every detail of your rental, your job isn’t quite done yet. The next steps are just as important—they ensure your documentation is officially recognized and can be used to protect you if any issues arise later.

Complete a Move-In Inspection Form

If your landlord or property manager provides a move-in inspection checklist or form, fill it out as soon as possible. Use your photos and videos as a reference to make sure nothing is missed. Be detailed and specific when describing any damage or concerns, no matter how minor they may seem.

If no form is provided, consider creating your own written summary organized by room. This helps create a clear, structured record of the property’s condition at the start of your lease.

Email Your Landlord or Property Manager

After completing your documentation, send everything to your landlord or property manager. This step creates a formal record and ensures both parties are aware of the property’s condition from day one. Your email should include:

  • Photos and videos attached or linked (via cloud storage if needed)

  • Written notes outlining any damage, wear, or issues you observed

  • A clear statement that this reflects the condition at move-in

Sending this information promptly—ideally within the first 24–48 hours—helps avoid disputes about timing or responsibility.

Keep Copies for Your Records

Always keep your own copies of everything you send. Store your photos, videos, inspection forms, and email correspondence in a secure place, such as cloud storage (Google Drive, iCloud, etc.). Having easy access to these files ensures you’re prepared if questions come up at any point during your tenancy—or when it’s time to move out.

Documentation is only as strong as the record you create around it. By completing inspection forms, communicating clearly with your landlord, and keeping organized copies, you turn your photos and videos into solid proof. These final steps help protect your security deposit, reduce misunderstandings, and make your entire rental experience smoother and more transparent.


Common Mistakes to Avoid During Move-In Documentation

Move-in documentation is one of the most effective ways to protect your security deposit—but only if it’s done correctly. Many renters unintentionally weaken their own protection by overlooking small but important details. Avoiding these common mistakes can make a big difference when it’s time to move out.

Waiting Too Long

One of the biggest mistakes is delaying your documentation. If you wait until after furniture is moved in or boxes are unpacked, you may block or cover areas that need to be recorded. This can make it difficult to prove the original condition of floors, walls, or fixtures.

Always complete your inspection before bringing in your belongings. The earlier you document, the stronger your evidence will be.

Only Taking Photos of Major Damage

It’s easy to focus only on obvious issues like large stains or broken fixtures—but small details matter just as much. Minor scratches, tiny holes, or light scuffs can still lead to deductions if they aren’t documented.

Be thorough and photograph everything, even if it seems insignificant at the time.

Not Backing Up Files

Taking photos and videos is only helpful if you can access them later. If your phone is lost, damaged, or replaced, you could lose all your documentation.

Always back up your files to a secure location like cloud storage (Google Drive, iCloud, etc.). Organize them by room or date so they’re easy to find if needed.

Failing to Notify the Landlord in Writing

Even the best documentation won’t fully protect you if it’s never shared. Some renters forget to formally notify their landlord about existing damage, which can lead to disputes later.

Always send your findings in writing—preferably by email—with photos, videos, and notes attached. This creates a clear, time-stamped record that both parties can reference.

Avoiding these common mistakes is just as important as the documentation itself. By acting quickly, being thorough, backing up your files, and communicating clearly, you create a strong layer of protection for your tenancy. A little extra care at move-in can save you from unnecessary stress—and unexpected costs—when it’s time to move out.

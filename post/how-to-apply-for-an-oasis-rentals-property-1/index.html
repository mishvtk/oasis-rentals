How to apply for an Oasis Rentals property

Finding the right rental home is an exciting step — and understanding the application process makes it smooth and stress-free. Whether you’re relocating, upgrading your living space, or searching for a long-term residence, applying for an Oasis Rentals property is designed to be clear, transparent, and efficient.

Here’s a complete guide to help you prepare and submit a strong application.



1. Browse Available Properties

Finding the right rental home begins with careful research. Before submitting an application, take time to explore the available listings through the official Oasis Rentals website or approved property listing platforms. This first step helps ensure that the home you choose aligns with both your financial plans and your lifestyle needs.

Start with Trusted Sources

Always begin your search on the official website or verified rental platforms. Official listings provide the most accurate details about availability, rental terms, and contact information. Using trusted sources also protects you from scams or outdated property information.

Compare Key Details

As you review properties, focus on the following important factors:

1. Location Consider proximity to work, schools, public transportation, grocery stores, beaches, or entertainment areas. The right location can significantly impact your daily routine and overall satisfaction.

2. Rental Price Review the monthly rent carefully and compare it with similar properties in the area. Make sure it fits comfortably within your budget. Financial experts commonly recommend spending no more than 30% of your monthly income on housing costs.

3. Lease Terms Check whether the property offers short-term or long-term lease options. Pay attention to lease duration, renewal policies, and any early termination conditions.

4. Included Amenities Look at what is included in the rental price. Amenities may include parking, utilities, internet, appliances, on-site laundry, security, pool access, or fitness facilities. Knowing what’s included helps you calculate your total monthly expenses more accurately.

5. Pet Policies (If Applicable) If you have pets, confirm whether the property is pet-friendly. Review any breed or size restrictions, pet deposits, or additional monthly pet fees before proceeding.

Think About Lifestyle Fit

Beyond price and features, consider how the property supports your daily lifestyle. Is the layout practical? Does the community environment match your preferences? Is the commute manageable? Taking time to reflect on these questions will help you avoid unnecessary stress later.

By thoroughly browsing and comparing available properties, you set a strong foundation for a smooth rental application process. Careful selection at this stage increases your chances of long-term satisfaction with your new home.


2. Schedule a Viewing

After identifying a property that matches your needs and budget, the next important step is to schedule a viewing. While photos and online descriptions provide helpful information, nothing replaces seeing the property in person. A viewing allows you to confirm details, assess the home’s condition, and make a confident decision before applying.

Why a Viewing Matters

Scheduling a showing gives you the opportunity to carefully evaluate the property beyond what is listed online. During your visit, you can:

Inspect the Condition of the Home Look closely at walls, floors, windows, appliances, plumbing fixtures, and electrical outlets. Check for signs of wear, water damage, or maintenance issues. Pay attention to cleanliness and overall upkeep, as this often reflects how the property is managed.

Ask Questions About Lease Terms Use the viewing as a chance to clarify lease length, renewal options, deposit requirements, and policies regarding early termination. Understanding these terms clearly before applying can prevent misunderstandings later.

Confirm Included Utilities or ServicesAsk which utilities are included in the rent and which are your responsibility. This may include water, electricity, trash collection, internet, parking, or maintenance services. Knowing this helps you calculate your total monthly housing costs accurately.

Get a Feel for the Neighborhood Take a few minutes to observe the surroundings. Notice noise levels, nearby businesses, traffic flow, parking availability, and general safety. If possible, visit at different times of the day to better understand the environment.

Be Prepared and Professional

Coming prepared with thoughtful questions shows seriousness and professionalism. Property managers appreciate applicants who are organized and respectful of their time. Bring identification if required, arrive on time, and take notes during the tour to help you compare properties later.

A viewing is more than just a tour—it is your opportunity to ensure the property truly fits your expectations and lifestyle before moving forward with the application process.



3. Prepare Your Documents

Once you’ve viewed the property and decided to move forward, preparing your documents is a crucial step in the rental application process. Having everything ready in advance not only speeds up approval but also shows property managers that you are organized, responsible, and serious about securing the home.

Incomplete or delayed paperwork can slow down your application — and in competitive rental markets, timing matters.

Commonly Required Documents

While specific requirements may vary by property, most landlords or property managers request the following:

1. Government-Issued Photo ID A valid passport, driver’s license, or national ID card is typically required to verify your identity. This ensures the lease agreement is legally binding and protects both parties.

2. Proof of Income Property managers want to confirm that you can comfortably afford the rent. Acceptable proof may include:

  • Recent pay stubs

  • An employment verification letter

  • Bank statements

  • Tax returns (for self-employed applicants)

In general, most property managers require applicants to earn approximately 2.5 to 3 times the monthly rent in gross income. For example, if rent is $1,000 per month, you may need to show monthly income of $2,500–$3,000 to qualify.

3. Rental History or Landlord References Previous landlord contact information or written references help demonstrate that you paid rent on time, maintained the property, and followed lease terms. A strong rental history can significantly improve your approval chances.

4. Completed Rental Application Form The application form typically includes personal information, employment details, rental history, and authorization for background or credit checks (if applicable). Make sure all information is accurate and complete.

Why Preparation Matters

Submitting a complete and well-organized application can make a strong first impression. In situations where multiple applicants are interested in the same property, a prepared applicant often stands out.

Before submitting, double-check:

  • All forms are signed

  • Copies are clear and legible

  • Contact information is correct

  • Supporting documents are included

Taking the time to prepare your documents carefully increases your chances of a smooth and timely approval process.


4. Complete the Application Form

After gathering your required documents, the next step is to complete the rental application form. This is a critical part of the approval process, as it provides the property manager or landlord with the information needed to evaluate your eligibility.

Accuracy and honesty are essential. Incomplete or incorrect information can delay processing — or even result in a denied application.

Information Typically Requested

While application forms may vary slightly by property, most will request the following details:

1. Personal Information This includes your full legal name, date of birth, contact number, email address, and government-issued ID number. Some applications may also request emergency contact details.

2. Employment HistoryYou will typically need to provide your current employer’s name, job title, length of employment, and income details. If applicable, previous employment information may also be requested to demonstrate stable income history.

3. Current and Previous Addresses Listing your rental history allows the property manager to verify your past housing record. Be prepared to include addresses, dates of residence, and landlord contact information.

4. References Some applications ask for professional or personal references who can speak to your reliability and character. Providing accurate contact information ensures a smoother verification process.

Be Thorough and Transparent

Fill out every section carefully and honestly. Property managers may conduct background, credit, or reference checks (where legally permitted), so inconsistencies can raise concerns. Transparency builds trust and improves your chances of approval.

Before submitting your application:

  • Review all entries for spelling errors

  • Confirm dates and contact numbers are correct

  • Ensure all required signatures are included

  • Attach any requested supporting documents

Taking a few extra minutes to double-check your application can prevent unnecessary delays and help move you smoothly to the next stage of the leasing process.


5. Pay the Application Fee

As part of the rental application process, some properties require applicants to pay a non-refundable application fee. This fee is typically submitted at the time you turn in your completed application form and supporting documents.

While not all rentals charge this fee, it is common practice in many markets, especially where background and credit screening are involved.

What the Application Fee Covers

The application fee is generally used to offset the administrative costs associated with processing your application. These may include:

Background Checks Landlords or property managers may verify your rental history and check for prior evictions or legal issues, where permitted by law.

Credit ChecksA credit report may be reviewed to assess financial responsibility and payment history. This helps determine whether an applicant is likely to pay rent on time.

Administrative Processing The fee may also cover staff time spent reviewing documents, verifying employment, contacting references, and preparing approval documentation.

Important Considerations

Before paying the application fee, make sure you:

  • Confirm that the fee is non-refundable

  • Understand the total amount being charged

  • Verify accepted payment methods (bank transfer, online payment, cash, etc.)

  • Request a receipt or payment confirmation for your records

If you are applying for multiple properties, keep track of each fee paid, as these costs can add up quickly.

Be Informed Before You Pay

Application fees are typically separate from security deposits and first month’s rent. Paying the fee does not guarantee approval — it only initiates the review process. For this reason, it’s wise to ensure the property truly meets your needs before submitting payment.

Understanding the purpose of the application fee helps you move forward confidently and professionally in the rental process.


6. Application Review and Approval

Once you have submitted your completed application, supporting documents, and any required fees, the property management team begins the review process. This step is essential in determining whether you meet the qualification criteria for the rental property.

While timelines may vary depending on the number of applicants and verification procedures, the review process typically takes a few business days.

What Property Managers Review

During the evaluation stage, several key areas are assessed:

1. Income VerificationThe property manager will confirm that your income meets the required threshold, commonly around 2.5–3 times the monthly rent. This helps ensure you can comfortably afford ongoing rental payments.

2. Credit History Where legally permitted, a credit check may be conducted to review payment history, outstanding debts, and overall financial responsibility. A strong credit profile can improve your approval chances.

3. Rental Background Previous landlord references or rental records may be contacted to confirm on-time payments, property care, and lease compliance. A positive rental history demonstrates reliability.

4. Employment Stability Stable employment history indicates consistent income. Employers may be contacted to verify your position and length of employment.

How to Help Speed Up Approval

Although much of the process is handled by the property management team, you can help move things along by:

  • Responding promptly to emails or phone calls

  • Providing additional documentation quickly if requested

  • Ensuring your references are informed and available

Delays often occur when information cannot be verified or when applicants are slow to respond to follow-up questions.

Notification of Approval

Once the review is complete, you will typically be notified whether your application has been approved, conditionally approved (such as requiring a co-signer or higher deposit), or declined. If approved, you will receive instructions on the next steps, including signing the lease agreement and arranging payment of the security deposit and first month’s rent.

Staying organized, responsive, and professional throughout this stage increases the likelihood of a smooth and timely approval process.



7. Lease Signing and Deposit

Congratulations — if your application has been approved, you are now ready for one of the most important steps in the rental process: signing the lease agreement and submitting your required payments.

This stage makes the agreement legally binding and officially secures the property for you.

What the Lease Agreement Includes

The lease agreement outlines the rights and responsibilities of both the tenant and the landlord. Carefully review every section before signing. Key details typically include:

Monthly Rent The exact rental amount, due date, acceptable payment methods, and any late fee policies.

Security Deposit The amount required upfront to cover potential damages or unpaid rent. The lease should clearly state the conditions for refunding the deposit at the end of the tenancy.

Lease Duration Whether the agreement is month-to-month, six months, one year, or longer. It should also specify renewal terms and notice requirements for ending the lease.

Property Rules and Policies This may include rules regarding pets, guests, noise levels, maintenance responsibilities, parking, and property use.

Review Before You Sign

Take your time reading the lease thoroughly. If anything is unclear, ask questions before signing. Once signed, the lease becomes a legally enforceable contract.

Before signing, confirm:

  • The rental amount matches what was advertised

  • All verbal agreements are included in writing

  • Move-in date is clearly stated

  • Any included utilities or services are listed

Payment to Secure the Property

In most cases, you will be required to pay:

  • Security deposit

  • First month’s rent

These payments officially secure the property and remove it from the market. Be sure to request a receipt or written confirmation of payment for your records.

Final Step Before Move-In

After signing the lease and submitting payment, you will receive instructions regarding move-in procedures, key collection, and any required inspections.

Taking the time to carefully review and understand your lease agreement ensures a smooth start to your tenancy and helps prevent misunderstandings in the future.


Tips for a Strong Application

In competitive rental markets, a strong and well-prepared application can make the difference between securing your desired property and missing out. Property managers often review multiple applications for the same home, so presenting yourself as organized, qualified, and professional is essential.

Here are key tips to improve your chances of approval:

1. Apply Promptly

Desirable properties often rent quickly. Once you’ve viewed a home and decided it’s the right fit, submit your application as soon as possible. Delays can result in another qualified applicant securing the property first.

Being decisive and prepared shows that you are serious about moving forward.

2. Ensure Your Income Meets Minimum Requirements

Most property managers require applicants to earn approximately 2.5 to 3 times the monthly rent in gross income. Before applying, confirm that your income meets this standard.

If your income is close to the minimum requirement, consider providing additional documentation, such as savings statements or a co-signer (if permitted), to strengthen your application.

3. Provide Complete and Honest Information

Accuracy and transparency are critical. Double-check all details in your application, including employment history, rental background, and contact information.

Incomplete applications can cause delays, while inaccurate information may result in automatic denial if discrepancies are discovered during verification.

4. Maintain Professional Communication

From your first inquiry to final approval, maintain respectful and prompt communication. Respond quickly to emails or phone calls, and provide requested documents without delay.

Professional behavior leaves a positive impression and reassures property managers that you will be a responsible tenant.

5. Prepare All Documents in Advance

Having your documents ready before applying can significantly speed up the process. Keep digital copies of:

  • Government-issued photo ID

  • Proof of income

  • Rental references

  • Completed application form

Being organized allows you to act quickly when the right opportunity arises.

A strong application reflects reliability, financial stability, and professionalism. By acting promptly, staying organized, and communicating clearly, you improve your chances of securing the property you want with minimal stress.

Applying for an Oasis Rentals property is straightforward when you’re organized and prepared. By understanding each step — from viewing to lease signing — you can increase your chances of approval and move confidently into your new home.

Citizens vs. private insurers: proof-of-coverage requests your landlord may require in each county

Why landlords request proof of insurance

Landlords routinely require tenants (especially long-term tenants, vacation rental tenants, or those leasing whole properties) to provide proof of insurance coverage or name the landlord as an additional insured. The reasons include:

  • Ensuring the unit/house is insured in case of damage (fire, windstorm, vandalism) which could otherwise expose the landlord’s investment.

  • Protecting against liability claims (guest injuries, property damage) that may involve the leased property.

  • Verifying that the tenant (or owner/occupant) is using the correct carrier and policy type — which matters in a high-risk insurance environment like Florida.

In Florida particularly, the distinction between a policy with Citizens (the state-insurer of last resort) and a private insurer has relevance for both eligibility and cost.Landlords may therefore incorporate checks (or request documentation) accordingly.

What is Citizens vs. private insurer coverage?

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation (Citizens) is a state‐backed insurer created to provide coverage for homeowners who cannot find coverage in the private market. Private insurers are commercial carriers offering homeowners or dwelling insurance in the open market.

Some differences relevant to landlords/tenants:

Private Insurers Often Offer Broader Coverage Options, Higher Limits, Better Service, or More Flexibility

When shopping for insurance—whether for a home, rental property, or business—many Floridians compare the state-backed Citizens Property Insurance Corporation with private insurers. While Citizens provides an important safety net for those unable to find affordable private coverage, private insurers often offer significant advantages that go beyond basic protection.

1. Broader Coverage Options

Private insurers generally have more freedom to design their policies. That means they can include optional add-ons like water backup coverage, service line protection, or higher-value personal property endorsements. In contrast, Citizens’ policies are standardized and typically exclude many of these enhancements.

For homeowners and renters near the coast, this flexibility can be crucial. Private carriers may cover risks such as screened enclosures, outdoor structures, or upgraded contents coverage—areas where Citizens coverage is limited or unavailable.

2. Higher Coverage Limits

Private insurance companies often allow policyholders to select higher limits for both dwelling and personal property coverage. This is especially valuable for homeowners with luxury finishes, custom upgrades, or high-value belongings. Citizens imposes strict coverage caps, which can leave gaps in protection for those with more valuable assets.

3. Better Customer Service

Private insurers compete directly for customers, which often translates into faster claims processing, more accessible customer support, and user-friendly online tools. Many private companies also assign dedicated agents or adjusters to guide clients through policy renewals and claims—a level of personal service that large, state-backed insurers may not always match.

4. More Flexibility and Discounts

Private insurers typically offer greater pricing flexibility through multi-policy discounts, loyalty programs, or smart-home device credits. They may also allow more payment options and policy customizations, giving homeowners and renters better control over cost and coverage.

5. Balancing Price with Value

While Citizens may initially appear cheaper, private insurers can provide better overall value when considering broader protection, personalized service, and fewer coverage restrictions. In many cases, private coverage helps avoid the risk of being forced out of Citizens later if eligibility changes or premiums rise.

In short: For many Floridians, private insurance offers a more complete, convenient, and customer-friendly experience—especially for those seeking robust protection and personalized support.


Citizens May Charge Lower Premiums, But Policyholders Face Higher Assessment Risk

Florida’s Citizens Property Insurance Corporation was created as a safety net for homeowners and renters who cannot find affordable coverage in the private market. While Citizens policies sometimes come with lower premiums compared to private insurers, policyholders should be aware of one major trade-off: the potential for assessments—additional charges that can be levied if Citizens runs out of money after major disasters.

1. Lower Premiums Can Come with Hidden Risks

Citizens’ mission is to provide affordable insurance to those who are unable to secure private coverage. In some markets, especially along Florida’s coast, Citizens’ rates are lower than what private insurers offer. However, those lower upfront costs can mask a unique financial risk.

If a large hurricane or series of catastrophic events causes Citizens’ claim payouts to exceed its financial reserves, the insurer has the statutory authority to impose assessments on nearly all Florida insurance policyholders—including Citizens customers themselves.

2. Understanding the Assessment Mechanism

Unlike private insurance companies, Citizens can require its policyholders—and even non-Citizens policyholders in some cases—to help cover its deficit. These assessments are not optional. They can be added as surcharges to future premiums until the shortfall is resolved.

For example, Citizens can impose:

  • Policyholder Surcharges of up to 45% on its own customers.

  • Regular Assessments on other property and casualty policyholders statewide if the deficit continues.

This means that even Floridians who don’t have Citizens policies could pay part of the bill for widespread losses after a major storm season.

3. Why It Matters for Homeowners and Renters

The potential for assessments makes Citizens a different kind of insurer. While its lower premiums may be attractive in the short term, the long-term financial exposure is higher in a catastrophic loss year. Homeowners and renters who can find comparable coverage in the private market often prefer to do so—reducing both their personal risk and their share of potential statewide assessments.

4. Balancing Cost and Stability

Citizens serves an important role in maintaining insurance availability in Florida, but policyholders should carefully weigh the cost savings against the potential assessment risk. Private insurers, while sometimes more expensive, cannot levy assessments beyond the terms of the policy, offering more financial certainty.

Citizens’ lower premiums can be appealing, but the insurer’s ability to charge assessments if claims exceed reserves introduces a level of financial risk that private policyholders typically avoid.



Florida’s “Clearinghouse” Rule: Who Qualifies for Citizens Property Insurance

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida’s state-backed insurer, was designed to serve as an insurer of last resort — not a first choice. To ensure that Citizens remains a safety net rather than a substitute for private insurance, Florida law limits who can qualify for a Citizens policy.

The state enforces these limits through what’s known as the Clearinghouse process, which screens applicants before they can obtain coverage from Citizens.

1. What Is the Citizens Clearinghouse?

The Citizens Property Insurance Clearinghouse is an online system that connects homeowners with participating private insurance companies. When someone applies for a Citizens policy, their application is automatically submitted to the Clearinghouse for comparison with private insurer offers.

This process ensures that if private coverage is available — and reasonably priced — homeowners are directed to those private insurers instead of Citizens.

2. The 20% Rule Explained

Under Florida law, homeowners are only eligible for Citizens if:

  • No private insurance company is willing to write the property, or

  • The private insurer’s comparable policy premium is more than 20% higher than Citizens’ offer.

In other words, if a private insurer offers a policy that costs less than 20% more than Citizens’ rate, the homeowner must take the private policy and is ineligible for Citizens coverage.

This rule helps balance affordability for consumers with financial stability for the state, ensuring that Citizens is used as a true last-resort insurer.

3. Why the Rule Matters

The Clearinghouse system protects both taxpayers and the broader insurance market. By redirecting eligible policyholders to private insurers, it prevents Citizens from growing too large — reducing the risk of statewide assessments after major storms.

It also encourages competition among private carriers, who can offer broader coverage, more options, and faster claims service than Citizens in many cases.

4. What Homeowners Should Do

Before applying for Citizens coverage, homeowners should:

  • Work with a licensed insurance agent who can submit the application through the Clearinghouse.

  • Compare all available private offers carefully — even if they’re slightly higher.

  • Understand that Citizens policies come with more limitations and possible assessment risks.

Florida’s Clearinghouse process ensures that Citizens remains an insurer of last resort. Homeowners must demonstrate that no comparable private offer exists or that private coverage is at least 20% more expensive before qualifying for a Citizens policy.


Citizens Is “Depopulating”: Florida’s Push to Move Policies Back to Private Insurers

Florida’s property insurance market is undergoing a major transformation — and Citizens Property Insurance Corporation is at the center of it. Created as a safety net for homeowners unable to find affordable private coverage, Citizens is now actively reducing its size through a process known as “depopulation.”

This effort aims to stabilize Florida’s overall insurance system by transferring Citizens policies to private carriers whenever feasible.

1. What “Depopulation” Means

Depopulation refers to Citizens’ ongoing program to transfer existing policies to private insurance companies that agree to assume the risk. These private carriers are vetted and approved by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR) before being allowed to take on Citizens policyholders.

The process helps shift exposure away from Citizens, which currently holds a large portion of Florida’s property insurance risk — and reduces the chance that taxpayers will be impacted by major storm-related losses.

2. Why Florida Is Encouraging Depopulation

Citizens was never meant to be a long-term solution or a competitor to private insurers. Its growing policy count in recent years raised concerns about potential financial exposure after hurricanes.

By moving policies back to the private market, Florida’s insurance regulators aim to:

  • Reduce statewide assessment risk if Citizens runs out of reserves.

  • Encourage private competition and attract new insurers to the state.

  • Limit Citizens’ financial liability during catastrophic storm seasons.

3. What Happens to Policyholders

When a private company offers to assume a Citizens policy, the homeowner receives a “takeout offer.” Policyholders can usually choose whether to stay with Citizens or move to the private carrier — but in some cases, remaining with Citizens may no longer be an option if private coverage is available and reasonably priced.

Private insurers may offer broader coverage or better customer service, but premiums and deductibles can vary. Policyholders are encouraged to carefully review each offer with their insurance agent before deciding.

4. The Bigger Picture

Depopulation is part of a broader effort to restore stability to Florida’s property insurance system after years of high claim costs, litigation, and natural disasters. While Citizens remains an important fallback option, the long-term goal is to keep the majority of policies in the private market — where competition can drive innovation and financial resilience.

Citizens is no longer expanding; it’s streamlining. Through the depopulation program, Florida is shifting thousands of policies back to private insurers to balance affordability, reduce risk, and strengthen the state’s insurance market overall.

For a landlord, the key takeaway is that a tenant’s policy with Citizens may signal higher risk or limited options — which a landlord may want to factor into lease requirements or insurance‐proof procedures.

Understanding Your Proof of Coverage: The Certificate of Insurance and Declarations Page

Whether you’re a renter, homeowner, or landlord, proof of insurance is more than just a formality — it’s documentation that confirms your property is properly insured. The two most common forms of proof are the Certificate of Insurance and the Declarations Page, both of which contain essential details about your policy.

1. What Is a Certificate of Insurance?

A Certificate of Insurance (COI) is a one-page summary provided by your insurance company or agent that verifies the existence of an active insurance policy. It’s often requested by landlords, property managers, or mortgage lenders as proof that you carry the required coverage.

This document typically includes:

  • The insurance carrier’s name (the company providing coverage)

  • The policy number

  • The effective and expiration dates of the policy

  • The named insured (the person or entity covered)

  • The insured property address

  • The type of coverage (such as renters, homeowners, or liability insurance)

For renters, landlords often require a COI before move-in to confirm that personal property and liability coverage are in place — and that the landlord is listed as an “additional interest” to receive renewal notices.

2. What Is a Declarations Page?

The Declarations Page (also called the “dec page”) is part of your actual insurance policy. It provides a detailed breakdown of coverage, limits, deductibles, and premiums.

In addition to confirming policy information, the dec page shows:

  • Coverage types (dwelling, personal property, loss of use, liability, medical payments, etc.)

  • Policy limits for each coverage area

  • Deductibles, including special hurricane or windstorm deductibles

  • Endorsements or additional coverages

Because it includes more detail than a COI, the declarations page is often used for policy review and claims purposes.

3. When and Why You’ll Need These Documents

Landlords, lenders, or associations may request one or both documents to ensure the property and its occupants are properly insured. Common scenarios include:

  • Renters: Landlords request proof of liability coverage before issuing keys.

  • Homeowners: Mortgage lenders require proof before loan closing or annual escrow renewals.

  • Condo owners: Associations verify that unit owners meet required coverage levels.

Having these documents ready can help prevent move-in delays, loan funding issues, or compliance problems with lease or association rules.

Both the Certificate of Insurance and the Declarations Page serve as key proof-of-coverage documents. The certificate provides quick verification of active insurance, while the declarations page offers a complete snapshot of your coverage details — ensuring transparency and compliance for renters, homeowners, and landlords alike.



Understanding the “Additional Insured” or “Loss Payee” Endorsement in Rental Insurance

When renting a home, apartment, or commercial space, landlords often ask tenants to provide proof of insurance — and in many cases, they also require being listed as an “additional insured” or “loss payee.” These endorsements modify your insurance policy to extend certain rights or protections to the landlord. Understanding how they work can help tenants stay compliant and avoid issues with lease agreements.

1. What Is an “Additional Insured”?

An additional insured is a person or organization (such as your landlord or property management company) added to your insurance policy through an endorsement. This means the landlord gains limited protection under your liability coverage.

For example, if a guest is injured in your rental unit and sues both you and the landlord, the landlord may be protected under your renter’s liability coverage if they’re named as an additional insured.

This endorsement does not give the landlord coverage for their own property — it simply extends your liability protection to them in specific circumstances arising from your tenancy.

2. What Is a “Loss Payee”?

A loss payee designation, on the other hand, is typically used when someone has a financial interest in insured property. For renters or leaseholders, this might apply when a landlord wants to ensure they’re notified if there’s damage to the property or if a claim payout affects their asset.

If the landlord is listed as a loss payee, the insurance company may issue payments jointly to the tenant and the landlord in the event of covered damage involving the landlord’s property.

3. Why Landlords Require These Endorsements

Landlords request additional insured or loss payee status to:

  • Ensure they’re notified about policy renewals, lapses, or cancellations.

  • Receive claim payments in cases where their property suffers covered damage.

  • Gain protection against liability claims arising from tenant actions.

These endorsements provide an extra layer of financial and legal assurance for property owners — especially in shared or multi-unit buildings.

4. How Tenants Can Add the Endorsement

Tenants can request their insurer or agent to issue an additional insured or loss payee endorsement. It’s a simple process that often involves providing:

  • The landlord’s full legal name or entity name

  • The rental property address

  • The landlord’s mailing address (for notices or claim correspondence)

Afterward, your insurer will issue an updated Certificate of Insurance or Declarations Page showing the landlord’s name and role on the policy.

If your lease requires the landlord to be listed as an additional insured or loss payee, make sure your renters insurance policy includes the proper endorsement. It protects both you and your landlord by clarifying rights, responsibilities, and coverage in the event of an accident or property loss.


Proof of Active Insurance: Why Evidence of Coverage Matters for Tenants and Landlords

When renting or managing property, insurance isn’t just about having a policy — it’s about proving that the coverage is active and hasn’t been canceled or allowed to lapse. Landlords, property managers, and even homeowners associations frequently require evidence of an active insurance policy to ensure continuous protection against liability and property risks.

1. What Counts as Evidence of an Active Policy

Proof that your insurance policy is active typically comes in one of the following forms:

  • A Certificate of Insurance (COI) confirming the policy’s effective and expiration dates.

  • A Declarations Page from your insurance company showing that coverage is in force.

  • A Verification Letter or renewal notice from your insurer stating that the policy remains active with no lapse or pending cancellation.

These documents include key details such as the insurer’s name, policy number, coverage period, and insured property address — all of which confirm that your protection is current.

2. Why Landlords Require It

Landlords ask for active proof of insurance to:

  • Ensure ongoing coverage for potential tenant-related damages or liabilities.

  • Comply with lease requirements that mandate renter’s or liability insurance.

  • Receive notification if a tenant’s policy is canceled or non-renewed.

If a policy lapses, the landlord could face increased exposure — for example, if a tenant accidentally causes fire or water damage and has no active insurance to cover the loss.

3. How Tenants Can Prevent Lapses

To maintain continuous proof of active insurance, tenants should:

  • Set up automatic payments or reminders for renewal dates.

  • Update payment information promptly if cards or accounts change.

  • Review policy notices to catch renewal terms or pending cancellations early.

Some insurers also offer automatic renewal options or email alerts to help tenants avoid unintended coverage gaps.

4. Why This Matters for Everyone Involved

A lapse in insurance doesn’t just affect the tenant — it can expose the landlord, property manager, and even neighboring units to risk. Maintaining active coverage protects all parties, ensures compliance with lease terms, and avoids costly disputes or uncovered losses.

Evidence of an active insurance policy is more than paperwork — it’s proof of financial responsibility and ongoing protection. Both tenants and landlords benefit from maintaining and verifying continuous coverage, ensuring that everyone stays safeguarded against unexpected losses.


Why Landlords May Request Extra Verification for Citizens-Insured Properties

Florida’s Citizens Property Insurance Corporation plays a crucial role in the state’s insurance landscape by providing coverage when private insurers won’t. However, because Citizens policies come with certain limitations and financial risks, some landlords and property managers take extra precautions before accepting proof of coverage from tenants insured through Citizens.

1. Understanding Citizens’ Role

Citizens is Florida’s insurer of last resort, designed to serve property owners who can’t obtain affordable or available coverage in the private market. While its policies are essential for maintaining statewide coverage access, they often differ from those offered by private insurers in several important ways — prompting landlords to seek additional verification.

2. Why Landlords Request Extra Proof

Landlords may ask for extra documentation or confirmation when a tenant or property is insured through Citizens to ensure that:

  • The policy is active and fully paid, without pending cancellation.

  • The coverage is adequate for the property’s risk level.

  • Any limitations — such as reduced liability protection or exclusions — are understood and disclosed.

Because Citizens policies can include restricted coverage types, landlords want to confirm that both the tenant and property are properly protected in case of damage, liability, or loss of use.

3. Key Limitations of Citizens Policies

Some of the reasons for added scrutiny include:

  • Narrower coverage: Citizens policies are standardized and may exclude certain optional protections available from private carriers, such as water backup or service line coverage.

  • Assessment risk: If Citizens’ claim costs exceed its financial reserves after a major storm, it can levy assessments (surcharges) on policyholders statewide to recover funds — something private insurers cannot do.

  • Lower coverage limits: Citizens often caps dwelling and liability coverage, which may not fully protect higher-value homes or contents.

4. What Extra Verification Might Include

Landlords may request:

  • A Certificate of Insurance or Declarations Page confirming active coverage.

  • Written confirmation that premium payments are current.

  • Details about policy limits and exclusions, especially for liability and hurricane coverage.

  • Evidence that the landlord is listed as an additional insured or loss payee, ensuring notification if the policy lapses.

5. The Bottom Line for Tenants and Landlords

For tenants, providing thorough documentation helps reassure landlords that adequate protection is in place — even with Citizens. For landlords, verifying coverage details ensures financial and legal protection in the event of loss or damage.

In summary:Because Citizens Property Insurance policies can have narrower coverage and unique financial risks, landlords may reasonably require additional verification before accepting them as proof of insurance. This extra step helps ensure all parties remain protected and compliant with lease requirements.


Proof of Windstorm and Flood Coverage: What Florida Landlords May Require

In Florida, where powerful storms and coastal flooding are part of life, insurance requirements in lease agreements often go beyond standard renters or homeowners coverage. For properties located in wind- or hurricane-prone zones, landlords may require tenants to provide proof of windstorm or hurricane coverage — and in some cases, proof of flood insurance as well. These additional protections help ensure that both the tenant’s belongings and the property itself are financially safeguarded against the state’s most common natural hazards.

1. Why Windstorm and Flood Coverage Matter in Florida

Florida’s geography makes it uniquely vulnerable to hurricanes, tropical storms, and heavy rain events. Even inland areas can experience damaging winds and flooding during major weather systems.

Standard renters or homeowners insurance policies typically cover wind damage, but there are exceptions. In certain coastal regions or high-risk zones, windstorm coverage is excluded and must be purchased as a separate policy or endorsement. Likewise, flood damage is not covered under standard policies — it requires a separate policy through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private flood insurer.

2. Landlords’ Insurance Verification Requirements

To protect their property and ensure compliance with lease terms, landlords may request:

  • Proof of active windstorm or hurricane coverage, showing that the tenant’s policy includes wind-related damage.

  • Proof of flood coverage, especially if the property is in a FEMA-designated flood zone or near coastal or canal-front areas.

  • A Certificate of Insurance or Declarations Page listing the covered perils, effective dates, and insured address.

  • In some cases, confirmation that the landlord is named as an additional interest, ensuring they’re notified if coverage lapses.

These requirements are most common in beachfront, barrier island, or low-lying areas — such as Boca Raton, Deerfield Beach, Palm Coast, or the Florida Keys — where hurricane and flood risks are higher.

3. What Tenants Should Know

Tenants should review their insurance carefully to confirm:

  • Windstorm coverage is included or added as an endorsement.

  • Flood coverage is in place if the property is in a flood-prone area.

  • Deductibles for hurricanes or named storms are understood and affordable.

Providing clear documentation upfront can prevent move-in delays and ensure full compliance with the lease agreement.

4. The Bottom Line

Requiring proof of windstorm or flood insurance isn’t just about paperwork — it’s about ensuring everyone is protected when severe weather strikes. For landlords, it reduces risk to the property; for tenants, it safeguards personal belongings and minimizes financial loss after a storm.

If your Florida rental is located in a hurricane-prone or flood zone, expect your landlord to ask for proof of windstorm and, if applicable, flood coverage. These coverages are essential for comprehensive protection in one of the most weather-exposed states in the country.

Landlords’ Risk Tolerance: Private Insurance vs. Citizens Policies

In Florida’s rental market, landlords evaluate insurance policies not only to ensure coverage but also to manage risk exposure. The type of insurance a tenant carries—whether through a private insurer or the state-backed Citizens Property Insurance Corporation—can influence how a landlord structures lease terms.

1. Private Insurers Often Receive Greater Trust

Private insurance companies typically have strong financial ratings, broad coverage options, and flexible endorsements. Because of this:

  • Landlords may view private policies as reliable and comprehensive.

  • Lease terms may be more standard, with lower security deposits or routine inspection schedules.

  • Endorsements such as naming the landlord as an additional insured or loss payee are usually easier to implement.

The financial strength of private insurers gives landlords confidence that claims will be paid promptly and that tenants’ coverage is adequate for the property’s risk profile.

2. Citizens Policies Are Often Treated as a “Last Resort”

Citizens serves as Florida’s insurer of last resort for properties that cannot obtain private coverage. While essential, Citizens policies come with limitations:

  • Narrower coverage compared to private policies.

  • Lower coverage limits that may not fully protect high-value property.

  • Assessment risk, where policyholders could face surcharges if Citizens’ reserves are exceeded after a major storm.

Due to these factors, landlords often impose stricter lease requirements for tenants insured through Citizens, such as:

  • Higher security or damage deposits

  • More frequent property inspections

  • Additional documentation and proof of policy renewals

3. Balancing Coverage and Lease Terms

Landlords aim to reduce financial risk while maintaining compliance with Florida rental laws. By distinguishing between private and Citizens policies, they can:

  • Reward tenants with robust, private coverage by offering standard lease terms.

  • Protect their property when coverage is more limited by imposing stricter requirements.

4. What Tenants Should Know

  • Tenants insured through Citizens should be prepared to provide more frequent proof of coverage, including renewal notices and policy limits.

  • Private insurance may allow tenants to negotiate lower deposits or fewer inspections.

  • Understanding the landlord’s risk tolerance can help tenants choose the most appropriate insurance and avoid delays during move-in or lease renewal.

A landlord’s risk tolerance often varies depending on the insurer. Private insurance with strong financial ratings and broad coverage is generally accepted with standard lease terms, whereas Citizens policies may trigger stricter requirements due to narrower coverage and assessment risk.

Florida leases may require tenants to carry insurance from a private carrier, meet minimum coverage levels (such as $300,000 liability), and name the landlord as an additional insured or loss payee. These requirements protect both the tenant and landlord and ensure compliance with lease terms.

Florida Coastal Counties Face Higher Insurance Scrutiny for Tenants

Tenants renting property along Florida’s coastline — in counties such as Miami-Dade, Broward, Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Sarasota — often encounter unique challenges when it comes to insurance. These areas are particularly vulnerable to wind, hurricane, and storm surge risks, which affects both policy availability and landlord requirements.

1. Higher Risk Along the Coast

Florida’s coastal counties are more exposed to hurricanes and tropical storms due to their proximity to the Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Mexico. Properties in these areas face:

  • Strong winds capable of structural damage

  • Flooding from storm surges or heavy rainfall

  • Higher insurance claim frequency compared to inland areas

These elevated risks make private insurers more selective, sometimes refusing coverage or offering policies at substantially higher premiums.

2. Citizens Insurance as a Last Resort

Tenants in high-risk coastal counties who cannot obtain private insurance may turn to Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida’s state-backed insurer of last resort. While Citizens provides essential coverage, its policies often have:

  • Narrower coverage options than private policies

  • Lower limits on property and liability coverage

  • Potential assessment risk if claims exceed reserves

Because of these limitations, landlords are more cautious when accepting Citizens policies.

3. Stricter Proof Procedures for Landlords

Landlords in coastal counties often impose stricter insurance verification requirements, such as:

  • Requiring frequent proof of policy renewal

  • Requesting Certificates of Insurance or Declarations Pages showing active coverage

  • Verifying wind/hurricane coverage and, if applicable, flood coverage

  • Confirming policy limits meet lease minimums and that the landlord is listed as an additional insured or loss payee

These procedures help landlords mitigate risk and ensure tenants’ coverage aligns with the property’s exposure to severe weather.

Tenants in Florida’s coastal counties face higher wind and hurricane risk, making private coverage harder to secure. Citizens, Florida’s state-backed insurer of last resort, has been actively “depopulating” its policies by transferring coverage to private insurers whenever feasible. In the Tampa Bay region, for example:

  • Tens of thousands of policies have been transferred to private carriers.

  • Private insurers are increasingly writing new policies, reflecting a more competitive market.

This depopulation effort reduces Citizens’ overall exposure and encourages a return to private-market coverage.

2. Implications for Landlords

  • Frequent policy changes: Tenants may switch carriers or receive “take-out” offers from private insurers.

  • Documentation updates: Additional proof may be requested to ensure coverage limits, wind/hurricane endorsements, and landlord naming requirements are met.

The result is that landlords must remain vigilant, especially when tenants are transitioning from Citizens to private insurers, to ensure compliance with lease requirements and adequate risk protection.

3. What Tenants Should Know

Understanding Assessment Risk for Citizens Policyholders in Florida Rentals

Florida landlords should be aware of unique considerations when tenants carry Citizens Property Insurance Corporation policies. One key factor is the assessment risk, which, while not directly affecting the landlord’s property coverage, can indicate potential financial vulnerabilities in the tenant’s insurance plan.

1. What Is Assessment Risk?

Citizens is Florida’s insurer of last resort, providing coverage when private insurers are unavailable. Because it is state-backed, Citizens has the legal ability to levy assessments on policyholders if claims from catastrophic events exceed its reserves.

  • These assessments are typically calculated as a percentage of the policyholder’s premiums.

  • While rare, they can represent unexpected out-of-pocket costs for tenants.

2. Why Landlords Should Consider It

Assessment risk does not reduce the landlord’s property coverage, but it may signal:

  • Weaker financial risk management compared to private insurers

  • Potential challenges for tenants in paying premiums or maintaining coverage during market fluctuations

  • Greater overall exposure in counties with high concentrations of Citizens policyholders

Being aware of assessment risk helps landlords gauge the stability of a tenant’s insurance and anticipate any indirect impacts on the tenancy.

3. Practical Steps for Landlords

  • Require proof of active coverage and timely policy renewal, regardless of assessment risk.

  • Consider the tenant’s financial stability if relying on a Citizens policy.

  • Maintain clear lease language specifying minimum coverage limits, required endorsements, and proof submission timelines to mitigate indirect risks.

4. Balancing Risk and Coverage

While Citizens provides essential coverage for tenants unable to secure private insurance, landlords should treat these policies with caution. Ensuring documentation is current, and understanding the potential for assessments, helps maintain a strong risk management strategy for the property.

Landlords should be aware of the assessment risk associated with Citizens policies.

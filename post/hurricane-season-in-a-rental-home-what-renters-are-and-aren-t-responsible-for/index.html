Hurricane Season in a Rental Home: What Renters Are and Aren’t Responsible For

Hurricane season is a reality for renters across Florida. From June 1 through November 30, tropical storms and hurricanes can bring heavy rain, strong winds, flooding, and power outages. While landlords are responsible for maintaining a safe and habitable property, renters also have important responsibilities before, during, and after a storm.

Understanding who is responsible for what can help prevent confusion, reduce damage, and make the recovery process easier if severe weather strikes.


Landlord Responsibilities During Hurricane Season

Hurricane season can bring powerful winds, heavy rain, flooding, and unexpected property damage across Florida. While no landlord can prevent a hurricane from occurring, property owners have legal responsibilities to maintain safe and habitable rental homes before and after a storm. Understanding these responsibilities helps both landlords and tenants know what to expect if severe weather affects a rental property.

Keeping the Property in Good Condition

One of the most important landlord responsibilities is maintaining the property's structural integrity throughout the year. Proper maintenance helps reduce the risk of damage when storms occur.

Landlords are generally responsible for keeping the following in good condition:

  • Roofs that help protect the home from water intrusion.

  • Exterior walls that maintain the structural stability of the building.

  • Windows and exterior doors that close securely and help protect the property from weather.

  • Other structural components that contribute to the overall safety of the rental home.

Routine inspections and preventive maintenance can help identify issues before hurricane season begins.

Repairing Storm-Related Structural Damage

After a hurricane passes and conditions are safe, landlords are typically responsible for repairing structural damage that affects the property's habitability.

Examples may include:

  • Damaged roofing.

  • Broken windows or exterior doors.

  • Water intrusion caused by storm damage.

  • Structural damage that creates safety hazards.

Repair timelines may vary depending on contractor availability, insurance claims, and the severity of the storm, but landlords should begin the repair process as soon as reasonably possible.

Restoring Essential Building Systems

A rental property should provide essential services whenever possible. If a hurricane damages important systems, landlords are generally responsible for arranging repairs once it is safe and practical to do so.

These systems include:

  • Electrical service.

  • Plumbing.

  • Heating and air conditioning (HVAC), when provided as part of the rental.

  • Other building systems necessary for normal occupancy.

Major disasters may temporarily delay repairs due to widespread power outages, supply shortages, or emergency conditions, but landlords are generally expected to restore these services as soon as circumstances allow.

Removing Hazardous Debris

Storms often leave behind fallen trees, broken building materials, damaged fencing, and other debris that can create safety hazards.

Landlords are generally responsible for removing debris that:

  • Blocks safe access to the property.

  • Creates dangerous conditions.

  • Interferes with the safe use of the rental home.

Prompt cleanup helps reduce additional risks and allows repair work to begin more quickly.

Meeting Building and Housing Code Requirements

Property owners must continue complying with applicable local building and housing codes, even after severe weather.

This may include:

  • Making required structural repairs.

  • Addressing safety violations.

  • Obtaining permits when necessary.

  • Ensuring repairs meet current building standards.

Following local codes helps protect both the property and its occupants.

Communication Is Essential After a Hurricane

If a hurricane causes significant damage that makes the home unsafe or uninhabitable, clear communication between the landlord and tenant becomes especially important.

Landlords should provide updates regarding:

  • The extent of the damage.

  • Safety concerns.

  • Planned repair schedules.

  • Access restrictions, if any.

  • Next steps regarding the tenancy if the property cannot be occupied.

Keeping tenants informed helps reduce uncertainty during a stressful situation and allows everyone to make informed decisions.

Hurricanes are unpredictable, but landlord responsibilities remain focused on providing a safe and habitable rental property whenever possible. Maintaining the building, repairing storm-related damage, restoring essential systems, removing hazards, and complying with local building codes are all important parts of responsible property management.

Understanding these responsibilities helps create realistic expectations for both landlords and tenants before, during, and after hurricane season.


What Renters Are Responsible For During Hurricane Season

Hurricane season requires preparation from both landlords and tenants. While property owners are responsible for maintaining the rental home, renters also have important responsibilities that help protect themselves, their belongings, and the property. Understanding these obligations can reduce damage, improve safety, and make the recovery process smoother after a storm.

Follow Evacuation Orders

The safety of residents should always come first. If local emergency officials issue a mandatory evacuation order, renters should leave the property as instructed.

Ignoring evacuation orders can put lives at risk and may make it more difficult for emergency responders to provide assistance during dangerous weather conditions. Before hurricane season begins, renters should know their evacuation zone, identify evacuation routes, and prepare an emergency plan.

Secure Outdoor Belongings

Before a hurricane arrives, renters should secure or bring inside any personal items that could become dangerous in high winds.

Common items include:

  • Patio furniture

  • Bicycles

  • Grills

  • Potted plants

  • Trash bins

  • Decorations and outdoor equipment

Loose objects can become airborne during strong winds, causing damage to buildings, vehicles, and neighboring properties.

Report Property Damage Promptly

After the storm has passed and it is safe to inspect the property, renters should notify their landlord as soon as possible if they notice damage.

Examples include:

  • Roof leaks

  • Broken windows

  • Water intrusion

  • Damaged doors

  • Electrical concerns

  • Plumbing problems

Prompt reporting allows repairs to begin sooner and may help prevent additional damage from developing.

Help Prevent Further Damage When Safe

If conditions are safe and doing so does not place anyone in danger, renters should take reasonable steps to reduce additional damage while waiting for repairs.

Examples may include:

  • Moving personal belongings away from leaking areas.

  • Placing containers under active leaks.

  • Drying wet areas when possible.

  • Turning off water to a leaking fixture if it can be done safely.

These temporary measures can help minimize property damage until professional repairs are completed.

Maintain the Property Responsibly

Renters are generally responsible for keeping the rental property reasonably clean and avoiding damage caused by negligence.

This includes:

  • Properly disposing of trash.

  • Keeping drains and walkways clear when appropriate.

  • Using the property responsibly.

  • Avoiding actions that could create unnecessary damage before or after a storm.

Good property care helps maintain a safe living environment throughout the hurricane season.

Don't Delay Reporting Problems

One of the most important responsibilities after a hurricane is reporting problems immediately. Even a small roof leak or minor water intrusion can quickly become a much larger issue if left unreported.

Delaying notification may lead to:

  • Mold growth

  • Structural damage

  • Damage to flooring and walls

  • More expensive repairs

  • Longer restoration times

Early communication gives landlords the opportunity to assess the damage and arrange repairs as quickly as possible.

Preparing for hurricane season is a shared responsibility between landlords and renters. By following evacuation orders, securing personal belongings, reporting damage promptly, helping prevent additional damage when it is safe, and maintaining the rental property responsibly, tenants can help protect both their household and the home they rent.

Working together before and after a storm helps improve safety, reduce property damage, and support a faster recovery for everyone involved.


Is the Landlord Responsible for Personal Belongings?

One of the most common questions renters ask after a hurricane or other natural disaster is whether the landlord is responsible for replacing damaged personal belongings. In most cases, the answer is no.

A landlord's insurance policy is generally designed to protect the building and certain property owned by the landlord. It does not typically cover a tenant's personal possessions. Understanding this distinction can help renters prepare financially before hurricane season begins.

What Does a Landlord's Insurance Usually Cover?

Landlord insurance generally protects the rental property's structure and other items owned by the property owner. Depending on the policy, coverage may include damage to:

  • The building's roof, walls, and foundation

  • Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems

  • Permanently installed fixtures and appliances provided by the landlord

  • Common areas of the property, where applicable

While this coverage helps repair or rebuild the rental property after a covered event, it usually does not extend to a tenant's personal belongings.

What Belongings Are the Renter's Responsibility?

Personal property owned by the tenant is generally not covered under the landlord's insurance policy.

Examples include:

  • Furniture

  • Clothing

  • Electronics

  • Jewelry

  • Kitchen appliances owned by the tenant

  • Bicycles

  • Personal documents

  • Computers and tablets

  • Sporting equipment

  • Decorative items

If these belongings are damaged or destroyed by a hurricane, flood, fire, or another covered event, the renter is typically responsible for replacing them unless they have their own insurance coverage.

Why Renters Insurance Is Important

Renters insurance is one of the simplest ways to protect personal belongings from unexpected events. In hurricane-prone areas like Florida, having a renters insurance policy can provide valuable financial protection.

Depending on the policy and the cause of the loss, renters insurance may help cover:

  • Replacement or repair of personal belongings

  • Temporary living expenses if the rental becomes uninhabitable due to a covered event

  • Personal liability coverage if the tenant is legally responsible for accidental damage or injuries to others

  • Medical payments for certain guest injuries, depending on the policy

It is important to read the policy carefully, as standard renters insurance often does not cover flood damage. Renters in flood-prone areas may need to purchase separate flood insurance if they want protection against flooding.

Document Your Belongings

Before hurricane season begins, renters should create a home inventory. Taking photographs or videos of valuable items and keeping receipts when possible can make the insurance claims process much easier.

A simple inventory should include:

  • Photos or videos of each room

  • Serial numbers for electronics and appliances

  • Purchase dates and approximate values

  • Copies of important receipts stored digitally

Keeping these records in cloud storage or another secure location ensures they remain accessible even if physical documents are lost.

While landlords are generally responsible for maintaining the rental property itself, they are usually not responsible for replacing a tenant's personal belongings after a hurricane or other disaster. Protecting personal property is primarily the renter's responsibility.

Purchasing renters insurance, understanding policy exclusions, and maintaining an up-to-date inventory of belongings can help renters recover more quickly after unexpected events and reduce financial losses.


Why Renters Insurance Matters During Hurricane Season

Hurricane season can bring strong winds, heavy rain, power outages, and unexpected property damage. While a landlord's insurance typically protects the rental building itself, it usually does not cover a tenant's personal belongings. That's why renters insurance is an important part of being prepared, especially for those living in hurricane-prone areas.

A renters insurance policy can help reduce the financial impact of unexpected events by protecting your personal property and providing additional benefits when covered losses occur.

Protecting Your Personal Belongings

One of the primary benefits of renters insurance is personal property coverage. If your belongings are damaged or destroyed by a covered event, your policy may help pay for repair or replacement costs.

Items commonly covered include:

  • Furniture

  • Clothing

  • Electronics

  • Kitchen appliances you own

  • Bicycles

  • Books

  • Home décor

  • Personal items and household goods

Coverage limits and exclusions vary by policy, so it's important to understand exactly what your insurance includes.

Temporary Living Expenses

If a covered event makes your rental home unsafe or uninhabitable, renters insurance may help pay for temporary housing while repairs are being completed.

Depending on your policy, this may include expenses such as:

  • Hotel accommodations

  • Short-term rental housing

  • Restaurant meals beyond your normal food expenses

  • Other necessary living costs incurred while you cannot occupy your home

This coverage is often referred to as Additional Living Expenses (ALE) or Loss of Use coverage.

Personal Liability Protection

Renters insurance often includes personal liability coverage, which may help protect you financially if you are found legally responsible for accidental injuries to another person or damage to someone else's property.

For example, liability coverage may help if:

  • A guest is injured inside your rental home.

  • You accidentally cause damage to a neighboring apartment.

  • Legal expenses arise from a covered liability claim.

Policy limits and exclusions vary, so reviewing your coverage is essential.

Additional Living Expenses During Repairs

Major storms can leave rental properties uninhabitable for days or even weeks. If your policy includes Additional Living Expenses coverage, it may help offset the extra costs of maintaining your normal standard of living while your home is being repaired after a covered loss.

This financial support can make a significant difference during an already stressful recovery period.

Understand What Isn't Covered

Although renters insurance provides valuable protection, it does not cover every type of damage. One of the most important exclusions for renters in hurricane-prone regions is flooding.

Standard renters insurance policies typically do not cover damage caused by:

  • Floodwaters

  • Storm surge

  • Rising rivers or overflowing bodies of water

If your rental property is located in or near a flood-prone area, you may want to consider purchasing a separate flood insurance policy.

Review Your Policy Before Hurricane Season

Before hurricane season begins, take time to review your insurance policy and make sure your coverage meets your needs.

Consider checking:

  • Coverage limits for personal belongings

  • Deductible amounts

  • High-value item coverage for jewelry, electronics, or collectibles

  • Additional Living Expenses coverage

  • Liability coverage limits

  • Whether flood insurance is needed

Understanding your policy before a storm can help prevent surprises during the claims process.

Renters insurance is one of the most effective ways to protect yourself financially during hurricane season. While it cannot prevent storm damage, it can help cover the cost of replacing personal belongings, provide temporary housing if your rental becomes uninhabitable, and offer liability protection in certain situations.

Because standard renters insurance generally does not include flood coverage, renters should carefully review their policy and consider separate flood insurance if they live in an area at risk of flooding.


Preparing Before a Hurricane

Hurricane season is a fact of life for many coastal communities, and being prepared before a storm arrives can make a significant difference. Taking simple steps in advance can help protect your belongings, reduce stress during an emergency, and support a faster recovery afterward.

Whether you are a renter or a landlord, having a hurricane preparedness plan is one of the best ways to stay safe and minimize potential losses.

Create a Hurricane Preparedness Checklist

Preparing ahead of time allows you to focus on your safety when severe weather is approaching. A well-organized checklist ensures that important tasks are not overlooked.

Consider including the following items in your hurricane preparation plan.

Photograph Valuable Belongings

Take clear photos or videos of your personal belongings before hurricane season begins. Documenting your possessions can make the insurance claims process easier if items are damaged or lost.

Include valuable items such as:

  • Furniture

  • Electronics

  • Jewelry

  • Appliances you own

  • Bicycles

  • Collectibles

Store your photos in cloud storage or another secure digital location so they remain accessible if your devices are damaged.

Back Up Important Documents

Keep digital copies of essential documents in a secure location. Important records may include:

  • Identification documents

  • Insurance policies

  • Lease agreements

  • Medical records

  • Financial documents

  • Vehicle registration and title information

Having electronic copies can save valuable time if the originals are lost or damaged.

Charge Phones and Backup Batteries

Power outages are common during hurricanes and may last for several days. Before a storm arrives, fully charge:

  • Mobile phones

  • Portable power banks

  • Rechargeable batteries

  • Flashlights

  • Medical devices that require charging, if applicable

Keeping backup charging options available helps you stay informed and maintain communication during emergencies.

Prepare Emergency Supplies

Emergency management agencies recommend keeping enough supplies to be self-sufficient for several days.

A basic emergency kit may include:

  • Drinking water

  • Nonperishable food

  • Prescription medications

  • Flashlights

  • Extra batteries

  • First aid supplies

  • Battery-powered or hand-crank radio

  • Personal hygiene items

  • Pet supplies, if needed

Review your supplies each year and replace expired items as necessary.

Move Valuable Items Away from Windows

If time allows before the storm, move valuable belongings away from windows and areas that could be vulnerable to wind-driven rain or broken glass.

Consider placing important items:

  • On higher shelves if flooding is a concern

  • In waterproof storage containers

  • In interior rooms away from exterior windows

These simple precautions may help reduce damage to your personal property.

Know Your Evacuation Routes

If local officials issue an evacuation order, knowing where to go ahead of time can save valuable time.

Before hurricane season:

  • Identify your evacuation zone.

  • Review local evacuation routes.

  • Decide where you will stay if evacuation becomes necessary.

  • Plan transportation for family members and pets.

Always follow instructions from local emergency management officials.

Save Important Emergency Contacts

Keep a list of important phone numbers that you can access even if your mobile device is unavailable.

Helpful contacts include:

  • Your landlord or property manager

  • Insurance company

  • Local emergency management office

  • Utility providers

  • Family members or emergency contacts

  • Nearby medical facilities

Consider saving these numbers on paper as well as digitally.

Preparation Benefits Everyone

Hurricane preparedness is a shared responsibility. Renters who protect their belongings and follow emergency guidance can reduce personal losses, while landlords who maintain their properties and communicate effectively can help speed the recovery process.

Preparing before a hurricane allows everyone to respond more confidently and recover more quickly once the storm has passed.

No one can control the weather, but everyone can take steps to be better prepared. Creating a hurricane checklist, protecting important documents, assembling emergency supplies, and planning for evacuation can make a difficult situation much more manageable.

A little preparation today can help protect your safety, your property, and your peace of mind when hurricane season arrives.


What to Do After the Storm

The hours and days following a hurricane can be overwhelming, but taking the right steps can help protect your safety, simplify insurance claims, and speed up the recovery process. Whether you are a renter or a landlord, staying organized and documenting any damage is essential.

Once local officials announce that it is safe to return, carefully assess the situation before beginning any cleanup.

Inspect the Property Carefully

Before entering the property, look for any visible hazards such as downed power lines, broken glass, damaged roofs, or unstable structures. If you suspect the building has suffered significant structural damage, avoid entering until it has been declared safe by the appropriate authorities.

As you inspect the property, check for:

  • Roof damage

  • Broken windows or doors

  • Water intrusion

  • Flooding

  • Electrical hazards

  • Plumbing issues

  • Fallen trees or debris

Your safety should always come first. If you notice dangerous conditions, contact the appropriate emergency services or utility providers.

Photograph Any Damage

Before moving debris or cleaning up, take clear photos and videos of all visible damage.

Document items such as:

  • Damaged rooms

  • Water damage

  • Broken furniture

  • Damaged electronics

  • Structural damage

  • Exterior damage to the building

Photographs provide valuable evidence for both insurance claims and repair documentation. Be sure to save digital copies in a secure location.

Notify Your Landlord Promptly

If you are renting the property, contact your landlord or property manager as soon as possible after discovering any damage.

Provide:

  • A description of the damage

  • Photos or videos if available

  • Information about any urgent safety concerns

Prompt communication allows the landlord to arrange inspections and begin repairs as quickly as possible.

Contact Your Insurance Company

If your personal belongings were damaged by a covered event, notify your renters insurance company as soon as possible.

Be prepared to provide:

  • Your policy number

  • A list of damaged items

  • Photos or videos

  • Receipts, if available

  • Any other documentation requested by your insurer

Starting the claims process early can help reduce delays and ensure your claim is handled more efficiently.

Keep Receipts for Emergency Purchases

During the recovery process, you may need to purchase emergency supplies or temporary accommodations. If your insurance policy includes reimbursement for covered expenses, keeping detailed records is essential.

Save receipts for items such as:

  • Hotel stays

  • Temporary housing

  • Emergency meals, if covered

  • Cleaning supplies

  • Temporary repairs

  • Replacement necessities

Organizing these receipts can make it easier to support your insurance claim.

Avoid Making Permanent Repairs Too Soon

While it is often appropriate to take temporary measures to prevent additional damage, avoid making permanent repairs until your landlord or insurance company has had an opportunity to inspect the property, unless immediate action is necessary for safety.

Temporary measures may include:

  • Covering a damaged roof with a tarp

  • Placing containers under leaks

  • Boarding up broken windows if it is safe to do so

Always follow the guidance provided by your landlord and insurance company regarding repairs.

Good Documentation Makes Recovery Easier

Accurate documentation is one of the most important parts of recovering after a hurricane. Keeping organized records of photos, receipts, communications, and insurance documents can help reduce disputes and speed up the claims process.

A simple record should include:

  • Photos and videos of damage

  • Dates and times of inspections

  • Copies of emails and text messages

  • Insurance claim numbers

  • Receipts for emergency expenses

These records can be invaluable if questions arise later.

Recovering after a hurricane takes time, but following a clear plan can make the process more manageable. By inspecting the property carefully, documenting damage, notifying your landlord promptly, contacting your insurance provider, and keeping receipts for emergency expenses, you can help protect your interests and support a smoother recovery.

Preparation and thorough documentation are key to rebuilding after severe weather and minimizing unnecessary stress.

Can Rent Be Reduced if the Property Is Damaged?

Hurricanes can cause anything from minor roof leaks to severe structural damage that makes a rental home unsafe to occupy. When this happens, many tenants wonder whether they are still required to pay full rent. The answer depends on several factors, including the extent of the damage, the terms of the lease agreement, and Florida law.

Understanding your rights and responsibilities can help you make informed decisions after a storm.

The Extent of the Damage Matters

Not all hurricane damage affects a rental property in the same way. A broken fence or damaged landscaping may have little impact on the home's livability, while significant structural damage or the loss of essential services may make the property partially or completely uninhabitable.

Examples of serious damage may include:

  • Major roof damage causing extensive water intrusion

  • Structural damage that creates safety hazards

  • Extended loss of electricity, plumbing, or other essential services

  • Severe flooding that prevents safe occupancy

The severity of the damage often determines what options may be available to both the landlord and the tenant.

Review Your Lease Agreement

Your lease is one of the first documents you should review after hurricane-related damage occurs. Some lease agreements include provisions addressing natural disasters, repairs, temporary relocation, or what happens if the property becomes uninhabitable.

Review the lease for information about:

  • Repair responsibilities

  • Temporary housing arrangements

  • Rent obligations during repairs

  • Lease termination provisions

  • Notice requirements

Understanding these terms can help clarify the next steps.

Florida Law May Provide Certain Rights

Florida law addresses situations in which a rental property is damaged or destroyed to the point that it substantially affects its habitability. Depending on the circumstances, tenants may have certain legal rights regarding rent obligations or the ability to terminate the lease.

However, each situation is unique. The outcome may depend on factors such as:

  • The extent of the damage

  • Whether the property can still be safely occupied

  • How quickly repairs can reasonably be completed

  • The specific terms of the lease agreement

Because every case is different, there is no single answer that applies to all hurricane-related damage.

Communicate with Your Landlord Promptly

If your rental home has been damaged, notify your landlord or property manager as soon as it is safe to do so.

When contacting your landlord:

  • Describe the damage clearly.

  • Provide photographs if possible.

  • Ask about the repair timeline.

  • Keep copies of emails, text messages, and written correspondence.

Open communication can help both parties understand the situation and work toward an appropriate resolution.

Keep Detailed Records

Maintaining thorough documentation is important if questions arise about repairs, insurance claims, or rent obligations.

Helpful records include:

  • Photos and videos of the damage

  • Copies of written communications

  • Repair estimates, if available

  • Inspection reports

  • Receipts for temporary housing or emergency expenses, when applicable

Well-organized documentation can be valuable if the situation becomes more complicated.

Seek Legal Guidance if Necessary

If the property is significantly damaged and there is uncertainty about rent payments, repairs, or lease termination, tenants may wish to seek legal advice.

An attorney or qualified tenant assistance organization can explain how Florida law applies to the specific circumstances and help tenants understand their available options.

Whether rent can be reduced after hurricane damage depends on the condition of the property, the lease agreement, and applicable Florida law. If a rental home becomes partially or completely uninhabitable, tenants may have legal rights regarding rent or lease termination, but those rights vary based on the facts of each case.

The best first steps are to document the damage, communicate promptly with the landlord, review the lease carefully, and seek professional legal guidance if needed. Taking these actions can help ensure that both landlords and tenants navigate the recovery process fairly and in accordance with the law.


Communication Is Key During Hurricane Season

When hurricane season approaches, one of the most effective ways to reduce stress and confusion is through clear communication between landlords and tenants. While no one can control the weather, discussing expectations before a storm arrives can help everyone respond more effectively and recover more quickly.

Establishing a plan before hurricane season begins ensures that both parties understand their responsibilities and know how to stay in contact if an emergency occurs.

Share Emergency Contact Information

Reliable communication starts with having up-to-date contact information.

Both landlords and tenants should exchange:

  • Primary phone numbers

  • Email addresses

  • Emergency contact information

  • Alternative methods of communication if phone service is interrupted

Keeping this information current can help prevent delays when important updates need to be shared.

Understand Maintenance Reporting Procedures

Storms can cause roof leaks, broken windows, flooding, and other property damage. Tenants should know exactly how to report maintenance issues before an emergency happens.

Discuss questions such as:

  • Who should be contacted if damage occurs?

  • Is there an emergency maintenance phone number?

  • How should after-hours emergencies be reported?

  • What information should be included when reporting damage?

Prompt reporting allows landlords to assess the situation and arrange repairs as soon as it is safe to do so.

Discuss How to Secure the Property

Preparing the property before a hurricane is a shared responsibility. Landlords and tenants should discuss what each person is expected to do before severe weather arrives.

Topics may include:

  • Securing outdoor furniture and decorations

  • Protecting windows, if applicable

  • Moving personal belongings away from windows

  • Storing bicycles, grills, and other outdoor items

  • Following any property-specific hurricane preparation guidelines

Clarifying these responsibilities ahead of time helps reduce misunderstandings when a storm is approaching.

Review Insurance Coverage

Insurance is another important topic to discuss before hurricane season.

Landlords should understand the coverage provided by their property insurance, while tenants should review their renters insurance policy to determine what is and is not covered.

Helpful questions include:

  • Does the tenant have renters insurance?

  • Does the policy include Additional Living Expenses coverage?

  • Is separate flood insurance needed?

  • Who is responsible for personal belongings after storm damage?

Understanding insurance coverage before an emergency can help avoid surprises during the recovery process.

Make an Evacuation Plan

If local officials issue an evacuation order, everyone should know what to do.

Landlords and tenants should discuss:

  • Local evacuation zones

  • Safe evacuation routes

  • How communication will continue during an evacuation

  • When tenants should notify the landlord if they evacuate

  • Procedures for returning to the property after officials declare it safe

Planning ahead allows everyone to respond more quickly if evacuation becomes necessary.

Keep Communication Open After the Storm

After the hurricane has passed, continued communication remains essential.

Tenants should:

  • Report damage promptly.

  • Share photos when possible.

  • Inform the landlord of any urgent safety concerns.

Landlords should:

  • Acknowledge reports of damage.

  • Provide updates on inspections and repairs.

  • Communicate expected timelines when possible.

  • Inform tenants if temporary access restrictions are necessary.

Regular updates help build trust and reduce uncertainty during the recovery process.

Preparation Benefits Everyone

Clear communication benefits both landlords and tenants. When expectations are discussed before hurricane season, emergencies can be handled more efficiently, repairs can begin sooner, and misunderstandings are less likely to occur.

Working together before, during, and after a storm helps protect people, property, and peace of mind.

Good communication is one of the most valuable tools for hurricane preparedness. By discussing emergency contacts, maintenance procedures, property preparation, insurance coverage, and evacuation plans before a storm arrives, landlords and tenants can work together more effectively when severe weather occurs.

Knowing everyone's responsibilities ahead of time can reduce confusion, improve safety, and support a smoother recovery after the storm.

Hurricane season requires preparation from both landlords and renters. Property owners are generally responsible for maintaining the building and making necessary repairs, while renters are responsible for protecting their personal belongings, following safety instructions, and reporting damage promptly.

By understanding these responsibilities, carrying appropriate insurance, and preparing before storms arrive, renters can better protect themselves and recover more efficiently after severe weather.
